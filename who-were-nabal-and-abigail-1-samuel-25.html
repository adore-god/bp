Who Were Nabal and Abigail? 1 Samuel 25

1 Samuel 25 gives you the key itself. Abigail says of her husband: "as his name is, so is he" (25:25). In this course the answer to why the story runs as it does is in the names. Nabal means "fool", Abigail means "source of joy", David means "beloved", and the field is Carmel, "fruitful field". A fool stands in a fruitful field, Beloved comes up with four hundred swords, and Source of Joy steps between them. Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The passage (BBE, selected verses):

And death came to Samuel; and all Israel came together, weeping for him, and put his body in its resting-place in his house at Ramah. Then David went down to the waste land of Maon. Now there was a man in Maon whose business was in Carmel; he was a great man and had three thousand sheep and a thousand goats: and he was cutting the wool of his sheep in Carmel. Now this man was named Nabal, and his wife's name was Abigail: she was a woman of good sense and pleasing looks: but the man was cruel and evil in his ways; he was of the family of Caleb. And Nabal gave them his answer and said, Who is David? who is the son of Jesse? there are a number of servants in these days running away from their masters. Am I to take my bread and my wine and the meat I have got ready for my wool-cutters and give it to men coming from I have no idea where? And David said to his men, Put on your swords, every one of you. And every man put on his sword; and David did the same; and about four hundred men went up with David, and two hundred kept watch over their goods. And she said to her young men, Go on in front of me and I will come after you. But she said nothing to her husband Nabal. Now David had said, What was the use of my taking care of this man's goods in the waste land, so that there was no loss of anything which was his? he has only given me back evil for good. May God's punishment be on David, if when morning comes there is so much as one male of his people still living. And when Abigail saw David, she quickly got off her ass, falling down on her face before him. And falling at his feet she said, May the wrong be on me, my lord, on me: let your servant say a word to you, and give ear to the words of your servant. Let my lord give no attention to Nabal, that good-for-nothing: for as his name is, so is he, a man without sense: but I, your servant, did not see the young men whom my lord sent. So now, my lord, by the living God and by your living soul, seeing that the Lord has kept you from the crime of blood and from taking into your hands the punishment for your wrongs, may all your haters, and those who would do evil to my lord, be like Nabal. A blessing on your good sense and on you, who have kept me today from the crime of blood and from taking into my hands the punishment for my wrongs. And Abigail went back to Nabal; and he was feasting in his house like a king; and Nabal's heart was full of joy, for he had taken much wine; so she said nothing to him till dawn came. And in the morning, when the effect of the wine was gone, Nabal's wife gave him an account of all these things, and all the heart went out of him, and he became like stone. And about ten days after, the Lord sent disease on Nabal and death came to him. And David, hearing that Nabal was dead, said, May the Lord be praised, who has taken up my cause against Nabal for the shame which he put on me, and has kept back his servant from evil, and has sent on Nabal's head the reward of his evil-doing. And David sent word to Abigail, desiring to take her as his wife. Then Abigail got up quickly and went on her ass, with five of her young women, after the men whom David had sent; and she became David's wife. — 1 Samuel 25:1-3, 10-11, 13, 19, 21-26, 33, 36-39, 42 (BBE)

The meanings used: Nabal is "fool" (H5037, the same word as H5036, "foolish, senseless"; Strong's glosses it "dolt"). Abigail is "source of joy" (H26, from H1, father or source, and H1524, joy). David is "beloved" (H1732, from the same word as H1730, "loving"). Carmel is "fruitful field" (H3760) and Maon is "habitation" (H4584). Samuel is "heard of God" (H8050), Israel is "he will rule as God" (H3478) and Ramah is "height" (H7414). Jesse is "extant" (H3448, from H3426, "there is"). Caleb is "dog" (H3612). The Lord (YHVH) is "the Existing One" (H3068) and "God" (Elohim) is "the Judges". As in Lesson 1, the Existing One is you, aware right now.

Four notes on the meanings. First, verse 1: the BBE says David went down to "the waste land of Maon". The Hebrew text reads "the wilderness of Paran" there (H6290: Strong's says "ornamental", the Blue Letter Bible says "place of caverns", so the two sources differ). This lesson follows the BBE, and nothing below depends on that verse. Second, verse 26: the BBE says "by the living God", but the Hebrew reads "as YHVH lives", and YHVH is also the one who "has kept you". So the meanings version says "the Existing One" in both places. In verse 22 the Hebrew is Elohim, so "the Judges" is right there, though the Hebrew oath says "to the enemies of David" where the BBE says "on David". Third, Jesse: Strong's says "extant", and the Blue Letter Bible glosses the name "I possess". This lesson uses Strong's and flags the other. Fourth, the family of Caleb: Strong's says the Hebrew word in 25:3 is H3614, probably a mis-transcription of "Calebite", and that Caleb (H3612) is "perhaps a form of keleb, dog, or from a root meaning forcible". Nothing below is built on it.

How to read it, step by step:

  1. The names. The text states the rule itself: "as his name is, so is he, a man without sense" (1 Samuel 25:25, BBE). That is Lesson 9 inside the verse: a name tells you the state before the story starts. The man is named Fool, and he is "cruel and evil in his ways" (25:3, BBE). His wife is named Source of Joy, and she is "a woman of good sense and pleasing looks" (25:3, BBE). Two states are set side by side in one house, as Cain and Abel were in Lesson 17. Beloved, meanwhile, goes down to the waste land after "Heard of God" is put to rest at the Height (25:1).
  2. Day three and day six: the scene is built. Fool "had three thousand sheep and a thousand goats" and "his business was in Carmel" (25:2, BBE), where "he was cutting the wool of his sheep" (25:2, BBE). Carmel is "fruitful field": day three, the ground bringing fruit (Genesis 1:11). The sheep and goats are day six, the animals man is given rule over (Genesis 1:26). He is "a great man" (25:2, BBE) and later feasts "like a king" (25:36, BBE). So the Genesis 1 scene is complete around him. What is missing is not the scene. It is the sense.
  3. The question. Beloved's young men come "in David's name" and ask for "anything you may have by you" (25:8-9, BBE). Fool answers: "Who is David? who is the son of Jesse?" (25:10, BBE). With the meanings in: Who is Beloved? Who is the son of Extant? He asks the question of the name and answers it with a runaway servant. Then he lists what he holds: "my bread and my wine and the meat I have got ready" (25:11, BBE), and gives it to no one "coming from I have no idea where". The text never says why he refuses. It shows a man whose whole answer is about what he has.
  4. The swords. "Put on your swords, every one of you", and "about four hundred men went up with David" (25:13, BBE). Then the oath: "May God's punishment be on David, if when morning comes there is so much as one male of his people still living" (25:22, BBE). The oath is made in front of the Judges. Beloved is about to do the judging himself, on a whole house, for a word. This is the Cain scene again (Lesson 17): the attack is coming, and "he has only given me back evil for good" (25:21, BBE) is the anger behind it. In Genesis 1 the judging is the Judges' work: they look at what they have spoken and rule (Genesis 1:12).
  5. Source of Joy steps in. "Go on in front of me and I will come after you. But she said nothing to her husband Nabal" (25:19, BBE). This is Lesson 8, Genesis 2:24: step away from the old state to join the new one. Source of Joy leaves the house of Fool without a word to Fool. She meets Beloved and falls on her face: "May the wrong be on me, my lord, on me" (25:24, BBE). She does not defend Fool: "give no attention to Nabal, that good-for-nothing" (25:25, BBE). And she names what Beloved is about to do: "the crime of blood and ... taking into your hands the punishment for your wrongs" (25:26, BBE). She turns the name into a state anyone can fall into: "may all your haters ... be like Nabal" (25:26, BBE). David's answer names what stopped him: "A blessing on your good sense" (25:33, BBE). The good sense named in 25:3 is what stops the sword.
  6. The feast, the heart and the stone. She goes back and finds Fool "feasting in his house like a king; and Nabal's heart was full of joy, for he had taken much wine" (25:36, BBE). The joy is in the wine, in the house of Source of Joy. In the morning, "when the effect of the wine was gone ... all the heart went out of him, and he became like stone" (25:37, BBE). In Lesson 18 the heart is the mind. The mind goes out, and what is left is stone, a state with nothing in it that moves. About ten days later "the Lord sent disease on Nabal and death came to him" (25:38, BBE). David says the Lord "has sent on Nabal's head the reward of his evil-doing" (25:39, BBE). What he did comes back on him, after its kind (Genesis 1:11). As with Pharaoh in Lesson 18, the heart going out and the Existing One sending are one event seen from two sides. And David's sword never falls: the Existing One "has kept back his servant from evil" (25:39, BBE).
  7. Leave and join, finished. David "sent word to Abigail, desiring to take her as his wife" (25:39, BBE), and "she became David's wife" (25:42, BBE). Source of Joy, who left the house of Fool, is now joined to Beloved. That is Genesis 2:24 completed: leave the old state, join the new identity. The name of the old state is still on the ground in Carmel, and the one with sense is no longer in it.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. 1 Samuel 25 keeps the field, the animals, the judging and the leaving, and changes who is doing what.

PartGenesis 1 and 2This scene
Field and animalsThe ground brings fruit (day three, Genesis 1:11), and man has rule over the animals (day six, Genesis 1:26)Fool has Fruitful Field and three thousand sheep and a thousand goats (25:2). The scene is built, and the one in it is "a man without sense" (25:25)
The judgingThe Judges look at what they have spoken and rule (Genesis 1:12)Beloved swears to do the judging himself (25:22). Source of Joy brings it back: "the punishment for your wrongs" is not his to take (25:26, 33)
Leave and joinLeave the old state and join the new one (Genesis 2:24)Source of Joy leaves Fool's house without telling him (25:19) and ends up Beloved's wife (25:42)
After its kindSeed brings forth after its kind (Genesis 1:11)"The reward of his evil-doing" comes back on Fool's own head (25:39), and "be like Nabal" becomes a state others can fall into (25:26)

What changed is who is judging. In Genesis 1 the Judges look and rule. Here Beloved is about to do it himself with four hundred swords, and he is stopped by the part of the scene named Source of Joy, who has sense. Then the name Fool gives back what it was: a man with a built scene and no sense in it.

See where the attack on a brother is the same move: Cain and Abel.

Remember: Nabal means fool, Abigail means source of joy, and David means beloved. The text says it plainly: "as his name is, so is he." Fool sits in a fruitful field with everything Genesis 1 built and no sense in it. Beloved goes up with four hundred swords to do the judging himself. Source of Joy leaves the house of Fool, stops him, and is joined to him. What Fool did comes back on his own head.
