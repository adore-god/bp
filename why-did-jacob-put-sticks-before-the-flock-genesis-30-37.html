Why Did Jacob Put Sticks in Front of the Flock? Genesis 30-31

Jacob names his payment, and the same day Laban takes away every animal that matches it. Jacob is left with a flock that has none of the marks, and the flock still bears marked young. People ask whether this is a breeding trick or God doing it. The text gives both: Jacob sets the branches (Genesis 30:37-41), and Jacob says God took the cattle and gave them to him (31:9). In this course it is one event seen from two sides, and it is the Genesis 1 picture running on a farm. Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The passage (BBE, selected verses):

The meanings used: Jacob is yaʿăqōb (H3290), "heel-catcher (i.e. supplanter)", from aqab (H6117), "to follow at the heel" and so "to circumvent"; Genesis 27:36 uses that verb of him. Laban is lavan (H3837), "white", the same word as the colour white (H3836). God is Elohim (H430), the Judges, as in Lesson 3.

Three notes on the meanings. First, the BBE says "young branches of trees", but the Hebrew names the trees, and the first is the poplar, libneh (H3839). It comes from the same root as Laban's name (H3835) and is used only twice in the Bible, here and in Hosea 4:13. Second, the BBE says "banded" or "bands" for three different things: the goats Laban takes out (30:35), the sticks (30:37-38) and the young (30:39). For the animals the Hebrew word is aqod (H6124), used seven times (30:35, 39, 40; 31:8 twice; 31:10, 12), so the marks Laban removes in 30:35 are the marks the flock bears in 30:39 and the marks Laban names in 31:8. The sticks are not called aqod. For the sticks the Hebrew says white (H3836, twice in 30:37), the same word used in 30:35 of the goats with white marks. Third, the common readings say either that this was a breeding method or that God overrode the flock. The text says Jacob did the sticks (30:37-41), that God took and gave (31:9), and that Jacob saw the marked goats in a dream at the time the flock were with young (31:10).

How to read it, step by step:

  1. Two names carry the story. Jacob is the heel-catcher, the one who takes the place of what stood there before (H3290). Laban is "white" (H3837), the plain colour. The story acts out both names, and the next steps show how.
  2. The payment is named first. Jacob says "these will be my payment" (Genesis 30:32) and sets his own test: "if you see among my flocks any goats which are not marked or coloured, or any sheep which is not black, you may take me for a thief" (30:33, BBE). Laban answers, "Let it be as you say" (30:34). Nothing marked is in Jacob's hands yet. The claim comes before any proof.
  3. The proof is removed the same day. Laban takes out every banded, marked or coloured goat and every black sheep, and sends them three days away (30:35-36). The one named White takes the white marks out of the flock (30:35). Then Jacob cuts the skin off the branches "so that the white wood was seen" (30:37), and the same word, white, shows again in front of the flock. What was taken out is shown back to the flock.
  4. The picture is held where the flock drinks. The sticks go "in the drinking-places where the flock came to get water", and the flock "became with young when they came to the water" (30:38). The text says it again: "so that they might become with young when they saw the sticks" (30:41, BBE). In this reading the sticks are the named payment made visible, set where the flock keeps coming back. Jacob holds the same picture himself: "at the time when the flock were with young, I saw in a dream that all the he-goats ... were banded and marked and coloured" (31:10, BBE). The young are born marked, though the flock he was left with had none of the marks (30:39).
  5. The picture is set only where it is meant to stand. Jacob puts the sticks before the stronger ones and not before the feeble, "so that the feebler flocks were Laban's and the stronger were Jacob's" (30:42, BBE). What is held is held on purpose, and the result follows where it is held.
  6. Ten changes, the same result. Jacob says Laban changed his payment ten times (31:7). Whatever Laban named as Jacob's pay, the flock bore it: "if he said, All those in the flock which have marks are to be yours, then all the flock gave birth to marked young; and if he said, All the banded ones are to be yours, then all the flock had banded young" (31:8, BBE). The terms keep moving and the result moves with them. The one named heel-catcher takes the place of each new term.
  7. Who gave it. Jacob does not say it was the sticks. He says "God has taken away your father's cattle and has given them to me" (31:9, BBE). In this course God here is Elohim, the Judges. Jacob names and holds the picture, and the Judges take and give. That is one event seen from two sides, as in the Pharaoh lesson.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. The same three things from the third day of Genesis 1 are here, in the same order: the waters, the tree, and the sort. So is the sixth day: man given rule over the animals.

What changed is where the sort comes from. In Genesis 1 the seed is in the thing itself, and each one brings forth after its own sort. Here the marks are not in the flock at all, because Laban took every marked animal out (30:35-36). The young still come marked, and in this reading that is because the picture stands in front of the flock where it drinks (30:38, 41) and in front of Jacob in his dream (31:10). The sort follows what is held, not what was in the flock he was given. It stands as firmly as what the Judges spoke in Genesis 1, even with the proof taken away.

The sixth day explains why the flock answers to Jacob and not to its own parents or to Laban. Man is made to "have rule over" the cattle (Genesis 1:26), and the flock is what he is given rule over. In this reading Jacob is the one who holds the picture, so the flock follows him, and Laban changing the terms ten times does not move it. The text does not use the word rule of Jacob. This is the Genesis 1 picture applied to him: the rule is man's, and the Judges enforce it.

Compare the Pharaoh lesson, where the state held was the old one and it stood. Here the state held is the new one, and it stands in the same way.

Remember: Jacob names the payment before anything marked is left in his flock, and holds the picture where the flock drinks. The marks come from what is held, not from the flock he was given, and the Judges are the ones who give it. Laban changes the terms ten times and the result follows the held picture each time.
