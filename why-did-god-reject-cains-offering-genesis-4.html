Why Did God Reject Cain's Offering? Genesis 4

Genesis 4 never says why God accepted Abel's offering and rejected Cain's. In this course the answer is in the names and in the pleasure: Cain means "possession", Abel means "breath", and Eden, where the identity first stood, means "pleasure". Cain's offering brought none. Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The passage (BBE, selected verses):

And the man had connection with Eve his wife, and she became with child and gave birth to Cain, and said, I have got a man from the Lord. Then again she became with child and gave birth to Abel, his brother. And Abel was a keeper of sheep, but Cain was a farmer. And after a time, Cain gave to the Lord an offering of the fruits of the earth. And Abel gave an offering of the young lambs of his flock and of their fat. And the Lord was pleased with Abel's offering; But in Cain and his offering he had no pleasure. And Cain was angry and his face became sad. And the Lord said to Cain, Why are you angry? and why is your face sad? If you do well, will you not have honour? and if you do wrong, sin is waiting at the door, desiring to have you, but do not let it be your master. And Cain said to his brother, Let us go into the field: and when they were in the field, Cain made an attack on his brother Abel and put him to death. And the Lord said, Truly, if Cain is put to death, seven lives will be taken for his. And the Lord put a mark on Cain so that no one might put him to death. And Cain went away from before the face of the Lord, and made his living-place in the land of Nod on the east of Eden. — Genesis 4:1-8, 15-16 (BBE)

The same verses with the meanings put in:

And the man had connection with Eve his wife, and she became with child and gave birth to Possession, and said, I have got a man from the Existing One. Then again she became with child and gave birth to Breath, his brother. And Breath was a keeper of sheep, but Possession was a farmer. And after a time, Possession gave to the Existing One an offering of the fruits of the earth. And Breath gave an offering of the young lambs of his flock and of their fat. And the Existing One was pleased with Breath's offering; But in Possession and his offering he had no pleasure. And Possession was angry and his face became sad. And the Existing One said to Possession, Why are you angry? and why is your face sad? If you do well, will you not have honour? and if you do wrong, sin is waiting at the door, desiring to have you, but do not let it be your master. And Possession said to his brother, Let us go into the field: and when they were in the field, Possession made an attack on his brother Breath and put him to death. And the Existing One said, Truly, if Possession is put to death, seven lives will be taken for his. And the Existing One put a mark on Possession so that no one might put him to death. And Possession went away from before the face of the Existing One, and made his living-place in the land of Nod on the east of Pleasure.

The meanings used: Cain is "possession" (H7014), a play on the verb to get or acquire (H7069). Abel is "breath" (H1893), the same word as hebel, breath or vapour, which Ecclesiastes uses for "vanity" (H1892). Eden is "pleasure" (H5731). The Lord (YHVH) is "the existing One" (H3068).

Three notes on the meanings. First, the BBE says "pleased" and "no pleasure", and the KJV says "respect". The Hebrew verb behind both is shaah, "to look at, regard" (H8159). So "pleasure" is the English, and the Hebrew says the Existing One looked at one offering and not the other. The link to Eden's pleasure comes through the verses around it (steps 2 and 5). Second, the text never calls the fruit bad. In this reading the point is that it is fruit already gathered, not seed. Third, the common readings say Cain brought the wrong kind of gift or brought it in the wrong spirit. Genesis 4 states neither.

How to read it, step by step:

  1. The names. Eve says "I have got a man from the Lord" (Genesis 4:1, BBE). That is Cain's name acted out: possession, from the word for get. Then comes Abel, whose name, hebel (H1892), is breath or vapour, which Strong's glosses as something transitory. So the names set two states side by side: one that grips, and one that passes. As with Isaac in Lesson 16, the story acts out the name.
  2. Day three and day six. Cain works the ground and brings "the fruits of the earth" (Genesis 4:3, BBE). That is day three: the ground and the fruit with its seed "in" it (Genesis 1:11). Fruit is what has already been gathered, already possessed. It is not the seed. Abel keeps animals and brings "young lambs of his flock and of their fat" (4:4, BBE). That is day six: man made like the Judges, with "rule over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the air and over the cattle and over all the earth" (Genesis 1:26, BBE). Cain brings a result he holds. Abel brings the identity ruling.
  3. The look and the pleasure. The identity first stood in Eden, "pleasure" (Genesis 2:8), and in Genesis 1 the Judges look at what they have spoken and call it good (Genesis 1:12). Here the Existing One looks at Abel's offering and does not look at Cain's: "the Lord was pleased with Abel's offering; But in Cain and his offering he had no pleasure" (4:4-5, BBE). No look, no "good", no pleasure. Cain is named before his offering, so it is the state it comes from that brings no pleasure. Then "his face became sad" (4:5, BBE), literally "fell", from the verb naphal (H5307), the same verb behind the Nephilim in Lesson 15.
  4. The door, the rule and the field. The Existing One asks: "If you do well, will you not have honour?" (Genesis 4:7, BBE). Do well is the verb for being good or pleasing (H3190), and honour is a lifting up (H7613), the opposite of a fallen face. Then: "sin is waiting at the door, desiring to have you, but do not let it be your master" (4:7, BBE). Master is the Hebrew for rule, mashal (H4910): the same job man was given on day six. Cain was told to rule it. Instead he said "Let us go into the field" and Possession killed Breath (4:8). What grips attacks what passes. But "the voice of your brother's blood is crying to me from the earth" (4:10, BBE), and Eve names a new son Seth, "another seed in place of Abel" (4:25, BBE). Seed brings forth after its kind (Genesis 1:11), so what was killed returns as the same kind.
  5. East of Eden. The Existing One does not destroy Possession. "The Lord put a mark on Cain so that no one might put him to death" (Genesis 4:15, BBE), and Cain goes "away from before the face of the Lord" to live "on the east of Eden" (4:16, BBE), away from pleasure, and builds a town. Pleasure returns later in Lesson 16: Sarah's "pleasure" in Genesis 18:12 is the same word family as Eden, and it is the laugh that names Isaac. Only after Seth do "men first made use of the name of the Lord in worship" (Genesis 4:26, BBE).

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. Genesis 4 keeps the ground, the animals, the looking and the speaking, and changes what each is doing.

PartGenesis 1This scene
Fruit and ruleThe fruit has its seed in it (day three, Genesis 1:11), and man has rule over the animals (day six, Genesis 1:26)Cain brings the fruit, already gathered, not seed; Abel brings the animals he rules (Genesis 4:3-4)
The lookThe Judges look at what they spoke and call it good; the identity stands in Eden, "pleasure"The Existing One looks at Abel's offering and not at Cain's; Cain ends up east of Eden (Genesis 4:4-5, 16)
Who speaksThe Judges speak the identityThe Existing One questions Cain (4:6-15), and the Judges give the new seed (4:25)

What changed is the grip. Cain holds his identity as something he has got, and a possession brings no pleasure. Abel is breath, which passes. The Judges answer the killing by giving a new seed, and the name of the Existing One is called on again.

See where pleasure returns with a laugh: Abraham and Isaac.

Remember: Cain means possession, which is held. Abel means breath, which passes. Eden means pleasure. Cain brought fruit already gathered, and Abel brought what he ruled. A state that holds its identity as something it owns brings no pleasure, attacks what passes, and ends up east of Eden. The Judges answer with another seed.
