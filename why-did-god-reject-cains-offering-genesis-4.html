Why Did God Reject Cain's Offering? Genesis 4

Genesis 4 never says why God accepted Abel's offering and rejected Cain's. In this course the answer is in the names and in the pleasure: Cain means "possession", Abel means "breath", and Eden, where the identity first stood, means "pleasure". Cain's identity is built from the ground as it now is, already cursed, and that brings no pleasure. Abel keeps the animals, the rule man was given in Genesis 1. Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The passage (BBE, selected verses):

And the man had connection with Eve his wife, and she became with child and gave birth to Cain, and said, I have got a man from the Lord. Then again she became with child and gave birth to Abel, his brother. And Abel was a keeper of sheep, but Cain was a farmer. And after a time, Cain gave to the Lord an offering of the fruits of the earth. And Abel gave an offering of the young lambs of his flock and of their fat. And the Lord was pleased with Abel's offering; But in Cain and his offering he had no pleasure. And Cain was angry and his face became sad. And the Lord said to Cain, Why are you angry? and why is your face sad? If you do well, will you not have honour? and if you do wrong, sin is waiting at the door, desiring to have you, but do not let it be your master. And Cain said to his brother, Let us go into the field: and when they were in the field, Cain made an attack on his brother Abel and put him to death. And the Lord said, Truly, if Cain is put to death, seven lives will be taken for his. And the Lord put a mark on Cain so that no one might put him to death. And Cain went away from before the face of the Lord, and made his living-place in the land of Nod on the east of Eden. — Genesis 4:1-8, 15-16 (BBE)

The same verses with the meanings put in:

And the man had connection with Eve his wife, and she became with child and gave birth to Possession, and said, I have got a man from the Existing One. Then again she became with child and gave birth to Breath, his brother. And Breath was a keeper of sheep, but Possession was a farmer. And after a time, Possession gave to the Existing One an offering of the fruits of the earth. And Breath gave an offering of the young lambs of his flock and of their fat. And the Existing One was pleased with Breath's offering; But in Possession and his offering he had no pleasure. And Possession was angry and his face became sad. And the Existing One said to Possession, Why are you angry? and why is your face sad? If you do well, will you not have honour? and if you do wrong, sin is waiting at the door, desiring to have you, but do not let it be your master. And Possession said to his brother, Let us go into the field: and when they were in the field, Possession made an attack on his brother Breath and put him to death. And the Existing One said, Truly, if Possession is put to death, seven lives will be taken for his. And the Existing One put a mark on Possession so that no one might put him to death. And Possession went away from before the face of the Existing One, and made his living-place in the land of Wandering on the east of Pleasure.

The meanings used: Cain is "possession" (H7014), a play on the verb to get or acquire (H7069). Abel is "breath" (H1893), the same word as hebel, breath or vapour, which Ecclesiastes uses for "vanity" (H1892). Eden is "pleasure" (H5731). Nod is "wandering" (H5113). The Lord (YHVH) is "the existing One" (H3068). As in Lesson 1, the Existing One is you, aware right now. Four more Hebrew words appear in the steps: "fell" (naphal, H5307), "do well" (H3190), "honour" (a lifting up, H7613) and "master" (rule, H4910).

Three notes on the meanings. First, the BBE says "pleased" and "no pleasure", and the KJV says "respect". The Hebrew verb behind both is shaah, "to look at, regard" (H8159). So "pleasure" is the English, and the Hebrew says the Existing One looked at one offering and not the other. The link to Eden's pleasure comes through the verses around it (steps 3 and 5). Second, the text never calls the fruit bad. In this reading the point is where it comes from: ground already under the curse (Genesis 3:17). Third, the common readings say Cain brought the wrong kind of gift or brought it in the wrong spirit. Genesis 4 states neither.

How to read it, step by step:

  1. The names. Eve says "I have got a man from the Lord" (Genesis 4:1, BBE). That is Cain's name acted out: possession, from the word for get. Then comes Abel, whose name, hebel (H1892), is breath or vapour, which Strong's glosses as something transitory. So the names set two states side by side: one that possesses, and one that passes. As with Isaac in Lesson 16, the story acts out the name.
  2. Day three and day six. Cain works the ground and brings "the fruits of the earth" (Genesis 4:3, BBE). That is day three: the ground and the fruit with its seed "in" it (Genesis 1:11). Fruit is what the ground has already yielded, gathered and owned. And the ground it comes from is the ground already cursed in Genesis 3: "the earth is cursed on your account; in pain you will get your food from it all your life" (Genesis 3:17, BBE). So Cain's identity is built from the ground as it now is, after the collapse of the Genesis 1 scene. Abel keeps animals and brings "young lambs of his flock and of their fat" (4:4, BBE). That is day six, where man is given "rule over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the air and over the cattle and over all the earth" (Genesis 1:26, BBE). He is only a keeper of sheep, a small start, but it is the rule of the Genesis 1 picture at work, and the fat is the richest part, not what was left over. Cain brings what the cursed ground gives. Abel brings the dominion the first scene gave man.
  3. The look and the pleasure. The identity first stood in Eden, "pleasure" (Genesis 2:8), and in Genesis 1 the Judges look at what they have spoken and call it good (Genesis 1:12). Here it is the Existing One who looks, and the Existing One is you, aware right now: the look is what you notice in yourself: the Genesis 1 picture at work in Abel, and the cursed ground in Cain. The Existing One looks at Abel's offering and does not look at Cain's: "the Lord was pleased with Abel's offering; But in Cain and his offering he had no pleasure" (4:4-5, BBE). No look, no "good", no pleasure. Cain is named before his offering, so it is the state it comes from that brings no pleasure. Then "his face became sad" (4:5, BBE), literally "fell", from the verb naphal, the same verb behind the Nephilim in Lesson 15.
  4. The door, the rule and the field. The Existing One asks: "If you do well, will you not have honour?" (Genesis 4:7, BBE). Do well is the verb for being good or pleasing, and honour is a lifting up, the opposite of a fallen face. Then: "sin is waiting at the door, desiring to have you, but do not let it be your master" (4:7, BBE). Master is the Hebrew for rule: the same job man was given on day six. Cain was told to rule it. Instead he said "Let us go into the field" and Possession killed Breath (4:8). The small start of dominion is wiped out at once. "The voice of your brother's blood is crying to me from the earth" (4:10, BBE), and the ground Cain worked, day three, now withholds: "no longer will the earth give you her fruit as the reward of your work" (4:12, BBE). Then Eve names a new son Seth, "another seed in place of Abel" (4:25, BBE). Seed brings forth after its kind (Genesis 1:11), so what was killed returns as the same kind.
  5. East of Eden. The Existing One does not destroy Possession. "The Lord put a mark on Cain so that no one might put him to death" (Genesis 4:15, BBE), and Cain goes "away from before the face of the Lord" to the land of Nod, "on the east of Eden" (4:16, BBE). Nod means "wandering", and 4:12 had already called him "a wanderer in flight". He is away from pleasure, and he builds a town (4:17). From that cursed ground the collapsed scene keeps multiplying after its kind (Genesis 1:11): Cain's town named for his son, then Lamech, who says "I would put a man to death for a wound" (4:23, BBE). The curse, the town and Lamech's words are in the text; reading them as one state multiplying is this course's reading of Genesis 1:11. Pleasure returns later in Lesson 16: Sarah's "pleasure" in Genesis 18:12 is the same word family as Eden, and it is the laugh that names Isaac. Only after Seth do "men first made use of the name of the Lord in worship" (Genesis 4:26, BBE).

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. Genesis 4 keeps the ground, the animals, the looking and the speaking, and changes what each is doing.

What changed is the ground the identity is built from. Cain's identity is built from the ground as it now is, so the collapse of the first scene carries on after its kind, and a possession brings no pleasure. Abel's identity has the dominion of Genesis 1. It is wiped out at once, but the Judges give another seed in its place, and the name of the Existing One is called on again.

See where pleasure returns with a laugh: Abraham and Isaac.

Remember: Cain means possession, Abel means breath, and Eden means pleasure. Cain's identity is built from ground already cursed, so it brings no pleasure, ends up east of Eden, and the collapse multiplies after its kind. Abel keeps the animals, the dominion of Genesis 1: a small start, wiped out at once. The Judges answer with another named seed in its place.
