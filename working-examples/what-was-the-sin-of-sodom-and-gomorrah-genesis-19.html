What Was the Sin of Sodom and Gomorrah? Genesis 19

This is one of the most argued-over stories in the Bible, and people usually argue it from outside the text. This page stays inside Genesis 13, 18 and 19 and lets the story give its own reasons, then reads it a second time with the name meanings put in and sets it beside Genesis 1.

The short answer: the story never gives one label for the sin. It records three things in order: the men of Sodom are called evil and great sinners (13:13), an outcry against the town reaches the court and the court goes down to check it (18:20-21), and on the night itself the whole town surrounds a house, makes a demand, threatens worse, and says of the stranger inside, "will he now be our judge?" (19:4-9). Read with the meanings, the town is a burning state with no dissenting voice left in it, and the court enforces what is held, after its kind. The story then takes the scene down through the same categories Genesis 1 used to build it.

The passage (BBE, Genesis 18:20-21; 19:4-5, 8-9):

And the Lord said, Because the outcry against Sodom and Gomorrah is very great, and their sin is very evil, I will go down now, and see if their acts are as bad as they seem from the outcry which has come to me; and if they are not, I will see. (18:20-21) But before they had gone to bed, the men of the town, all the men of Sodom, came round the house, young and old, from every part of the town; And crying out to Lot, they said, Where are the men who came to your house this night? Send them out to us, so that we may take our pleasure with them. (19:4-5) See now, I have two unmarried daughters; I will send them out to you so that you may do to them whatever seems good to you: only do nothing to these men, for this is why they have come under the shade of my roof. (19:8) And they said, Give way there. This one man, they said, came here from a strange country, and will he now be our judge? now we will do worse to you than to them; and pushing violently against Lot, they came near to get the door broken in. (19:9) — Genesis 18:20-21; 19:4-5, 8-9 (BBE)

The same passage with the meanings put in:

And the Existing One said, Because the outcry against Burning and Submersion is very great, and their sin is very evil, I will go down now, and see if their acts are as bad as they seem from the outcry which has come to me; and if they are not, I will see. (18:20-21) But before they had gone to bed, the men of the town, all the men of Burning, came round the house, young and old, from every part of the town; And crying out to Covering, they said, Where are the men who came to your house this night? Send them out to us, so that we may take our pleasure with them. (19:4-5) … And they said, Give way there. This one man, they said, came here from a strange country, and will he now be our judge? now we will do worse to you than to them; … (19:9)

The meanings used:

  • Sodom (Sedom, H5467): "burning". Strong's gives "burnt (i.e. volcanic or bituminous) district", from a root meaning "to scorch".
  • Gomorrah (Amorah, H6017): Blue Letter Bible gives "submersion". Strong's gives "a (ruined) heap", from amar. The two lexicons differ, so the page uses "submersion" and says so. The story does not depend on which is right.
  • Lot (Lot, H3876): "covering" (Blue Letter Bible), the same as H3875.
  • Zoar (Tsoar, H6820): "little", "insignificance", from tsa'ar. Genesis 19:22 gives the name its reason.
  • Moab (H4124): Strong's gives "from (her [the mother's]) father", Blue Letter Bible gives "of his father".
  • Ben-ammi (H1151): "son of my people".
  • Judge (shaphat, H8199): the verb "to judge". It is the word in 18:25 ("the judge of all the earth") and twice in 19:9. I pulled the occurrence list and all three are on it.
  • Outcry (tsa'aqah, H6818): "a cry, outcry". Its list has "outcry against" in 18:21 and 19:13, 21 occurrences in all.
  • Seed (zera, H2233): the word of Genesis 1:11-12 ("in which is its seed"). It is also on the list for Genesis 19:32 and 19:34.
  • The Lord (YHVH, H3068): the Existing One, you aware right now (Lesson 2). God: the Judges, the plural court (Lesson 3).

How to read it, step by step

  1. The names state the kind. In this course a name is the nature of the state being held, and the story then unfolds to match it (after its kind). Sodom means burning, and the town ends in fire. Gomorrah means submersion or ruined heap, and the town ends as one. Lot means covering, and he is the one the story covers and carries out of the town. This is the course's reading. The text itself does not say the names are the cause.
  2. The court looks before it rules. Genesis 13:12-13 says Lot moved his tent as far as Sodom, and that "the men of Sodom were evil, and great sinners before the Lord" (BBE). Then, in chapter 18, comes the outcry. The Lord says he will "go down now, and see if their acts are as bad as they seem" (18:21, BBE). The court checks the report against the scene. That is the same act as Genesis 1: the court sees the scene and gives a verdict on it. In Genesis 1 the verdict is "good". Here the verdict is open until the court has looked.
  3. The court is named "judge" before the town rejects it. Abraham asks, "will not the judge of all the earth do right?" (18:25, BBE). He then lowers the number from fifty down to ten (18:24-32), and each time the answer is mercy for that number. The story stops at ten and does not say how many were found. What it shows is Lot warning his sons-in-law, who "did not take him seriously" (19:14, BBE). Then 19:9 uses the same verb for the town: of the one man in the house they say, "will he now be our judge?" The court is standing in the house, and the town refuses its judging.
  4. No voice left that disagrees. Genesis 19:4 is total: "all the men of Sodom ... young and old, from every part of the town". In Lesson 3 a mind is many voices. In Sodom the many voices are all in one state, and the one voice outside it is the stranger. The verdict the court was looking for, whether anything upright is left in the town, is answered by the way the whole town stands round the door.
  5. What the night records. The demand is in 19:5. The BBE prints "take our pleasure with them", and the KJV and YLT print "know them", so the translations differ on the wording. The threat and the force are in 19:9: "we will do worse to you than to them", and they push to break the door. Lot's offer of his daughters in 19:8 is also in the text, and the text gives no verdict on it, so this page does not add one. The story's own charges are the ones listed: evil and great sinners (13:13), a very evil sin and an outcry (18:20-21), this demand, this threat, and the refusal of the judge (19:9).
  6. The ones who cannot find the exit. Lot shuts the door behind him (19:6). The men outside are made blind, "small and great, so that they were tired out with looking for the door" (19:11, BBE). Those holding the state cannot find the way out of it.
  7. Leaving is not joining. The messengers take Lot, his wife and his daughters "by the hand" and put them outside the town (19:16), with the word "Go for your life, without looking back" (19:17, BBE). Lot's wife is out of the town and "looking back, became a pillar of salt" (19:26, BBE). This is the first half of Genesis 2:24 done and the second half not. The same shape is in the Jude 5 worked example: a place is left, the old state is still held, and what is held decides the outcome.
  8. Zoar: the name is the request. Lot says of the town, "it is a little one: O, let me go there" (19:20, BBE). The reply is that "the town was named Zoar" (19:22, BBE), and Zoar means little. He asked for little and the name records it. Then he says he cannot reach the mountain (19:19), but by 19:30 "fear kept him from living in Zoar" and he is living on the mountain "in a hole in the rock" (BBE).
  9. The seed comes out after its kind. The daughters say there is "no man to be a husband to us in the natural way" and plan to "have offspring by our father" (19:31-32, BBE). The word for seed there is the Genesis 1:11-12 word. The names of the sons are the kind they came from: Moab, "from (her) father", and Ben-ammi, "son of my people" (19:37-38). Each name names its source and nothing outside it. The story does not comment. The name does the describing.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. In Genesis 1 the court builds the scene, looks at it and calls it good, and only then is the man set in it. In Sodom the order is the same pieces run the other way. The scene is already built and the state set in it is burning. The court looks, rules after its kind, and takes the scene down through the same categories it was built from:

And God said, Let the waters under the heaven come together in one place, and let the dry land be seen: and it was so. — Genesis 1:9 (BBE) And grass came up on the earth, and every plant producing seed of its sort, and every tree producing fruit, in which is its seed, of its sort: and God saw that it was good. — Genesis 1:12 (BBE) And he sent destruction on those towns, with all the lowland and all the people of those towns and every green thing in the land. — Genesis 19:25 (BBE)

What changed is what is put in the ground. In Genesis 1 the seed in the ground is the court's, and what comes up is called good. In Sodom the state in the ground is burning, so the same law that grows a garden takes the plain down, because the court makes real what is actually held. The name had said so from the start.

Remember: the story gives its own reasons: the outcry, the court going down to look, the whole town round the door, the judge refused. The names say what the state was, and after its kind is the law that finishes it. Leaving the town is the first half of the way out. Holding the old state, or asking for little, or building the seed from a closed source, decides what comes next.

Words checked

  • Same word, confirmed: shaphat (H8199) is in Genesis 18:25 and twice in 19:9. Outcry (H6818) is in 18:21 and 19:13. Seed (zera, H2233) is in Genesis 1:11-12 and 19:32, 34. Each was checked against its occurrence list.
  • Not the same word: the BBE's "every green thing in the land" (19:25) is the Hebrew tsemach (H6780), "sprouting, growth". It is not on the Genesis 1 list for vegetation. The link to Genesis 1 here is the category, vegetation on the ground, not a shared word.
  • Lot and "the shade of my roof": Lot means "covering" and the BBE has "shade" in 19:8. I did not check that these are the same Hebrew word, so this page does not link them by word and leaves the picture to the reader.
  • Gomorrah and Moab: the lexicons differ, as noted in the meanings above. Neither reading changes the order of events.
  • 19:5: the BBE reads "take our pleasure with them". The KJV and YLT read "know them". The page quotes the BBE and notes the difference rather than choosing one to fit.
  • "Angels": the BBE prints "angels" in 19:1 and the YLT prints "messengers". This page treats them as the court's messengers and relies on neither word for the argument.
  • The name switch in 19:29: the BBE prints "Lord" in 19:13 and 19:24, and "God" in 19:29, where the verdict is carried out and the word to Abraham is kept. The WEB makes the same split between Yahweh and God.
  • Staying in Genesis: other books mention Sodom. This page uses only Genesis 13, 18 and 19, plus Genesis 1 for the comparison.
