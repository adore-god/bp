Who Was Hannah? 1 Samuel 1

People ask who Hannah was, why she prays without a sound, and why the priest takes her for drunk. The passage is 1 Samuel 1. Read the verses first, then read them again with the meanings put in.

The short answer: Hannah is "Favored" (H2584). She stands on ground named for double fruit and rest, with one piece missing: seed. The word she asks for in verse 11 is zera, "seed", the word of Genesis 1:11 (the BBE prints "man-child"). She files the claim in her heart, the priest called "Lofty" reads only the outside of it and rules "wine", and the ruling is given in the name of Elohim. Her face is no longer sad before anything has arrived. Then the chapter runs the same sequence as Genesis 4:1: he knew his wife, she conceived, she bore, and the name is explained. The verse explains Samuel's name with "asked", and that one verb runs to the last verse of the chapter. Two older stories sit behind it: Peninnah and Hannah are Leah and Rachel again (Genesis 30), and the vow that no razor will touch the boy's head uses the same words as Samson's birth (Judges 13).

The passage (BBE, 1 Samuel 1:1-2):

Now there was a certain man of Ramathaim, a Zuphite of the hill-country of Ephraim, named Elkanah; he was the son of Jeroham, the son of Elihu, the son of Tohu, the son of Zuph, an Ephraimite: And he had two wives, one named Hannah and the other Peninnah: and Peninnah was the mother of children, but Hannah had no children. — 1 Samuel 1:1-2 (BBE)

The same passage with the meanings put in:

Now there was a certain man of Double-height-of-watchers, of the hill-country of Double-fruit, named Judges-have-obtained; he was the son of Compassionate, the son of Judges-of-him, the son of Abasement, the son of Honeycomb, an Ephraimite: And he had two wives, one named Favored and the other Jewel: and Jewel was the mother of children, but Favored had no children.

And verses 17-20, where the claim is ruled on and the seed comes:

Then Eli said to her, Go in peace: and may the God of Israel give you an answer to the prayer you have made to him. And she said, May your servant have grace in your eyes. So the woman went away, and took part in the feast, and her face was no longer sad. And early in the morning they got up, and after worshipping before the Lord they went back to Ramah, to their house: and Elkanah had connection with his wife; and the Lord kept her in mind. Now the time came when Hannah, being with child, gave birth to a son; and she gave him the name Samuel, Because, she said, I made a prayer to the Lord for him. — 1 Samuel 1:17-20 (BBE)
Then Lofty said to her, Go in peace: and may the Judges of Israel give you an answer to the prayer you have made to him. And she said, May your servant have grace in your eyes. So the woman went away, and took part in the feast, and her face was no longer sad. And early in the morning they got up, and after worshipping before the Existing One they went back to Ramah, to their house: and Judges-have-obtained had connection with his wife; and the Existing One kept her in mind. Now the time came when Favored, being with child, gave birth to a son; and she gave him the name Heard-of-the-Judges, Because, she said, I made a prayer to the Existing One for him.

The meanings used:

  • Ramathaim-zophim (H7436): "double height of watchers" (Strong's). The BBE prints "Ramathaim, a Zuphite"; see the notes below.
  • Ephraim (H669): "double fruit" (Strong's). BDB's outline in Blue Letter Bible gives "double ash-heap: I shall be doubly fruitful".
  • Elkanah (H511): "Judges have obtained" (Strong's: "God has obtained"), built from El (H410, read here as the Judges) and qanah (H7069). BDB's outline gives "the Judges have possessed" or "the Judges have created".
  • Jeroham (H3395): "compassionate" (Strong's).
  • Elihu (H453): "Judges of him" (Strong's: "God of him"). BDB's outline gives "He is my Judge".
  • Tohu (H8459): "abasement" (Strong's), from an unused root "to depress". BDB's outline gives "lowly". It has its own number and root; no link is drawn here to any other word that sounds like it in English.
  • Zuph (H6689): "honeycomb" (Strong's).
  • Hannah (H2584): "favored" (Strong's), from chanan (H2603). BDB's outline gives "grace".
  • Peninnah (H6444): Strong's gives no gloss ("probably feminine from H6443 contracted"). BDB's outline gives "jewel".
  • Shiloh (H7887): "place of rest" (BDB's outline). Strong's says only "from the same as H7886", and H7886 is "tranquil".
  • Eli (H5941): "lofty" (Strong's), from alah (H5927), "to go up". BDB's outline gives "ascension".
  • Hophni (H2652): "perhaps pugilist" (Strong's).
  • Phinehas (H6372): "mouth of a serpent" (Strong's). BDB's outline gives "mouth of brass".
  • Samuel (H8050): "heard of the Judges" (Strong's: "heard of God"), from the passive participle of shama (H8085) and El (H410). BDB's outline gives "his name is El".
  • Ramah (H7414): the home named in verse 19. BDB lists Ramathaim-zophim as the same place.
  • The Lord (YHVH, H3068): the Existing One, you aware right now (Lesson 2). God in "the God of Israel" (H430, Elohim): the Judges, the plural court. Where this page quotes the BBE, Elohim is put back as the Judges (Lesson 3) and the Lord is put back as the Existing One.

Three notes on the meanings. First, the BBE reads verse 1 as "Ramathaim, a Zuphite". BDB (under H6689) says most modern readers take the Hebrew that way. Strong's reads one place name, Ramathaim-zophim. The meanings-in version above follows Strong's, and the lexicons disagree. Second, the BBE says "Ephraimite" where the Hebrew of verse 1 says "Ephrathite" (H673). Third, Samuel's name is explained two ways, once by the verse and once by Strong's. Step 5 sets them side by side.

How to read it, step by step

Reading from the reader's side (Lesson 1), the people and places in this chapter are states inside the reader. Nothing in it is an outside power acting on Hannah.

  1. The ground is named before she stands on it. Lesson 5: Genesis builds the ground first. "And the Judges said, Let grass come up on the earth, and plants producing seed, and fruit-trees giving fruit, in which is their seed, after their sort: and it was so" (Genesis 1:11, BBE, with Elohim read as the Judges). In 1 Samuel 1 the ground is named the same way: Double-height-of-watchers, in the hill-country of Double-fruit, and the place she goes up to is Shiloh, "place of rest". Nothing about the ground is lacking. What the text says is missing is on the woman: "Hannah had no children" (1:2). The match between Shiloh's "rest" and the rest of Genesis 2:2-3 is a matching picture, not a word link.
  2. The state is named, and one piece is missing. Elkanah has two wives, two states on one ground. Peninnah has "sons and daughters" (1:4). Hannah is "very dear to him" (1:5), "but the Existing One had not let her have children" (1:5, and again in 1:6). The Hebrew verb is sagar, "shut up" (H5462), and its object is the womb (H7358). Year by year, "whenever she went up to the house of the Existing One", she is attacked, "so that Hannah gave herself up to weeping and would take no food" (1:7). Peninnah, "Jewel", the wife with the children, "did everything possible to make her unhappy" (1:6). Elkanah sees it and speaks: "Hannah, why are you weeping? and why are you taking no food? why is your heart troubled? am I not more to you than ten sons?" (1:8, BBE). He offers himself, the husband on the ground, and the weeping and the not eating stay exactly as they were. The one thing missing is seed, and he is not that. Genesis 1 puts food in the picture before the man is placed in it: "they will be for your food" (1:29). In this reading YHVH, you aware right now, is the one holding "shut", and Elohim hold what is held after its kind, year after year. The text does not say Hannah did anything wrong. It says what state was held and that it was held every year.
  3. The claim is filed in the heart, in the court's words.
    And she made an oath, and said, O Existing One of armies, if you will truly take note of the sorrow of your servant, not turning away from me but keeping me in mind, and will give me a man-child, then I will give him to the Existing One all the days of his life, and his hair will never be cut. — 1 Samuel 1:11 (BBE, with the Lord put back as the Existing One)
    She "made an oath" (neder, H5088). Three words in it sit beside Genesis. "Man-child" is zera 'anashim, "seed of men". Zera (H2233) is the word of Genesis 1:11, 1:12 and 1:29, and it is on the same Strong's list as 1 Samuel 1:11. She asks for the seed of Genesis 1:11. "Keeping me in mind" is zakar (H2142), set against shakach, "forget" (H7911). "Armies" is tsaba (H6635), the word Genesis 2:1 uses of the whole finished scene (see the notes at the end). The filing is complete: what is asked (seed), who must hold it (remember, not forget), and where it goes afterward ("I will give him to the Existing One all the days of his life"). It is also silent: "Hannah's prayer came from her heart, and though her lips were moving she made no sound" (1:13, BBE).
  4. The ruling is given from outside the claim. "Eli was watching her mouth" (1:12). Eli is "Lofty", the priest on the seat by the doorpost of the Temple (1:9), the court's officer at the door. He reads the outside of the claim, lips moving and no sound, and rules "wine" (1:13-14). Hannah answers with where the claim actually is: "I have not taken wine or strong drink, but I have been opening my heart before the Existing One" (1:15, BBE; the Hebrew is "poured out my soul"). She adds: "Do not take your servant to be a good-for-nothing woman: for my words have come from my stored-up sorrow and pain" (1:16, BBE). She is asking not to be ruled on by what shows on the outside. Then comes the ruling: "Go in peace: and may the Judges of Israel give you an answer to the prayer you have made to him" (1:17). The BBE prints "God" there, but the word is Elohim (H430), the plural court. The ruling is given in the name of the judges, to a claim addressed to YHVH. That is Lesson 2 in two verses: YHVH is asked, Elohim give. The verb "give" (nathan, H5414) is in her request (1:11), in his ruling (1:17), and in her report (1:27).
    Her reply is "May your servant have grace in your eyes" (1:18). "Grace" is chen (H2580), and Strong's derives it from chanan (H2603), the same root it gives for her own name. She asks to find in his eyes what her name already says she is. Then: "So the woman went away, and took part in the feast, and her face was no longer sad." The verb for eating (akal, H398) is the one used in 1:7 for what she would not do. Before anything has arrived, the state has moved. The narration also stops using her name at this point. "The woman" (ha-ishshah, H802) is the Genesis 2:23 word, and it is what the text calls her at 1:18, 1:23 and 1:26.
  5. The seed comes after its kind. "They went back to Ramah, to their house: and Elkanah had connection with his wife; and the Existing One kept her in mind" (1:19). The order is: back on the same ground, union, then the verdict. No old state is left in this chapter. The ground is kept, and what is held on it changes. Genesis 4:1 is built the same way: "And the man had connection with Eve his wife, and she became with child and gave birth to Cain, and said, I have got a man from the Existing One" (BBE, with the Lord put back as the Existing One). The Hebrew gives the same "knew" (yada, H3045), the same "his wife" (H802), the same "conceived" (hara, H2029) and the same "bore" (yalad, H3205) as 1 Samuel 1:19-20. The sequence is identical. What differs is how the name is explained. Eve says qanah, "I have got" (H7069), and Strong's says Cain's name plays on that word. Elkanah's own name is built from the same qanah. Hannah says she asked.
    The chapter also changes what YHVH is said to do. In 1:5 the verb is sagar, "shut". In 1:19 it is zakar, "kept in mind", the word of her own filing in 1:11. Strong's reads "Samuel" as "heard of the Judges", from shama (H8085). BDB's outline reads "his name is El". The verse's own reason is "I made a prayer to the Existing One for him", and the Hebrew there is she'iltiv, "I asked him". The root Strong's gives for the name, shama, is the word of 1:13, where her voice "was not heard". The claim nobody heard comes back with a name built from hearing. The name is in the passive form, "heard", not "hearer". The other half comes later in the book. In 1 Samuel 3 the voice of the Existing One says his name three times and he does not know whose voice it is, because "Samuel had no knowledge of the Existing One" (3:7, BBE, with the Lord put back as the Existing One). Eli, who ruled on the outside in chapter 1, now has eyes that "were becoming clouded so that he was not able to see" (3:2), and he tells the boy how to answer. Samuel says "Say on, Existing One; for the ears of your servant are open" (3:10, BBE, with the Lord put back as the Existing One). The Hebrew there is shama, the verb of his name and of 1:13. The claim nobody heard becomes the child who hears.
  6. The seed is asked, then lent back, and the same verb closes the chapter. She does not go up until the child is weaned (1:22-23), then takes him to Shiloh with "a three-year old ox and an ephah of meal and a skin full of wine" (1:24). Her last words in the chapter: "O my lord, as your soul is living, my lord, I am that woman who was making a prayer to the Existing One here by your side: My prayer was for this child; and the Existing One has given him to me in answer to my request: So I have given him to the Existing One; for all his life he is the Existing One's" (1:26-28, BBE, with the Lord put back as the Existing One). The BBE uses "prayer", "request" and "given". The Hebrew uses one verb, sha'al (H7592), five times between 1:17 and 1:28: "thou hast asked" (1:17), "I have asked him" (1:20), "I asked" (1:27), and twice in 1:28, where the KJV prints "lent". The seed was asked, it was given, and it is lent back to the one it was asked of. That matches the oath of 1:11: "then I will give him to the Existing One".

Peninnah and Hannah: Rachel and Leah again

Genesis already has this pair (Jacob, Leah and Rachel). One husband, two wives, one with sons and daughters and one with none. "Now Rachel, because she had no children, was full of envy of her sister" (Genesis 30:1, BBE). In Hannah's chapter the loved wife is the one with no children, "very dear to him" (1:5), and the other wife with the children makes her unhappy (1:6-7). It is the same set-up, and it ends the same way. "Then God gave thought to Rachel, and hearing her prayer he made her fertile" (Genesis 30:22, BBE, with God read as the Judges). "Gave thought to" is zakar (H2142), the word of Hannah's filing in 1:11 and of the verdict in 1:19. The loved wife with no children asks, and the Judges remember her.

The ground matches as well. Hannah's husband stands in the hill-country of Ephraim, "Double-fruit", and Ephraim is Joseph's son (Genesis 41:52), Rachel's line. Rachel names her son Joseph with the words "May the Existing One give me another son" (Genesis 30:24, BBE, with the Lord put back as the Existing One), and Hannah's chapter closes with more children coming to her (2:21). The pair is Peninnah and Hannah here, Leah and Rachel there, and in both the verdict comes to the one who asked.

The hair and the wine: the same words as Samson's mother

Hannah's vow ends "his hair will never be cut" (1:11). The Hebrew is morah lo ya'aleh al rosho, "a razor shall not come up on his head". Morah (H4177) is a rare word. It is used three times in the whole Bible: Judges 13:5, Judges 16:17 and 1 Samuel 1:11. Judges 13 is the birth of Samson, and its opening matches Hannah's: a wife who "had never given birth to a child" (13:2), told "you will be with child and give birth to a son" (13:3), told "take care to have no wine or strong drink" (13:4), and told "his hair is never to be cut, for the child is to be separate to God from his birth" (13:5, BBE, with God read as the Judges).

Now go back to Eli. He rules that Hannah is "overcome with wine" (1:13), and she answers "I have not taken wine or strong drink" (1:15). Wine and strong drink (yayin and shekar, H7941) is the exact pair given to Samson's mother (Judges 13:4, 7, 14) and the pair in the rule for the one set apart (Numbers 6:3). The officer at the door rules "wine" on a woman who has just filed the hair-vow, and she answers with the pair that goes with it. The outside reading and the claim in the heart are far apart, which is the whole of this chapter.

Two differences. Judges 13 says the boy is "separate" (nezir), and 1 Samuel 1 never uses that word. Samson's mother is told by the court, while Hannah files the vow herself. And the hair goes on to different places. Samson tells Delilah "my head has never been touched by a blade" and that if his hair is cut his strength will go (Judges 16:17, BBE). Hannah's vow is the other half of the same rule: the razor never comes, and the child is given back to the one it was asked of.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. Here the change is one piece. The ground is named for fruit and rest the whole way down, and the woman is named Favored. What is missing is the seed of Genesis 1:11.

The chapter never says what was done to Hannah. It says what she asked, says it five times, and shows each step in the order the court works: a state held, a claim filed in the heart, a ruling from the officer at the door, a face that changes before the evidence, the seed after its kind, and the seed lent back.

Remember: Hannah, "Favored", asked for seed in a place no one could hear, took the ruling before anything arrived, and the seed came after its kind. Then it went back to the one it was asked of.

Words checked

  • Seed: the BBE's "man-child" in 1:11 is zera 'anashim, "seed of men" (H2233, H376). Genesis 1:11, 1:12 and 1:29 and 1 Samuel 1:11 are all on the H2233 list. BDB files 1 Samuel 1:11 under "a particular child (son)". Both readings are given: the word is the Genesis word, and in this verse it names a child.
  • Remember and shut: zakar (H2142) is in 1:11 and 1:19 and in Genesis 30:22, and BDB lists all three together under remembering a person with kindness and granting requests. Sagar (H5462) is in 1:5 and 1:6. The BBE prints "keeping me in mind", "kept her in mind" and "had not let her have children".
  • Asked: sha'al (H7592) is in 1:17, 1:20, 1:27 and twice in 1:28. The noun she'elah (H7596) is in 1:17 and 1:27. The BBE uses other English words for all of them, so the repeated verb does not show in the BBE.
  • Grace and Hannah: chen (H2580) is in 1:18. Strong's gives chanan (H2603) as the root of both H2580 and H2584. Hannah (H2584) occurs 13 times, all in 1 Samuel 1 and 2.
  • The woman: H802 is in 1:2 (plural, "wives"), 1:15, 1:18, 1:23 and 1:26, and in Genesis 2:22-24 and 4:1. The "I am" of 1:26 is ani (H589), "I", not the word of Exodus 3:14, so no link is drawn to it.
  • Hosts: tsaba (H6635) is in 1:3 and 1:11 and in Genesis 2:1. The BBE has "all things in them" at Genesis 2:1 where the KJV and ESV have "all the host of them". BDB reads the Genesis 2:1 use as "of the entire creation" and reads the title "YHVH of hosts" as armies. The word is the same word. Reading the title from Genesis 2:1 is this course's reading.
  • "One part": in 1:5 the BBE says "one part". The ASV and NASB say "double portion" and the KJV says "worthy portion" (Hebrew apayim, H639). This page uses the BBE, and nothing above rests on that phrase.
  • Soul: in 1:15 the BBE says "opening my heart". The Hebrew is shaphak nephesh, "poured out my soul" (H8210, H5315).
  • Verse 9: it says they ate and drank in Shiloh before Hannah rose. The page reads the not-eating as the year-by-year state of 1:7 and the eating of 1:18 as that state lifting. The text does not say whether she ate at 1:9.
  • Ramathaim: the ISBE entry (via Bible Hub's atlas) says the compound "as it stands is grammatically incorrect". The reading is open, as the first note says.
  • Phinehas: Strong's builds the name from the word for "mouth" (peh, H6310), the word in 1:12 for the mouth Eli watches. Strong's gloss is "mouth of a serpent" and BDB's outline is "mouth of brass", so the gloss is a reading. A serpent speaks to the woman in Genesis 3. That is a matching picture only. The word behind "serpent" in Strong's (a variation of H5175) has not been checked here.
  • Razor: morah (H4177) occurs three times, in Judges 13:5, Judges 16:17 and 1 Samuel 1:11. The BBE prints "his hair is never to be cut" at Judges 13:5 and "his hair will never be cut" at 1 Samuel 1:11. Judges 13:5 and 1 Samuel 1:11 carry the same phrase, "a razor shall not come up on his head".
  • Wine and strong drink: shekar (H7941) is in Numbers 6:3, Judges 13:4, 13:7, 13:14 and 1 Samuel 1:15, each time beside yayin, "wine". BDB lists Numbers 6:3 and Judges 13:4, 7, 14 as the uses for the one set apart, and lists 1 Samuel 1:15 among the uses.
  • Separate: nezir is in Judges 13:5 and 16:17 ("Nazirite to God" in the ESV). It is not in 1 Samuel 1.
  • Rachel and Leah: BBE Genesis 30:1, 30:22 and 30:24. Ephraim as Joseph's son is Genesis 41:52.
  • Verses 6, 8 and 16: the BBE reads "did everything possible to make her unhappy" (1:6), "am I not more to you than ten sons?" (1:8) and "a good-for-nothing woman" (1:16).
  • Heard: shama (H8085) is in 1:13 ("her voice was not heard"), 3:9 and 3:10 ("your servant hears"). Strong's builds Samuel (H8050) from the passive participle of H8085 and El (H410), "heard of God". BDB's outline is "his name is El". The BBE prints "the ears of your servant are open" at 3:9 and 3:10, and "Samuel had no knowledge of the Lord" at 3:7.
  • Cain: Genesis 4:1 and 1 Samuel 1:19-20 share yada (H3045), H802, hara (H2029) and yalad (H3205). Strong's says Cain's name (H7014) plays on qanah (H7069), and BDB's outline for Cain is "possession".

Checked: the BBE text of 1 Samuel 1 and 3, Judges 13 and Genesis 1, 2, 4:1 and 30; the Hebrew interlinear of 1 Samuel 1:1-28 (WLC, Blue Letter Bible); the Strong's and BDB entries for every name and word above, including the occurrence lists for H4177, H7941, H2142, H8085, H8050, H2233, H2584, H6444, H6635, H7436 and H802.
