Who Was Mary Magdalene? The Woman in the Garden (John 20)

Now on the first day of the week, very early, while it was still dark, Mary Magdalene came to the place and saw that the stone had been taken away from it. — John 20:1 (BBE)

Most people know Mary Magdalene as the loyal friend or the forgiven sinner. The text gives her one clear job. She is the woman standing in a garden, and the scene is Genesis 3 played backwards. Two things to keep in mind from the course: everything happens in the reader, and the story acts out the names (Lesson 9).

The names, with the meanings put in

  • Mary is Miriam (Strong's H4813), "rebellion". It comes from H4805 and Strong's gives it as "rebelliously".
  • Magdalene (G3094) is "a tower", the name of someone from Magdala. A tower is something built up.
  • Jesus is "Existing One is salvation".
  • Simon (G4613) is "hearing", and Peter (G4074) is "a rock or a stone".

With the meanings in, the opening reads like this: Rebellion, built up like a tower, comes to the place where the Existing One is salvation has been laid, while it is still dark. The reader is standing in a built-up state of rebellion, looking for the identity that was put away.

It begins on day one

"The first day of the week… while it was still dark" is Genesis 1:2-5. Dark comes first, then light. Mary is at the very start of the original scene, before anything has been placed.

Hearing and Stone go home, Rebellion stays

Mary runs to Simon Peter and the other disciple. Hearing and Stone run to the place, look in, and then:

So then the disciples went away again to their houses. — John 20:10 (BBE)

They go back to their old houses. Mary stays outside, weeping. The one who is built on rebellion is the one who stays in the garden.

The Eve scene, played backwards

Genesis 2-3John 20
A woman is in the garden.Jesus calls Mary "Woman" (John 20:13, 15).
The man is put in the garden "to do work in it and take care of it" (Genesis 2:15).Mary takes him for "the gardener" (John 20:15).
"What have you done?" (Genesis 3:13)"Woman, why are you weeping? who are you looking for?" (John 20:15)
The tree in the middle of the garden is one they may not "put your hands on" (Genesis 3:3)."Do not put your hand on me" (John 20:17).
The woman takes the fruit and gives it to her husband (Genesis 3:6). It leads out of the garden.Mary is sent to "my brothers" with the word "I go up to my Father and your Father" (John 20:17-18). It leads up.
The man names his wife Eve, "the mother of all who have life" (Genesis 3:20).Jesus says one word, "Mary!", and she turns (John 20:16).

The BBE uses the same wording in the two "hand" lines. That is worth knowing, but it is the English. The Greek has not been checked against the Genesis word yet.

What it means

Eve is the identity that reaches for the wrong thing in the garden. Mary is the same woman coming back to the same garden, and this time the hand is held back. She is the first to be named, the first to be sent, and the Twelve ("the brothers") come after her. In Genesis 3 the woman handed the man the fruit. In John 20 she hands the brothers the word.

That is why Mary is loved. She is the point in the storyline where the woman stops taking and starts being sent. It is still the same reader, standing in the same garden, being asked who they are looking for.

Three questions to ask of the scene: What is the ground, and what does its name mean? Who is standing on it, and what do their names mean? What has changed from the Genesis 1-3 picture?

See the woman as the assumed identity.

Remember: Mary Magdalene is Eve coming back to the garden, with her hand held back and a word to carry.
