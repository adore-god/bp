The Floating Axe Head: What 2 Kings 6:1-7 Means

People ask what the floating axe head is about. A group of men have outgrown the place they live, so they go down to the Jordan to cut trees. One man loses a borrowed iron blade in the water. Elisha asks where it fell, cuts a stick, throws it in, and the iron comes up. The man puts out his hand and takes it. The story is only seven verses because it is the end of a run. The chapters just before it (2 Kings 4 and 5) set it up, and the verses just after it (6:8 to 8:6) show what the same court does next. Read the verses first, then read them again with the meanings put in.

The short answer: two men in the same company hold something that is not theirs. Gehazi, Elisha's own servant, takes silver that Elisha refused, hides it, and says he went nowhere (5:20-25). The man at the Jordan loses an iron blade that belongs to someone else, cries out at once, and says so (6:5). The court marks the first man and answers the second with one cut stick. Elisha's name means "God is salvation", so the name says the court is the deliverance (the court setting is Lesson 1). Nobody hands the iron over. The man is told to lift it, and he does.

The passage (BBE, 2 Kings 6:1-7):

Now the sons of the prophets said to Elisha, There is not room enough for us in the place where we are living under your care; So let us go to Jordan, and let everyone get to work cutting boards, and we will make a living-place for ourselves there. And he said to them, Go, then. And one of them said, Be pleased to go with your servants. And he said, I will go. So he went with them. And when they came to Jordan, they got to work cutting down trees. But one of them, while cutting a board, let the head of his axe go into the water; and he gave a cry, and said, This is a bad business, my master, for it is another's. And the man of God said, Where did it go in? and when he saw the place where it had gone into the water, cutting a stick, he put it into the water, and the iron came up to the top of the water. Then he said, Take it up. So he put out his hand and took it. — 2 Kings 6:1-7 (BBE)

The same passage with the meanings put in:

Now the sons of the prophets said to The-Judges-are-salvation, There is not room enough for us in the state where we are living under your care; So let us go to Descender, and let everyone get to work cutting boards, and we will make a living-place for ourselves there. And he said to them, Go, then. And one of them said, Be pleased to go with your servants. And he said, I will go. So he went with them. And when they came to Descender, they got to work cutting down trees. But one of them, while cutting a board, let the iron go into the water; and he gave a cry, and said, This is a bad business, my master, for it is asked-for. And the man of the Judges said, Where did it go in? and when he saw the state where it had gone into the water, cutting a stick, he put it into the water, and the iron came up to the top of the water. Then he said, Raise it to yourself. So he put out his hand and took it.

The meanings used:

  • Elisha (H477): "God is salvation" (BDB, Blue Letter Bible). Where the BBE prints "God" this course reads the Judges (Lesson 3), so the name is read "The-Judges-are-salvation". See the open points below.
  • Elijah (H452): El (H410) plus Yah (H3050), the short form of YHVH. Strong's reads it "God of Jehovah" and BDB reads it "Yah(u) is God". This course reads it as the Judges and the Existing One side by side.
  • Yasha (H3467): "to save, to be delivered". It is the second half of Elisha's name.
  • Gehazi (H1522): "valley of a visionary" in Strong's, which says "apparently" from gay (valley) and chazah (to see).
  • Naaman (H5283): "pleasantness".
  • Jordan (H3383): "a descender", from yarad, "to go down".
  • Place (maqom, H4725): Strong's also gives "a condition of body or mind", so what has become too narrow is a state.
  • The iron (ha-barzel, H1270): "iron (as cutting)". The BBE itself prints "the iron" in verse 6. "Axe" and "head" are the translators' words in verse 5.
  • Another's (sha'ul, from sha'al, H7592): the form means "asked" or "borrowed".
  • Stick and trees (etz, H6086): the word for tree and wood.
  • Cast (shalak, H7993): "to throw, to cast".
  • Float (tsuph, H6687): "to overflow, to flow". The word is used in three verses only (below).
  • Showed (vayyar'ehu, H7200): "and he showed him", from ra'ah, "to see". The BBE prints "when he saw the place".
  • Eyes opened (paqach, H6491): "to open the eyes".
  • Samaria (Shomron, H8111): "watch-station".
  • The man of God (ish ha-Elohim): "the man of Elohim". In this course Elohim is read as the Judges (Lesson 3).
  • Raise it to yourself: the Hebrew is "raise to you", as in Young's Literal Translation.

How to read it, step by step

  1. Start with the servant. The verses just before this one are about Gehazi. Naaman, a commander with a skin disease, goes down seven times into the Jordan and comes out clean, with his flesh "like the flesh of a little child again" (5:14). Elisha takes nothing from him (5:16). Gehazi runs after Naaman anyway. He says two young men "of the sons of the prophets" have come and need silver and clothing (5:22), puts what he gets "away in the house" (5:24), and when Elisha asks where he has been he says "Your servant went nowhere" (5:25). Elisha names what he was really reaching for: "money, and clothing, and olive-gardens and vine-gardens, and sheep and oxen, and men-servants and women-servants" (5:26). That is the Genesis 1 list of plants, animals and people under rule, taken without the identity that goes with it. The court gives him Naaman's disease, "and your seed after you, for ever" (5:27). The sons of the prophets in his lie are the same company who speak in 6:1, and none of the silver reaches them.
  2. Find the ground. Names and places are codes (Lesson 9). The men's place has become too narrow, and the word for place can mean a state, so they leave the narrow state and go down to Descender (Lesson 8). "Place" is the word Naaman used when he wanted "a wave of his hand over the place" (5:11), and it comes seven times between 5:11 and 6:10. It is also the word in Genesis 1:9, where the waters are gathered to one place and the dry land appears (Lesson 5). The word list in Lesson 4 puts trees and dry land together on day three, and the men go down to cut trees. Naaman went down to the Descender and came up clean, and the sons go down to the same river. The court goes down before it brings you out.
  3. Watch what is lost. The man is cutting a board when the iron goes into the water. Iron is the cutting craft, and it first appears in scripture in Genesis 4:22, with Tubal-cain in Cain's line. What is lost is the tool for cutting, and it is lost in the water.
  4. Hear the cry. He does not ask for the iron back. He says whose it is: it is "another's" in the BBE, and the Hebrew form means asked-for or borrowed. Gehazi hid what was not his and said "nowhere". This man says in the same breath that the iron is not his. This is the first step of asking (the asking page): the man holds something he did not own and says so. He says "my master", and Elisha does not answer the cry. He asks one thing.
  5. Take the question. Elisha asks "Where did it go in?" and is shown the place. The Hebrew says he showed him the place, with the seeing word ra'ah. He asks for the state, the place where it is, and nothing else: not who, and not why. The court works from where the thing is held.
  6. Count the stick and the throw. He cuts a stick and puts it in the water. Etz is the word for the trees of verse 4, so the stick is from the same group the men were cutting. Genesis 1:11 says every plant gives after its kind (Lesson 7). Iron cuts wood, and here the wood lifts the iron. The throw has a history in this run. The same verb (shalak) is used when Elijah's mantle is thrown on Elisha (1 Kings 19:19), for the salt thrown into the spring (2 Kings 2:21), for the meal thrown into the pot of soup (4:41), and for the stick thrown into the water (6:6). The soup is worth reading on its own: a man cut fruit from "a vine of the field" into the pot "having no idea what it was" (4:39), the men cried "there is death in the pot" (4:40), and after the meal went in "there was nothing bad in the pot" (4:41). Each time something ordinary from the ground goes into a place that has gone wrong, and the place is put right.
  7. Read the float. The word tsuph is used in three verses. In Deuteronomy 11:4 the water of the Red Sea goes over the army chasing the people. In Lamentations 3:54 the waters go over a head. In 2 Kings 6:6 it is the iron that comes up. It is the same word and the same kind of water, with the opposite result.
  8. Follow the hand. "Take it up. So he put out his hand and took it." Elisha does not pick up the iron. He tells the man to raise it to himself, and the man puts out his own hand. In Genesis 3:22 the court speaks about a hand put out to take from the tree, and there it is stopped. Here the court commands it. The difference is that this iron was asked for. The one who asked is the one who takes.

What the same court does next (6:8 to 8:6)

The following scenes use the same words, so they belong to the same story.

  • Eyes. Aram plans to wait "in secret in some named place" (6:8), and the word is "place" again. At Dothan Elisha's servant, an unnamed one and not Gehazi, sees an army all round the town. Elisha asks the Lord to open his eyes, and he sees "all the mountain was full of horses and carriages of fire round Elisha" (6:17). Eyes opened is paqach, the word in Genesis 3:5. Then the Aramaean army is made blind and led with the words "This is not the way, and this is not the town" (6:19) into Samaria, the watch-station. Their eyes are opened and "they saw that they were in the middle of Samaria" (6:20). Once they have seen where they are, they are fed and sent home, and "no more bands of Aramaeans came into the land of Israel" (6:23).
  • The siege and the gate. Then Aram shuts Samaria in "on all sides" (6:24), the narrow place at full size. The king asks where help will come from, "the grain-floor or the grape-crusher" (6:27). Elisha says that tomorrow meal and barley will sell for a shekel at the market-place of Samaria (7:1). Four lepers at the doorway into the town find the Aramaean camp empty. They eat, take "silver and gold and clothing", and put them "in a secret place" (7:8), the same things Gehazi took and hid. Then they say "We are not doing right" (7:9) and go and tell the news. The captain who doubted Elisha is told "Your eyes will see it, but you will not have a taste of the food" (7:2), and he is crushed in the doorway (7:20).
  • The return. In 8:4-6 the king asks Gehazi for "all the great things Elisha has done". While Gehazi tells how Elisha gave life to the dead, the woman comes with her request for her house and her land, and the king orders "Give her back all her property, and all the produce of her fields". What was lost comes back, as the iron did.

In each scene the court does the delivering, and the person has a part to play: say whose it is, say where it is, put out the hand, tell the news. Where someone hides, as Gehazi did, the court enforces the hiding.

Elijah and Elisha: what the two names say, and whether the stories agree

The picture first. Think of a courtroom. The Judges are the ones who make a claim real (Lesson 3). The Existing One is the person standing in the room right now, aware, saying who they are (Lesson 2). Both names start with El, the Judges' word, and each adds one more piece.

  • Elijah is El plus Yah. Yah is the short form of YHVH, the Existing One. So the name puts the Judges and the Existing One side by side: the court is open, and someone is standing in front of it.
  • Elisha is El plus yasha. Yasha means to save or deliver. So the name puts the Judges and deliverance side by side: the court says yes.

Elijah opens the court, and Elisha is where the court's answers show up. That fits the order of the stories. Elijah's robe falls to Elisha and he takes it up (2 Kings 2:13), and the stories that follow are the rescues.

A name is only a good reading if its story acts it out. So both names were tested against the text, including the places where they might fail.

Elijah's test. At Carmel the question is who answers by fire: "it will be clear that the one who gives an answer by fire is God" (1 Kings 18:24). When the fire comes, the people say "The Lord, he is God, the Lord, he is God" (18:39). That is the two halves of the name said as a verdict, the Existing One and the Judges named as one. Then Elisha strikes the Jordan with Elijah's robe and asks "Where is the Lord, the God of Elijah?" (2 Kings 2:14). The whole name comes out as a question, and the waters part. The sons of the prophets say "The spirit of Elijah is resting on Elisha" (2:15), which matches what Elisha asked for: "a special measure of your spirit" (2:9). The limit is that the Carmel verses use Elohim, not the short El that is in the name.

Elisha's test. Before reading each story, three rules were set down. First, a true filing (saying what is wrong, naming it, acting on it) gets deliverance. Second, a false or mocking filing is enforced as filed. Third, the deliverance comes through Elisha's word and not through a king. The results:

  • The spring at Jericho (2:19-22). The men say the water is bad and the young of the cattle are born dead. Salt goes in, and "no longer will it be death-giving or unfertile". Rescue predicted, rescue found.
  • The search party (2:16-18). The sons of the prophets press to search three days for Elijah, who has already been taken, and find nothing. Elisha says "Did I not say to you, Go not?" Nothing predicted, nothing found.
  • The boys and the bears (2:23-24). They mock "Go up, old no-hair!", and "two she-bears came out of the wood and put forty-two of the children to death". This fits rule two, but it is the hard case. The rule was written to take it in, so it does not count as confirmation.
  • The Shunammite's son (4:12-37). Elisha first offers to speak for her "to the king or the captain of the army", and she says "I am living among my people" (4:13). When her son dies she goes to Elisha, who does not know her trouble: "the Lord has kept it secret from me" (4:27). She says "I will not go back without you" (4:30). Gehazi's stick on the child's face brings "no voice, and no one gave attention" (4:31), almost the BBE's words for the prophets of Baal at Carmel, "no voice, or any answer, or any who gave attention to them" (1 Kings 18:29). Only when Elisha goes in himself does "the child's body became warm" and "the child's eyes became open" (4:34-35). A true cry is answered, but not by a delegate.
  • Naaman and Gehazi (chapter 5). Naaman does what he is told at the Jordan and is made clean. Gehazi hides what he took and gets the disease with it. Both as predicted.
  • The three kings (chapter 3). Jehoram says the Lord has gathered the kings "to give them into the hands of Moab" (3:10), and Elisha answers "What have I to do with you?" (3:13). Jehoshaphat says "The word of the Lord is with him" (3:12), and Elisha says he will only look at Jehoram for the respect he has for Jehoshaphat (3:14). The water comes: "Though you see no wind or rain, the valley will be full of water" (3:17). The Moabites see the water "as red as blood" and say "This is blood" (3:22-23). The rescue is real but incomplete, and it ends with "great wrath against Israel; and they went away" (3:27). A partial pass, with the deliverance coming for Jehoshaphat's sake and not through the king.
  • The woman's cry (6:26-27). This is the one place in the run where the name's own verb is spoken. The woman cries "Help! my lord king" (yasha), and the king says "If the Lord does not give you help, where am I to get help for you?" The king cannot deliver, and the next day Elisha's word at the gate does (7:1).
  • The dying scene (13:14-21). The name's own noun appears: "The Lord's arrow of salvation, of salvation over Aram" (13:17). Earlier in the same chapter "the Lord gave Israel a saviour" (13:5), which is also on the yasha list, though the verse does not say who. Elisha lays his hands on the king's hands on the bow (13:16), but the king still has to do the striking himself. He sends the arrows "down into the earth" three times "and no more" (13:18), and the man of God is angry: "now you will only overcome them three times" (13:19). Three is what he filed, and three is what comes: "Three times Jehoash overcame him" (13:25). Then a dead man is put quickly into the place where Elisha's body was, and "on touching Elisha's bones, came to life again, and got up on his feet" (13:21), so the name goes on working after death.

Two patterns nobody was looking for. First, Joash's cry in 13:14, "My father, my father, the war-carriages of Israel and its horsemen!", repeats Elisha's own cry when Elijah went up (2:12), with "war" added in the BBE. The mantle has changed hands again. Second, the enemy misreads what is in front of them three times: Moab sees water as blood (3:22), Aram is made blind (6:18), and Aram hears horses that are not there (7:6). Each time a wrong seeing leads to a fall, and in chapter 6 the right seeing follows. That makes the eyes-and-place thread the text's own.

Where the test stops. It was not blind, because the stories were known in outline before the rule was set. The rule can also take in a lot, so a result should only count against the names if a true filing gets nothing, and none has been found so far. And the stories test the deliverance half of Elisha's name and the pairing in Elijah's, but they cannot prove that El means the Judges. That part is the course's reading.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. In Genesis 1 the plants give back their own kind:

And God said, Let grass come up on the earth, and plants producing seed, and fruit-trees giving fruit, in which is their seed, after their sort: and it was so. — Genesis 1:11 (BBE)

What changed is the hand. In Genesis 3 the reaching is stopped. In 2 Kings 6 it is told to go ahead, because the thing reached for was asked for and the court has answered.

Remember: the man said whose the iron was and where it fell, so the court lifted it and told him to take it. Gehazi hid what he took and was given the disease with it. The Judges answer with a single stick of the same kind that was cut, and the hand that takes is the hand that asked.

Words checked

Checked this time against the lexicon and the verse text:

  • The BBE text of 2 Kings 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8, read from the full chapter pages.
  • Elisha (H477): "God is salvation" (BDB, Blue Letter Bible). Fifty-eight uses. Strong's says it is contracted for H474.
  • Gehazi (H1522): "valley of a visionary", with Strong's saying "apparently". Twelve uses, from 2 Kings 4:12 to 8:5, and none in chapter 6.
  • Naaman (H5283): "pleasantness". Sixteen uses.
  • Jordan (H3383): Strong's reads it "a descender", from yarad.
  • Place (maqom, H4725): the BibleTools list gives seven uses from 2 Kings 5:11 to 6:10 (5:11, 6:1, 6:2, 6:6, 6:8, 6:9, 6:10). Genesis 1:9 uses the same word.
  • Float (tsuph, H6687): used in three verses only, Deuteronomy 11:4, 2 Kings 6:6 and Lamentations 3:54.
  • Cast (shalak, H7993): in the form wayyashlekh ("and he cast"), 1 Kings 19:19, 2 Kings 2:21, 4:41 and 6:6 are all on the Englishman's Concordance list of 16 uses.
  • Showed (vayyar'ehu, H7200): the interlinear reads "and he showed him" in 2 Kings 6:6. The form is used twice, in Deuteronomy 34:1 and 2 Kings 6:6.
  • Eyes opened (paqach, H6491): twenty uses. The list read includes Genesis 3:5, 2 Kings 4:35, 6:17 and 6:20, but not all twenty were read.
  • Elijah (H452): from El (H410) and Yah (H3050). Strong's reads it "God of Jehovah" and BDB "Yah(u) is God" or "my God is Jehovah". Seventy-one uses.
  • Yasha (H3467): "to save, be saved, be delivered" (BDB). The lists read show the verb in 2 Kings 6:26, 6:27 (twice) and 13:5, but not every use was read.
  • More BBE text: 2 Kings 2, 3, 4 and 13 in full from chapter pages, and 1 Kings 18:18-40 in full. Every quotation in the Elijah and Elisha section was checked against those pages.
  • Samaria (H8111): Strong's reads it "watch-station", from shamar.
  • The iron (barzel, H1270): "iron (as cutting)" in the NASB lexicon entry for 2 Kings 6:6.

Carried over from the earlier version of this page and not re-checked here: sha'ul as "asked" or "borrowed" (H7592) in 6:5; that the BBE "trees" in 6:4 is the same word as the stick (the stick's etz was re-seen in 6:6); the full Strong's gloss of maqom as "a condition of body or mind"; iron first appearing in Genesis 4:22; the exact Hebrew of Genesis 3:22; the "raise it to yourself" verb matching the one used of Elijah's mantle at the Jordan in 2 Kings 2:13-14.

Open points:

  • God in Elisha's name: the first part is El (H410), the ordinary word for God. It is not the plural Elohim (H430). The lexicons give "God", so reading it as "the Judges are salvation" follows how this course reads "God" everywhere, and is not a dictionary entry.
  • The rest of the name: Elisha (H477) is contracted from Elishua (H474). The earlier version of this page recorded that the lexicons disagree on the second half of H474, with Strong's giving "supplication (or riches)" and others giving "salvation". That was not re-checked here, so "is salvation" is the common reading and not the only one.
  • Two readings of Elijah: Strong's gives "God of Jehovah" and BDB gives "Yah is God". They run in opposite directions, but both put the Judges and the Existing One in the one name. The Carmel verses use Elohim, not El, so the stories confirm the pairing and not what El alone means.
  • The name test: it was not blind, and the rule can take in almost any result. The bears do not count as confirmation. Not every Elisha story has been tested, so the result so far is only that no true filing has been found that got nothing.
  • Carmel and the stick: the BBE words for Gehazi's stick in 4:31 and for the prophets of Baal in 1 Kings 18:29 are nearly the same. The Hebrew was not compared, so this rests on the English only.
  • The throws: the concordance shows the same verb form in each throw. No verse says the throws are linked, and the salt in 2:21 was checked in the Hebrew listing only, because the BBE of 2 Kings 2 and 3 was not read for this page.
  • Gehazi's name: Strong's says "apparently" for the two parts of the name, so the meaning is a reading and not certain.
  • Living-place: the BBE prints "living-place" in 6:2 and again in 8:1. The Hebrew was not pulled, so no claim rests on it.
  • Eyes and eating: Genesis 3:7 was not seen on the paqach list, and the BBE of Genesis 3 was not read for this page. Setting the eyes of 6:17 and 6:20 beside the eating of Genesis 3 is this course's reading.
