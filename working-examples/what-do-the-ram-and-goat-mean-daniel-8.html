What Do the Ram and the Goat Mean in Daniel 8?

In Daniel 8, a beast with two horns is pushed over by a he-goat that crosses the earth without touching it. Then a horn grows until it throws part of the army of heaven to the ground, and the vision ends with a count of evenings and mornings. The Bible in Basic English says "sheep" where most Bibles say "ram". The chapter itself says which kingdoms the two beasts are (8:20-21). What it does not say is how the scene works. In this course the scene is read as taking place inside the reader. The two beasts are two identities, the horns are claims, and the count at the end is the court's own. The words come from Genesis 1. Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The passage (BBE, Daniel 8, key verses):

And I saw in the vision; and when I saw it, I was in the strong town Shushan, which is in the country of Elam; and in the vision I was by the water-door of the Ulai. (8:2) I saw the sheep pushing to the west and to the north and to the south; and no beasts were able to keep their place before him, and no one was able to get people out of his power; but he did whatever his pleasure was and made himself great. (8:4) And while I was giving thought to this, I saw a he-goat coming from the west over the face of all the earth without touching the earth: and the he-goat had a great horn between his eyes. (8:5) And the he-goat became very great: and when he was strong, the great horn was broken, and in its place came up four other horns turned to the four winds of heaven. (8:8) And it became great, even as high as the army of heaven, pulling down some of the army, even of the stars, to the earth and crushing them under its feet. (8:10) And he said to him, For two thousand, three hundred evenings and mornings; then the holy place will be made clean. (8:14) Now while he was talking to me, I went into a deep sleep with my face to the earth: but touching me, he put me on my feet where I had been. (8:18) The sheep which you saw with two horns, they are the kings of Media and Persia. And the he-goat is the king of Greece: and the great horn between his eyes is the first king. (8:20-21) — Daniel 8 (BBE)

The same verses with the meanings put in:

(8:2) I saw the strong one (ram) pushing to the west and to the north and to the south; and no beasts were able to keep their place before him, and no one was able to get people out of his power; but he did whatever his pleasure was and grew himself large. (8:4) And while I was giving thought to this, I saw a prancing one (he-goat) coming from the west over the face of all the earth without laying a hand on the earth: and the he-goat had a large horn between his eyes. (8:5) And the he-goat grew very large: and when he was strong, the large horn was broken, and in its place came up four other horns turned to the four winds of heaven. (8:8) And it grew large, even as high as the army of heaven, pulling down some of the army, even of the stars, to the earth and crushing them under its feet. (8:10) And he said to him, For two thousand, three hundred evenings and dawns; then the holy place will be put right (made righteous). (8:14) Now while he was talking to me, I went into a deep sleep with my face to the earth: but laying a hand on me, he put me on my feet where I had been. (8:18) The strong one which you saw with two horns, they are the kings of Middle-land and Persia. And the prancing one is the king of Greece: and the large horn between his eyes is the first king. (8:20-21)

The meanings used:

  • Shushan (H7800): "lily". Outside Daniel 8:2 it is used in Nehemiah and Esther.
  • Elam (H5867): "hidden, distant". Elam is also a name in Genesis 10:22, a son of Shem.
  • Sheep (ayil, H352): "strength"; also "a ram (from his strength)". The BBE gives "sheep" here and at Genesis 15:9 and 22:13.
  • He-goat (tsaphir, H6842): "a he-goat (as prancing)".
  • Made himself great (gadal, H1431): "to be large", in the causing form, "to make great". It is used at Daniel 8:4, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 25.
  • Great (gadol, H1419): the matching adjective, "great", used for the two great lights (Genesis 1:16), the great sea-beasts (Genesis 1:21), and the goat's great horn (Daniel 8:8, 21).
  • Touch (naga, H5060): "to touch, i.e. lay the hand upon; by implication, to reach; violently, to strike". Its first use is Genesis 3:3.
  • Army (tsaba, H6635): "a mass of persons, especially organized for war (an army)". Its first use is Genesis 2:1.
  • Made clean (tsadaq, H6663): "to be (causatively, make) right in a moral or forensic sense". "Forensic" is courtroom.
  • Madai (H4074): "middle land". The BBE gives "Media". Javan (H3120): "Ionia" or "Greece", which Strong's gives as probably from the same root as yayin (H3196), "wine".
  • Gabriel (H1403): from geber (H1397), "a valiant man or warrior", and El (H410), "strength; mighty; especially the Almighty". It reads as Strong-man of El. See the note on El below.

Who is in the scene

The text names the one who sees, a place, two beasts, a horn that grows, an army, and one who explains. In this course's reading, where the many voices are gathered under one present awareness (see the court language), all of them are in you.

In the passageIn you
Daniel, the one who sees (8:2)You aware right now (Lesson 2)
Lily, in the country of Hidden (8:2)The state you are placed in before anything moves
The sheep (8:3-4)An identity that pushes out in every direction
The he-goat (8:5)A second identity from the same house, with no ground under it
The horn that grows (8:9-11)A claim that makes itself great
The army of heaven (8:10)What the judges have already set in place
Strong-man of El (8:16)The voice in you that explains what you have seen

This is how the Bible is set up, not an extra layer on top of it. Genesis 1:26 makes man the one made in the judges' own image (Lesson 6), so every scene has the same three parts: you, the identity you claim, and the judges that make it real. Daniel sees the vision, the beasts and horns are identities and claims, and the count at the end is the judges'.

How to read it, step by step:

  1. The place. Daniel is put somewhere before anything moves: Lily, in the country of Hidden, beside a stream (8:2). Names say what state is being lived in (Lesson 9). Shushan is also where the book of Esther is set. The reader sees this vision from a hidden place and does not step into it.
  2. Two kinds from one house. The chapter says the sheep is Media and Persia and the he-goat is Greece (8:20-21). In Genesis 10:2 (BBE), "The sons of Japheth: Gomer and Magog and Madai and Javan and Tubal and Meshech and Tiras." Madai is the name the BBE gives as Media, and Javan is Greece, so the two beasts are brothers named in one verse. Persia is not on that list. The sheep is also the animal Abraham finds in Genesis 22:13 (BBE): "Abraham saw a sheep fixed by its horns in the brushwood: and Abraham took the sheep and made a burned offering of it in place of his son." This is Rule One: each kind gives back only what it is, and two kinds cannot become one. So the two push against each other.
  3. "Made himself great." In Genesis the judges make things great and give them their rule. "God made the two great lights: the greater light to be the ruler of the day" (1:16, BBE). "God made great sea-beasts" (1:21, BBE). In Daniel the same family of words turns round. The sheep "made himself great" (8:4), the he-goat "became very great" (8:8), and the horn "became great" (8:10). The verb is the same each time (gadal, H1431), and the BBE and most Bibles add "himself". Here the greatness is claimed by the horn. The chapter never has the judges give it. Whatever is filed gets enforced, and it runs only as far as it goes.
  4. Never touching the ground. The he-goat crosses the whole earth "without touching the earth" (8:5). The word for touch (naga) first appears at Genesis 3:3, where the BBE has "If you take of it or put your hands on it, death will come to you." So the first use of touch is the one the court forbids. A kind with no ground under it can only run over what others stand on. Later in the chapter, "touching me, he put me on my feet where I had been" (8:18). From Lesson 5, the identity is placed on ground. The goat is not placed. Daniel is.
  5. The army thrown down. The horn pulls down "some of the army, even of the stars, to the earth" (8:10). The word for army (tsaba) first appears in Genesis 2:1, where the BBE says "all things in them were complete" and the Hebrew word is the one for the army, or host, of heaven and earth. So what is pulled down is what the judges had finished. One lexicon says some read the army in 8:10 as the stars and others as a figure for Israel. This reading does not choose between them, because in either case it is the finished order being brought to the ground.
  6. Four horns, four winds. The great horn breaks and four come up facing the four winds (8:8). In Lesson 3 the many voices are gathered into one fold. Here a single identity breaks at its strongest point into four that face four ways, and nothing gathers them. The same four winds are brought against Elam in Jeremiah 49:36, and Elam is the country Daniel is standing in.
  7. The rebellion and the shock. The word for rebellion in 8:12-13 (pesha, H6588) first appears in Genesis 31:36, where Jacob asks Laban what his wrong is. It is joined in 8:13 to shamem (H8074), "to stun, devastate", so the rebellion that causes horror. The same root is used again of Daniel at 8:27, where the lexicon has him stunned and the BBE says "full of wonder". That root is not found in Genesis. Its earliest use in the lists is Leviticus 26. The horror is in the filing, and Daniel feels it too.
  8. The count and the verdict. The count is "evenings and mornings" (8:14), and verse 26 calls the vision "the evening and the morning". That is the day-unit of Genesis 1: "there was evening and there was morning" (1:5, BBE), repeated for each of the six days. The Hebrew in 8:14 puts evening and morning together as one word-pair, which the lexicon calls unusual. One lexicon suggests 2300 half-days and others read 2300 days. This reading leaves the number open, because the point that stands is the unit. The limit on the horns is set in the days of creation. The verdict is tsadaq, "make right in a forensic sense". The BBE says "made clean". The same root is Judah's "more righteous than I" in Genesis 38:26. After the count, the holy place is put right by the court.
  9. The one who explains. The voice that explains is Strong-man of El. The chapter calls the speaker "a man's voice" (8:16) and calls Daniel "son of man" (8:17). The man here is the same word that names the first identity in Genesis 1:26. A second speaker in 8:13 is called "a certain one" (palmoni, H6422), used only here. Daniel is ill for days, then gets up and goes back to the king's business (8:27). The one who saw carries away a count and a verdict.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. Daniel 8 uses its words in the same order, and each one is turned.

In Genesis the judges make each part and give it its place. In Daniel 8 the parts are all there, but nobody is gathering them. Each beast and horn runs its own claim. What stops it is not another claim. It is the evening and the morning the court set on the first day.

A short note on the words

El is not Elohim. This course reads "God" as Elohim (H430), the plural judges and rulers. Gabriel's name has El (H410), which is a different word and is singular. Strong's gives it as "strength; mighty". The lexicons do not agree on where the word comes from. Strong's says it is shortened from ayil (H352), the same word as the sheep, and another lexicon says it comes from alah, "to be strong". Both give strength. So the name is read as Strong-man of El. It is not read as "man of the judges", because the text does not use that word here.

Where the BBE softens the words. At Genesis 3:3 it says "put your hands on it" for touch. At Genesis 2:1 it says "all things in them" for the army. At Daniel 8:27 it says "full of wonder" where the lexicon says stunned. The lists above show where each word falls in the Hebrew.

What is not settled here. The number in 8:14, and whether the army in 8:10 is the stars or Israel, are left open.

Where this sits in the storyline

This is the court keeping time. In Genesis 1 the judges build the scene in six days, each closed with an evening and a morning. In Daniel 8 the scene is knocked about by kinds that make themselves great, and the court closes it with the same unit and then puts the holy place right. The reader does not stop the horns. The reader sees them, is touched and stood on his feet, and carries the count.

Remember: the sheep and the he-goat are two kinds from one house, each making itself great, and the he-goat has no ground under it. The army the judges finished is pulled to the ground, and the horns break into four with nobody gathering them. The vision ends with the court's own count of evenings and mornings, and the verdict word means to make right in a courtroom sense.
