What Does the Bible Say About Money? 12 Verses in Context

Money verses are usually read as rules about cash. This course reads them the way it reads every other passage: there is a ground, an identity standing on it, and a court that makes real whatever is claimed, after its kind. So each verse below gets the three questions from Lesson 5: what is the ground, who is standing on it, and what has changed from the Genesis 1 picture?

The words are from the Bible in Basic English (BBE), which sometimes reads differently from the version you know. Where it does, the entry says so.

Start with Genesis 1

Genesis 1 builds the whole scene before the man appears, and the food is already in it:

And God said, See, I have given you every plant producing seed, on the face of all the earth, and every tree which has fruit producing seed: they will be for your food. — Genesis 1:29 (BBE)

The beasts and birds are fed the same way: every green plant is given for their food (Genesis 1:30). So in the original picture the need is already met. Every money verse below is that picture changed. Someone owes, someone holds back, someone gives, someone fixes the heart on having. The court is the same court (Lesson 2), and it makes real the identity that is actually held.

1 Timothy 6:10: Is money the root of all evil?

For the love of money is a root of all evil: and some whose hearts were fixed on it have been turned away from the faith, and been wounded with unnumbered sorrows. — 1 Timothy 6:10 (BBE)

The BBE says "a root", not "the root", and it says the love of money, not money. Verse 8 gives the picture the heart has left: if we have food and a roof, let that be enough. That is Genesis 1:29 in other words, the supply already in the scene. Verse 9 says those who want wealth are "taken as in a net". Verse 18 tells the rich to do good and be quick to give, so having wealth is not the target.

Plot point: a heart fixed on getting is an identity standing off the original picture. The court rules to match, after its kind (Lesson 7), and the verse names what comes back: "unnumbered sorrows".

Matthew 6:24: Can you serve two masters?

No man is able to be a servant to two masters: for he will have hate for the one and love for the other, or he will keep to one and have no respect for the other. You may not be servants of God and of wealth. — Matthew 6:24 (BBE)

Where many Bibles say "mammon", the BBE says "wealth". "God" is Elohim, the court of judges (Lesson 3), and wealth is the other ruling state a person can hold. A few lines later the birds are the example for having no cares about life (6:25-26). That is Genesis 1:30 again: the birds are fed because the scene already supplies them.

Plot point: only one identity rules at a time (Lesson 6), and the court enforces the one that rules. The verse is not about having money. It is about which state is master.

Philippians 4:19: Will my needs be supplied?

And my God will give you all you have need of from the wealth of his glory in Christ Jesus. — Philippians 4:19 (BBE)

With the names put in: the gift came through Epaphroditus, "Lovely" (G1891), and Paul, "little", calls it "a perfume of a sweet smell, an offering well pleasing" (4:18). The Philippians had given, and Paul says what they gave is "fruit which may be put to your credit" (4:17). Fruit is the Genesis 1:11-12 word: what is planted gives fruit after its kind. "Credit" is account language, the court keeping the record.

Plot point: verse 19 is the return on what was planted, and in the story the "you" is the people who gave. It is the seed rule applied to a gift. Lesson 26 sets out the same pattern as ask and receive.

Malachi 3:10: Does tithing open the windows of heaven?

Let your tenths come into the store-house so that there may be food in my house, and put me to the test by doing so, says the Lord of armies, and see if I do not make the windows of heaven open and send down such a blessing on you that there is no room for it. — Malachi 3:10 (BBE)

With the name put in: Malachi is "My messenger" (H4401). The people are holding back the tenth, and the verses before say the whole nation is under a curse for it (3:8-9). A curse on the ground is where Lesson 17 starts. The tenth goes into the store-house so there is food in the house, and the test is what comes back: a blessing with "no room for it".

Plot point: holding back presents lack, and the court rules in favour of what is presented. Putting in is the seed, and the court gives back a full store after its kind. Proverbs 3:9-10 says it the other way round: honour with your wealth and your store-houses will be "full of grain".

Proverbs 22:7: Is the borrower a servant to the lender?

The man of wealth has rule over the poor, and he who gets into debt is a servant to his creditor. — Proverbs 22:7 (BBE)

In Genesis 1:26 the man is given rule over the fish, the birds, the cattle and the creeping things. He is not given rule over other people. Here one person has rule over another, and the one in debt is a servant, so the original picture has changed. The sayings around it say a good name is worth more than great wealth (22:1), that the rich and the poor "come face to face" (22:2), and that you should not make yourself responsible for debts (22:26-27).

Plot point: this is an observation, not a command (Lesson 25 shows how Proverbs is read). Owing puts the debtor under another's rule, the reverse of the man who was placed to rule.

Deuteronomy 8:18: Who gives the power to get wealth?

Say not then, in your hearts, My power and the strength of my hands have got me this wealth. But keep in mind the Lord your God: for it is he who gives you the power to get wealth, so that he may give effect to the agreement which he made by his oath with your fathers, as at this day. — Deuteronomy 8:17-18 (BBE)

With the names put in: this is said to Israel, "he shall prevail", and the agreement is with the fathers, the line of Abraham, "father of a multitude" (H85). The speech comes before they go in to take the land (8:1), the ground they are about to be placed on. Verse 17 is the warning: do not say my own power got me this wealth.

Plot point: the power comes from the court giving effect to the agreement, not from the one claiming it. In the story the wealth is tied to that agreement and to the identity in the names, the way a name is a code for its state (Lesson 9).

Luke 6:38: Give, and it will be given to you?

Give, and it will be given to you; good measure, crushed down, full and running over, they will give to you. For in the same measure as you give, it will be given to you again. — Luke 6:38 (BBE)

The lines before are about being merciful, not judging, and forgiving (6:36-37). Judging is the court's work (Lesson 3). The measure in the last line is the measure used on other people.

Plot point: "in the same measure" is the after-its-kind rule said plainly (Lesson 7). What goes out as mercy, forgiveness or giving comes back as the same kind. In the story the things being measured are mercy and forgiveness as well as giving.

Proverbs 13:22: Does a good man leave an inheritance?

The heritage of the good man is handed down to his children's children; and the wealth of the sinner is stored up for the upright man. — Proverbs 13:22 (BBE)

Earlier in the chapter, wealth "quickly got" becomes less, but a store got "by the work of his hands" is increased (13:11). A heritage handed down to children's children is the seed rule: what is planted gives back after its kind, and it carries on into the next generation (Genesis 1:11).

Plot point: the verse sets two kinds side by side, the good man and the sinner, and Proverbs does this all the way through (Lesson 25). In this course sin means missing the mark. What is held on a false claim does not stay, and what is built on the right ground is handed on.

Hebrews 13:5: Be content with what you have?

Be free from the love of money and pleased with the things which you have; for he himself has said, I will be with you at all times. — Hebrews 13:5 (BBE)

The BBE ends differently from the version you know. "I will be with you" is the name's own root: at the bush the name is "I will be", "I AM" (Exodus 3:14, Lesson 2). So the reason given for being free of the love of money is the I AM present in the scene, not extra supply. "Pleased with the things which you have" is Genesis 1 again: the scene is already supplied.

Plot point: contentment here is not settling for less. It is standing in the scene as it was built, with the I AM present in it.

Mark 10:25: Why is it hard for a rich man to enter the kingdom?

It is simpler for a camel to go through a needle's eye, than for a man of wealth to come into the kingdom of God. — Mark 10:25 (BBE)

With the name put in: a man comes to Jesus, "Existing One is salvation", and says he has kept the laws since he was young (10:20). He is told one thing is needed: turn his goods into money, give it to the poor, and come with Jesus (10:21). That is Rule Two, leave and join (Lesson 8). The man goes away sad, because he had much property (10:22). Property is ground, and his identity is built on the ground he holds.

Plot point: verse 25 describes the result. The identity of a man of wealth does not pass through the needle's eye while it is still held. The kingdom is the new identity in place, and he has not left the old one. The disciples ask who then can be saved (10:26), and verse 27 answers that what cannot be done one way can be done another.

Ecclesiastes 10:19: Does money answer all things?

A feast is for laughing, and wine makes glad the heart; but by the one and the other money is wasted. — Ecclesiastes 10:19 (BBE)

Many Bibles end this verse "money answers all things". The BBE reads the other way: money is wasted. The translations disagree here, so this page does not build on either one. What is clear is the ground. Verses 16 to 18 are about a land whose rulers feast in the morning, and a house where the roof falls in because no work is done.

Plot point: in the BBE this is a verse about waste on ground that is not being worked, not about the power of money. If you know it as "money answers all things", that comes from the other translations.

3 John 1:2: Does the Bible promise prosperity?

My loved one, it is my prayer that you may do well in all things, and be healthy in body, even as your soul does well. — 3 John 1:2 (BBE)

With the name put in: the letter is to Gaius, "lord" (G1050). The BBE does not say "prosper". It says "do well in all things", and it is the greeting at the start of a short letter. The wish has a pattern in it: things go well on the outside "even as" they go well on the inside. The inside is named first and the outside follows.

Plot point: that is the court's order in one line. The identity held on the inside is what the outside matches, after its kind (Lesson 7).

What the twelve have in common

Each verse is the Genesis 1 scene changed, and each is read the same way. Put together, they say:

  • Where the heart is fixed: 1 Timothy 6:10, Matthew 6:24, Hebrews 13:5.
  • What is put in comes back after its kind: Luke 6:38, Philippians 4:19, Malachi 3:10, Proverbs 13:22, 3 John 1:2.
  • Who rules when something is owed or received: Proverbs 22:7, Deuteronomy 8:18.
  • What the identity is told to leave: Mark 10:25.
  • Where the translations disagree: Ecclesiastes 10:19.

Where the word lists differ

  • Epaphroditus (G1891): Thayer gives "charming" and another lexicon gives "lovely". Strong's builds the name from "devoted to" and Aphrodite, so the gloss "Lovely" is a reading.
  • Gaius (G1050): Strong's says only that it is a Latin name. The "lord" meaning comes from Thayer and other lexicons.
  • Malachi (H4401): "My messenger" is the Strong's meaning. One lexicon notes it may not be a personal name at all.

Remember: every money verse is the Genesis 1 scene changed. Ask what the ground is, who is standing on it, and what the court is enforcing after its kind.
