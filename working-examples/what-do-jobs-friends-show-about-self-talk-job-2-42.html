What Do Job's Friends Show About How We Talk to Ourselves? Job 2:11 to 42:9

Job's three friends arrive in silence and stay silent for seven days. Then Job speaks, and for most of the book they answer him. Their speeches run from chapter 4 to chapter 25, and they repeat one another, escalate and run dry. At the end the court speaks to them directly (42:7-9) and rules on how they spoke. This page reads that dialogue as the way a mind talks to itself when it meets pain, because the course reads every person in the text as a voice in the reader (Lesson 1).

The short answer: the friends use a true rule on a seed nobody has shown. Eliphaz states the rule in 4:8: what is ploughed and planted is what comes back. That is the after-its-kind rule of Lesson 7. But they read the harvest (Job's suffering) back to a planting they never see, and they fill the gap with accusations (22:5-9). They also speak about the court, to Job, instead of to the court. That is the move the voice makes in Genesis 3:1, and the blame that follows is the Genesis 3 chain. The court's ruling in 42:7-9 is about how they spoke, and the repair is to own the filing, go to the court, and be prayed for by the one they accused.

In seven lines:

  1. They come from three different places to one meeting-place and sit silent while the pain is plain (2:11-13).
  2. When Job speaks, every answer opens with a ruling, not a question (4:7, 8:3-4, 11:2).
  3. The rule is true (4:8). The seed they name is not shown, and the court's own description of Job (2:3) says the opposite of what they accuse (22:5-9).
  4. They speak about the court to Job. Job speaks to the court (16:20).
  5. They repeat one another: the same question about "upright" and "clean" goes round three speeches (4:17, 15:14, 25:4), and the "wind" accusation goes round four (8:2, 15:2, 16:3, 20:3).
  6. It escalates from praise (4:3-4) to invented specifics (22:6-9), then runs out. Bildad's last speech is six verses, and Zophar has no third speech.
  7. The court speaks (42:7). The friends amend their filing, Job prays for them, and the comfort they tried to give in 2:11 lands in 42:11.

The passage with the meanings put in

Here are the same two passages with every name and place turned into its meaning. Job becomes Hated, the Lord becomes the Existing One, and each friend and his ground is read as a state.

And now, take seven oxen and seven sheep, and go to my servant Hated, and give a burned offering for yourselves, and my servant Hated will make prayer for you, that I may not send punishment on you; because you have not said what is right about me, as my servant Hated has. Read with the names in, the one called Hated is visited by God-of-gold from the South, Confusing-love from the Dell and Departing from the Pleasant place: three grounds, one meeting-place. At the end the Existing One speaks to God-of-gold first, sends all three to Hated, and gives ear to him. What each name does across the speeches is under "What the names add" below, and Lesson 12 reads the name Hated itself.

The meanings used

  • Job (H347): "hated (i.e. persecuted)", from H340 (Strong's).
  • The Lord (YHVH, H3068): the Existing One.
  • Eliphaz (H464): "God of gold", from el (H410) and paz (H6337). BDB: "God is fine gold?".
  • Teman (H8487): "south". BDB places it as a northern district of Edom, so the gloss is a direction and the location is a district. Eliphaz is "the Temanite".
  • Bildad (H1085): Strong's says "of uncertain derivation" and gives no meaning. BDB suggests "Bel has loved". The Blue Letter Bible outline gives "confusing (by mingling) love". This page uses the Blue Letter Bible gloss.
  • Shuhite (H7747): a descendant of Shuach (H7744). Strong's glosses Shuach "dell". The Blue Letter Bible outline gives "wealth". Shuah is a son of Abraham by Keturah (Genesis 25:2). This page uses Strong's "dell".
  • Zophar (H6691): "departing", from tsaphar (H6852). The Blue Letter Bible outline gives "sparrow". This page uses Strong's.
  • Naamathite (H5284): from a place "corresponding in name (but not identical)" with Naamah (H5279), "pleasantness". BDB calls it a people from an unused proper name. This page uses the place-name reading, "the Pleasant place".
  • Friend (rea, H7453): the word at Job 16:20 ("my friends"), 16:21 ("his neighbour") and 42:7 ("your two friends").
  • Right (nekonah, from kuwn, H3559): BDB lists the feminine participle nekonah as "what is right, the right" at Job 42:7-8. Strong's root: "to be erect (stand perpendicular); hence to set up ... establish, fix, prepare ... right". The Blue Letter Bible counts 219 uses. The same root is behind "prepare" in Bildad's 8:8 and Zophar's 11:13 (KJV "prepare", BBE "put your heart right").
  • To me (elay, from el, H413): Job 42:7 reads dibbartem elay nekonah. The Lexham English Bible and the Open English Translation give "to me". The BBE and KJV give "about/of me".
  • Lift the face (nasa, H5375, with paniym, H6440): Zophar 11:15 (tissa paneka, "you will lift your face"), Job 42:8 (panaw essa, "him will I accept"), and Job 42:9 (vayyissa YHVH et-pene Iyyov). Genesis 4:7 has se'et (H7613, "from nasa") for the lifting up that is set against Cain's fallen face.
  • Folly / punishment (nebalah, H5039): Job 42:8. Strong's: "foolishness, i.e. (morally) wickedness; concretely, a crime; by extension, punishment". KJV "folly". BBE "punishment". It is the feminine of nabal (H5036), the word behind the name Nabal on the Nabal and Abigail page.

How to read it, step by step

  1. Three grounds, one meeting-place. Each comes "from his place" (2:11): a direction, a dell, a pleasant place. They "came together to a meeting-place". Three different grounds end on one verdict, which is how a single mind can hold several voices that all say the same thing.
  2. The silence is right. They do not recognise him (2:12), and they stay seven days without a word "for they saw that his pain was very great" (2:13). The failure does not start with the visit. It starts when speech starts.
  3. Every answer opens with a ruling. Job speaks (3:3, "Let destruction take the day of my birth"). Eliphaz answers with a question that already has its answer: "Have you ever seen destruction come to an upright man?" (4:7). Bildad: "If your children have done evil against him, then their punishment is from his hand" (8:4). Zophar: "is a man seen to be right because he is full of talk?" (11:2). Nobody asks Job what he planted.
  4. The rule is true and the seed is not shown. Eliphaz: "those by whom trouble has been ploughed, and evil planted, get the same for themselves" (4:8). The rule is Genesis 1:11. The planting is the gap. Their reading runs backwards from harvest to seed. By chapter 22 Eliphaz fills the gap with specifics: "Is not your evil-doing great? and there is no end to your sins" (22:5), then goods taken, bread kept back, widows sent away (22:6-9). The court's own description of Job is "a man without sin and upright, fearing God and keeping himself far from evil" (2:3). Lesson 12 reads the seed that is actually shown: the fear Job states in 3:25. The friends name a different seed.
  5. They speak about the court, to Job. Eliphaz's authority is a voice he could not see: "Something was present before me, but I was not able to see it clearly ... a quiet voice came to my ears, saying: May a man be upright before God?" (4:16-17). Zophar wishes the court would "take up the word" and "make clear to you the secrets of wisdom" (11:5-6). Eliphaz asks, "Were you present at the secret meeting of God?" (15:8). Job's direction is the other way: "My friends make sport of me; to God my eyes are weeping" (16:20).
  6. The line goes round. The question about "upright" and "clean" appears in Eliphaz's first and second speeches and in Bildad's last, nearly word for word: 4:17 ("May a man be upright before God? or a man be clean before his Maker?"), 15:14 ("What is man, that he may be clean? and how may the son of woman be upright?"), 25:4 ("How then is it possible for man to be upright before God? or how may he be clean who is a son of woman?"). It begins as the unseen voice's line in 4:17 and ends as Bildad's closing line. Job notices the loop: "Such things have frequently come to my ears" (16:2).

Job hands their lines back and can say why. The "wind" charge is Bildad's (8:2), Eliphaz's ("the east wind", 15:2), Zophar's (20:3), and Job's own answer to the second: "May words which are like the wind be stopped?" (16:3). He also says the voices are not a different kind of speaker: "It would not be hard for me to say such things if your souls were in my soul's place; joining words together against you, and shaking my head at you" (16:4). That is the reading of this page in Job's own words. Anyone outside a pain can produce these lines.

The dialogue ends by running out. Elihu's section opens: "So these three men gave no more answers to Job, because he seemed to himself to be right" (32:1), and reports that Elihu was angry with the three "because they had been unable to give him an answer, and had not made Job's sin clear" (32:3).

The Hebrew behind the ruling

To me, not about me. The Hebrew has elay, "to me". That is how the Lexham English Bible and the Open English Translation render it, and it fits what the text shows the two sides doing. The friends talk to Job about the court (4:17, 11:5-6, 15:8). Job talks to the court (16:20-21). The preposition can also mean "concerning", so the page does not rest on it alone. The direction of speech is visible in the speeches themselves.

The right thing, set up. Nekonah is from kuwn, "to set up, establish". The friends use that root as advice for Job: "prepare" yourself to the search of the fathers (8:8), "put your heart right" (11:13). The court then says they have not spoken the nekonah to it (42:7-8). The set-right they offered Job is the thing they are told they lack. This is an echo of one root, and the page marks it so.

The face. Zophar's formula is "if you put your heart right ... your face will be lifted up" (11:13-15). Genesis 4 has the same formula from the court to Cain. Cain's face "became sad", and the court asks, "Why are you angry? and why is your face sad?" (Genesis 4:5-6, BBE). Genesis 4:7 answers: "If you do well, will you not have honour?" (BBE). The Hebrew word behind "honour" is se'et, a lifting up, set against the doing-well condition, so the BBE hides the idiom here as it does at 42:9. So the friends' whole system is the Genesis 4 pairing of doing right and a lifted face (Cain's offering), pointed at a man who is not the seed they say. In 42:8-9 the same idiom is spoken of Job, and by the court: "him will I accept" (literally, I will lift his face) and "the Lord lifted the face of Job". The BBE has "the Lord gave ear to Job" at 42:9, which hides the idiom. The friends were warned about it earlier. Job tells them the court will put them right "if you have respect for persons in secret" (13:10, BBE), and the Hebrew there is the same pair, tissa'un paniym, lifting faces. Eliphaz uses it again in 22:26 for the face lifted up to the court. In Job the lifted face is both the reward the friends promise and the partiality Job accuses them of. The lifting Zophar promised on his terms arrives by the court's act, and it is the thing the friends now need from Job: "my servant Job will make prayer for you" (42:8).

Folly. The BBE reads "that I may not send punishment on you". The Hebrew word is nebalah, which Strong's ranges from "foolishness" to "by extension, punishment" and the KJV renders "folly". It is the feminine of the word behind Nabal's name. Zophar called Job "a man full of talk" (11:2). The word the court uses of what the friends risk is the word for what talk without a seen seed produces.

What changed from Genesis 1 to 4

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis picture, changed. In Genesis 1 the Judges speak each part into place and see it good. The voices in Job do the opposite: they speak about the court's verdict and they have not seen the seed.

What changed is the direction of the speech. In Genesis 1 the Judges speak and the scene is so. In Genesis 3 a voice speaks about the court's word to the one in the garden, and the court's questions are met with blame. In Job the voices are three, each from its own ground. They carry the Genesis 3 move into long argument, and the court's single sentence in 42:7 ends it. The friends' rule is Genesis 1:11. The face is Genesis 4. What fails is the seed they name for Job.

What the names add

A name shows the state, and the story runs after its kind (Lesson 9). The readings below are marked as readings.

  • Eliphaz, God of gold. He leads: he is first in 2:11, first in each round, and he is the one the court addresses (42:7). His voice is the valued, authoritative one, and his authority is an unseen voice at night (4:16). His last speech ends in gold: "put your gold in the dust ... Then the Ruler of all will be your gold" (22:24-25, BBE). The Hebrew words for "gold" in 22:24-25 were not checked against paz, so this is a matching picture, not a word link.
  • Bildad, from the Dell. He leans on the past ("the past generations", 8:8) and admits he has no knowledge of his own (8:9). The gloss of his own name is uncertain, so nothing is built on it.
  • Zophar, Departing, of Pleasantness. His speech is the pleasant promise: "your life will be brighter than day" (11:17). His name is "departing", and he speaks twice and leaves. The occurrence list for his name (H6691) has four verses: 2:11, 11:1, 20:1, 42:9.

A question to ask of any speech in the dialogue: where is the seed? If the speech names a harvest and a verdict but not a planting that the text shows, it is one of the friends' lines.

Remember: the friends' rule is the true rule of the seed. Their failure is the seed they file for Job, and the direction of their speech: about the court, to Job, round and round. The court's repair is plain. They file for themselves, the one they accused prays, and the comfort lands where the verdict used to be.

The limits of this reading:

  • The ruling is narrow. 42:7 says the friends have not spoken the right thing to the court "as my servant Job has". It does not say every word of Job was right. In 42:6 he withdraws what he said, and Lesson 12 reads the fear he states in 3:25 as the seed that is shown.
  • "To me" versus "about me". Elay is "to me" in some translations and "about me" in the BBE and KJV. The preposition can mean either. The direction-of-speech reading is also carried by 16:20-21 and the friends' own lines.
  • The kuwn echo. The root (H3559) is behind 8:8, 11:13, 42:7 and 42:8. The Blue Letter Bible counts 219 uses, and the occurrence list was not read for this page. It is an echo, not a proof.
  • The face idiom. The nasa and paniym idiom is behind 11:15, 13:10, 42:8 and 42:9, and nasa is behind the lifting of Genesis 4:7. The Hebrew of 13:10 was checked on an interlinear page. 22:26 is in the BBE as "lifted up to God", but its Hebrew was not checked here. The full occurrence list was not read. The link to Genesis 4 rests on the idea of a lifted face against a fallen one. The BBE wording of 4:7 was taken from one source (ebible.org) and should be confirmed against Blue Letter Bible.
  • Names. Bildad, Shuhite, Zophar and Naamathite carry glosses that differ between Strong's, BDB and the Blue Letter Bible, and those are marked in the list. Strong's says the Naamathite's place only "corresponds" with Naamah. Teman is a direction in Strong's and a district of Edom in BDB.
  • Matching pictures, not word links: "ploughed" and "planted" in 4:8 and the seed of Genesis 1:11, the "gold" of 22:24-25 and Eliphaz's name, and the "wind" lines (8:2, 15:2, 16:3, 20:3), which are linked in the BBE English only.
  • "Folly". The BBE's "punishment" is within Strong's range for nebalah. The page notes the KJV "folly" and the link to Nabal, and does not say the BBE is wrong.
  • Zophar's absence. That he has no third speech comes from the occurrence list of his name and from 32:1. It is not a claim about why.
  • The comfort in 42:11 is given by "all his brothers and sisters, and his friends of earlier days". It is not the three. The page notes the same BBE phrases ("make clear their grief for him", "gave him comfort") in 2:11 and 42:11. The Hebrew behind them was not compared.
