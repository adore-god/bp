Whose Son Is the Christ? How Can David Call Him Lord? (Matthew 22:41-46)

Short answer: in Matthew 22:42 Jesus asks the separatists whose son the Christ is. They answer from the family line: "The Son of David". Jesus then sets David's own words beside that answer: David, in the Spirit, calls him Lord, so how is he his son? Nobody can answer. Genesis 5:1-3 gives two ways a man is made: in the image of the Judges by their word, and in the image of his father by the line. The separatists answer with the line. David's words point to the seat the Existing One gives.

People search this as "whose son is the Christ", "how can David call him Lord" and "the Lord said to my Lord". It is the last of four questions in Matthew 22, and the only one Jesus asks. The same scene is in Mark 12:35-37 and Luke 20:41-44. Here is the passage with the meanings put in, and then the story step by step.

Whose son with the meanings put in: Matthew 22:41-46

This is the BBE with the meaning of each name put in, in bold. Where each meaning comes from is in the words list further down.

Now while the separatists were together, Existing-One-is-salvation put a question to them, saying, (22:41) What is your opinion of the Anointed? whose son is he? They say to him, The Son of Beloved. (22:42) He says to them, How then does Beloved in the Spirit give him the name of Lord (controller), saying, (22:43) The Existing One said to my lord (controller), Be seated at my right hand, till I put under your feet all those who are against you? (22:44) If Beloved then gives him the name of Lord (controller), how is he his son? (22:45) And no one was able to give him an answer, and so great was their fear of him, that from that day no one put any more questions to him. (22:46)

How to read it, step by step

  1. The names set the scene. "While the Pharisees were together" (22:41): the separatists, gathered. Existing-One-is-salvation puts the question to them. Three groups have already put questions to him in this chapter: the tax, the seven brothers and the chief rule in the law. He answered each. Now the roles turn round. He asks, and at the end nobody asks anything more (22:46).
  2. The same word again. "What is your opinion of the Christ? whose son is he?" (22:42, BBE). "Whose" is tinos, the word that opened "whose is this image" (22:20) and "whose wife will she be" (22:28). Christ (christos) means "anointed". The separatists answer "The Son of David", and David means "beloved". They answer by the line. The line is real: it runs from Perez to David (Ruth 4:18-22), and it was carried by the same name-keeping rule the Sadducees quoted. Ruth 4:12 speaks of "Perez, the son whom Tamar gave to Judah", and Ruth 4:17 calls Obed "the father of Jesse, the father of David". A son in a line is a son after his father's kind: Adam "had a son like himself, after his image" (Genesis 5:3, BBE; after its kind).
  3. David's own words. "How then does David in the Spirit give him the name of Lord" (22:43, BBE). Three things are in that line. First, the Spirit: in Genesis 1:2 the Spirit is "moving on the face of the waters" before the Judges say anything, and David is speaking "in the Spirit", not out of his line. Second, the name: David "gives him the name" (the Greek verb is kaleo, "to call"), and a name tells the state (Lesson 9). The name is Lord, kyrios, "supreme in authority, i.e. controller". Third, the words are from Psalm 110:1, headed "A Psalm. Of David" in the BBE: "The Lord said to my lord". In the Hebrew the first Lord is YHVH, the Existing One, and the second is adoni, "sovereign, i.e. controller". The Existing One speaks, and David names the one addressed his controller.
  4. Be seated. "Be seated at my right hand, till I put under your feet all those who are against you" (22:44, BBE). Only one thing is asked of the one addressed: sit. The putting under is done by the speaker: "till I put". That is the model in one line: the identity is offered and the rest is made to follow. Compare Genesis 1:26-28, where the man is made in the Judges' image and told "let him have rule" and "be masters of it". In the psalm the rule comes with a seat, and what is against him is brought down.
  5. The two answers. "If David then gives him the name of Lord, how is he his son?" (22:45). A son comes after his father's kind and is under his father's name. A Lord is over. Genesis 5:1-3 holds both ways a man is made in three verses: "In the day when God made man, he made him in the image of God" (5:1), and then Adam "had a son like himself, after his image" (5:3). One is made by the Judges' word and seated before any father. The other is born by the line. The separatists give the second answer. David's words point to the first. The page on Son of God and Son of man follows the same two through Genesis 6 and Luke 3:38.
  6. They cannot answer. "And no one was able to give him an answer, and so great was their fear of him, that from that day no one put any more questions to him" (22:46, BBE). The question stays with the reader: what is your opinion of the Anointed? The line gives a son, the word gives a Lord, and both are asked about in one person.

What the Son of God page adds

The Son of God and Son of man page follows two sides of one person from Genesis to Luke. Set beside this scene, they sort the two answers.

  • "Son of David" is the ground side. It is a line, a father's kind, someone who stands in the story and claims by where he comes from. That is the "Son of man" side: the man formed from the ground (Genesis 2:7), whose son is "after his image" (Genesis 5:3). The Hebrew of Ezekiel 2:1 says it as ben-adam, "son of the man".
  • "Lord" is the court side. It is a seat given by what is said, not by descent. In Genesis 1:27 the man is made "in the image of God" by the Judges, and Luke 3:38 ends the line at Adam, "the son of God". That is the side the court issues and makes real, and it is the side a voice tests in the temptation: "If you are the Son of God" (Matthew 4:3).
  • Genesis keeps both in one person, and the Gospels put them back together. The separatists see only the ground side and ask what line it came from. David's words bring in the court side. So "how is he his son?" is asked about someone who is both, and the answer is not a choice between the two.
  • That is why no one can answer. A mind that works only from the line cannot hold the seat as well. It can say whose son, but it cannot say who is seated.

Three "whose" questions, one chain

One Greek word, tinos, opens the three questions in Matthew 22 that ask what something belongs to. Each one is answered from a different place in Genesis, and the three come in Genesis order.

Words and sources

  • Pharisees (pharisaios, G5330): "a separatist, i.e. exclusively religious".
  • Jesus (Iesous, G2424): Existing-One-is-salvation (Lesson 2).
  • Christ (christos, G5547): "anointed, i.e. the Messiah", from chrio (G5548).
  • David (H1732): "beloved".
  • Whose (tinos, G5101): Matthew 22:20, 22:28 and 22:42.
  • Spirit (pneuma, G4151): "a current of air, i.e. breath (blast) or a breeze; by analogy or figuratively, a spirit".
  • Lord (kyrios, G2962): "supreme in authority, i.e. controller; by implication, master (as a respectful title)".
  • The Lord in Psalm 110:1 (YHVH, H3068): "(the) self-existent or eternal", read as the Existing One. My lord (adoni, from adon, H113): "sovereign, i.e. controller (human or divine)".
  • Be seated (yashab, H3427): "to sit down".
  • Give the name: the Greek verb in 22:43 is kaleo, "to call". Son (huios, G5207).
  • Chapters and verses read in the BBE: Matthew 22, Luke 20, Psalm 110, Genesis 1 and 5, Ruth 4.

Check this yourself

  1. Read Matthew 22:41-46 in the BBE, with the chapter around it.
  2. Read Psalm 110:1 and see which word is YHVH and which is adoni.
  3. Read Genesis 5:1-3 and see the two ways a man is made in three verses.
  4. Read Ruth 4:12 and 4:17-22 and follow the line from Perez to David.
  5. Set Luke 20:41-44 beside Matthew's account.
  6. Ask whether the reading fits the words on the page. If it does not, say so.

Common questions

Whose son is the Christ in Matthew 22:42?

The separatists answer 'The Son of David', and Jesus does not say that is wrong. He asks how David, in the Spirit, gives him the name of Lord (22:43-45). In this course the line gives the Son of David, and what the Existing One says gives the Lord.

Why did Jesus ask how David can call the Christ Lord?

A son comes after his father's kind (Genesis 5:3) and is under his father's name, but David gives him the name of Lord. The question sets the line against the name. Nobody could answer (22:46).

Where is 'The Lord said to my Lord' from?

Psalm 110:1, headed 'A Psalm. Of David' in the BBE. Matthew 22:44 and Luke 20:42-43 quote it, and Mark 12:36 has it too.

Who are the two Lords in Psalm 110:1?

In the Hebrew the first is YHVH (H3068), the Existing One, and the second is adoni (H113), 'sovereign, i.e. controller'. In the Greek both are kyrios (G2962). The Existing One speaks, and David names the one addressed his controller.

What does 'sit at my right hand' mean in Matthew 22:44?

The one addressed is told to be seated, and the speaker says 'till I put' what is against him under his feet. He does nothing but sit. In this course that is the model in Lesson 2: an identity is offered and the rest is made to follow.

What does Christ mean?

Christ (christos, G5547) means 'anointed', from chrio. 'The Christ' in 22:42 is 'the Anointed'.

What does 'Son of David' mean?

David means 'beloved' (H1732), so the Son of David is the son in the Beloved's line. The line runs from Perez to David in Ruth 4:18-22, and it was carried by the rule about raising up seed (Genesis 38; Ruth 4).

Why did no one dare ask him any more questions?

They could not answer the question he asked (22:46). The chapter ends with the questions stopped, and the question stays with the one reading: what is your opinion of the Christ?

Where else is this story in the Bible?

Mark 12:35-37 and Luke 20:41-44. Luke has Jesus ask 'Why do they say that the Christ is the son of David?' (20:41), without the separatists' answer.
