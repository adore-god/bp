What Does "Holy" Mean in the Bible?

Holy means set apart. The Hebrew verb is qadash (H6942), "to be set apart, consecrated". The word for holy, qadosh (H6918), comes from it, and BDB defines it as "separate, apart, and so sacred, holy". Strong's list for the verb starts at Genesis 2:3:

And God gave his blessing to the seventh day and made it holy: because on that day he took his rest from all the work which he had made and done. — Genesis 2:3 (BBE)

The first thing set apart is a day, and it is set apart from the six days of work before it. The "God" who gives the blessing and makes the day holy is Elohim (H430), the plural judges and rulers (Lesson 3). So the court is the one that sets apart: it blesses the day and rules it separate from the work. In the Genesis 1 picture the man has not been told to do anything yet. So holy begins as a state the judges declare, not a rule.

The ground. At the bush, the BBE reads:

And he said, Do not come near: take off your shoes from your feet, for the place where you are is holy. — Exodus 3:5 (BBE)

Exodus 3:1 puts Moses at the back of "the waste land", at Horeb. Horeb (H2722) means "waste", from the verb "desolate" (H2717). The next verse is the "I am" line: "I am the God of your fathers" (Exodus 3:6, BBE). In this reading, a place is holy where the I AM stands in it, however waste its name (Lesson 2).

The Hebrew of Exodus 3:5 has a word the BBE leaves out. It reads admat-qodesh: adamah (H127), "soil", joined to qodesh (H6944), "holiness". So it is "soil of holiness", soil that is set apart. Adamah is the soil the man was formed from (Genesis 2:7), the soil that was cursed on his account (Genesis 3:17), and the soil he was sent out to work (Genesis 3:23). The same soil word that carried the curse is named holy where the I AM stands on it. The Greek of Acts 7:33 uses the New Testament word for holy, hagios (G40), for this same ground.

The identity.

Say to all the people of Israel, You are to be holy, for I, the Lord your God, am holy. — Leviticus 19:2 (BBE)

In the Hebrew, "your God" is Elohim (H430), the judges, and both "holy" words here are qadosh (H6918): the people are told to be it, and the judges are it. The judges are the set-apart standard, and the identity claimed is told to match them. That is Rule Two, leave and join: step away from the old state, then join the new one.

The order in Joshua. "Make yourselves holy" comes first (Joshua 3:5, BBE). Five verses later: "the living God is among you" (3:10). See the living God.

The breath. The New Testament word for holy is hagios (G40), and Strong's gives it as "sacred (physically pure, morally blameless or religious, ceremonially consecrated)". Thayer says the Greek Old Testament uses it for qadosh (H6918). So it is the same idea in Greek: set apart. Now put two verses side by side. In Genesis 2:7 the man is formed from the soil and given breath:

And the Lord God made man from the dust of the earth, breathing into him the breath of life: and man became a living soul. — Genesis 2:7 (BBE)

In Acts 2:4, on the day of Pentecost:

And they were all full of the Holy Spirit, and were talking in different languages, as the Spirit gave them power. — Acts 2:4 (BBE)

"Spirit" is pneuma (G4151), "breath". "Holy Spirit" is pneuma hagion: breath that is set apart. The breath that made the man a living being in Genesis 2:7 comes back in Acts 2, and this time it is named as set apart. The full run from Genesis to Revelation is on how the Genesis scene finishes in Acts and Revelation.

The town. The first thing set apart was a day (Genesis 2:3). The last thing set apart in the Bible is a town:

And I saw the holy town, new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, like a bride made beautiful for her husband. — Revelation 21:2 (BBE)

Jerusalem (H3389) means "founded peaceful", so the holy town is a founded place of peace. Being set apart still keeps its edge: "nothing unclean may come into it" (Revelation 21:27, BBE). But the same chapter says "the doors of it will never be shut by day" (21:25, BBE). In Genesis 3 the way to the tree of life was kept shut after the man was sent out. In Revelation 22:14 the right to the tree of life comes with going in by the doors into the town. Holy begins as a day set apart from the work, and it ends as a town set apart and open.

Where the word lists differ.

  • Exodus 3:5: many Bibles read "holy ground". The BBE reads "the place where you are is holy" and leaves out "ground". The Hebrew was checked: admat-qodesh, adamah (H127) with qodesh (H6944). Adamah is on the Strong's list at Genesis 2:7, 3:17 and 3:23, and BDB places Exodus 3:5 under a particular place or spot. The Greek hagios (G40) is on its list at Acts 7:33 for the same ground; the BBE of Acts 7:33 was not read, so it is cited but not quoted.
  • Horeb (H2722): BDB treats it as a name for the same mountain as Sinai, and the meaning "waste" is from Strong's.
  • Hagios (G40): Strong's says "from hagos, an awful thing", and Thayer gives the root as religious awe and reverence. Blue Letter Bible counts 229 uses in 219 verses of the Textus Receptus. Acts 2:4 is on the list as "Holy Spirit" (pneuma hagion). The meaning of pneuma (G4151) is taken from Strong's, as on the Acts and Revelation page.
  • Revelation: the Greek for "holy" is confirmed on the hagios list at Revelation 21:10. The BBE has the same "holy town" at 21:2 and 22:19, but those two verses were not seen on the list, so the Greek there is not claimed. Jerusalem (H3389), "founded peaceful", is from Strong's as set out on the Acts and Revelation page and was not pulled again here.
  • Genesis 2:7: the Hebrew adamah is checked. The BBE wording is from a BBE text at williamsbible.link, not from Bible Study Tools or Blue Letter Bible.

Remember: holy means set apart. A day, the soil, an identity, the breath or a town is holy when it is held apart from the rest.
