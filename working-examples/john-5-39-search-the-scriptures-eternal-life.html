"You Search the Scriptures": What John 5:39 Means

People ask what John 5:39 is saying. The speaker tells a crowd that they search the holy Writings because they think the Writings give them eternal life, and then says the Writings are about him. The BBE prints "You make search in the holy Writings". Read the verses first, then read them again with the meanings put in.

The short answer: the verse does not say stop searching (the court setting is Lesson 1). It says what the search is for. The Writings "give witness", which is the word for a witness in court. A witness is not the verdict. The crowd hold the evidence, think it gives life, and still have "no desire to come to me so that you may have life" (John 5:40). In this course that is the court rule from Lesson 7: what is held is what is made real. Searching the record is one thing, and taking the identity it points to is another.

The passage (BBE, John 5:37-40):

And the Father himself who sent me has given witness about me. Not one of you has ever given ear to his voice; his form you have not seen. And you have not kept his word in your hearts, because you have not faith in him whom he has sent. You make search in the holy Writings, in the belief that through them you get eternal life; and it is those Writings which give witness about me. And still you have no desire to come to me so that you may have life. — John 5:37-40 (BBE)

The same passage with the meanings put in:

And the Father himself who sent me has given witness about me. Not one of you has ever given ear to his voice; his form you have not seen. And you have not kept his word in your hearts, because you have not faith in him whom he has sent. You investigate the holy Documents, in the thought that through them you get perpetual life; and it is those holy Documents which give witness about me. And still you have no desire to come to me so that you may have life.

The meanings used:

  • Father (pater, G3962): "a father (literally or figuratively, near or more remote)". It is in verse 37, not in verses 39-40. See the notes below.
  • Make search (eraunao, G2045): "to seek, i.e. (figuratively) to investigate". The New Testament uses it six times: John 5:39, John 7:52, Romans 8:27, 1 Corinthians 2:10, 1 Peter 1:11 and Revelation 2:23.
  • Writings (graphe, G1124): "a document, i.e. holy Writ".
  • In the belief that (dokeo, G1380): "to think; by implication, to seem (truthfully or uncertainly)".
  • Eternal (aionios, G166): "perpetual".
  • Life (zoe, G2222): "life".
  • Give witness (martureo, G3140): "to be a witness".
  • The Jews (Ioudaios, G2453): "Judaean, i.e. Belonging to Jehudah" (Strong's), from G2448, Judah, "praised". See What do Jew, Gentile and the -ites mean?
  • Jesus (Iesous, G2424): from the Hebrew Yehoshua (H3091), "Jehovah is salvation". Jehovah is YHVH, the Existing One, so the name reads Existing-One-is-salvation.
  • Moses (Mosheh, H4872): "drawing out (of the water), i.e. rescued".
  • Bethesda (G964): "house of kindness" (Strong's), "house of mercy" (Thayer's). The BBE prints Beth-zatha.
  • Jerusalem (H3389): "founded peaceful" (Strong's).

Three notes on the meanings. First, "Father" is not in verse 39, so the passage above starts at verse 37 to put it in. The BBE prints "Father" in verses 20-23, 26, 36, 37, 43 and 45 of this chapter. The Greek was checked for verse 37 only, where it is pater (G3962). The course lessons do not give a reading for the capital-F Father, so this page keeps the word as printed and does not give it a meaning of its own. The "father" a man goes away from in Genesis 2:24 is Hebrew and the course reads it as the old state. English uses one word for both, so the two have not been shown to be the same word. Second, the "meanings put in" version uses the lexicon's words ("investigate", "thought", "perpetual"). They are glosses, and the BBE's wording stays in the quotation. Third, the link from Genesis 2:9 (the two trees) to John 5:39 is by picture and not by a shared word, because one is Hebrew and the other Greek.

How to read it, step by step

  1. Find the ground. Names and places are codes (Lesson 9). The speech comes straight after the man at the pool (John 5:1-18). Bethesda is "house of kindness" and it stands in Jerusalem, "founded peaceful". The man lay there for thirty-eight years and had nobody to put him in the water (5:5-7). Then a voice says to get up, and he is well (5:8-9). The voice came first and the result followed, as in Genesis 1. "The Jews" turned against the speaker (5:16, 5:18). The word is Ioudaios (G2453), Strong's "Judaean, i.e. Belonging to Jehudah", from Judah, "praised". The Jew, Gentile and -ites page reads it as Praised-line, and reads the true one as the inward one, "whose praise is not from men, but from God" (Romans 2:29, BBE). So the ones turning on him belong to Praise, in a chapter that later says "you take honour one from another and have no desire for the honour which comes from the only God" (5:44). So the verse is spoken in a scene where a word, not a place or a document, has just done the work.
  2. Read "make search" as investigate. The verb is eraunao, "to investigate". In Romans 8:27 and Revelation 2:23 the same verb is used of the one who searches hearts. In John 5:39 it is the crowd who investigate documents. The verse does not criticise the investigating. It says what they think it gives.
  3. Read "in the belief that" as thought. The verb is dokeo, "to think; to seem (truthfully or uncertainly)". They think the Writings give them eternal life. John 5:24 has the same phrase, "has eternal life", of the man "whose ears are open to my word". So in 5:24 it is held, and in 5:39 it is only thought.
  4. Count the witnesses. The chapter uses one verb, martureo, "to be a witness", for several: the speaker's own (5:31, which the verse says "would not be true"), another (5:32), the work (5:36), the Father (5:37) and the Writings (5:39). Verse 34 gives the reason: "I only say these things so that you may have salvation". Jesus is the name that says the Existing One is salvation (Lesson 2). The Writings are one witness among five, so they are evidence, not the verdict. This is the court of Lesson 3: a claim about yourself is made real by the court, not by your own say-so.
  5. Take voice before form. Verse 37 says "Not one of you has ever given ear to his voice; his form you have not seen". Voice comes first, then form, as in Genesis 1, where each part is spoken and then appears. The crowd look for the form in the document without having given ear to the voice.
  6. Name the missing step. Verse 40 says "you have no desire to come to me". The same verb for desire (thelo, G2309) is in the pool question "Is it your desire to get well?" (5:6) and in 5:21 and 5:35. The crowd have the Writings and do not come. After its kind, what the court makes real is what is held. A search held as only a search comes back as more search.
  7. Hear the last witness. The speaker says "the one who says things against you is Moses, on whom you put your hopes" (5:45), and "his writings are about me" (5:46). Moses means "drawing out (of the water)", and Exodus 2:10 gives the reason in the BBE: "I took him out of the water". The writings carry the name of the one drawn out, and they speak against the ones who rest on them while refusing what they point to.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. In Genesis 1 the seed is planted and gives back its own kind:

And God said, Let grass come up on the earth, and plants producing seed, and fruit-trees giving fruit, in which is their seed, after their sort: and it was so. — Genesis 1:11 (BBE)
PartGenesis 1John 5:37-40 (and the chapter around it)

The voiceGod said, and it was so (1:11)The Father sends and gives witness. His voice is never given ear to and his form is not seen (5:37). "As the voice comes to me so I give a decision" (5:30)

The split between the Father and the Son is the split already in Genesis 1: a voice speaks, and an identity is made and given rule. John 5 sets it in a courtroom. The crowd hold the witnesses, here the Writings, and stay outside the identity those witnesses point to. What changed is that the identity is on offer and not taken up ("no desire to come", 5:40). The seed they sow is a study of the record, and it comes back after its kind (1:11).

Remember: the Writings are a witness, not the verdict. You can search them and still not come. The court makes real what is held, so the step that is missing is taking the identity the witness points to.

Words checked

  • Son: the Greek word was not checked. The reading of the Son as the identity and the Father as the voice is this course's reading, built on 5:22, 5:26, 5:30 and 5:37, and not a dictionary entry.
  • Father: pater (G3962), "a father (literally or figuratively, near or more remote)". Checked in the Greek of John 5:37 only. The BBE prints "Father" in 5:20-23, 26, 36, 37, 43 and 45. Not in verses 39-40.
  • Make search: eraunao (G2045), six New Testament uses, all listed above.
  • Eternal life: the phrase "to have eternal life" (G166 with G2222) is listed at John 5:24 and 5:39.
  • Give witness: martureo (G3140) is listed at John 5:31, 32, 36, 37 and 39.
  • Desire: thelo (G2309) is listed at John 5:6, 5:21, 5:35 and 5:40. The "desire" in 5:44 was not checked and may be a different Greek word.
  • Bethesda: the BBE prints Beth-zatha. The Strong's entry is under the spelling Bethesda (G964).
  • Moses: the link from "drawing out" to the writings being "about me" is this course's reading, not a word in the verse.
  • The Jews: Ioudaios (G2453) is "Judaean, i.e. Belonging to Jehudah" in Strong's, from Judah (G2448). Thayer's lists John 5:10 and 5:15, and the Bible Hub topical entry lists 5:18. The "Praised-line" reading and the inward-Jew reading come from the linked page. In 5:44 the "desire" is still unchecked.
