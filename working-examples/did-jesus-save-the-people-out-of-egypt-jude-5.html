Did Jesus Save the People Out of Egypt? Jude 5

People ask why the New Testament says Jesus saved a people out of Egypt, when the Exodus is told around Moses. The verse is Jude 5. The BBE and KJV print "the Lord". The ESV prints "Jesus", and some early Greek manuscripts read "Jesus" too. Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The short answer: there is no clash. Jude 1:4 already puts "Master and Lord" and "Jesus Christ" on one person, so verse 5 says the same thing whether it reads "the Lord" or "Jesus". In this course the Lord is the Existing One, you aware right now (Lesson 2), and Jesus is the name that says the Existing One is salvation. Moses is not named in the verse at all. The verse is really about what happens after the exit: a people is taken out of Egypt, and some of them "had no faith".

The passage (BBE, Jude 1:4-5):

For certain men have come among you secretly, marked out before in the holy Writings for this evil fate, men without the fear of God, turning his grace into an unclean thing, and false to our only Master and Lord, Jesus Christ. Now it is my purpose to put you in mind, though you once had knowledge of all these things, of how the Lord, having taken a people safely out of Egypt, later sent destruction on those who had no faith; — Jude 1:4-5 (BBE)

The same passage with the meanings put in:

For certain men have come among you secretly, marked out before in the holy Writings for this evil fate, men without the fear of the Judges, turning his grace into an unclean thing, and false to our only Absolute Ruler and Existing One, Existing-One-is-salvation, the Anointed. Now it is my purpose to put you in mind, though you once had knowledge of all these things, of how the Existing One, having taken a people safely out of Double Straits, later sent destruction on those who gave no credit;

The meanings used:

  • Master (despotes, G1203): "an absolute ruler".
  • Lord (kurios, G2962): "he to whom a person or thing belongs, about which he has power of deciding" (Thayer's). The Greek Old Testament uses it for YHVH (H3068), the Existing One. The verse itself does not carry the Hebrew name, so that link is this course's.
  • Jesus (Iesous, G2424): from the Hebrew Yehoshua, "Jehovah is salvation". Jehovah is YHVH, the Existing One, so the name reads Existing-One-is-salvation.
  • Christ (christos, G5547): "anointed".
  • God (theos): the Judges, the plural court of Lesson 3.
  • Egypt (Mitsrayim, H4714): "double straits", the two-fold form of matsor (H4693).
  • Had no faith (pisteuo, G4100): "to have faith (in, upon, or with respect to), i.e. credit". So the course reads it as "gave no credit".
  • Moses (Mosheh, H4872): "drawing out (of the water), i.e. rescued". He is not named in Jude 5.

Two notes on the meanings. First, verse 4 is read two ways. The BBE, the ASV and Young's give one person, "our only Master and Lord, Jesus Christ". The KJV gives two, "the only Lord God, and our Lord Jesus Christ". Step 1 follows the BBE. Second, "faith" is the BBE's word for a Greek verb that the lexicon glosses as "credit", so the page uses "gave no credit" for the meaning and keeps the BBE's word in the quotation.

How to read it, step by step

  1. One subject, two names. Verse 4 ends "our only Master and Lord, Jesus Christ". Master is the one who rules, Lord is the one things belong to, and Jesus is Existing-One-is-salvation. That is one person before verse 5 starts. So when verse 5 says "the Lord" it names him by what he is, the Existing One, you aware right now. When it says "Jesus" it names him by what he does, which is salvation. The certain men in verse 4 are "false to" him, and that is what Jude is warning about.
  2. The ground is left. Egypt is "double straits", a hemmed-in place. The people are "taken safely out" of it, and the verse gives that leaving to the Lord, not to Moses. Moses means "drawing out", so the name is the act: the drawing out told as a person in Exodus and as the Existing One in Jude. This is the first step of Genesis 2:24, leave the old state.
  3. Leaving is not joining. Genesis 2:24 has two steps, leave and join. Verse 5 gives the first and then says "later sent destruction on those who had no faith". They were out of the double straits but gave no credit, so they did not hold the new claim. After its kind, the court makes real what is held, not where you are standing.
  4. The same line again in verse 6. The angels "did not keep to their kingdom but went out from the place which was theirs" and are held "in eternal chains and in dark night till the great day of the judging" (Jude 1:6, BBE). Again a place is left, and again what follows is a day-one word, dark night (Lesson 4). The court goes down before it brings you out.
  5. The Master and the way of Cain. Jude 1:4 is about being false to "our only Master", and Jude 1:11 says "They have gone in the way of Cain" (BBE). In Genesis 4:7 the warning is "sin is waiting at the door, desiring to have you, but do not let it be your master" (BBE). The question is the same in both: what rules the scene? The Greek and the Hebrew words differ, so this link rests on the picture and not on a shared word. Read Lesson 17 for that story.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. In Genesis 1 the scene is built and called good before the man is placed in it. The deep comes first:

And the earth was waste and without form; and it was dark on the face of the deep: and the Spirit of God was moving on the face of the waters. — Genesis 1:2 (BBE)
PartGenesis 1Jude 1:4-6
The groundDry land is made and named before the man stands on it (Lesson 5)Egypt, "double straits", the ground a people is taken out of (1:5)
The dark"dark on the face of the deep" (1:2)"eternal chains and ... dark night" (1:6)
The identityThe Judges make man "in our image" (1:26)"our only Master and Lord" (1:4), the one the certain men are false to
The verdictThe court saw that it was good (1:12)"sent destruction on those who had no faith" (1:5)

What changed is that the people are taken out of a scene but not all of them are placed in the new one. In Genesis 1 the identity is made and set over what is made. In Jude 5 the ones who gave no credit meet a verdict of destruction, because the court makes real what is actually held.

Remember: the Lord and Jesus are one subject named twice, once by what he is and once by what he does. A people taken out of the double straits has done only the first half of Genesis 2:24. The ones who gave no credit meet the court's verdict, because the court makes real what is held.

Words checked

  • Jude: Ioudas (G2455), "he shall be praised" (Blue Letter Bible), of Hebrew origin from Yehudah (H3063). The writer's name is in Jude 1:1, outside the passage above.
  • Translations: in verse 5 the BBE and KJV print "the Lord", and the ESV prints "Jesus". The ESV reading is supported by some early manuscripts, so this page treats both readings as naming one subject.
  • Moses: the name is not in Jude 5. The link between "drawing out" and the Lord's act is this course's reading, not a word in the verse.
  • Same question, other pairs: asking which name a verse uses, as with "the Lord" and "Jesus" here, is question 2 on Does the Bible Contradict Itself?, which applies it to three other pairs.
