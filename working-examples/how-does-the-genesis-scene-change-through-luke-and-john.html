How Does the Genesis Scene Change Through Luke and John?

The point: read Luke and John in order, and the scene Genesis sets up is put back one piece at a time. Luke starts in the waste land where Adam's line ends, and John starts with "In the beginning". Each main event then repairs one part of the Genesis scene, in the order Genesis built it. At the end the first scene is reversed: in Luke the eyes that were opened in Genesis 3 are opened again over broken bread, and in John the breath of Genesis 2:7 is given again.

In five lines:

  1. Genesis 1 builds the scene by the Judges' word, Genesis 2 puts the man in the garden with breath, and Genesis 3 loses it: a voice, a taking, eyes opened to lack, and the man sent out.
  2. Luke begins where Genesis 3 ended (3:38, then 4:1). John begins where Genesis 1 begins (1:1).
  3. In between, the scene returns in Genesis order: the word, light, sea and fish, rule, food, the garden.
  4. Luke closes on the Genesis 3 words "eyes opened" (24:31), and John closes on the Genesis 2:7 word "breathed on" (20:22).
  5. The scene is not made by the hands. Each piece arrives by the word, one step at a time.

The scene at the start

Genesis gives three scenes to measure the Gospels against. In Genesis 1 the Judges speak and it is so: light, waters, land, plants, the creatures of the sea and the land, and then the man, with food given and rule given to the image (1:26-29). In Genesis 2 the man is formed from the ground, breath is put in him, and he is placed in a garden to work it (2:7, 2:15). In Genesis 3 a voice comes, the fruit is taken, the eyes are opened and see lack, fig leaves are sewn, thorns come up, bread now comes by hard work, and the man is sent out (3:1-24). Luke and John take those scenes in turn and put them back.

John runs the other way round. It begins on the first day, works through the marriage of Genesis 2:24, the waters, light, seed and food, then goes into a garden, through thorns and "the man", and out again into a garden with the breath. Luke ends on the Genesis 3 word "eyes opened", and John ends on the Genesis 2:7 word "breathed on".

What the two show together

Both Gospels take the Genesis scene in its own order and put it back. First the word and the light. Then the sea and its creatures, with rule over them. Then food, which grows from the bread already in hand after the blessing is said. Then the garden. The last two pieces are the two Genesis lost: the eyes that saw lack are opened to know him (Luke), and the breath that made the man a living being is given again (John).

It is not done in one stroke. The scene comes back a piece at a time, and each piece comes by the word, not by the hands. That is the answer the temptation gave in the waste land, and the rest of Luke and John is what that answer looks like when it is lived out.

The words checked, in one place

  • Breathed on (emphysaō, G1720): used once in the New Testament, at John 20:22. The Septuagint uses it at Genesis 2:7 and five other times (1 Kings 17:21, Job 4:21, Ezekiel 21:31, Ezekiel 37:9 and Nahum 2:1).
  • Eyes were opened (dianoigō, G1272): "to open thoroughly". It is in Luke 24:31, and Thayer lists the Septuagint's Genesis 3:5 and 3:7 with it. Luke 24:32 and 24:45 use it for the Writings opened and the mind opened.
  • Gods (theos, G2316) in John 10:34: Strong's gives "a deity, especially the supreme Divinity; figuratively, a magistrate", and Thayer lists "of magistrates and judges". It is the word the course reads as the Judges (Lesson 3).

Where this fits in the course

A man claims an identity and the court makes it real (Lesson 6). Rule One says what is planted gives back only its own kind (Lesson 7). Luke and John show the planting from the waste land to the table and the breath, and every step grows after its kind from the one before.

A question to ask of any story: which part of the Genesis scene is being put back at this moment?

Remember: Luke ends with the eyes opened, which is Genesis 3 reversed, and John ends with the breath, which is Genesis 2:7 given again. Everything between them is the scene returning in Genesis order.
