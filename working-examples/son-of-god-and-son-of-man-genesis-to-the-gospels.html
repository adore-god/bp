What Do "Son of God" and "Son of Man" Mean? Following the Two Words from Genesis

"Son of God" and "Son of man" are usually read as titles. This page asks a plainer question: where do the two ideas first sit side by side in the Bible, and what does the story do with them? The answer it finds is a three-step story. Genesis puts a "God side" and a "man side" together in one person. Genesis 6 shows what happens when the two sides meet badly, which Lesson 15 on the Nephilim reads in full. Then Luke and the Gospels put the two sides back together in one person.

The short answer: the Bible tells one story about a person who has two sides, a side made from the ground, the man who makes claims, and a side that belongs to the court, the judging side. The same two words, Elohim and adam, carry it all the way from Genesis 1 to Luke 3. The text puts the words side by side. It never explains why. What the pairing means is the course's reading, and the page says so each time.

The passage with the meanings put in:

And God made man in his image, in the image of God he made him. (Genesis 1:27) Adam had been living for a hundred and thirty years when he had a son like himself, after his image. (Genesis 5:3) The sons of God (the sons of the Judges) saw that the daughters of men (the daughters of the man) were fair. (Genesis 6:2) The son of Seth, the son of Adam (the man), the son of God (the son of the Judges). (Luke 3:38) If you are the Son of God, give the word for these stones to become bread. (Matthew 4:3)

In the Hebrew, "God" in these verses is Elohim, which the course reads as "the Judges and Rulers", and "man" is adam, "the man", the one who makes claims. The BBE says "God" and "men" and hides that the same two words are doing the work.

One person with two sides (Genesis 1 to 3)

The first man is described two ways. He is made "in the image of God", in his image, and "in the image of God he made him" (1:27). He is also made of dust from the ground, which is why the Hebrew word for him and the word for the ground (adamah) sound alike. In 3:22 the court puts both in one sentence: "Now the man has become like one of us". The man is the ground side, and "us" is the court side. Right before that, in 3:21, the court clothes him in skin. The fig leaves and skins page follows that part.

A son after the image (Genesis 5)

Genesis 5 repeats the same shape one step down. God makes man "in the image of God" (5:1). Then Adam has a son "like himself, after his image" (5:3). So the word "son" in Genesis starts as likeness: a son is one who carries the image of the one who made him. Seen that way, Luke 3:38 calling Adam "the son of God" is not a new idea. Adam is the man who was made in God's image.

The two sides meet badly (Genesis 6)

Genesis 6 splits the two sides into two groups: "the sons of God" and "the daughters of men" (6:2). They come together, and the text says "when the sons of God had connection with the daughters of men, they gave birth to children" (6:4). Lesson 15 reads what happens there: the judging side chooses by looks, and what it breeds is a name. This page adds only one thing, about the words. In the Hebrew, the first group is the sons of the Elohim, the Judges, and the second is the daughters of ha-adam, the man. These are the same two words as Genesis 1 to 3. So Genesis 6 is not a new cast. It is the two sides of the first man, seen as two groups.

Luke closes the loop

Luke 3 does two things in a row. The voice from heaven says "You are my dearly loved Son" (3:22), and then the family line runs back, "the son of Enos, the son of Seth, the son of Adam, the son of God" (3:38). So the line ends where Genesis began: Adam, the man, called the son of God. Immediately after that, a voice tests the claim. "If you are the Son of God," the Evil One says, and asks for bread from stones (Matthew 4:3; Luke 4:3). In Genesis 3 a voice also offered an identity: "you will be as gods, having knowledge of good and evil" (3:5, BBE). The Hebrew word behind "gods" is the same Elohim, so the offer is to be as the Judges. In both, a voice talks about the God side of a person at the moment it is being tested. That is a reading. The text does not call the two voices the same.

The Son of man

In the Gospels Jesus speaks of himself as "the Son of man", and three of those verses are read for this page. He has "nowhere to put his head" while foxes and birds have homes (Matthew 8:20). He has "authority for the forgiveness of sins on earth" (Mark 2:10). He is "lord even of the Sabbath" (Mark 2:28). So the same person is the Son of God in the voice from heaven and the Son of man in his own mouth. Matthew 8:20 sets him against the animals the man named in Genesis 2:20, and Mark 2:28 names the seventh day of Genesis 2:2-3. Those are echoes by idea. No word is shared between the verses.

What this means in the course's terms

In the course, a man claims an identity and the court makes it real (Lesson 6; Lesson 1). Read that way, "Son of man" is the side that stands in the story and claims, made of the ground. "Son of God" is the side the court gives and makes real. The Bible never lets either word stand alone for long. Genesis keeps them in one man, Genesis 6 shows them meeting badly as two groups, and the Gospels put them on one person again. That is why the two phrases travel together.

What the text does not say

No verse says "Son of man" means the ground side, or that "Son of God" means the court side. No verse links the skins of 3:21 to either phrase. The Gospel verses share no word with the Genesis verses, so the links to Genesis are echoes. Whether the Gospel phrase "Son of man" comes from Daniel 7:13 or from Ezekiel is a question scholars disagree on, and this page does not decide it. Daniel 7:13 in the BBE says "one like a man" and the Aramaic is kevar enash, "like a son of man". Ezekiel 2:1 has the Hebrew ben-adam, "son of man".

A question to ask of any story: which side of the person is speaking here, the ground side or the court side?

Remember: in Genesis the man is made in the image of God and from the ground. Adam's son is "after his image". In Genesis 6 the two sides are two groups, the sons of the Judges and the daughters of the man, and the judging side chooses by looks (Lesson 15). In Luke 3:38 Adam is "the son of God", and in the Gospels the same person is both Son of God and Son of man. The text puts the words together. The meaning is the course's reading.

Words and sources

Checked: the BBE text of Genesis 1:26-27, 3:5, 3:22, 5:1-3, 6:2 and 6:4; Job 1:6; Daniel 7:13; Luke 3:22, 3:38 and 4:3; Matthew 4:1, 4:3 and 8:20; and Mark 2:10 and 2:28. The Hebrew of Genesis 4:1 and 6:4 and the Aramaic of Daniel 7:13 were read on Biblehub, the Hebrew of Ezekiel 2:1 on OpenBible, and the occurrence lists for adamah (H127) and aphar (H6083). Genesis 2:20 is from the naked page.

Not checked: the Greek for "Son of God"; the Hebrew of Genesis 6:2 and Job 1:6 (the BBE says "sons of the gods" in Job 1:6); how many times "Son of man" appears in the Gospels and in Ezekiel (a secondary source gives 81 and 93, which were not checked against a primary list); Psalm 8:4; and the BBE text of Ezekiel 2:1.

Limits: This page does not repeat Lesson 15's reading of Genesis 6, and nothing here is meant to replace it. "The Judges" for Elohim is the course's wording. The BBE hides two Hebrew verbs in Genesis 4:1 and 6:4 behind one phrase, "had connection with" (yada, "knew", and bo, "came in"), so the two verses do not share a word.
