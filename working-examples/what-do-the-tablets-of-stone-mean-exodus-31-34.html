What Do the Tablets of Stone Mean? The Commandments, the Names and the Tablet of the Heart

The stones were the work of God [the Judges], and the writing was the writing of God [the Judges], cut on the stones. — Exodus 32:16 (BBE)

Most people picture the tablets of stone as a list of rules called the Ten Commandments. The BBE helps that picture along: it says "the two stones of the law" and "the ten rules". The Hebrew says something different. The tablets are "the tablets of the testimony", the ten are "words", and the writing is the writing of Elohim. The same Bible also cuts names into stone in Exodus 28, and it puts a tablet on the heart in Jeremiah 17:1 and 2 Corinthians 3:3. This page goes through those words one at a time and sets them beside Genesis 1.

How this page reads the Bible. This course reads every story as having the same three parts: you, the identity you claim, and the judges that make it real, because Genesis 1:26 makes man the one made in the judges' own image (Lesson 6). In the Bible a name tells you what the state is like before the story starts, and a place name tells you the ground (Lesson 9). Elohim is read as the Judges, the plural word the course reads as one court acting as one (Lesson 3). Every step is then checked against the verse (BBE) and Strong's. If this is your first page, The Big Idea is the place to start.

The short answer: the text does not call the tablets a list of rules. It calls them the tablets of the testimony, and a testimony is a witness record. The writing on them is the writing of the Judges, and the ten are "words". Exodus 32 to 34 then show that record broken and written again with the same words. Separately, Exodus 28 cuts the names of the twelve sons of Israel in stone the way a seal is cut and carries them over Aaron's heart, and Jeremiah 17:1 and 2 Corinthians 3:3 put a tablet on the heart. In this reading all of these are one picture: a record is cut into a hard surface, and the question each time is who cut it and where it is carried.

The verses, one line each

  • Exodus 31:18. "he gave him the two stones of the law... two stones on which was the writing made by the finger of God [the Judges]" (BBE).
  • Exodus 32:15-16. "the stones had writing on their two sides, on the front and on the back. The stones were the work of God, and the writing was the writing of God, cut on the stones" (BBE).
  • Exodus 32:19. "in his wrath Moses let the stones go from his hands, and they were broken at the foot of the mountain" (BBE).
  • Exodus 34:1. "Make two other stones like the first two; and I will put on them the words which were on the first stones" (BBE).
  • Exodus 34:28. "he put in writing on the stones the words of the agreement, the ten rules of the law" (BBE).
  • Deuteronomy 4:13. "the ten rules which you were to keep... which he put in writing on the two stones of the law" (BBE).
  • Exodus 28:21, 28:29. The names of the twelve sons of Israel are "cut on it as on a stamp" and carried "over his heart... to keep the memory of them before the Lord [the Existing One]" (BBE).
  • Jeremiah 17:1. "The sin of Judah is recorded with a pen of iron, and with the sharp point of a jewel: it is cut on their hearts of stone, and on the horns of their altars" (BBE).
  • 2 Corinthians 3:3. "recorded not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God [the Judges]; not in stone, but in hearts of flesh" (BBE).

The meanings used

  • Tablet (luach, H3871): "a tablet (as polished), of stone, wood or metal". Used 43 times in 33 verses, from the tablets of stone to the boards of an altar and the plates of 1 Kings 7:36.
  • Testimony (edut, H5715): "testimony, witness", feminine of ed (H5707), "witness". 59 uses in 57 verses. The KJV renders it "testimony" 55 times and "witness" 4 times.
  • Stone (even, H68).
  • Write, written (kathab, H3789) and writing (miktav, H4385): the verb and noun of Exodus 31:18 and 32:16.
  • Engraved (charut, H2801): the word in Exodus 32:16, where the BBE says "cut". In Jeremiah 17:1 the verb is charash (H2790), and the craftsman of Exodus 28:11 is a charash (H2796), an engraver. Engravings (pittuach, H6603) is the word of 28:11, and seal is chotham (H2368).
  • Finger (etsba, H676): "the finger of Elohim" in 31:18.
  • Word (dabar, H1697), covenant (berit, H1285) and ten (eser, H6235): "the words of the covenant, the ten words" (34:28). The BBE says "agreement" for covenant.
  • Pen (et, H5842, a stylus), iron (barzel, H1270) and the point of a diamond (shamir, H8068): the tools of Jeremiah 17:1. The BBE says "jewel".
  • Tablets (plax, G4109) in 2 Corinthians 3:3, with of stone (lithinos, G3035), heart (kardia, G2588) and of flesh (sarkinos, G4560). Inscribed is engrapho (G1449).
  • YHVH (H3068): the Existing One, the BBE's "the Lord". Elohim (H430): the Judges, as in the other worked examples (Why is "God" plural in Genesis 1?).
  • The names: Moses (H4872) is "a drawing out" in Strong's, from mashah (H4871), to draw. Aaron is "Light-bringer", as on the Aaron page. Israel (H3478) is "God prevails", compounded of sarah (H8280), to contend, and El (H410), God; Strong's gives "he will rule as God". Judah (H3063) is "celebrated" in Strong's, from yadah (H3034), to praise or give thanks. Jeremiah (H3414) is "Jah will rise", Jah (H3050) being a short form of YHVH.

The Judges' own writing (Exodus 31:18 and 32:15-16)

Exodus 32:16 gives the tablets three marks. They are "the work of Elohim". The writing (miktav) is "the writing of Elohim". And it is "engraved" (charut) on them. Exodus 31:18 adds that the tablets are written "with the finger of Elohim", and 32:15 says they were written on both sides. With the word put in, these are the Judges' own stones and the Judges' own writing.

In Genesis 1 the same Judges speak and name: "And God [the Judges] said, Let there be light: and there was light" (1:3), and "Naming the light, Day" (1:5). In Exodus 31 and 32 the Judges write. What they said in Genesis 1 was a word that stood. What they write there is a record that stands.

Why "testimony" matters

The Hebrew name for the tablets is luchot ha'edut, the tablets of the testimony. Edut comes from ed, a witness, and Strong's gives it as "testimony, witness". A testimony is what a witness gives in a court. So the name the text uses for the tablets is the name of a court record. The BBE turns it into "law", and "law" sounds like a list of orders. The text keeps both words: the same Exodus 34:28 that says "words of the covenant" says "the ten words", and nowhere on this page is a tablet called a list of rules. The course's picture of the Bible as a courtroom (Lesson 1) fits this word. The John 5:39 page reads the Writings as a witness in the same way.

The record is broken, and the same words are put on new stones. Nothing new is written. This is the pattern of Lesson 7: what was set comes back as its own kind. The one difference the text states is who makes the stone. The first pair is called the work of Elohim, and the second pair is made by Moses, "drawn out", at the word of the Existing One (the BBE's "Lord"). The page does not say more than that.

The names cut in stone (Exodus 28)

Exodus 28 has a second set of stones with writing on them, and it is separate from the tablets of the testimony. In the Hebrew of Exodus 28:11 the two stones are the work of an engraver in stone (ma'aseh charash), with the names (shemot, H8034) of the sons of Israel cut as engravings (pittuchei) of a seal (chotham). They go on Aaron, "Light-bringer", the priest, and the BBE has them "over his heart... to keep the memory of them before the Lord [the Existing One]" (28:29), with "the power of making decisions" (28:30). The Aaron page goes through the priest's clothing, and the river, gold and stones page goes through the stone.

So the two are not the same object, and the text does not say the names are on the tablets. What they share is that both are stone, both are cut, and both are a record in front of the Existing One (YHVH, "the Lord" in the BBE). The cutting words are different (charut and pittuach), so this page rests on the picture here and not on one Hebrew word. The words of 28:11 are the ones of the seal in Song of Solomon 8:6.

The tablet of the heart (Jeremiah 17:1 and 2 Corinthians 3:3)

Jeremiah 17:1 uses the same word, luach. In the Hebrew, the sin of Judah ("celebrated", from the word for praise) is "written" (kathab) with a pen of iron and the point of a diamond, and "engraved" (charash) "upon the tablet (luach) of their heart" and on the horns of the altars. The BBE reads "recorded with a pen of iron, and with the sharp point of a jewel: it is cut on their hearts of stone". Two things stand out. The heart is called a tablet, and the cutting is hard work: an iron pen and a diamond point. And what is cut on the heart in this verse is a record against them, not a command. The name of the one carrying it, Judah, is "celebrated", and the prophet who speaks is Jeremiah, "Jah will rise", Jah being a form of YHVH, the Existing One. The text puts these names beside the record, and this page does not say more than that. Proverbs 3:3 and 7:3 use the same word for the tablet of the heart. Their wording is not quoted here.

Paul uses the same picture. "You are a letter... recorded not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God [the Judges]; not in stone, but in hearts of flesh" (2 Corinthians 3:3, BBE). The Greek says plax, tablet, twice: "tablets of stone" and "tablets of hearts of flesh". The verb is engrapho, "inscribed". So the Bible itself describes a second place for the record: first stone, then a tablet in the heart. In Jeremiah what is cut there is a sin, and in Paul it is written with the Spirit.

The same word in the Song (8:9)

The same word is in Song of Solomon 8:9, where the family would shut her in with luach of cedar. The BBE says "cedar-wood". The seal page reads the family's tablets as a writing put on her from outside, and sets against it 8:6, the seal, a name set on the heart. That is the course's reading, not something the verse says in so many words. That is taken in full on the Song of Solomon 8:6 page.

What changed is where the record sits and who writes it. In Genesis 1 the Judges say it and it is so. In Exodus 31 and 32 they write it on stone, the record is broken and written again, and names are cut on the priest. Then Jeremiah and Paul move the tablet into the heart.

Which part moved?

In the course's reading, the Judges rule from what is on the record, so the thing that moved is the record: where it is cut and by whom. The text gives three places: a stone tablet, the stones over the priest's heart, and the tablet of the heart. Each time two questions can be asked. Who cut it? Elohim for the tablets, an engraver for the names, an iron pen for the sin of Judah, the Spirit in Paul. And where is it carried? Exodus 32 to 34 add that a broken record is written again with the same words. The link between the three places is a shared picture and the word luach, and the page marks where the Hebrew words differ.

Three questions to ask of the scene:

  1. What is the ground, and what does its name mean? The foot of the mountain (32:19). This page has not looked up the mountain's name.
  2. Who is standing on it, and what do their names mean? Moses is the one who lets the stones go and makes the second pair (34:1). Strong's gives his name as "drawn out".
  3. What has changed from the Genesis 1 picture? The word that was spoken is written, and the record is cut in stone, then in the heart.

Remember: the tablets are the tablets of the testimony, the Judges' own writing, and the ten are words. The same word, a tablet, carries the names in Exodus 28 and the heart in Jeremiah and Paul. The question is always who cut it.
