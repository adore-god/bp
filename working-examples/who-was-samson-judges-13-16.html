Who Was Samson? Judges 13-16

People ask who Samson was, why his hair mattered, and why Delilah could not take his strength until he spoke. The passage is Judges 13 to 16. Read the verses first, then read them again with the meanings put in.

The short answer: Samson is "Sunlight" (H8123). He is born into a family of "Judge" (Dan), in a place called "Hornet", to a wife who had never given birth, in the years "He-will-rule-as-the-Judges" (Israel) is handed to the "Immigrants" (Philistines). His mother is told: no wine, no strong drink, and "his hair is never to be cut, for the child is to be separate to God". The hair is the one thing kept apart. When he is pressed day after day by "Languishing" (Delilah), in the valley of "Vine", he opens his whole heart and the hair is cut. The Hebrew words of his birth are the same words as Hannah's vow. Then "the growth of his hair was starting again", and his last prayer uses the same verb as Hannah's: "have me in mind".

The passage (BBE, Judges 13:1-5):

And the children of Israel again did evil in the eyes of the Lord; and the Lord gave them into the hands of the Philistines for forty years. Now there was a certain man of Zorah of the family of the Danites, and his name was Manoah; and his wife had never given birth to a child. And the angel of the Lord came to the woman, and said to her, See now! though you have never given birth to children, you will be with child and give birth to a son. Now then take care to have no wine or strong drink and to take no unclean thing for food; For you are with child and will give birth to a son; his hair is never to be cut, for the child is to be separate to God from his birth; and he will take up the work of freeing Israel from the hands of the Philistines. — Judges 13:1-5 (BBE)

The meanings used:

  • Israel (H3478): "he will rule as God" (Strong's), read here as "He-will-rule-as-the-Judges". BDB's outline gives "God prevails".
  • Philistines (H6430): an inhabitant of Pelesheth (Strong's). BDB's outline gives "immigrants".
  • Zorah (H6881): "hornet" (BDB's outline). Strong's says only "another form for H6880".
  • Dan: "Judge". Genesis 30:6 explains the name: "God has been my judge, and has given ear to my voice, and has given me a son; so he was named Dan" (BBE). The Danites are those who belong to that name.
  • Manoah (H4495): "rest" (Strong's).
  • Samson (H8123): "sunlight" (Strong's), from H8121, the sun. BDB's outline gives "like the sun".
  • Mahaneh-dan (13:25): "the camp of Dan" (YLT's reading of the same place).
  • Eshtaol (H847): "intreaty" (Strong's), "probably from H7592", sha'al, "to ask", the verb of Samuel's naming in 1 Samuel 1:20.
  • Delilah (H1807): "languishing" (Strong's). BDB's outline gives "feeble".
  • Sorek (H7796): "a vine" (Strong's). BDB's outline gives "choice vines".
  • The Lord (YHVH, H3068): the Existing One, you aware right now (Lesson 2). God (H430, Elohim): the Judges, the plural court. Where this page quotes the BBE, Elohim is put back as the Judges (Lesson 3) and the Lord is put back as the Existing One.

Two notes on the meanings. First, the BBE prints "feeble" in chapter 16 (verses 6, 7, 11, 13, 17 and 19), and Delilah's name means "languishing". The Hebrew verb behind "feeble" is a different word from the one her name comes from, so the link here is the story acting out the name, not a shared word. Second, the BBE reads Samson's first stirring as "Mahaneh-dan", and the Hebrew is the camp of Dan; the meanings-in version above says so.

How to read it, step by step

Reading from the reader's side (Lesson 1), the people and places in these chapters are states inside the reader. Nothing in them is an outside power acting on Samson.

  1. The ground and the missing seed (13:1-2). The state of "He-will-rule-as-the-Judges" does evil again and is handed to the "Immigrants" for forty years. Then the scene narrows to one man of "Hornet", of the family of "Judge", named "Rest", and "his wife had never given birth to a child". It is the same missing piece as Hannah, and it sits in the tribe whose name comes from a barren wife asking. Dan was born when Rachel said "God has been my judge, and has given ear to my voice" (Genesis 30:6, BBE). That is the rival-wives story of Jacob, Leah and Rachel, and Samson comes out of it. The name of his family is already a woman asking and being heard.
  2. The word comes to the woman, before any evidence (13:3-5). The messenger of the Existing One tells her she will give birth to a son. The word has three parts: no wine or strong drink, nothing unclean for food, and "his hair is never to be cut, for the child is to be separate to God from his birth". Later the messenger adds "nothing which comes from the vine" (13:14). All of it is about what grows and what is kept apart. This is the after its kind rule: a thing that is kept separate stays what it is. Manoah asks for instruction and "God gave ear to the voice of Manoah" (13:9, BBE), the same hearing verb, shama, as Samuel's name.
  3. He is named "Sunlight", and first stirs between Hornet and Entreaty (13:24-25). The sun is the great light of Genesis day four (1:14-19). "The blessing of the Existing One was on him", and the spirit first comes on him "between Zorah and Eshtaol", between "Hornet" and "Entreaty". Entreaty is built from the root of Hannah's "asked". He starts in the place of asking.
  4. He chooses by what is right in his eyes, and the secret goes out through a woman (chapters 14-15). He goes down and takes a wife from the Immigrants. At the feast he puts a riddle to the men. "Truly you have no love for me but only hate", his wife says, weeping (14:16, BBE), and under that pressure he tells her the answer, and she tells her people. She is later given to his companion, and the Immigrants have "her and her father's house burned" (15:6, BBE). With a jawbone he then "sent destruction on a thousand men" (15:16, BBE). The pattern is set here: what he holds inside goes out when a woman presses for it. Genesis 2:24 says a man is "joined to his wife". He keeps being joined to a state that is not his own.
  5. Delilah, in the valley of Vine (16:4-16). The name Sorek, "a vine", sits beside a man who may take nothing from the vine. The chiefs of the Immigrants say to Delilah, "see what is the secret of his great strength", and each will give "eleven hundred shekels of silver" (16:5, BBE). She asks three times. Seven new bow-cords never dried (16:7), then new thick cords (16:11), then "the seven twists of my hair worked into the cloth you are making and fixed with the pin" (16:13). Each time "the Philistines are on you, Samson" and he breaks free. Each answer comes closer to the hair. Then she says "Why do you say you are my lover when your heart is not mine?" (16:15), and "day after day she gave him no peace, for ever questioning him till his soul was troubled to death" (16:16).
  6. The hair is cut, and the Existing One has gone (16:17-20). He opens "all his heart", and what he says is the whole of Judges 13:5: no blade, separate, from birth. The word he keeps is now handed outside as an explanation. The chiefs of the Immigrants come "with the money in their hands", the twists are cut, and "he became feeble". The story acts out the name Languishing. His next words are "I will go out as at other times", the old picture of himself, and "he was not conscious that the Existing One had gone from him" (BBE, with the Lord put back as the Existing One). In this reading the Existing One, you aware right now, has already moved off the claim, and he is still acting as if it were held.
  7. What the Immigrants do, and what starts again (16:21-22). They put out his eyes, take him down, chain him "with bands of brass", and set him to crush grain in the prison-house. Then: "the growth of his hair was starting again after it had been cut off" (16:22, BBE). The hair is cut, and the kind it belongs to is still there. This is Genesis 1:11 on one head: growth after its kind, which comes back as the same.
  8. The prayer, the pillars, and the resting-place (16:28-31). He cries out to the Existing One and then to the Judges: "O Lord Existing One, do have me now in mind, and do make me strong only this once, O Judges" (16:28, BBE, with the Lord put back as the Existing One and God as the Judges). "Have me in mind" is zakar (H2142), the verb of Hannah's vow and of the answer in 1 Samuel 1:19, and "make me strong" is chazaq (H2388). He puts his arms round the two middle pillars, and "the dead whom he sent to destruction by his death were more than all those on whom he had sent destruction in his life" (16:30, BBE). He is buried "between Zorah and Eshtaol in the resting-place of Manoah his father" (16:31, BBE), the same place where the spirit first came, in the resting-place of "Rest".

The same words as Hannah

This page and the Hannah page share three things in the Hebrew. A barren wife is told, or asks for, a son whose head no razor will touch, and morah (H4177, "razor") appears only in Judges 13:5, Judges 16:17 and 1 Samuel 1:11. Wine and strong drink, yayin and shekar (H7941), are given to Samson's mother (13:4, 7, 14) and are Hannah's answer to Eli (1 Samuel 1:15). And zakar is the word that brings the son in Hannah's chapter and the word Samson prays with at the end. The differences carry the meaning. Judges 13 says the child is "separate" (nezir), and 1 Samuel 1 never uses that word. Samson's mother is told by the messenger, and Hannah files the vow herself in silence. Hannah's claim is made without a sound, and it is heard. Samson's is said out loud to the wrong ear, and it is cut.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. Here the rule of Genesis 1 is kept on one body, and the change is in who he tells.

The chapters never say Samson was wicked or good. They show what he held, what he said out loud, who he said it to, and what came back: the hair cut, the strength gone, the growth again, and the asking at the end.

What it means for the reader

The claim that sets you apart is held, not explained. Hannah files hers in silence and the court hears it. Samson gives his away as an answer to a question he was pressed on day after day, and the Existing One, you aware right now, is no longer on it. The strength was never in the hair. It was in the claim the hair stood for. What comes back is the asking: the same verb Hannah used, "have me in mind", said by the one who is back at the beginning of the growth.

Remember: Samson, "Sunlight", was born into "Judge" and "Rest", separate from the start, and strong while the claim was kept. When he said all of it out loud to "Languishing", he became feeble, and the growth and the asking started again.

Words checked

  • Razor: morah (H4177) occurs three times, in Judges 13:5, Judges 16:17 and 1 Samuel 1:11. The BBE prints "his hair is never to be cut" at Judges 13:5 and "My head has never been touched by a blade" at Judges 16:17.
  • Wine and strong drink: shekar (H7941) is in Numbers 6:3, Judges 13:4, 13:7, 13:14 and 1 Samuel 1:15, each time beside yayin, "wine".
  • Separate: nezir is in Judges 13:5 and 16:17 ("Nazirite to God" in the ESV). It is not in 1 Samuel 1.
  • Remember: zakar (H2142) is in Judges 16:28, followed by chazaq (H2388), "strengthen". It is also in 1 Samuel 1:11 and 1:19 and in Genesis 30:22.
  • Hear: shama (H8085) is in Judges 13:9 ("God listened to the voice of Manoah"). Samuel (H8050) is built from it in 1 Samuel 1:20.
  • Dan: the name is explained in Genesis 30:6 (BBE).
  • Names: Strong's and BDB entries for H3478, H6430, H6881, H4495, H8123, H847, H1807 and H7796.
