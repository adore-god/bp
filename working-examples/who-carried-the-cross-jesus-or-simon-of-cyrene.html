Who Carried the Cross, Jesus or Simon of Cyrene?

The walk to Golgotha is told two ways. John says Jesus "went out with his cross on him" (19:17). Matthew, Mark and Luke say the men of the army made a man named Simon, from Cyrene and coming in from the country, take the cross "after Jesus" (Luke 23:26). The text does not say how the two fit. What it does give is a hearer, a field, a tree, a place called Dead Man's Head, a garden beside it, and one in the middle. In this course the scene is read as taking place inside the reader. Jesus is the identity going out, Simon is the hearing in you, and the cross is the tree the scene ends on. The scene is built from the same pieces as Genesis 3, with Genesis 1 behind it: a tree, thorns, a voice that is heard, a field, being sent out, a garden and a middle. In Genesis 3 the way to the tree of life is shut, and here a tree is carried out with a garden beside it, so in this reading the walk is the reader trying to get back to the tree of life. Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The meanings used:

  • Jesus (G2424): the Greek form of the Hebrew Yehoshua (H3091), which Strong's gives as from H3068 (YHVH, the Existing One) and H3467 (yasha, "to save"). Blue Letter Bible glosses it "Jehovah is salvation". In this course: Existing One is salvation.
  • Simon (G4613): from the Hebrew Shimon (H8095), "hearing", which is from shama (H8085), "to hear intelligently (often with implication of attention, obedience, etc.)".
  • Cyrene (G2956, "an inhabitant of Cyrene", from the place name G2957): Strong's says the place name is "of uncertain derivation". The name gloss on Blue Letter Bible, from Thayer's, is "supremacy of the bridle". That gloss is Thayer's, not Strong's own. The story follows it: the hearing from that name is the one taken hold of and directed.
  • Country (agros, G68): "a field (as a drive for cattle); genitive case, the country". The BBE gives "country" and many Bibles give "field". The KJV translates this word "field" 22 times and "country" 8 times.
  • Bear (bastazo, G941): "to lift, literally or figuratively (endure, declare, sustain, receive, etc.)". Blue Letter Bible lists "to carry on one's person" among its senses. It is the word in John 19:17 and in Luke 14:27.
  • Press into service (angareuo, G29): "to press into public service", used in Matthew 27:32 and Mark 15:21. Luke 23:26 uses a different word, "laid hold of" (G1949).
  • Golgotha (G1115): "the skull", of Chaldee origin; compare gulgoleth (H1538), "a skull (as round); by implication a head". The BBE gives "Dead Man's Head".
  • Alexander (G223): "man defender". Rufus (G4504): "of Latin origin; red". It is a Latin name, so no Hebrew link is drawn.
  • Matthew (G3156): Strong's says "Matthitjah"; Blue Letter Bible gives "gift of Jehovah", so here "gift of the Existing One". Thayer's entry also discusses a different possible derivation, so the gloss is the Blue Letter Bible one.
  • Mark (G3138): Strong's says "of Latin origin; Marcus". The name gloss (Thayer's, on Blue Letter Bible) is "a defense".
  • Luke (G3065): "contracted from Latin Lucanus" in Strong's. The name gloss (Thayer's) is "light-giving".
  • Begin to grow light (epiphosko, G2020): "to begin to grow light", from G2017. It is used only twice, in Luke 23:54 and Matthew 28:1. The BBE gives "coming on" in Luke 23:54, so the light is not visible in the English.
  • John (G2491): "of Hebrew origin (H3110)", Jochanan. Blue Letter Bible glosses it "Jehovah is a gracious giver", here "the Existing One is a gracious giver". Thayer's: "to whom Jehovah is gracious".
  • Pilate (G4091): "of Latin origin; close-pressed, i.e. firm". Thayer's gives "armed with a spear". He is the one who sits in the judge's seat (John 19:13) and gives Jesus up (John 19:16).
  • Jerusalem (H3389): "founded peaceful" in Strong's. Blue Letter Bible glosses it "teaching of peace".

Who is in the scene

The text names the one who goes out, the ones who give him up, a hearer from the field, women who weep, a tree, a named place, a garden and two others. In this course's reading, where the many voices are gathered under one present awareness (see the court language), all of them are in you.

This is how the Bible is set up, not an extra layer on top of it. Genesis 1:26 makes man the one made in the judges' own image (Lesson 6), so every scene has the same three parts: you, the identity you claim, and the judges that make it real. Here the identity goes out, the hearing takes the tree after it, and the place is named before anyone is put on it.

How to read it, step by step:

  1. Given up, then out. Pilate, "firm" (G4091), sits in "the judge's seat" (John 19:13) and "gave him up to them to be put to death on the cross" (19:16). Then comes the leaving: "he went out" (John 19:17), and in Matthew "while they were coming out" (27:32). Genesis 3 ends the same way: "the Lord God sent him out of the garden of Eden to be a worker on the earth from which he was taken" (3:23, BBE). The question from Lesson 8 applies: which state is being left? Here the identity leaves the judge's seat and goes out toward a named place. Just before, he comes out "with the crown of thorns" (John 19:5). Thorns are the first plant named when the ground is cursed "on your account": "Thorns and waste plants will come up" (Genesis 3:17-18, BBE).
  2. The identity bears it. John says "he went out with his cross on him" (19:17). The word for bearing is bastazo (G941), "to carry on one's person". Luke 14:27 uses the same word for the one who bears his cross and comes after. In this reading John tells the walk from the side of the identity going out. The tree is on its own person.
  3. A hearer comes in from the field. The other three tell the walk from the other side. Simon is "hearing" (G4613, from H8095, from H8085), of Cyrene, "supremacy of the bridle" (G2957, Thayer's gloss). Shama is first used in Genesis 3: "they heard" the sound (3:8), "Hearing your voice in the garden I was full of fear" (3:10, BBE), and "you gave ear to the voice of your wife" (3:17, BBE). In Genesis 3 the hearing is the part that hides and is held to account. Here it comes "from the country", the field (G68), which is the ground the man was sent out to work (3:23). Hearing is also how Simeon got his name: "Because it has come to the Lord's ears that I am not loved" (Genesis 29:33, BBE). Simon is not asked. Matthew and Mark use a word for being pressed into public service (G29), and Luke has "they put their hands on" him. The hearing in you is taken hold of and has the tree laid on it. The names turn out the same way: the hearing comes from "supremacy of the bridle" and is the one led and directed, while the ruling that gave the order is named "firm" (Pilate) and the order stands (John 19:16).
  4. "After Jesus." Luke ends the verse "made him take the cross after Jesus" (23:26). Earlier in Luke the pattern is set out:
    And he said to them all, If any man has a desire to come after me, let him give up all, and take up his cross every day, and come after me. — Luke 9:23 (BBE)
    Simon is shown doing that pattern in order: he takes up the cross and comes after. John 19:17 and Luke 14:27 use the one word, bastazo, so the one who bears the tree and the one who comes after it carry the same thing. Mark alone adds that Simon is "the father of Alexander and Rufus" (15:21). In this course a son is an identity, like seed (Rule One). Alexander is "man defender" (G223) and Rufus is "red" (G4504).
  5. The women and the tree. The women are told to turn their weeping from the one going out to "yourselves and ... your children" (Luke 23:28). Jerusalem is "founded peaceful" (H3389), so the weeping is turned to the settled state they stand on and the identities it holds. Then comes the tree: "when the tree is green, what will they do when it is dry?" (23:31). In Genesis 1:12 every tree "producing fruit, in which is its seed, of its sort" is called good before the man appears (Lesson 5). The text does not explain the dry tree, so this reading leaves that question open. It notes only that the tree is in the scene twice, carried and then green or dry.
  6. Dead Man's Head, and a garden beside it. The ground is named before the identity is put on it, as Eden was (Lesson 5). Matthew 27:33 gives "the place named Golgotha, that is to say, Dead Man's Head", and Golgotha is "skull". Genesis 3:19 gives the road that ends there: "for dust you are and to the dust you will go back" (BBE). Then John says "there was a garden near the cross, and in the garden a new place for the dead in which no man had ever been put" (19:41). The first scene had a garden and "a flaming sword turning every way to keep the way to the tree of life" (Genesis 3:24, BBE). Here the garden is by the skull.
  7. In the middle. "with two others, one on this side and one on that, and Jesus in the middle" (John 19:18). Luke calls the two "evil-doers" (23:32). Genesis 3:3 has "the tree in the middle of the garden" (BBE). The BBE says "in the middle" in both places. The Greek and Hebrew words behind them have not been checked here, so this is a matching picture, not a word link.

The Genesis words in the walk: what changed from Genesis 1 to 3

This is the part to watch. Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed, and here nearly every object in the walk is already in Genesis 1-3. The walk to Golgotha uses Genesis 3's words and turns each one.

In Genesis 3 the tree is taken, the hearing hides, and the man is sent out to the field. In the walk to Golgotha the same parts are all there, but the order is turned: the identity goes out first, the hearing is brought in from the field, and the tree is carried to a named place.

Four tellings, one walk

There are four gospels, so the walk to Golgotha is told four times. In this course's reading that is the reader going through the same scene four times, and each telling goes the way its own name says, just as the story in a scene goes the way the names in it say. The walk is the same each time. What changes is how it is told.

GospelThe nameHow the walk is told
Matthew"gift of the Existing One" (G3156)Simon is made to go and take the cross (27:32). The whole telling is full of giving. In the BBE, Jesus is "given up" (27:2, 27:26), is given wine mixed with bitter drink (27:34), gives up his spirit (27:50), and the body is given (27:58)
Mark"a defense" (G3138)Simon is pressed in, and Mark alone names his sons, Alexander "man defender" and Rufus "red" (15:21)
Luke"light-giving" (G3065)Simon is taken hold of and the cross is put on him to take after Jesus (23:26). Luke alone has the women and the green and dry tree (23:28-31). Luke alone says "The light of the sun went out" (23:45), and his Sabbath is "coming on" (23:54), a Greek word that means to begin to grow light
John"the Existing One is a gracious giver" (G2491)No Simon, and nobody is pressed in. "He went out with his cross on him" (19:17)

John's name says gracious, and John is written in the gracious way: the identity carries the tree himself and nothing is forced on anyone. Matthew's name says gift, and Matthew is the telling that keeps giving. Mark's name says a defense, and Mark is the one that names the defender. The BBE's "gave" in Matthew is English wording; the Greek words behind each one have not been checked here. Luke's name says light-giving, and Luke is the telling where the light is shown. At the sixth hour the light of the sun goes out, and only Luke says so (23:45). Then the Sabbath begins to grow light (23:54). In Genesis 1:15 and 1:17 the lights are put in the sky "to give light on the earth", and in 1:16 the greater light is the ruler of the day. In Luke the ruler of the day gives no light at the cross, and light begins again after it. The Hebrew words behind Genesis 1:15 and 1:17 have not been checked here, so this is a matching picture, not a word link. The same Greek word for beginning to grow light is used once more, in Matthew 28:1. All four stay, and they are read side by side, not chosen between.

A short note on the words

John and the other three. The text does not say how "he went out with his cross on him" and "made him take the cross after Jesus" fit together. This page keeps both and reads each from the side it is told from, the way the contradiction page reads a clashing pair. It does not choose between them.

Three ways of saying "made". The BBE gives "made him go with them" (Matthew), "made one ... go with them" (Mark) and "put their hands on" (Luke). Matthew and Mark share the word angareuo (G29). Luke has a different word (G1949). The English phrase "put their hands on" here is not the Hebrew word behind "put your hands on it" in Genesis 3:3, so no link is drawn.

What is not settled here. The dry tree in Luke 23:31, and any word link behind "in the middle" are left open.

Where this sits in the storyline

This is the Genesis 3 road run forward. A voice is heard, a tree is taken, and the man is sent out to the field. In the walk to Golgotha the identity is given up and goes out with the tree on it, the hearing is pressed in from the field and takes it up after him, and both come to a named place with a garden beside it. The court's ruling is already given in the verse before the walk (John 19:16). What the walk shows is who carries what the ruling lays on the identity. In this reading that is the reader making the way back toward the tree of life that Genesis 3:24 shut off, and carrying the tree to do it, which is why it is a trial and not a single step.

Remember: John tells the walk from the side of the identity bearing the tree, and the other three tell it from the side of the hearing that is made to take it up after him. Simon means hearing, he comes from the field, and Genesis 3 is where hearing is first used. The tree is carried to Dead Man's Head, the skull, and there is a garden beside it. Four gospels, four tellings, each going the way its name says, and John's, the gracious one, is where nobody is pressed in.
