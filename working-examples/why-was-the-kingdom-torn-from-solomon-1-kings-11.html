Why Was the Kingdom Torn From Solomon? 1 Kings 11

In 1 Kings 11 the court tells Solomon, "I will take the kingdom away from you by force" (11:11, BBE). Later a prophet takes a new robe and, in the BBE's words, parts "it violently into twelve" (11:30). The BBE makes these sound like two different acts. In the Hebrew they are one. The verb in 11:11, 11:12, 11:13, 11:30 and 11:31 is qara (H7167), "to tear", the ordinary word for tearing clothes. So the robe is not a picture added on top of the story. The word that tears the cloth is the word that tears the kingdom, and the prophet hands over "ten of the parts" in the same breath that he names ten tribes (11:31). In this course the scene is read as taking place inside the reader. Solomon, "Peaceful", is the identity you hold, and the kingdom is the set of voices that identity rules. This page reads the chapter first as written, then again with the meanings put in, and asks three questions: why the robe is new, why it is twelve pieces, and what is torn out of Solomon's hand.

The passage with the meanings put in:

The nations of which the Existing One (Lord) had said to the children of He-shall-prevail (Israel), You are not to take wives from them... to these Peaceful (Solomon) clung (was united) in love. (1 Kings 11:2) For it came about that when Peaceful (Solomon) was old, his heart was turned away to other judges (other gods) by his wives; and his heart was no longer whole (true) to the Existing One (Lord) his Judges (God) as the heart of his father Beloved (David) had been. (11:4) So the Existing One (Lord) said to Peaceful (Solomon), Because you have done this, and have not kept my agreement and my laws, which I gave you, I will surely tear (take away by force) the kingdom from you and will give it to your servant. (11:11) I will not do it in your life-time, because of your father Beloved (David), but I will tear (take) it out of the hand of (from) your son. (11:12) And Brother-of-the-Existing-One (Ahijah) took his new robe in his hands, tearing (parting) it (violently) into twelve pieces. (11:30) And he said to People-will-contend (Jeroboam), Take ten of the pieces (parts), for this is what the Existing One (Lord) has said: See, I will tear (take away by force) the kingdom out of the hand of (from) Peaceful (Solomon), and will give ten branches (tribes) to you. (11:31)

The meanings used:

  • Tear (qara, H7167): Strong's gives "to rend, literally or figuratively". It is used 63 times. The first use is Genesis 37:29, when Reuben finds the pit empty, and the second is Jacob in 37:34. Blue Letter Bible's BDB entry counts 39 of the 63 as clothes torn as a sign of grief or distress, and it files 1 Kings 11:30 under "symbolic act". In 1 Kings 11 it is 11:11 (twice, "surely tear"), 11:12, 11:13, 11:30 and 11:31. The BBE says "take ... by force", "take" and "parting it violently".
  • Robe (salmah, H8008): "a wrapper, mantle", and Strong's says it is a transposition of simlah (H8071), "a dress". Simlah is the word for the clothes Jacob tears in Genesis 37:34. In 11:29 the Hebrew says Ahijah "had covered himself" (kasah, H3680) with it, and the BBE says "put on".
  • New (chadash, H2319): "new". Piece (qera, H7168): "a rag", a torn piece of cloth, from qara. It is used four times in all, and 11:30 is one.
  • Branch / tribe (shebet, H7626): Strong's gives "from an unused root probably meaning to branch off; a scion, i.e. (literally) a stick ... or (figuratively) a clan". It is used 190 times, and the KJV has "tribe" 140 times, "rod" 34, "sceptre" 10 and "staff" twice. 11:13, 11:31, 11:32, 11:35 and 11:36 are on the list.
  • Hand (yad, H3027): the BBE keeps "hand" in 11:26-27 ("his hand was lifted up against the king"), but drops it in 11:12, 11:31 and 11:35, where the Hebrew and the KJV read "out of the hand of".
  • Solomon (H8010): "peaceful", from shalom (H7965). Used 293 times.
  • Whole (shalem, H8003): "complete (literally or figuratively); especially friendly", and Strong's lists "full, just, peaceable, perfect, quiet, whole". Used 28 times. The BBE says "true" in 11:4.
  • Clung (dabaq, H1692): "properly, to impinge, i.e. cling or adhere". Used 54 times. Genesis 2:24 and 1 Kings 11:2 are both on the list.
  • Ahijah (H281): "brother of Jah" (Strong's, "Yah" being a short form of the Existing One). A prophet (nabi, H5030) is "a spokesman". Shilonite (H7888) is "from Shiloh" (H7887), which Strong's builds from the same word as H7886, "tranquil". Blue Letter Bible's outline gloss for Shiloh is "place of rest".
  • Jeroboam (H3379): Strong's gives "from riyb and am; people will contend". Rehoboam (H7346): "a people has enlarged", from H7337 and H5971. Ephraim (H669): "double fruit". Joseph (H3130): "let him add". Judah (H3063): "celebrated", from yadah (H3034), "praise".
  • Adversary (satan, H7854): the word in 11:14, where the BBE says "make trouble".

The scene before anything is torn

The chapter opens with Solomon and his wives. 11:1 names women "of the Moabites, Ammonites, Edomites, Zidonians, and Hittites", and 11:3 counts them:

He had seven hundred wives, daughters of kings, and three hundred other wives; and through his wives his heart was turned away. — 1 Kings 11:3 (BBE)

The five names in 11:1 are "-ite" words. The '-ites' page reads each -ite as belonging to the name in front of it, so a Moabite belongs to "from father", an Ammonite to "son of my people", an Edomite to "red" and a Hittite to "terror". It is about what the words do in the plot, not any people alive today. The same page quotes Judges 3:5-6, where Israel takes wives from the nations and becomes "servants to their gods", which is the 11:2 warning in other words. Read from your side, Solomon is clinging to voices that belong to other states.

Two Hebrew words matter here. The BBE says Solomon "was united in love" to these women (11:2). The word is dabaq, "to cling", and the first place on its list is Genesis 2:24, where a man "will go away from his father and his mother and be joined to his wife" (BBE). So the verb that joins one man to one wife in Genesis 2:24 now joins one man to a thousand. Lesson 8 gives the rule: you leave an old state and are joined to one identity, and the judges keep the union in place. In 11:2 the joining has no single identity in it. It is spread across many.

The court's measure is not the number, though. It is the heart:

For it came about that when Solomon was old, his heart was turned away to other gods by his wives; and his heart was no longer true to the Lord his God as the heart of his father David had been. — 1 Kings 11:4 (BBE)

"True" is shalem, "whole", and its three consonants (sh-l-m) are the ones in Solomon's name, which Strong's gives as "peaceful". The text does not say it is a play on the name, and this page does not say so. What the text shows is that the man named Peaceful is told his heart is not whole. The same phrase, a heart that is shalem with the Existing One, is in 1 Kings 8:61 and 11:4. "Other gods" is elohim acherim, "other judges". In this course's terms, a mind that gives the seat of ruling to other rulers has no one claim left to be whole about.

The court rules first

The text gives its reasons in order. The heart was turned "from the Lord, the God of Israel, who had twice come to him in a vision" (11:9), and the orders were not kept (11:10). Then comes the ruling:

So the Lord said to Solomon, Because you have done this, and have not kept my agreement and my laws, which I gave you, I will take the kingdom away from you by force and will give it to your servant. — 1 Kings 11:11 (BBE)

The Hebrew puts the verb twice, "tearing, I will tear" (qaro'a eqra), and the KJV renders it "I will surely rend". The BBE's "by force" has no matching word in the interlinear. The Hebrew is the verb alone. Nothing is torn yet. The ruling comes first, the robe comes nineteen verses later, and 1 Kings 12:15 says the result "came about by the purpose of the Lord, so that what he had said by Ahijah the Shilonite to Jeroboam, son of Nebat, might be effected" (BBE). That is the course's order: a claim, a ruling, and then the judges make it real.

The ruling is also cut short by the name David, "beloved":

I will not do it in your life-time, because of your father David, but I will take it from your son. — 1 Kings 11:12 (BBE)

"Still I will not take all the kingdom from him; but I will give one tribe to your son" (11:13, BBE). The heart that was whole in David is the reason one piece is left.

The BBE does the same thing in other places, so the pattern is not new. In Genesis 37:29 and 37:34 the BBE says Reuben and Jacob were "giving signs of grief", where the Hebrew says they tore their clothes. In 1 Samuel 15 the BBE has "the cloth came away" when Saul takes hold of Samuel's robe (15:27), and then "The Lord has taken away the kingdom of Israel from you this day by force" (15:28). The Hebrew has the robe tear and then the same verb for the kingdom. The word list for H7167 also puts it beside a kingdom or Israel in 1 Samuel 28:17, 1 Kings 14:8 and 2 Kings 17:21. BDB groups these under "tear away sovereignty (under figure of garment)".

So a torn garment is the Bible's own picture for a rule that is taken away. The sign in 11:30 is not a loose comparison. It is the verb the text was already using.

Why a new robe?

Now at that time, when Jeroboam was going out of Jerusalem, the prophet Ahijah the Shilonite came across him on the road; now Ahijah had put on a new robe; and the two of them were by themselves in the open country. — 1 Kings 11:29 (BBE)

The text does not say why the robe is new, so this page only lists what the verse gives. The robe is Ahijah's, not Solomon's ("that was on him", 11:30), and it is chadash, "new". Nothing in it is worn out. It is torn while it is whole. That matches the ruling, which is not about a kingdom that failed. The kingdom is in one piece, and what is lost is the heart that was whole (11:4). The sign is given away from the city, with no one else there, to the one man who will be handed the pieces. The one who speaks the ruling is "Brother of the Existing One" from a place that means "tranquil", while the one it falls on is named Peaceful.

Why twelve pieces?

Hebrew writes twelve as "two ten" (shnayim asar). Ahijah tears the robe into twelve and gives Jeroboam ten:

And he said to Jeroboam, Take ten of the parts, for this is what the Lord has said: See, I will take the kingdom away from Solomon by force, and will give ten tribes to you; — 1 Kings 11:31 (BBE)

"(But one tribe will be his, because of my servant David, and because of Jerusalem...)" (11:32, BBE). So the pieces of cloth become tribes in the next sentence, and the word for tribe, shebet, means a branch, a rod or a sceptre. The cloth pieces are named branches. Genesis 49:10 is on the same word's list, and the one tribe left in 12:20 is Judah, "praise". That is a matching picture, not something the text says.

The count does not close. Twelve pieces, ten given and one left makes eleven, and the text does not say what the twelfth piece is. 12:20-21 shows "only the tribe of Judah" at first, then "all the men of Judah and the tribe of Benjamin" with Rehoboam (BBE). This page does not force the numbers to agree. In the course's reading, twelve is the full set of voices in you, and the sign shows the full set pulled apart, ten pulled one way and the rest left on the other side.

Who is handed the pieces?

And Jeroboam was an able and responsible man; and Solomon saw that he was a good worker and made him overseer of all the work given to the sons of Joseph. — 1 Kings 11:28 (BBE)

The Hebrew says "the house of Joseph" and the work is a burden (sebel, H5447). Joseph means "let him add". Jeroboam is also called "an Ephraimite", and Ephraim is "double fruit", the name Joseph gave his second son in Genesis 41:52. Jeroboam's own name is "people will contend" in Strong's. And "his hand was lifted up against the king" (11:26, BBE), the same hand the kingdom is torn out of.

Two things are worth setting side by side. The tearing verb is first used over Joseph (Genesis 37:29, 37:34), and here the pieces go to the man over the house of Joseph. When the danger comes, Jeroboam goes "in flight to Egypt" and stays until Solomon dies (11:40), then comes back to his town "in the hill-country of Ephraim" (12:2). The pit to palace page follows that shape for Joseph. It is a matching picture here, not a word link. The one left with the single piece is Rehoboam, "a people has enlarged" (11:43).

The adversaries come before the robe

Between the ruling (11:11-13) and the sign (11:29-31), two more names are put in:

So the Lord sent Hadad the Edomite to make trouble for Solomon: he was of the king's seed in Edom. — 1 Kings 11:14 (BBE)

The BBE says "make trouble". The Hebrew of 11:14 is satan, "adversary". The BBE says "another trouble-maker, Rezon" in 11:23, and I did not pull the Hebrew of that verse. Both men have gone in flight, Hadad to Egypt (11:17). The text shows the edges of the rule being pulled at before the robe is torn. The Edom lesson covers how this course reads that name.

A short note on the words

What is checked. The BBE wording of 1 Kings 11, 1 Kings 12, 1 Samuel 15:26-28 and Genesis 37:29-34 was read from the verse text. The word lists for H7167 (63), H1692 (54), H8003 (28) and H7626 (190) were read as occurrence lists. I read the Hebrew word-by-word for 11:1-20, 11:29 and 11:30. For 11:31, 11:35 and the rest I used the word list and the KJV and NASB renderings.

"By force" and "violently". The BBE has these words in 11:11, 11:30 and 11:31. In the interlinear I read, I did not find a separate Hebrew word for either in 11:11 or 11:30. The Hebrew is the verb "tear" alone.

Where the lexicons differ. Strong's gives Jeroboam as "people will contend", while the NAS list in BibleHub gives "the people increase", so this page rests nothing on the name. Zeruah (H6871), Jeroboam's mother, is "leprous" in Strong's and "full breasted" in Blue Letter Bible's outline, and I did not use her name. I also did not pull the name meanings of Hadad or Rezon.

What is not claimed. The text does not say why the robe is new, why it is twelve, or what the twelfth piece is. 11:36 says "a light for ever burning" in the BBE, where other translations say "lamp", and I did not pull that Hebrew word, so I make no link to the lights of Genesis 1. The Joseph picture, the Judah picture and the shebet and Genesis 49:10 picture are matching pictures only.

Where this sits in the storyline

Genesis 1 sets a man over a named ground to rule. Genesis 2:24 joins him to one identity. In 1 Kings 11 the same verb joins a man to many, the heart is no longer whole, and the court rules before anything happens. Then a prophet tears a new robe with the verb the text uses for the kingdom, and the pieces go to the one over the house of Joseph. The chapter after it shows the pieces giving back after their kind. The one who got ten makes "two oxen of gold" and says, "see! these are your gods, O Israel" (12:28, BBE), which is the heart of 11:4 again, turned toward other judges. The first tearing in the Bible was at Joseph's empty pit, and this one hands its pieces to his house.

Two other garments sit on either side of this one. The first clothes in the Bible are "coats of leaves stitched together" (Genesis 3:7, BBE), made by people who had just been shown they were bare, and then "coats of skins for their clothing" (3:21, BBE). The naked page follows that covering and hiding. At the cross the picture is turned round. The coat is "without a join, made out of one bit of cloth", and the soldiers say "Let this not be cut up" (John 19:23-24, BBE). The temple curtain page notes that John keeps the Greek verb for "parted" for things that hold. So Solomon's robe is torn into twelve and handed out, while the coat at the cross is kept whole. These are matching pictures only. I have not checked whether the Hebrew of Genesis 3 uses the same word as the "covered" of 11:29, and the Greek of John 19 is not the Hebrew qara.

A question to ask of any story: what is the heart joined to, and what gets torn out of whose hand?

Remember: in 1 Kings 11 the court rules first, then a prophet tears a new robe into twelve, and the Hebrew word for the robe and for the kingdom is one verb, qara, "tear". The BBE hides it as "by force" and "parting". A heart joined to many (dabaq, as in Genesis 2:24) is not whole (shalem), and the kingdom it rules comes back in pieces after its kind.
