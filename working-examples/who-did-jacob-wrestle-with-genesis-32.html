Who Did Jacob Wrestle With? Genesis 32 and 33

Short answer: the text names the one who holds Jacob three ways. He is "a man" (Genesis 32:24). Jacob says he has seen Elohim "face to face" (32:30). And Hosea 12:4 calls him an angel. In this reading it is the judging side of you meeting the claim you have been living as. The claim is Jacob, "heel-catcher". The court asks him to say his name out loud, takes it off him at a river named Emptying, and gives him Israel, "Judges Prevail", in the dark, before he has met his brother. On the far side the brother he feared runs to him and kisses him.

People search this as "who did Jacob wrestle with", "Jacob wrestling with an angel meaning", "why did Jacob limp" and "what does Israel mean". This page reads Genesis 32:1 to 33:20 in order, then looks at where the texts differ. The brothers have their own pages: Edom, Esau and Seir and Leah and Rachel.

New here? The course in four lines.

  • Every story has three parts: someone present (the Existing One, which is you, aware right now), the identity they claim (I AM), and the judges who make it real (Elohim). Lesson 1 and Lesson 2.
  • Rule One, after its kind: what is planted gives back only what it is. Lesson 7.
  • Rule Two, leave and join: step away from the old state and join the new one (Genesis 2:24). Lesson 8.
  • Names and places are codes: the meaning tells you what the state is like before the story starts. Lesson 9.

Where this sits in the story

Jacob is walking back toward the land where his brother is. Years earlier Esau had read his name out loud as a verdict:

And he said, Is it because he is named Jacob that he has twice taken my place? for he took away my birthright, and now he has taken away my blessing. — Genesis 27:36 (BBE)
So Esau was full of hate for Jacob because of his father's blessing; and he said in his heart, The days of weeping for my father are near; then I will put my brother Jacob to death. — Genesis 27:41 (BBE)

So the name was already doing its work before this night. Genesis 32 is the night the court takes it up. Genesis 33 is the morning after.

The story, step by step

1. Two camps (Genesis 32:1-10)

And when he saw them he said, This is the army of God: so he gave that place the name of Mahanaim. — Genesis 32:2 (BBE)

With the meanings put in:

And when he saw them he said, This is the army of the Judges: so he gave that place the name of Two-camps.

Mahanaim is the "double camp" (Strong's, the dual of H4264). The very next thing the text does is split the people in two. The servants come back with news that Esau is "coming out to you, and four hundred men with him" (32:6), and:

Then Jacob was in great fear and trouble of mind: and he put all the people and the flocks and the herds and the camels into two groups; And said, If Esau, meeting one group, makes an attack on them, the others will get away safely. — Genesis 32:7-8 (BBE)

Two groups so that one can get away is a claim in two pieces. In his prayer Jacob says it himself: "now I have become two armies" (32:10, BBE). The place name is the picture. This is a matching picture and not a word link: only Mahanaim (H4266) was pulled, not the Hebrew behind "two groups" or "two armies".

2. The offering sent ahead to cover the face (Genesis 32:13-21)

Jacob sends his brother gifts, herd by herd, and tells the servants what to say:

And you are to say further, Jacob, your servant, is coming after us. For he said to himself, I will take away his wrath by the offering which I have sent on, and then I will come before him: it may be that I will have grace in his eyes. — Genesis 32:20 (BBE)

The BBE turns the Hebrew into "wrath" and "come before him". The Hebrew says face. The word for face (panim, H6440) appears four times in this one verse: "I will cover his face with the offering that goes before my face, and afterward I will see his face, perhaps he will lift up my face." (the Hebrew words are panav, lephanai, panav, panai; "cover" is kaphar, H3722; the offering is minchah, H4503). The plan is to settle the outcome with proof sent first. Then:

So the servants with the offerings went on in front, and he himself took his rest that night in the tents with his people. — Genesis 32:21 (BBE)

The offering goes first and Jacob stays behind it. The offering and the face are also the pair in Cain's story; the Hebrew words in Genesis 4 were not pulled here, so that is a matching picture only.

3. Emptied, and by himself (Genesis 32:22-24)

And in the night he got up, and taking with him his two wives and the two servant-women and his eleven children, he went over the river Jabbok. He took them and sent them over the stream with all he had. Then Jacob was by himself; and a man was fighting with him till dawn. — Genesis 32:22-24 (BBE)

With the meanings put in (Jacob is "heel-catcher", H3290; Jabbok is "emptying", H2999):

And in the night he got up, and taking with him his two wives and the two servant-women and his eleven children, he went over the river Emptying. He took them and sent them over the stream with all he had. Then Heel-catcher was by himself; and a man was fighting with him till dawn.

This is the first half of Rule Two, done completely. Wives, children, herds and gifts all go over the river, and nothing is left on his side but the claim itself.

"By himself" is the word bad (H905), and it is on the list at Genesis 2:18 as well:

And the Lord God said, It is not good for the man to be by himself: I will make one like himself as a help to him — Genesis 2:18 (BBE)
And the Existing One of the Judges said, It is not good for the man to be by himself: I will make one like himself as a help to him (Genesis 2:18)

In Genesis 2 the court answers "by himself" by making the one the man can be joined to (2:23-24). In Genesis 32 the same state is answered with a name. The word is also used inside this chapter for the herds, "every herd by itself" (32:16), so each herd was set apart one by one before the man himself was.

4. The fight, and the footing (Genesis 32:24-25)

But when the man saw that he was not able to overcome Jacob, he gave him a blow in the hollow part of his leg, so that his leg was damaged. — Genesis 32:25 (BBE)

The verb for the fight is abaq (H79). It is used twice in the whole Bible, here, and Strong's gives it as "to bedust, i.e. grapple", from the word for dust (H80). So the fight is the kind that raises dust. "Not able to overcome" is yakol (H3201), and it is the same verb as in the name speech three verses later.

Notice what the fight is. The claim holds all night. This is the same test as Jacob and the rods: the proof is taken away, and the claim is held anyway. Here Jacob has sent every piece of proof over the river and still does not let go.

The blow lands on his leg. Jacob means "heel-catcher", from the verb aqab (H6117), "to take by the heel, to supplant", and that verb is tagged in both Genesis 27:36 ("supplanted") and Hosea 12:3 ("took by the heel"). The old name stood on its footing, and the footing is what is struck. The heel (H6119) and the thigh (H3409) are different words, so this is a matching picture, not a word link.

5. The name asked, the name traded (Genesis 32:26-29)

And he said to him, Let me go now, for the dawn is near. But Jacob said, I will not let you go till you have given me your blessing. Then he said, What is your name? And he said, Jacob. And he said, Your name will no longer be Jacob, but Israel: for in your fight with God and with men you have overcome. — Genesis 32:26-28 (BBE)

Read the order. Jacob asks for the blessing. The court asks for the name. Jacob has to say "Jacob" out loud, the state he has been filing under, and the same breath is where it is taken off him. The new name comes with its reason inside it: "you have overcome" is the same verb (H3201) as "not able to overcome" in 32:25. The man could not overcome Jacob, and the name says Jacob overcame.

With the meanings put in:

Then he said, What is your name? And he said, Heel-catcher. And he said, Your name will no longer be Heel-catcher, but Judges-Prevail: for in your fight with the Judges and with men you have overcome.

Israel (H3478) is built on sarah (H8280), "to persist, to prevail". Strong's, Blue Letter Bible and BDB word it slightly differently (see the sources list below). "God" in the BBE's "your fight with God and with men" is Elohim (H430), the plural judges and rulers (Lesson 3). So the verdict is that the claim held before both the Judges and the other voices.

Then Jacob said, What is your name? And he said, What is my name to you? Then he gave him a blessing. — Genesis 32:29 (BBE)

The name Jacob asked for is not given. The blessing is. The court names the one who claims, and it does not hand its own name over to be held.

6. The face of El (Genesis 32:30-32)

And Jacob gave that place the name of Peniel, saying, I have seen God face to face, and still I am living. And while he was going past Peniel, the sun came up. And he went with unequal steps because of his damaged leg. — Genesis 32:30-31 (BBE)

With the meanings put in:

And Heel-catcher gave that place the name of Face-of-the-Judges, saying, I have seen the Judges face to face, and still I am living. And while he was going past Face-of-the-Judges, the sun came up. And he went with unequal steps because of his damaged leg.

Peniel (H6439) is "face of God" in Strong's, from H6437 and H410. El (H410) is read here as the Judges, so the name is Face-of-the-Judges. The word for face (H6440) is in "face to face" twice. Look back at 32:20. Jacob's plan was to cover Esau's face and then see it. What happens is the other way round: he sees the face of Elohim first, at night, and Esau's face comes after.

The night ends in the order Genesis 1 began: dark ("in the night", 32:22), then the light ("the sun came up", 32:31). That is a matching picture. The leg stays damaged: the old footing is replaced, not repaired.

For this reason the children of Israel, even today, never take that muscle in the hollow of the leg as food, because the hollow of Jacob's leg was touched. — Genesis 32:32 (BBE)

This is Rule One. The name goes into the seed. The people after him are "the children of Israel", after its kind. Notice also that the BBE keeps calling him Jacob here and through chapter 33, and the name is given again at Beth-el:

Jacob is your name, but it will be so no longer; from now your name will be Israel; so he was named Israel. — Genesis 35:10 (BBE)

The name is given first and lived into after. Hosea 12 puts the meeting at Beth-el (see the sources list), and Genesis 35 renames him there as well.

7. The far ground: Esau runs (Genesis 33:1-4)

Then Jacob, lifting up his eyes, saw Esau coming with his four hundred men. So he made a division of the children between Leah and Rachel and the two women-servants. He put the servants and their children in front, Leah and her children after them, and Rachel and Joseph at the back. And he himself, going before them, went down on his face to the earth seven times till he came near his brother. Then Esau came running up to him, and folding him in his arms, gave him a kiss: and the two of them were overcome with weeping. — Genesis 33:1-4 (BBE)

The four hundred men are the same four hundred (32:6, 33:1). What has changed is the order. In chapter 32 Jacob said "Go on before me" (32:16) and stayed behind the offerings. In chapter 33 it is "he himself, going before them". No one fights. The brother he feared came running and kissed him.

That is the court answering the name, not the old filing. A claim of "heel-catcher" got four hundred men on the road. A claim of "Judges Prevail" gets an embrace. The men did not change. What was met them did.

8. The face again, the offering taken, the blessing returned (Genesis 33:8-11)

And Jacob said, Not so; but if I have grace in your eyes, take them as a sign of my love, for I have seen your face as one may see the face of God, and you have been pleased with me. — Genesis 33:10 (BBE)
Take my offering then, with my blessing; for God has been very good to me and I have enough: so at his strong request, he took it. — Genesis 33:11 (BBE)

With the meanings put in:

And Heel-catcher said, Not so; but if I have grace in your eyes, take them as a sign of my love, for I have seen your face as one may see the face of the Judges, and you have been pleased with me. Take my offering then, with my blessing; for the Judges have been very good to me and I have enough: so at his strong request, he took it. (Genesis 33:10-11)

Three words carry back from the night. "Face" (H6440) is in 33:10 twice, "your face" and "the face of the Judges (Elohim)". The offering (minchah, H4503) that was sent to cover the face in 32:20 is the one received here. And "my blessing" in 33:11 is berakah (H1293), the same word Esau used in 27:36: "now he has taken away my blessing". The one who took it gives it back. A heel-catcher takes. Israel gives.

So the face Jacob saw on the far ground, Esau's, is spoken of in the same words as the face he saw at Peniel. What was filed in the night is what met him in the morning.

9. The ground after the name (Genesis 33:14-20)

Do you, my lord, go on before your servant; I will come on slowly, at the rate at which the cattle and the children are able to go, till I come to my lord at Seir. — Genesis 33:14 (BBE)
So Esau, turning back that day, went on his way to Seir. And Jacob went on to Succoth, where he made a house for himself and put up tents for his cattle: for this reason the place was named Succoth. — Genesis 33:16-17 (BBE)

Jacob said Seir, and goes to Succoth. Esau's ground is the hill country of Seir (32:3, 33:16; see the Edom page for Seir and Edom). Jacob builds his own. The text gives the reason for the name: Succoth (H5523) is "booths", plural, and booths are what he makes. Then:

So Jacob came safely from Paddan-aram to the town of Shechem in the land of Canaan, and put up his tents near the town. And for a hundred bits of money he got from the children of Hamor, the builder of Shechem, the field in which he had put up his tents. And there he put up an altar, naming it El, the God of Israel. — Genesis 33:18-20 (BBE)

With the meanings put in:

And there he put up an altar, naming it El, the Judges of Judges-Prevail. (Genesis 33:20)

Shechem (H7927) is "ridge" in Strong's. Jacob gets a house, shelters, a field, and an altar with the new name on it. The name given in the dark is now on the ground, the same order as Genesis 1: the ground is named, then the identity stands on it.

What this means for you

The claim you have been living as has a name, and the court will ask you to say it. Gifts sent ahead cannot settle the outcome, because they are proof, and the court rules on the claim. What the night shows is a claim held with the proof out of reach, the old name said out loud, and a new one given before anything on the far side has moved.

Then the far side answers the new name. The brother runs, the offering is taken, the blessing goes back to the one it was taken from. The limp stays as a mark of where the old footing was.

The three parts are all in the night. The Existing One is Jacob, aware, at the river. The identity is the name Israel. The Judges are Elohim, who rule "you have overcome" and give the blessing.

Three questions for this story

  1. What is the ground, and what does its name mean? Two Camps, Emptying, Face of the Judges, Booths, Ridge.
  2. Who is standing on it, and what do their names mean? Heel-catcher, then Judges Prevail, with Hairy on the far ground.
  3. What has changed from the Genesis 1 picture? The man is by himself, the name is traded in the dark, and the brother's face is the face that was seen.

Remember: the claim is held with the proof out of reach, the old name is said and traded, and the far side answers the new one.

Words and sources

  • Jacob (Yaaqob, H3290): "heel-catcher (i.e. supplanter)" in Strong's, from aqab (H6117). 349 times in 319 verses (Blue Letter Bible). H6117 is tagged in Genesis 27:36 and Hosea 12:3; the full H6117 list was not pulled.
  • Israel (H3478): 2,507 times in 2,231 verses. Strong's: "he will rule as God", from H8280 and H410. The Blue Letter Bible outline: "God prevails". BDB: "El persisteth, persevereth (or Let El contend)". The lexicons word it three ways. This page reads El (H410) as the Judges, as the course does, so the name is put in as "Judges Prevail", following the verb sarah and the verse's own reason.
  • Prevail (sarah, H8280): "to prevail, have power (as a prince)" (Strong's), "persist, exert oneself, persevere" (BDB). Blue Letter Bible lists 2 uses in the Hebrew text, Genesis 32:28 and Hosea 12:3. The NAS list counts 3, and Hosea 12:4 carries a related verb tagged H7786 which BDB treats as the same meaning. The count was not resolved here, and the two verses used are certain.
  • Wrestle (abaq, H79): 2 uses, Genesis 32:24 and 32:25 (BDB cites them as 32:25 and 32:26 because it follows the Hebrew verse numbers). "To bedust, i.e. grapple", from dust (H80).
  • Overcome, prevail (yakol, H3201): tagged in both Genesis 32:25 and 32:28, and in Genesis 30:8 where Rachel says she has overcome her sister. The full list was not read verse by verse.
  • Jabbok (H2999): 7 uses in 7 verses, the first Genesis 32:22. Strong's: "pouring forth", "probably from" baqaq (H1238). The outline gives "emptying". Strong's hedges the root, and baqaq has two senses in the lexicons: "empty" (Isaiah 24:1, 24:3) and "luxuriant" (Hosea 10:1). This page uses "emptying" and says so.
  • Peniel, Penuel (H6439): "face of God" in Strong's, from H6437 and H410; put in here as "Face-of-the-Judges". 10 uses in 8 verses: Genesis 32:30-31, Judges 8:8, 8:9, 8:17, 1 Kings 12:25, 1 Chronicles 4:4 and 8:25. Genesis 32:30 spells it Peniel and 32:31 spells it Penuel.
  • Mahanaim (H4266): "double camp", the dual of H4264. 13 uses in 13 verses.
  • Esau (H6215): "rough (i.e. sensibly felt)" in Strong's, "hairy" in the Blue Letter Bible outline, and Genesis 27:11 (BBE) says "covered with hair". 97 uses in 82 verses. Seir and Edom were not pulled again; see the Edom page.
  • By himself (bad, H905): 202 uses in 179 verses. Genesis 2:18, 32:16 and 32:24 are all on the list.
  • Blessing (berakah, H1293): 69 uses in 64 verses. Genesis 27:36 and 33:11 are both on the list. BDB files 33:11 under "a gift, present" while the KJV and BBE read "blessing" (the BBE gives both: "my offering... with my blessing"), so the sense differs while the word is the same.
  • Face (panim, H6440): four times in Genesis 32:20 (interlinear), twice in 32:30 and twice in 33:10. The offering (minchah, H4503) is in 32:20 and 33:10. "Cover, appease" (kaphar, H3722) is in 32:20.
  • Succoth (H5523): "booths", plural of H5521, 18 uses in 16 verses. Shechem (H7927): "ridge" in Strong's, "back or shoulder" in the Blue Letter Bible outline.
  • Where the texts differ:
    • The one who fights is "a man" (Genesis 32:24), Elohim (32:28, 32:30) and "the angel" (Hosea 12:4, BBE). Hosea 12 also puts the meeting at Beth-el, and Genesis 35:10 renames him there too. Both are kept; neither is resolved here.
    • The BBE of Hosea 12:3 says "took his brother by the foot", and the Hebrew verb is the heel verb (H6117).
    • The BBE of Genesis 33:18 says "came safely" and the KJV reads a place named Shalem. The Hebrew was not pulled and nothing is built on it.
    • "Two groups" (32:7), "two armies" (32:10) and Esau's and Jacob's "I have enough" (33:9, 33:11) are BBE wordings. The Hebrew behind each was not pulled, so only the picture is claimed.
  • Checked: the BBE of Genesis 2:18, 27:11, 27:36, 27:41, 32, 33 and 35:10 and Hosea 12:3-4, and the Strong's, BDB and occurrence lists for the words above.

  3. Look up H3201 and find Genesis 32:25 and 32:28 on the list.

Common questions

Who did Jacob wrestle with in Genesis 32?

The text calls him "a man" (Genesis 32:24). Jacob names the place Peniel and says he has seen Elohim face to face (32:30), and Hosea 12:4 calls him an angel. In this reading it is the judging side of you meeting the claim you have been living as.

What does Israel mean in Genesis 32:28?

Israel (H3478) is built on the verb sarah (H8280), "to persist, prevail". The lexicons gloss it "God prevails", "he will rule as God" and "El persists". With El read as the Judges, the name is Judges Prevail. The verse gives the reason itself: "in your fight with God and with men you have overcome".

Why did Jacob limp after wrestling?

The man gave him "a blow in the hollow part of his leg" when he saw he could not overcome him (32:25, BBE). Jacob means "heel-catcher", so the strike lands on the footing the old name stood on.

What does Peniel mean?

Peniel or Penuel (H6439) means "face of God" in Strong's, and with El read as the Judges, Face-of-the-Judges. Jacob names the place for what he says he has seen (32:30).

What does Jabbok mean?

Jabbok (H2999) is "pouring forth" in Strong's, probably from baqaq (H1238), "to pour out, to empty". Jacob sends everything he has across it before the fight (32:22-23).
