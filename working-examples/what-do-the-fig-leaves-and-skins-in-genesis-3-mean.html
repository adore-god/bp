What Do the Fig Leaves and the Skins in Genesis 3 Mean? Two Coverings, Word by Word

Genesis 3 has two coverings. In 3:7 the man and the woman make one from leaves. In 3:21 the Existing One of the Judges (YHVH Elohim in the Hebrew, "the Lord God" in the BBE) makes one from skins. The BBE calls both "coats". The Hebrew has two different words, and the text says who makes each one, what it is made of, and where it sits in the order of the chapter. This page goes through them one word at a time.

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. The two people on the ground named Eden, "pleasure", are without clothing in 2:25. This page follows only the coverings: what each is made of, who makes it, and what the words for skin and clothing are. The nakedness words and the court's question are on the naked page.

The short answer: the man and the woman make a covering of leaves (3:7). The existing One with the Judges makes a covering of skins and clothes them (3:21). The leaf covering is sewn (taphar) from fig leaves (aleh, te'enah) into belts (chagorot). The skin covering is a tunic (kuttoneth) of skin (owr), and the verse adds a second verb, labash, "clothed them". Afterwards the man still says he was without clothing and afraid (3:10), and nobody in 3:9-19 mentions the leaves. The skins come after the name Eve (3:20) and before the sending out (3:22-24). The word for shame (bosh) is in 2:25 and not in chapter 3. The text does not say the leaves came off, that an animal was killed, that the fig was the forbidden tree, or that the skins undo the sending out.

One note on words: the Hebrew words and Strong's numbers below are the evidence. The links to Genesis 1 are a reading, and the page says where each link is a word match and where it is only an echo.

The verses, one line each

  • 2:8. "And the Lord God made a garden in the east, in Eden; and there he put the man whom he had made" (BBE).
  • 2:9. "And out of the earth the Lord made every tree to come, delighting the eye and good for food; and in the middle of the garden, the tree of life and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil" (BBE).
  • 2:25. "And the man and his wife were without clothing, and they had no sense of shame" (BBE).
  • 3:1. "Now the snake was wiser than any beast of the field which the Lord God had made" (BBE).
  • 3:7. "And their eyes were open and they were conscious that they had no clothing and they made themselves coats of leaves stitched together" (BBE).
  • 3:8. The sound of the Lord God walking in the garden comes, and the man and his wife go "to a secret place among the trees of the garden" (BBE).
  • 3:10. "Hearing your voice in the garden I was full of fear, because I was without clothing: and I kept myself from your eyes" (BBE).
  • 3:11. "Who gave you the knowledge that you were without clothing?" (BBE).
  • 3:19. "for dust you are and to the dust you will go back" (BBE).
  • 3:20. "And the man gave his wife the name of Eve because she was the mother of all who have life" (BBE).
  • 3:21. "And the Lord God made for Adam and for his wife coats of skins for their clothing" (BBE).
  • 3:22-24. The Lord God speaks of the tree of life, sends the man "out of the garden of Eden to be a worker on the earth from which he was taken" (3:23), and puts "winged ones and a flaming sword turning every way to keep the way to the tree of life" at the east of Eden (3:24) (BBE).

The two verses this page rests on:

And their eyes were open and they were conscious that they had no clothing and they made themselves coats of leaves stitched together. — Genesis 3:7 (BBE)
And the Lord God made for Adam and for his wife coats of skins for their clothing. — Genesis 3:21 (BBE)

The meanings used

  • Eden (Eden, H5731): "pleasure", from eden (H5730), "pleasure, delight". Used in Genesis at 2:8, 2:10, 2:15, 3:23, 3:24 and 4:16.
  • Naked (arom, H6174, in 2:25; eyrom, H5903, in 3:7, 3:10, 3:11) and shame (bosh, H954, in 2:25 only): covered on the naked page.
  • Sew (taphar, H8609): to sew together. Four uses: Genesis 3:7, Job 16:15, Ecclesiastes 3:7 and Ezekiel 13:18.
  • Leaf (aleh, H5929): a leaf, foliage. Eighteen uses in thirteen verses, first at Genesis 3:7.
  • Fig tree (te'enah, H8384): the fig tree or its fruit. Thirty-nine uses in thirty-five verses, first at Genesis 3:7.
  • Belt, loin-covering (chagorah, H2290; plural chagorot in 3:7): a belt for the waist, girdle, loin-covering. Seven uses in the Hebrew text (six in the KJV). The other uses are belts: a warrior's belt, a belt given as a reward, a belt traded for a rope (1 Samuel 18:4; 2 Samuel 18:11; 1 Kings 2:5; 2 Kings 3:21; Isaiah 3:24).
  • Tunic (kuttoneth, H3801): a shirt or tunic, "from an unused root meaning to cover". Twenty-nine uses in twenty-six verses, first at Genesis 3:21. Later uses include Joseph's tunic (Genesis 37) and the priests' tunics (Exodus 28-29).
  • Skin (owr, H5785): "skin (as naked); by implication, hide, leather". Ninety-nine uses in eighty-two verses, first at Genesis 3:21. It covers human skin and animal hides.
  • Clothe (labash, H3847): "wrap around", to put on a garment or clothe oneself or another. About 111 uses (the NAS count; the KJV count is 112). First at Genesis 3:21.
  • Eve (Chavvah, H2332): "life-giver". Two uses: Genesis 3:20 and 4:1.
  • YHVH (H3068): Strong's gives "(the) self-Existent or Eternal", from hayah (H1961), "to exist, to be or become". The course reads it as "the existing One".
  • Elohim (H430): plural of eloah (H433). Brown-Driver-Briggs lists "rulers, judges" first, then divine ones, angels, gods and God. The course reads it as "the Judges", the judges and rulers acting as one.
  • How the names are used in Genesis 1-4 (from the Hebrew word lists for H430 and H3068): the names speak as many and act as one. Elohim stands alone in 1:1-2:3, and every action verb is singular (said, saw, made, created). The one plural is speech: "Let us make" (1:26), then singular again (1:27). YHVH first appears at 2:4, joined as YHVH Elohim, and stays joined to 3:24 with singular verbs (formed, planted, took, made, sent). The plural returns once, in speech: "like one of us" (3:22). The snake and the woman say "Elohim" alone (3:1, 3:3, 3:5). From 4:1 the narrator says YHVH alone, and Eve says Elohim alone at 4:25.

How to read it, step by step

  1. Start with the ground. The ground is Eden, "pleasure" (2:8, 2:15). In 2:25 the two on it are naked (arom) and have no sense of shame (bosh). Those are the three questions of Lesson 5: what the ground is called, who stands on it, and what has changed.
  2. Note the two naked words. 2:25 uses arom and 3:7-11 uses eyrom. The BBE says "without clothing" for both. The naked page reads them in full.
  3. Read what they make (3:7). The verb is taphar, "sewed". The material is the aleh, "leaf", of the te'enah, "fig tree". The product is chagorot, belts or loin-coverings. The BBE leaves out "fig". The Hebrew has it. All of these words first appear in the Bible at 3:7, so the first things the pair make are named with new words. No verse says the court told them to make anything, and none says the court told them not to.
  4. Read the leaf against Genesis 1. Lesson 7 is the seed rule. Genesis 1:11 (BBE) defines plants by seed: "plants producing seed, and fruit-trees giving fruit, in which is their seed, after their sort". Neither 1:11 nor 1:12 names leaves. So 3:7 builds from the one part of the plant that Genesis 1 does not name. That is a reading. Aleh is used for leaves that fade or are driven off (Job 13:25, Isaiah 1:30, 34:4, 64:6, Jeremiah 8:13, Leviticus 26:36) and for a leaf that stays green by water (Psalm 1:3, Jeremiah 17:8). What they share is that a leaf depends on what it is attached to.
  5. Read the hiding and the man's report (3:8-11). With the coverings on, the pair go "to a secret place among the trees of the garden" (3:8). Asked where he is, the man says he was full of fear "because I was without clothing" (3:10). By his own report, the leaves did not change what he said about himself. The court asks who gave him the knowledge (3:11), and no speaker in 3:9-19 mentions the leaves. The text does not say why the leaves did not settle it. One reading: a belt covers less than a tunic. Another: the problem is fear before the voice, not how he looks.
  6. Note the word shame. Bosh is in 2:25, negated: "no sense of shame". It is not in 3:7, 3:10 or 3:11, and the BBE of Genesis 3 never says "shame". Chapter 3 names fear (3:10) and hiding (3:8, 3:10).
  7. Read the order of 3:19-21. 3:19 ends on dust. 3:20 has the man name his wife Eve, Chavvah, "life-giver", "the mother of all who have life". 3:21 has the skins. By Lesson 9 a name tells you the state. Here the text gives the order and no "because" linking the name to the skins, so the link is the order only.
  8. Read the second covering (3:21). The sentence has the same frame as 3:7: made, for them, a covering. The verb "made" is the same, but the maker is now the Existing One of the Judges (one doer, singular verbs), the garment word is kuttoneth and the material is owr, skin. The BBE folds the last verb into "for their clothing". The Hebrew has its own verb, labash, "clothed them". Owr is used for human skin (Job 7:5) and animal hides (Genesis 27:16, skins of kids). The verse does not say which skin, or whether a creature was killed. Labash and owr also stand together in Genesis 27:16 and Job 10:11. Those are word matches, marked for testing below.
  9. Read what comes next (3:22-24). The tree of life was put in the middle of the garden in 2:9. Genesis 3:3 speaks of "the tree in the middle of the garden" without saying which tree. After the skins, the court speaks of the tree of life (3:22), sends the man out (3:23) and guards the way to the tree (3:24). The ground is still called Eden, so the man is now outside "pleasure". Nothing in 3:22-24 mentions the skins, so the text does not say they undo the sending out.

Where else the Bible uses these words

The three Hebrew words of 3:21 come back elsewhere. What follows is from the full word lists for tunic (29 uses), skin and clothe (110 uses), with the BBE text of the verses. A pattern is marked as a pattern, not a statement.

  • Tunic (kuttoneth). 3:21 is the first of 29 uses. The rest are Joseph's tunic (Genesis 37:3, 37:23, 37:31-33); the priests' tunics (Exodus 28:4, 28:39-40, 29:5, 29:8, 39:27, 40:14; Leviticus 8:7, 8:13, 10:5, 16:4; Ezra 2:69; Nehemiah 7:70, 7:72); Tamar's long-sleeved garment (2 Samuel 13:18-19); a coat in 2 Samuel 15:32; and Job 30:18, Song of Songs 5:3 and Isaiah 22:21.
  • Tunic and "clothe" in one verse. Kuttoneth and labash are in the same verse nine times: Genesis 3:21; Exodus 29:5, 29:8 and 40:14; Leviticus 8:7, 8:13 and 16:4; 2 Samuel 13:18; and Isaiah 22:21. In the priest verses someone else puts the tunic on: Moses is told to put it on Aaron (29:5) and his sons (29:8), and the BBE adds "to make them priests by my order for ever" (29:9). In Isaiah 22:21 the speaker says of Eliakim, "I will put your robe on him, and put your band about him, and I will give your authority into his hand" (BBE). Genesis 3:21 has the same shape: one party puts a tunic on another. The text does not make 3:21 a priest scene, and it does not say the two are made priests.
  • The BBE hides the match. For this one Hebrew word it says "coats" (3:21), "long coat" and "coat" (Joseph, 37:23 and 37:33), "long robe" (2 Samuel 13:18) and "robe" or "robes" (Isaiah 22:21; Exodus 29:8).
  • Joseph's tunic is taken off. "They took off his long coat which he had on" (37:23). They put the blood of a young goat on it (37:31) and bring it to their father, who says, "It is my son's coat; an evil beast has put him to death" (37:33) (BBE).
  • Skin (owr). In the 28 uses of the plain form, 3:21 is first. The rest are the skin of Moses' face (Exodus 34:29-35); the hide of a bull (Leviticus 4:11) and the skin of a burnt offering that goes to the priest (7:8); skin disease and leather articles (Leviticus 13 and 15; Numbers 31:20); porpoise-skin coverings (Numbers 4); a man in a leather belt (2 Kings 1:8); "skin for skin" (Job 2:4); and skin put on a person (Job 10:11; Ezekiel 37:6 and 37:8).
  • Skin and "clothe" in one verse. Three verses have both ideas: Genesis 3:21, Genesis 27:16 and Job 10:11. Job 10:11 reads, "By you I was clothed with skin and flesh, and joined together with bones and muscles" (BBE): the one addressed is the one who clothes. In Genesis 27:16 "she put the skins of the young goats on his hands and on the smooth part of his neck", and in 27:23 Isaac "did not make out who he was, because his hands were covered with hair like his brother Esau's hands" (BBE). In each of the three, someone puts skin on someone else.
  • The Gospel opening. The Greek Old Testament has 3:21 as "garments of skins" (chitonas dermatinous) and "clothed them". It has 2 Kings 1:8 as a hairy man with a zonen dermatinen, a belt of skin, where the Hebrew is owr. Matthew 3:4 and Mark 1:6 (BBE) say John "was clothed in camel's hair, with a leather band about him", and the Greek for the band is the same word, dermatinos (G1193, from derma, skin). It is used only twice in the New Testament. So one Greek word for skin links 3:21, the man in 2 Kings 1:8 and John. None of the verses says which animal, and none says the skin is more than clothing.
  • The ground words around 3:21. The man is "dust" (aphar, H6083) "from the ground" (adamah, H127) in 2:7. 3:19 says he is dust and will return to the ground. 3:23 sends him out to work "the ground from which he was taken", and the BBE says "earth" there. This is adamah, not erets, the word for the Earth named in 1:10. The skins come between 3:19 and 3:23.

What this shows: the tunic is the garment that someone else puts on a person, in the priest verses and in Isaiah 22:21, and 3:21 has the same shape. In Genesis 27 skins are put on one person so that another takes him for someone else. Reading that as "a covering is an identity put on" is the course's reading. Genesis 3:21 does not say the skins change who the two are.

What this adds to the picture

This part is the reading. In the course's model the man claims an identity (Lesson 6) and the court makes it real (Lesson 1). Read that way, 3:7 is an identity put on by the one wearing it, made from the one part of the plant that Genesis 1 does not define. The man's report in 3:10 is that it did not remove what he said about himself. 3:21 is an identity put on by the Existing One of the Judges, in the verse after the name that means life-giver and before the sending out. The text does not say the second covering cancels the first. It says the court made it and clothed them.

Checking the pattern against the text

The pattern here is the course's own: the claim comes back after its kind, and the court is the same court. Each line below gives the verse and whether the text agreed.

  • 3:7: the leaf is outside the seed rule. It fits as a reading and not as a statement. Genesis 1:11-12 does not name leaves, and the leaf word is used both for leaves that fade and for a leaf that stays green by water (Psalm 1:3; Jeremiah 17:8). The pattern that holds across the uses is that a leaf depends on what it is attached to, not that it always withers.
  • 3:7: the leaf covering is self-made. It fits. "They made themselves" is in the verse. The text does not say it was forbidden or rejected.
  • 3:10: the leaves did not settle it. It fits the man's report, with a limit. He says he was without clothing, and he also gives fear as the reason he hid. "The leaves failed" is a reading.
  • 3:21: the skins replaced the leaves. It does not fit as a statement. No verse says the leaves were removed. The page does not claim "replace" or "fix".
  • 3:21: the skins came from an animal that was killed. It is not in the text. The verse says skins and names no creature.
  • 3:21: skin belongs to the sixth-day creatures. This is an echo only. Genesis 1:24-25 (BBE) makes the beasts and cattle on the day that closes at 1:31 with "the sixth day", but 3:21 names no creature, so no word links the skins to that day.
  • 3:21: the same court. It fits. The maker is "the Lord God", the same name as in 2:8 (the garden), 2:15 and 3:22.
  • 2:8, 3:20: names as codes (Lesson 9). They fit. Eden is "pleasure" and Eve is "life-giver". The meaning of Adam was not checked for this page.
  • 3:7: the shame word arrives in chapter 3. It does not fit. Bosh is in 2:25 and the BBE of chapter 3 has no shame word. Fear and hiding are what the text names.

What changed is the ground the two stand on and what they wear on it. The rule did not change: what is planted gives back its kind, and the court that planted the garden is the court that clothes them.

Remember: a covering is made twice in Genesis 3. The man and the woman sew one from the leaves of the fig tree, the part of the plant Genesis 1 does not define. The existing One with the Judges makes one from skin and clothes them. The chapter names fear and hiding, not shame, and it never says the leaves came off. Elsewhere the tunic is the garment another person puts on someone, as with the priests, and the same words for skin and clothing meet in Job 10:11 and Genesis 27:16.

The limits of this reading:

  • The names are the course’s reading. The Strong’s entries give "self-Existent" for YHVH and "rulers, judges" as the first sense of Elohim. "The existing One" and "the Judges acting as one" are the course’s wording (see Lessons 2 and 3), and the BBE says "the Lord God" for both names together. The reading of how the names are used (singular in action, plural in speech) comes from the occurrence lists above. Only Genesis 1:1-4:9 and 4:25 were read for YHVH and Elohim; later chapters were not checked.
  • The BBE carries part of the argument. It says "coats" in both 3:7 and 3:21 where the Hebrew has two words. It leaves out "fig" in 3:7. It says "wiser" for arum in 3:1, "without clothing" for both naked words, "Eve" for Chavvah, and "for their clothing" where the Hebrew has a separate verb, labash. The case rests on the Hebrew words listed above.
  • The lexicons disagree on counts. Chagorah is 7 in the Hebrew text and 6 in the KJV. Labash is 111 in the NAS count and 112 in the KJV count. Eden is 16 or 17. The counts of bosh run from about 100 to 119 across sources. The page relies on the verse lists and first uses, not the totals.
  • What the text does not say. It does not say the fig was the forbidden tree: 2:9, 2:17 and 3:3 call that tree "the tree of the knowledge of good and evil" or "the tree in the middle of the garden". It does not say the leaves were removed. It does not say an animal was killed. It does not say the skins reverse the sending out. It does not say the pair were ashamed in chapter 3.
  • The "same verb" in 3:7 and 3:21. This was read from the Hebrew lines on Blue Letter Bible, where both verses have a form of the same root for "made". The Strong's entry for asah was not pulled.
  • The reading on 3:10. The man's words are in the text. The explanations of why the leaves did not settle it (a belt covers less than a tunic; the problem is fear before the voice) are readings.
  • The leaf and the seed rule. Genesis 1:11-12 not naming leaves is a fact about the verses. Reading that as "the leaf cannot give back after its kind" is the course's reading and is not stated in the text.
  • The word lists have limits. The skin list is for the plain form of owr only (28 of the 99 uses), so the plural in Genesis 27:16 is not in it. The "NAS" lines are the Strong's site's English, not the BBE. The nine verses where tunic and "clothe" meet are a word match from comparing two lists, and the shape they share with 3:21 is a pattern, not a statement.
  • The sixth-day link. It is an echo only. No word shared between 3:21 and Genesis 1:24-25 ties the skins to that day.
  • Still to test: the BBE text of Leviticus 8 and 16 and Exodus 28, 39 and 40 (the priests' tunics); Ezekiel 37:6-8 (skin put on); Genesis 41:42 (Joseph clothed, with labash); Genesis 8:11 (the olive leaf, aleh); Genesis 9:21-23 (the next story of nakedness, not read for this page); and the Septuagint wording of Genesis 3:7, plus the full Septuagint list of dermatinos (G1193), which was not read.
