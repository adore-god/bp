What Does "If You Are the Son of God" Mean in the Temptation? Matthew 4 and Luke 4

The point: just after Jesus is named the Son, a voice tests the name in a waste land where there is no food. It asks the identity to build the scene itself and to prove itself while it does. The answers refuse. The scene comes by the Judges' word, as it did in Genesis 1, so the name is held in the lack and the test ends. It is the Genesis 3 scene run again, and this time it does not end in the same place.

In five lines:

  1. The name comes first: "You are my dearly loved Son" (Mark 1:11; Luke 3:22), and the family line ends "the son of God" (Luke 3:38).
  2. A voice comes at once and says "If you are the Son of God" (Matthew 4:3 and 4:6). It never says "Son of man".
  3. He is in the waste land with no food, so the scene is not built. The voice asks the name to build it: bread from stones, a drop from the top of the Temple, kingdoms for a bow.
  4. The answers are lines Moses spoke about Israel's own waste land (Deuteronomy 6 and 8): life is held by the Judges' word, and the scene is given, not made by the hands. No proof is shown.
  5. The voice goes away (4:11).

The passage with the meanings put in:

Then Jesus (Existing One is salvation) was sent by the Spirit into the waste land (lonely, empty ground) to be tested by the Evil One (the one testing). (Matthew 4:1) And the Evil One came and said to him, If you are the Son of God (the son of the Judges), give the word for these stones to become bread. (Matthew 4:3) But he made answer and said, It is in the Writings, Bread is not man's (the man's) only need, but every word which comes out of the mouth of God (the Judges). (Matthew 4:4) Then the Evil One took him to the holy town (Jerusalem, in Luke 4:9: probably "foundation of peace"); and he put him on the highest point (the top corner) of the Temple. (Matthew 4:5) If you are the Son of God (the son of the Judges), let yourself go down. (Matthew 4:6) Again it is in the Writings, You may not put the Lord your God (your Existing One of the Judges) to the test. (Matthew 4:7) Again, the Evil One took him up to a very high mountain, and let him see all the kingdoms of the world and the glory of them. (Matthew 4:8) All these things will I give you, if you will go down on your face and give me worship. The story, step by step

  1. The name is given, then tested. In Mark the voice from heaven names him Son (1:11), and the next verses send him out to be tested (1:12-13). Luke does the same: the name at 3:22, the line back to Adam at 3:38, and the test at 4:3. "If you are" goes after the name itself. The voice does not argue with it. It asks for proof.
  2. Test 1: bread from stones. He is in the waste land and has had no food for forty days (4:1-2). In Genesis 1 the scene is built first, with the plants and the food already there before the man (1:11, 1:29). Here the scene is not built, and the identity is not standing in the good part of it. The voice treats the name as the builder: give the word, and make the stones bread. The answer puts the building back with the Judges. Bread is not man's only need, and his life is in every word that comes out of the Judges' mouth. The line is from Deuteronomy 8, where Moses looks back at Israel's own waste land. The Lord let them go without food, then gave manna, to see what was in their heart (8:2-3). The same chapter names the claim being refused: "My power and the strength of my hands have got me this wealth" (8:17). So he stays in the lack and does not produce. The food has to come the way it came in Genesis 1, by the Judges' word.
  3. Test 2: the drop. The voice takes him to the highest point of the Temple, the house the Bible has Solomon (Shelomoh, "peaceful", H8010) build for the name. The name is put in that house, and the eyes and heart of the Existing One are there "at all times" (1 Kings 9:3). The voice stands the name at the top and asks it to fall, so that the angels will prove the promise. It quotes Psalm 91 to do it. But Psalm 91 puts the saying first: "Because you have said, I am in the hands of the Lord" (91:9), and only then the angels and the foot kept from the stone (91:11-12). The care follows the claim. The voice wants the care shown before the claim is held. The answer is from Deuteronomy 6:16, "as you did in Massah". There was no water at Massah, and the people tested the Lord by asking, "Is the Lord with us or not?" (Exodus 17:7). The voice's "If you are" is that same question. The words around the answer give the scene as already built: towns, houses and vines "not of your building" (Deuteronomy 6:10-11). Nobody has to prove it. The voice is "the one testing", and the answer uses a stronger form of the same word. The court is not put through a proof like that. For what happens to that house when the heart is not whole, see the Solomon page.
  4. Test 3: the offer. The voice lets him see all the kingdoms of the world and the glory of them (4:8), and the title drops out. It offers them for a bow (4:9). In Genesis 1 the rule over all the earth is given with the image and no bow is asked for (1:26, 1:28). The answer names the Existing One of the Judges again and says to serve him only. The word for serve in the Hebrew behind this line is the man's own job word from Genesis 2:15, where he is put in the garden to work it. So the last answer is given in the ground side's own word. The voice is named for what it is doing: Satan, "adversary".
  5. The test ends without proof. Nothing in the text shows bread, a catch or a kingdom. What the text shows is the voice going away and angels taking care of him (4:11). Luke adds that the voice goes "for a time" (4:13). In the course's terms, a claim held without proof, in a place where the scene is not yet built, is what ends the test.

The original scene beside it

Genesis 1 sets the order: the Judges speak, it is so, and they see that it is good. Genesis 3 is the scene where a voice comes to the man, and Matthew 4 sets a person back into it. Lay the two side by side and the lines match one for one, until the answers.

The voice reverses Genesis 1. There the Judges' word builds the scene and the man is placed in it. Here the scene is not built, and the voice asks the placed identity to build it. The trees in Genesis 2:9 were made "delighting the eye and good for food", so what the woman sees is the scene as the Judges built it. The question is what is followed, what is seen or what was said. She goes by what she sees and adds to the word. Matthew 4 opens in the place Genesis 3 ended, a person in need of food with bread to be got (Matthew 4:2; Genesis 3:17-19), and the voice offers the same three things: food, something to look at, and rule. Each answer goes by the word and not by what is shown. So the one who is sent away this time is the voice.

This is the same two-sided person as in the Son of God and Son of man page: the voice speaks to the God side, and the man side answers. The shape matches Abraham and Isaac and Jacob and the rods, where the claim is held while the proof is out of sight. The voice that promises is the same kind as in the foxes have holes page, and the shrewd voice of Genesis 3:11.

The words checked, in one place

  • Son of God (huios tou theou): huios is "son" (G5207) and theos is "God" (G2316). The same words are in Matthew 4:3 and 4:6 and Luke 4:3. Strong's gives theos as "a deity, especially the supreme Divinity; figuratively, a magistrate", and Thayer lists "of magistrates and judges". So the Son of God is the son of the Judges (Lesson 3).
  • Out of the mouth of God (Matthew 4:4): the Greek has theou (G2316), the Judges. Deuteronomy 8:3 has H3068, the Existing One. The Gospel puts the word in the Judges' mouth, and the Judges are the court that enforces it.
  • Man (anthrōpos, G444): the word in Matthew 4:4 and Luke 4:4. The line is from Deuteronomy 8:3, where the Hebrew has ha-adam, "the man" (H120), twice. It is the same adam as the man made from the ground in Genesis.
  • The Lord your God: in the Hebrew of Deuteronomy 6:13 and 6:16 the two words are H3068, the Existing One, and H430, the Judges. The Greek is kurion ton theon sou (G2962 and G2316). The page reads the pair as "your Existing One of the Judges".
  • The one testing (ho peirazōn, G3985): the word for the voice in Matthew 4:3, and the verb behind "to be tested" in 4:1. Put to the test (ekpeirazō, G1598) in 4:7 is built from ek and peirazō, and it is used four times: Matthew 4:7, Luke 4:12, Luke 10:25 and 1 Corinthians 10:9. The Hebrew in Deuteronomy 6:16 is nasah (H5254), "as you tested him at Massah".
  • Worship (proskuneō, G4352) and serve (latreuō, G3000) in Matthew 4:10. The Hebrew "serve" in Deuteronomy 6:13 is ta'avod, from avad (H5647). The Son of God page's list shows avad at Genesis 2:5 and 2:15, the man's work on the ground.
  • Satan (Satanas, G4567): "adversary, accuser", from the Hebrew satan (H7854), "adversary". The BBE's "the Evil One" is ho peirazōn in Matthew 4:3 and ho diabolos (G1228) in Luke 4:3.
  • Solomon (Shelomoh, H8010): Strong's gives "peaceful", from H7965. 1 Kings 6:14 says Solomon built the house, and 1 Kings 9:3 has the name put in it. Matthew 4 does not name Solomon.
  • Massah: in Exodus 17:7 (BBE) the place is named Massah and Meribah because the people "put the Lord to the test". Deuteronomy 6:16 points back to it.
  • Salvation (yeshuah, H3444): the last word of Psalm 91:16, "I will let him see my salvation". Strong's gives it as the passive participle of H3467.
  • Jesus (G2424): "Existing One is salvation", as read on the Luke 14:1-6 page.
  • Waste land (erēmos, G2048): "lonesome, i.e. (by implication) waste". It is used 48 times and includes Matthew 4:1, Mark 1:12-13 and Luke 4:1. The picture matches Horeb, "waste", on the holy page, but the words are not the same word.
  • The holy town and Jerusalem: Matthew 4:5 in the BBE says "the holy town", and Luke 4:9 names Jerusalem. The Hebrew is H3389, which Strong's gives as "probably 'foundation of peace'". BDB calls the meaning dubious, so the page says "probably" wherever it uses it.
  • Highest point (pterugion, G4419): "a winglet, i.e. (figuratively) extremity (top corner)". It is used only twice: Matthew 4:5 and Luke 4:9. No claim is built on the "wing" part.

Why he starts outside, and what comes next

The name is given, but the scene around it is not built. The telling does this on purpose. Luke ends the family line with Adam, "the son of God" (3:38), and the next verse puts Jesus in the waste land (4:1). So the story sets the name down where Adam's line ended: outside the garden, hungry, with bread to be got. He does not choose it. Matthew says he was "sent by the Spirit" into the waste land (4:1), Luke says he was "guided" there (4:1), and Mark says the Spirit "sent him out" (1:12), the same English words Genesis 3:23 uses for the man sent out of the garden. He is "in need" of food (4:2). It is where the name starts, not something he picks.

For the reader it is a familiar place. The name has been given and nothing around you has changed. The voice says, in effect, "If that is who you are, fix it now and prove it." The test is whether the name is held in the lack, or the scene is made by the hands. Holding is not the end of the story. It is where the scene starts to be built.

That is what comes next. He goes away to Galilee (Matthew 4:12), where "the people who were in the dark saw a great light" (4:16). Then he stands by the sea and says, "Come after me, and I will make you fishers of men" (4:19), and people are made well (4:24). In order that is light, then the sea and what lives in it, then people, the way Genesis 1 builds. Luke gives the same turn as a word coming true. He goes back to Galilee "in the power of the Spirit" (4:14) and says, "Today this word has come true in your hearing" (4:21). Luke also has the voice say the rule over the kingdoms "has been given to me" (4:6). In Genesis 1:26-28 it is the Judges who give rule, and they give it to the image. So the voice offers a rule that was never its to give.

So the waste land is where the name starts, not where it stays. The change is gradual, and the Gospels show it a step at a time.

Where this fits in the course

A man claims an identity and the court makes it real (Lesson 6). Here the identity is named first, and the voice goes after the name. The answers do not produce proof. They stand on the Judges' word from the ground side, and that is what the court enforces. Rule One says what is planted gives back only its own kind (Lesson 7). Nothing in these answers plants a different claim, so nothing different grows. And the scene is not made by the hands. It arrives by the Judges' word, which is why the name waits in the lack and does not produce.

A question to ask of any story: when a voice says "if you are", which side is it speaking to, and which side answers?

Remember: the voice says "Son of God" twice and "Son of man" never. It asks the new name to build the scene and prove itself. Every answer comes from the man side with the word of the Existing One of the Judges, and the test ends without any proof being shown.
