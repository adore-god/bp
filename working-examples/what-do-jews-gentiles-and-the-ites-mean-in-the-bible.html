What Do 'Jew', 'Gentile' and the '-ites' Mean in the Bible?

People ask who the Jews, the Gentiles and the Hittites, Amorites and Jebusites are. This page reads them the way the other worked examples read names: as words in the plot, taken from the verse and from Strong's. It is not about any people alive today. It asks what each word does in the story. Read the passages first, then read them again with the meanings put in. One reminder before you start: in the BBE, "God" stands for Elohim, which is plural, so wherever you see "God" below, read the Judges.

The short answer: three kinds of word, one pattern. Jew is built from the name Judah, and Judah is the name Praise. Gentile is the Hebrew word for a nation, and Strong's says it comes from a word for massing. Israel is called one too. And -ite is an ending that means belonging to the name in front of it. Even the word Jew has it: Strong's calls a Jew a "Jehudite". Read from your side, the Praised-line is the identity you hold under a claim of praise, a nation is a mass of voices gathered under one name, and each -ite is a voice that belongs to the state its name describes (About this reading).

The short version in six steps (BBE):

  1. Leah names her son Judah when she says she will give praise (Genesis 29:35).
  2. The word "Jew" does not appear until 2 Kings 16:6, and 52 of its 76 uses are in Esther.
  3. The text lets people become Jews (Esther 8:17), lets the nations take hold of one (Zechariah 8:23), and says the true Jew is the inward one, whose praise is from God, which reads as the Judges (Romans 2:28-29).
  4. The word for nation, goy, is used of Israel itself (Exodus 19:6), long before the word Jew is used at all.
  5. The ten named kinds in Genesis 15:19-21 are each "the -ite", one kind at a time.
  6. In Judges 3:5-6 the children of Israel live among six of them, take their daughters as wives, and become servants to their gods.

The passages (BBE, selected verses):

The same passages with the meanings put in:

...she said, This time I will give praise to the Lord: so he was named Praise... At that time the king of Red got Elath back for Red, and sent the Praised-line out of Elath; and the Red-ones came back to Elath where they are living to this day. ... And a great number of the people of the land became Praised-line: for the fear of the Praised-line had come on them. ... In those days, ten men from all the languages of the massed peoples will put out their hands and take a grip of the skirt of him who is Praised-line, saying, We will go with you, for it has come to our ears that the Judges are with you. ... The true Praised-line is not one who is only so publicly, and circumcision is not that which may be seen in the flesh: but he is Praised-line who is one inwardly, and circumcision is that of the heart, in the spirit not in the letter; whose praise is not from men, but from the Judges. ... And you will be a kingdom of priests to me, and a holy massed people. ... The Smith, the son of Hunter, and the Ancient, ... Now the children of Prevails were living among the Lowlanders, the sons of Terror, and the Sayers, and the Open-country-dwellers, and the Villagers, and the men of the Threshing-place: And they took as wives the daughters of these massed peoples and gave their daughters to their sons, and became servants to their gods.

The meanings used:

  • God (H430, Elohim): plural, the judges and rulers. In the BBE, "God" stands for Elohim and "the Lord" stands for YHVH. The page reads "God" as "the Judges" (Lesson 3).
  • Jew (H3064, Yehudi): Strong's gives it as "patronymically from" Judah (H3063) and glosses it "a Jehudite (i.e. Judaite or Jew), or descendant of Jehudah". The page reads it as "Praised-line".
  • Judah (H3063): "praised". The verse itself gives the reason: "This time I will give praise to the Lord" (Genesis 29:35). The page uses "Praise" for the name and "Praised-line" for the people who carry it, as on the Esther page.
  • Gentile / nation (H1471, goy): Strong's says "a foreign nation; hence a Gentile; also (figuratively) a troop of animals, or a flight of locusts". It comes "apparently from the same root as" H1465 (gevah, "back"), "in the sense of massing". The page reads it as "massed people".
  • Amorite (H567): Strong's says "probably a patronymic from an unused name derived from" H559 (amar, "to say") "in the sense of publicity, i.e. prominence; thus, a mountaineer". Blue Letter Bible glosses it "a sayer". The page uses "Sayers", because the verse text does not choose between the two.
  • Hivite (H2340): "a villager" (Strong's). The page uses "Villagers".
  • Jebusite (H2983): "a Jebusite or inhabitant of Jebus", patrial from H2982. Jebus (H2982) is glossed "trodden, i.e. Threshing-place" and named as the early name of Jerusalem. The page uses "men of the Threshing-place".
  • Hittite (H2850): "patronymically from Cheth; a Chittite, or descendant of Cheth". Cheth (H2845) is "terror" in Strong's. The page uses "sons of Terror".
  • Israel (H3478): Strong's says "he will rule as God" (from H8280 and H410). Blue Letter Bible glosses it "God prevails". The page uses "Prevails".
  • Canaanite (H3669): "inhabitant of Kenaan", and by implication a pedlar or trader. Canaan (H3667) is "humiliated" in Strong's and "lowland" in Blue Letter Bible. The page uses "Lowlanders".
  • Perizzite (H6522): "inhabitant of the open country" (Strong's), or "belonging to a village" (Blue Letter Bible). The page uses "Open-country-dwellers", so it does not run into the Hivite, "a villager".
  • Girgashite (H1622): "dwelling on a clayey soil" (Blue Letter Bible). Strong's says the name it comes from is of uncertain derivation. It is not in the passage box above, but it is the seventh name in the Judges 3 lists on other pages and the tenth in Genesis 15:21.
  • Kenite (H7017): "a Kenite or member of the tribe of Kajin" (Strong's), glossed "smiths" in Blue Letter Bible. The page uses "Smith".
  • Kenizzite (H7074): "a descendant of Kenaz" (Strong's). Kenaz (H7073) is "hunter", from a root meaning to hunt. The page uses "son of Hunter".
  • Kadmonite (H6935): "ancient, i.e. aboriginal" (Strong's). The page uses "Ancient".
  • Edom / Edomite: Edom means "red" (see Edom, Esau and Seir). The page uses "Red" and "Red-ones". The Edom reading is the BBE's, and most other translations read Aram here (see "Words checked").

How to read it, step by step

  1. Jew is a praise word. Judah is named by an act, and the act is giving praise: "This time I will give praise to the Lord: so he was named Judah" (Genesis 29:35). The name is made out of the claim. Then Strong's builds the word Jew on top of the name: Judah, then Jehudite, then Jew. So the Praised-line means the line of the one who was named Praise. From your side, it is the identity that carries praise as its nature, and by Lesson 9 the story that follows is the one the name already describes.
  2. The word is late, and it is mostly one book's word. All 76 places of H3064 were read from the concordance list. It is not in Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy, Joshua, Judges or Samuel. It first appears at 2 Kings 16:6, where the Jews are sent out of Elath. Of the 76, 52 are in Esther, 11 in Jeremiah, 10 in Nehemiah, 2 in 2 Kings and 1 in Zechariah. So most of the time the word is used, it is a label put on a people under a decree, as in Esther, where the Praised-line are marked for destruction and then turned. The word is a name for an identity that is being claimed, marked or turned, more than it is a description.
  3. The text lets you become one. After the second writing in Esther goes out, "a great number of the people of the land became Jews" (Esther 8:17). The word for "became Jews" is a different one (H3054, the "to become a Jew" verb), so the book has a verb for taking the name on. In Zechariah, ten men "from all the languages of the nations" grip the skirt of a Jew and say "God is with you" (Zechariah 8:23), which reads as the Judges are with you. The word for nations there is goyim, the plural of goy. In the plot, the masses of voices attach themselves to the identity they have heard the Judges stand with. Romans 2:28-29 says the same from the inside: the true Jew is the inward one, and "whose praise is not from men, but from God", that is, from the Judges. A Bible footnote on that verse in one translation notes that Paul is playing on the word, since "Jew" is literally "praise". So the text itself says the word is about what you hold inside, and not what shows (see Lesson 8 for joining an identity).
  4. Gentile is just "nation", and Israel is one. Strong's H1471 appears 561 times in the concordance list. The King James renders it nation 374 times, heathen 143, Gentiles 30 and people 11, so "Gentile" is only one of its English faces. Brown-Driver-Briggs lists it of Abraham's seed (Genesis 12:2), of Jacob and Esau as two nations (Genesis 25:23), and of Israel (Exodus 19:6). The BBE reads Exodus 19:6 as "a kingdom of priests to me, and a holy nation", and the Hebrew word for nation there is goy. So the word does not mean "not a Jew". The word goy is in use from Genesis 10:5, and the word Jew does not turn up until 2 Kings 16:6. First there are masses, then one of them is named Praise. From your side, a nation is a mass of voices gathered under one name. A holy nation is the mass of voices gathered under the one claim, and Elohim, the Judges, are what keep it gathered. This is the same word that the BBE prints as "God".
  5. The -ite is "belonging to". The same ending runs through the Strong's entries: a Jehudite (H3064), a Chittite, "descendant of Cheth" (H2850), an Emorite (H567), a Jebusite, "inhabitant of Jebus" (H2983), and the Agagite on the Esther page. So Jew, Hittite and Amorite are the same kind of word: one belonging to a name. The difference is the name in front. That is why the page keeps reading the name and not the ending. The BBE shows the same thing in Genesis 15:19, where the kinds are "The Kenite, the Kenizzite, and the Kadmonite" in the singular, one kind each. It is after its kind (Lesson 7): the Kenite is the one who belongs to Kajin, whose tribe is glossed "smiths", and the Hittite is the one who belongs to Terror.
  6. The land is given with the kinds already in it, and the story tests what the identity joins. Genesis 15:18-21 names ten kinds in the land that is given to Abram's seed. They are in the scene before the identity is placed in it, like the creatures in Lesson 5. Then Judges 3:5-6 shows the children of Israel (Prevails) living among the Canaanites (Lowlanders), Hittites (sons of Terror), Amorites (Sayers), Perizzites (Open-country-dwellers), Hivites (Villagers) and Jebusites (men of the Threshing-place), taking "the daughters of these nations" as wives, giving their own daughters to their sons, and becoming "servants to their gods". The word for gods in that verse is H430, Elohim, in a Strong's-tagged King James text, so the Judges they serve are now the other kinds' Judges. Taking a wife is joining an identity (Genesis 2:24), and the court rules what you join, after its kind. The scene is the same one, with a different set of Judges over it.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. In Genesis 1 the scene is built, each kind is made "after its kind", the man is made in the image of the Judges, and the man rules the creatures. Here the words are the scene's own names.

What changed is where the words sit. In Genesis 1 the man stands over the many. In these passages the identity is among the many, and the question is what it joins. The Praised-line is a name for the identity held under praise. The nations are the masses it stands among. The -ites are the named states in the scene. The court enforces whichever one the identity joins.

Remember: read the name in front of the ending. Jew is Praise, goy is a massing, and each -ite is a belonging to the state its name describes. The text itself says the Jew is the inward one (Romans 2:28-29), says people can become one (Esther 8:17), and calls Israel a goy (Exodus 19:6). Wherever the BBE says "God", read the Judges. So these are words for what an identity holds and what it joins, and the Judges rule after its kind.

Words checked

  • Jew (H3064): all 76 places were read from the concordance list (2 Kings 2, Nehemiah 10, Esther 52, Jeremiah 11, Zechariah 1). Strong's "patronymically from H3063" and "a Jehudite" were read. Jeremiah 40:15 is listed under the form "Yehudah" and counted as listed. The Aramaic word for Jew used in Ezra and Daniel is a different entry and was not pulled.
  • Judah (H3063): the meaning "praised" is from the Esther page's Strong's check. Genesis 29:35 was read in the BBE. Genesis 49:8 was not used.
  • 2 Kings 16:6: the BBE says "king of Edom" and "the Edomites". Most other translations say Rezin king of Aram and the Arameans, and one commentary note says the two words look alike in the Hebrew. The disagreement is not settled here, so the page uses only what every translation shares, that the Jews are sent out of Elath. The "Red" reading in the passage box is the BBE's, and nothing is built on it.
  • Esther 8:17: read in the BBE. "Became Jews" is H3054 in a Strong's-tagged King James text. The occurrence list for H3054 was not pulled.
  • Zechariah 8:23: read in the BBE. The Hebrew word for nations (goyim) was read in the Hebrew text of the verse.
  • Romans 2:28-29: read in the BBE. The "play on words" is from a footnote in one translation. The Greek words (Ioudaios, G2453, and the others) were not pulled. The Greek word for "God" there was not checked either, so reading it as "the Judges" follows the course rule that "God" stands for Elohim, not a word-for-word check.
  • Gentile / nation (H1471): Strong's, the 561-place concordance count and the King James counts (nation 374, heathen 143, Gentiles 30, people 11) were read. The uses for Abraham, Jacob and Esau, and Israel are from Brown-Driver-Briggs. Exodus 19:6 was read in the BBE and the Hebrew goy was confirmed in the concordance list. Genesis 12:2 and 25:23 were not read in the BBE. The root word H1465 (gevah) was only read as "back" on the same entry. The Greek word usually translated Gentile (ethnos, G1484) was not pulled.
  • The -ite names: Hittite (H2850) with Heth (H2845), Amorite (H567), Hivite (H2340), Jebusite (H2983) with Jebus (H2982), Canaanite (H3669) with Canaan (H3667), Perizzite (H6522), Girgashite (H1622), Kenite (H7017), Kenizzite (H7074) with Kenaz (H7073) and Kadmonite (H6935) were read in Strong's or Blue Letter Bible. Where the sources give more than one meaning the page picks one: Amorite is "prominence", "mountaineer" or "a sayer", and the page uses "Sayers"; Canaan is "humiliated" in Strong's and "lowland" in Blue Letter Bible, and the page uses "Lowlanders"; Perizzite and Hivite both point to villages and open country, so the page splits them. Kenite is "smiths" in Blue Letter Bible, and Strong's ties it to Kajin. Whether Kajin is the Cain of Lesson 17 was not checked here. The Hebrew names Elath and Rephaim were not checked and are left unchanged.
  • Israel (H3478): Strong's says "he will rule as God" and Blue Letter Bible says "God prevails". Genesis 32:28 reads "have prevailed" in the NASB. The BBE wording of that verse was not pulled. The page uses "Prevails".
  • Genesis 15:19 was read in the BBE. The BBE of 15:18 and 15:20-21 was not pulled, and the list of ten is from other translations. Judges 3:5-6 was read in the BBE. Judges 3:1-4 was not pulled, so the page does not say why the kinds were left in the land.
  • Gods in Judges 3:6: the word is H430 (Elohim) in a Strong's-tagged King James text. Other uses of that word in the passage were not compared.
  • English matches only: "daughters" and "wives" as identities rest on the course's reading of Genesis 2:24, and not on a word-for-word check.
  • Not covered here: Hebrew (Ivri, H5680), Israelite, Samaritan, and the Jew and Greek pairs in the New Testament.
