Whose Wife Will She Be? The Woman and the Seven Brothers (Matthew 22:23-33)

Short answer: the Sadducees describe one woman joined to seven brothers in turn, and not one of them leaves any seed. They ask whose wife she will be when the dead stand up again. Jesus does not pick a husband. He says there are no husbands and wives in the standing again, and he sends them to Moses' own scene at the thorn-tree, where the Judges say "I am" over three names of men long dead and are called the Judges "of the living". In this course the woman is the identity and the brothers are the ones who tried to claim her, so a name is not lost when the one who held it dies.

People search this scene as "the woman with seven husbands", "whose wife will she be in heaven" and "will there be marriage in heaven". It is the second of four questions in a row in Matthew 22, straight after render unto Caesar. The same scene is in Mark 12:18-27 and Luke 20:27-40. Here is the passage with the meanings put in, and then the story step by step.

The seven brothers with the meanings put in: Matthew 22:23-33

This is the BBE with the meaning of each name put in, in bold. Where each meaning comes from is in the words list further down.

On the same day there came to him the followers of Just, who say that there is no standing up again from the dead: and they put a question to him, saying, (22:23) Master, Drawn-out said, If a man, at the time of his death, has no children, let his brother take his wife, and stand up seed for his brother; (22:24) Now there were among us seven brothers; and the first was married and at his death, having no seed, gave his wife to his brother; (22:25) In the same way the second and the third, up to the seventh. (22:26) And last of all the woman came to her end. (22:27) When they stand up again from the dead, then, whose wife will she be of the seven? because they all had her. (22:28) But Existing-One-is-salvation said to them in answer, You are in error, not having knowledge of the Writings, or of the power of the Judges. (22:29) For when they stand up again from the dead there are no husbands and wives, but they are as the messengers in Heaven. (22:30) But about the dead standing up again, have you no knowledge of what was said to you by the Judges in the Writings: (22:31) I am the Judges of Father-of-a-multitude, and the Judges of Laughter, and the Judges of Heel-catcher? The Judges are not the Judges of the dead but of the living. (22:32) And the people hearing it were surprised at his teaching. (22:33)

The BBE says "get a family" in 22:24. The Greek says "stand up seed", and that matters below.

How to read it, step by step

  1. The names set the scene. The Sadducees are followers of Tsadok, "just". They come "on the same day", right after the separatists "went away from him" (22:22-23). They bring a statute from Moses, "drawn out" (as in Exodus 2:10, drawn out of the water), a case with its facts, and a question. It is a court scene before a word is said: the just ones, the law of the one drawn out, and a case to be ruled on.
  2. They deny the standing up again, and quote a standing-up rule. They "say that there is no coming back from the dead" (22:23, BBE). The Greek word is anastasis, "a standing up again". Then they quote Moses: the brother takes the wife and gets "a family for his brother" (22:24, BBE). The KJV has "raise up seed", and the Greek verb there is anistemi, the very verb anastasis is built from. The Hebrew behind it is qum, "to arise, stand up". It is "raise up seed" in Genesis 38:8, and in Deuteronomy 25:6-7 the first son "will take the rights of the brother who is dead, so that his name may not come to an end in Israel" (BBE), while the KJV has the wife complaining that he refuses "to raise up unto his brother a name". So the rule they quote is itself a rule for standing a dead man's name up again by seed through the wife. The ones who deny the standing up again are quoting the rule for it.
  3. The case: seven joinings and no seed. The first brother is married and dies "having no seed", and the wife goes to the next, "up to the seventh" (22:25-26). Last of all the woman dies (22:27). In Lesson 8 the man is the one who claims and the wife is the identity he is joined to. Here one identity is joined seven times, and each time the one who joined her ends with nothing sown (sperma, "that which is sown"). Genesis 1:28 blesses the man and the woman with "Be fertile and have increase", and Genesis 5:3 shows a line that carries on: Adam "had a son like himself, after his image, and gave him the name of Seth". This case is the opposite: no son after his image, and no name carried.
  4. The question is built on "was". "When they come back from the dead, then, whose wife will she be of the seven? because they all had her" (22:28, BBE). Everything in it is past: there were seven, they married, they died, they all had her. The question asks which dead husband owns the identity. The word for "whose" is tinos, the same word that opened "whose is this image" in 22:20.
  5. Answer, part one: the Writings and the power of the Judges. "You are in error, not having knowledge of the Writings, or of the power of God" (22:29, BBE). The Writings already hold this case twice. In Genesis 38:8 Judah says "get offspring for your brother", two brothers die, and the seed comes through Tamar, the wife: Perez (Judah and Tamar). In Ruth 4:10 Boaz takes Ruth, the wife of the dead Mahlon, "to keep the name of the dead man living in his heritage", and Ruth 4:12 names "Perez, the son whom Tamar gave to Judah". Both times the wife stays and the husbands change, and the seed is stood up through her after its kind (Lesson 7). That is the power of the Judges: what they name and sow comes after its kind, and it does not depend on the husband surviving.
  6. Answer, part two: no husbands and wives. "For when they come back from the dead there are no husbands and wives, but they are as the angels in heaven" (22:30, BBE). The Greek for angel is aggelos, "a messenger". Heaven is the name the Judges give the arch on day two (Genesis 1:8). The Sadducees picture the standing up again as more of the same joining, with a husband to be picked. Jesus says it is not run that way. Luke 20:34-36 puts it plainly: "The sons of this world are married and have wives", but those who come back "have no wives, and are not married", and "are sons of God". In Genesis 2:24 the joining ends with the two as "one flesh", and when the two are one there is no longer a husband and a wife to count.
  7. Answer, part three: the Judges say "I am" over three names. "Have you no knowledge of what was said to you by God in the Writings: I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob? God is not the God of the dead but of the living" (22:31-32, BBE). These are the words of Exodus 3:6, and Luke 20:37 calls the scene "the story of the burning thorn-tree". In Exodus 3:4 the Judges say Moses' name twice out of the tree, and in 3:6 they say "I am" over three fathers' names. The Greek is ego eimi, "I am". The names are Father-of-a-multitude, Laughter and Heel-catcher (supplanter). The three men are long dead, and the Judges still say "I am" over their names. The Sadducees count by who died. The Judges count by what is named. A name does not die with the one who held it, which is the very thing Deuteronomy 25 was trying to keep going by seed. The living God page follows the same word: the Judges rule for the identity alive now.
  8. So whose wife will she be? None of the seven. She is the identity, the one thing that stays through all seven joinings, and the Judges keep her name living. They were surprised at the teaching (22:33), and then the separatists hear "how the mouths of the Sadducees had been stopped" (22:34, BBE). The questions from this side end.

The seven brothers as states in the reader

In this course nobody in the passage is an outside person. They are parts of the reader's own mind, so here is who each one is.

  • The woman is the identity you want to be joined to. She is the one constant in the case. Seven men come and go, and she stays (Lesson 8).
  • The brothers are seven attempts to claim her from the same place. They are brothers "among us": one house, one kind. Each is the ground-side man, the one who claims by how things stand in the scene (the "Son of man" side on the Son of God and Son of man page). He holds the identity only as long as the circumstances he leans on hold. When they end, he ends, "having no seed". A claim planted in the ground gives back only ground, after its kind, so nothing carries on. The next brother takes the same identity from the same house and gets the same result. It is the mind that starts again with a new plan, the same footing and the same ending.
  • Seven is the whole run. "Up to the seventh" means every version of that attempt has had its turn, and she is still without a son.
  • The Sadducees are the mind that keeps the record. The followers of "just" argue from the case file: what was tried, who died, who had her. They say nothing stands up again, because every ended attempt counts as the end. It is the voice that says "that already failed", and it can only rule by the past.
  • "Whose wife will she be?" is that voice asking which dead attempt gets the credit. It cannot ask anything else, because all it has is "was".
  • The answer comes from the court side. The Judges are not the Judges of the dead but of the living. They say "I am" over a name, not over an attempt. The identity is held by the court's side of the person, the side that issues the image (Genesis 1:27; 5:3), and not by whichever husband tried to hold it. A failed attempt does not cancel the identity. It stands up again when it is claimed in the present, "I am", from the court's side and not from the ground.
  • "No husbands and wives" means no gap is left to argue over. Once the claimant and the identity are one flesh, there is no one left to ask whose she is.

How it ties to render unto Caesar and to the next question

The first question of the chapter asked "whose is this image" (22:20), and this one asks "whose wife will she be" (22:28). The Greek opens both with the same word, tinos. The last question of the chapter, "whose son is he" (22:42), opens with it too. In Genesis the same three come in the same order: the image (Genesis 1:26-27), the woman joined to the man (Genesis 2:23-24), and the son after his image (Genesis 5:3). Luke 20 sets the three questions in a row, with the tax first, the seven brothers second and David's son third. Read in order: whose image, whose wife, and whose son.

Words and sources

  • Sadducees (Saddoukaios, G4523): from Sadok (G4524), the Hebrew name Tsadok (H6659), "just".
  • Moses (Mosheh, H4872): "drawing out (of the water), i.e. rescued".
  • Jesus (Iesous, G2424): Existing-One-is-salvation (Lesson 2).
  • Standing up again (anastasis, G386): "a standing up again", from anistemi (G450).
  • Raise up (anistemi, G450) in Matthew 22:24. Hebrew qum (H6965), "to arise, stand up", in Genesis 38:8 and Deuteronomy 25:6-7.
  • Seed (sperma, G4690): "that which is sown, i.e. seed", used in Matthew 22:24-25.
  • Whose (tinos, G5101): Matthew 22:20, 22:28 and 22:42.
  • Angel (aggelos, G32): "a messenger".
  • God (theos, G2316): "figuratively, a magistrate", read as the Judges (Lesson 3).
  • I am (ego eimi, G1473 and G1510) in Matthew 22:32. Dead (nekros, G3498) and living (zaō, G2198).
  • Abraham (H85), "father of a multitude". Isaac (H3327), "laughter". Jacob (H3290), "heel-catcher (i.e. supplanter)".
  • Heaven: the arch named in Genesis 1:8.
  • Chapters read in the BBE: Matthew 22, Luke 20, Deuteronomy 25, Exodus 3, Genesis 1, 5 and 38, Ruth 4.

  2. Read Deuteronomy 25:5-10 and Genesis 38:8, and see the "raise up" rule in its own place.
  3. Look up G386 and G450 and see that the verb in 22:24 is the root of "resurrection".
  4. Read Exodus 3:1-15 and see who is named and what is said in the present.
  5. Set Luke 20:27-40 beside Matthew's account.

Common questions

What does the woman with seven husbands mean in Matthew 22?

The case is one woman joined to seven brothers in turn, each dying with no seed. In this course the woman is the identity and the brothers are the ones who tried to claim her. Jesus does not pick one of the seven. He says there are no husbands and wives in the standing again, and he points to the Judges saying 'I am' over three names.

Whose wife will she be in the resurrection?

Jesus' answer is that she is no one's of the seven. 'When they come back from the dead there are no husbands and wives' (Matthew 22:30, BBE).

Will there be marriage in heaven?

Matthew 22:30 says that when they come back from the dead there are no husbands and wives, but they are as the angels in heaven. Luke 20:34-36 adds that the sons of this world are married and have wives, and those who come back have no wives and are not married.

What does 'God is not the God of the dead but of the living' mean?

Jesus quotes Exodus 3:6, where the Judges say 'I am' over Abraham, Isaac and Jacob long after they died. In this course the names are identities, and the Judges keep a name alive after the one who held it is gone.

Where does 'I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob' come from?

Exodus 3:6, at the burning thorn-tree. Luke 20:37 names the scene as 'the story of the burning thorn-tree'.

What is 'raise up seed to his brother'?

It is the rule in Deuteronomy 25:5-10, also seen in Genesis 38:8 and Ruth 4:5-10: if a brother dies with no son, his brother takes the wife so that the dead man's name is not cut off. It is often called levirate marriage. The Hebrew verb is qum, 'to stand up' (H6965), and the Greek in Matthew 22:24 is anistemi, the verb that 'resurrection' (anastasis) is built from.

Who were the Sadducees?

The Greek word Saddoukaios (G4523) comes from Sadok (G4524), the Hebrew name Tsadok (H6659), 'just'. In Matthew 22:23 they are the ones who say there is no standing up again from the dead.

Where else is this story in the Bible?

Mark 12:18-27 and Luke 20:27-40. In Matthew and Luke it comes straight after the question about Caesar's tax. In Luke 20 the question about David's son follows it directly.
