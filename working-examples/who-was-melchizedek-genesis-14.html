Who Was Melchizedek? Genesis 14, Psalm 110 and Hebrews 7

People ask who Melchizedek was: a king with no father listed, who turns up for a few verses, brings out bread and wine, blesses Abram, takes a tenth, and is never seen again. His name comes up in only three places in the Bible: Genesis 14:18-20, Psalm 110:4 and the letter to the Hebrews. This page reads all three, starting with the story in Genesis 14, which is the only place he appears in person.

The short answer: Melchizedek is the court's own priest, "priest of the Most High Judges", meeting the identity in the open. His name says "king of righteousness", his city says "peace", and he is the first priest in the Bible. Abram has just won back Lot (the name means "covering") from the burning ground. Two kings meet him in one valley. The king of Sodom offers goods. The king of Salem offers bread, wine and a blessing. Abram takes the blessing and will not take so much as a thread from Sodom. The very next chapter gives him his seed and puts righteousness to his account. Psalm 110 and Hebrews then say the same priest-kind goes on for ever, and that it is not made by a father line.

The course in one minute

This course reads the Bible as a courtroom that takes place inside you, the reader. You do not need to know more than this to follow the page:

  • The Existing One (YHVH) is your present awareness, the one who holds an identity in front of the court. I AM is that identity. The Judges (Elohim, the plural court, which the Greek calls theos, "magistrate") make the identity real. See Lesson 1, Lesson 2 and Lesson 3.
  • Genesis 1 is the master picture: the scene is built and called good first, then the identity is placed in it. Every later story is that picture, changed (Lesson 5).
  • Rule one, after its kind: what you plant comes back as the same kind (Lesson 7). Rule two, leave and join: you step away from the old state and are joined to the new identity (Lesson 8).
  • Names are codes. A name tells you the state being lived in, and the story runs out in line with it (Lesson 9). A father-title tells you the state an identity is issued from.

The story in six steps (BBE):

  1. Four kings from the east make war on the five kings of the plain, including Sodom and Gomorrah. The kings of Sodom and Gomorrah fall into holes of sticky earth. The four take the goods and food of Sodom and Gomorrah, and they take Lot "who was living in Sodom" (14:1-12).
  2. Abram takes his trained men, "sons of his house", chases them as far as Dan, and gets back all the goods, Lot, the women and the people (14:13-16).
  3. On his way back the king of Sodom meets him in the valley of Shaveh, "the King's Valley" (14:17).
  4. Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of the Most High God, brings out bread and wine, blesses Abram and blesses the Most High. Abram gives him a tenth (14:18-20).
  5. The king of Sodom says, "Give me the prisoners and take the goods for yourself." Abram refuses all of it except food and his allies' shares (14:21-24).
  6. "After these things" the Existing One speaks to Abram, promises a very great reward, and Abram's seed is set like the stars (15:1-6).

The meanings used:

  • Melchizedek (H4442): Strong's "king of right", made of melek (king, H4428) and tsedeq (right, H6664). Hebrews 7:2 gives the same meaning, "King of righteousness". The Hebrew name occurs only twice, in Genesis 14:18 and Psalm 110:4.
  • Salem (H8004): "peaceful", the same as H8003. It occurs only in Genesis 14:18 and Psalm 76:2. Hebrews 7:2 gives "King of peace".
  • The Most High God (El Elyon): El (H410) is read as the Judges (Lesson 3). Elyon (H5945) is Strong's "an elevation, i.e. lofty; as title, the Supreme", from H5927, "to go up". Its first four uses are Genesis 14:18, 19, 20 and 22.
  • Possessor (qoneh, from qanah, H7069): Strong's "to erect, i.e. create; to procure; to own". The BBE says "maker". The same word is Eve's in Genesis 4:1, in the naming of Cain.
  • Abram (H87): "high father", Strong's, contracted from H48 (Abiram, "father of height").
  • Lot (H3876): "covering", the same as H3875, "a veil".
  • Sodom (H5467): Strong's "burnt district", from a root meaning to scorch; the Blue Letter Bible outline gives "burning", the reading used on the Sodom page.
  • Dan (H1835): "judge", from H1777. Its first use in the Bible is Genesis 14:14.
  • Chedorlaomer (H3540): Strong's "of foreign origin", a king of Elam. The Blue Letter Bible outline gives "handful of sheaves".
  • Shaveh (H7740): "plain", from H7737. The text itself adds "that is, the King's Valley".
  • Mamre (H4471): "lusty", from the sense of vigour. Eshcol (H812): "cluster", the name of the valley "because of the grapes" (Numbers 13:24, BBE). Aner (H6063): "boy", probably for H5288.
  • The Lord (YHVH, H3068): the Existing One.

How to read it, step by step

  1. The covering sits in the burning ground. Lot, "covering", chose the well-watered valley "like the garden of the Lord, like the land of Egypt" and moved his tent "as far as Sodom" (13:10-12), where "the men of Sodom were evil" (13:13). In chapter 14 the kings of that ground fall into "holes of sticky earth" (14:10), and the four kings take Sodom's goods and food and then "Lot ... who was living in Sodom, and all his goods" (14:11-12). Read from your side: an identity put on (a covering) that lives on burnt ground is exactly what gets carried off when that ground is ruled by others. The fig leaves and skins page follows the same word, covering, from Genesis 3.
  2. High-father goes after it, with the house he built. In Genesis 12:1 Abram was told to go out from "your father's house". Here his men are "sons of his house", so the house he now has is one he made after leaving the old one (Lesson 8). He chases the captors "as far as Dan", "judge". That is the first time the word Dan is used in the Bible, and Rachel later explains the name: "God has been my judge" (30:6, BBE). The covering is won back by going to the Judge.
  3. Two kings, one level plain. The king of Burning comes out to meet Abram in the valley of Plain (14:17), and Melchizedek is there in the same valley (14:18). Both offers are made on the same ground. The king of Sodom speaks only after Melchizedek has acted, and what Melchizedek brings out is bread and wine.
  4. Bread and wine without the sweat. Bread first appears in Genesis 3:19, as what the man will eat "in the sweat of your face" from the cursed ground. Its second use is here, in Genesis 14:18, where it is brought out to Abram. Wine first appears in Noah's tent (9:21, 9:24), where it ends in nakedness and a curse (Noah page). Its next use is here. Its use after that is Lot's own: in Genesis 19:32-35 his daughters give him wine, in the place he stayed. Bread and wine come from the seed-plants and the vine of Genesis 1:11-12 (Lesson 7). Here the ground's fruit is not worked for or taken. It is brought out and handed over with a blessing.
  5. The blessing is the court's own word. The first blessings in Genesis are the Judges': on the sea creatures and birds, then on the man, "be fruitful" (1:22, 1:28). Then the Existing One blesses Abram (12:2-3). Now Melchizedek blesses Abram "of the Most High Judges, Possessor of heaven and earth", and blesses the Most High (14:19-20). The one who blesses is the source the identity is issued from, so this is a father-title in another form: "High-father" is blessed by the "Most High". After its kind, what follows a blessing is increase, and the next chapter gives it: "let your eyes be lifted to heaven, and see if the stars may be numbered; even so will your seed be" (15:5).
  6. The tenth goes to the priest, not to Burning. The word for tenth (H4643) is used for the first time in Genesis 14:20. Abram gives it to the first priest in the Bible. The word for priest (kohen, H3548) is first used in 14:18, so there is no priest before this. What Abram has just won back is not handed to the king of Burning, and not kept. It is given where the blessing came from.
  7. The oath names both. Melchizedek is the bench's priest: right in his name, complete in his city, and he blesses before any seed line exists. Abram answers by joining the two sides. In 14:18-20 the BBE gives only "the Most High God". In 14:22 Abram says, "the Lord, the Most High God, maker of heaven and earth". In the Hebrew, Abram puts the Existing One (YHVH) together with the Most High Judges (El Elyon) and with the Possessor of heaven and earth. In course terms, the present awareness and the court are addressed as one, and he lifts his hand on it. Because the Possessor owns the scene (the same word as Cain's naming, a getting that came out of cursed ground), the king of Burning cannot say "I have given wealth to Abram". This is a turn from Genesis 12:16, where "because of her" Pharaoh "was good to Abram" with sheep, oxen and servants, and Abram came away with goods given by a ruler ("She is my sister" page). In 14:23 he will take no thread from the burning ground. He does take the food the men have eaten, and gives his allies, Vigour, Cluster and Boy, their share.
  8. Then righteousness is counted. Genesis 15 opens "After these things", with "Have no fear, Abram: I will keep you safe, and great will be your reward" (15:1), the reward the king of Burning could not give. Abram says he has no child (15:2-3), is shown the stars (15:5), and "he had faith in the Lord, and it was put to his account as righteousness" (15:6). The word for righteousness here (tsedaqah, H6666) has the same letters as the second half of Melchizedek's name, and this is its first use in the Bible. King-of-righteousness blesses, and then the verdict is entered: righteousness is put to Abram's account (promise page).
  9. Psalm 110 and Hebrews: a priest with no father-title, and where Aaron and Levi stand. Psalm 110:4 says the Existing One has made an oath: "You are a priest for ever, after the order of Melchizedek." Genesis 14 gives him no father, mother or line, and Hebrews 7:3 reads that silence. In this course a father-title is the state an identity is issued from, so a priest with none is not issued from a seed line. Aaron's priesthood is the other kind. It runs by seed (Exodus 28:43) and is the Genesis 3 scene fenced: a coat from the court, death at every edge, a door to keep. Melchizedek is the earlier scene, the Genesis 1 blessing out in the open, with bread and wine and no robe, tent or statute. Hebrews 7:9-10 says Levi, who has the right to take the tenth, gave it in Abraham, "still in his father's body", so the seed line pays to this priest before it exists. Aaron's priests are many "because death does not let them go on for ever" (7:23), while this priest's "life goes on for ever" (7:24). So Hebrews asks what need there was for another priest of Melchizedek's order, not Aaron's (7:11), and says our Lord comes out of Judah, a tribe Moses said nothing about for priests (7:14). This priest is made by an oath (7:20), and a "second priest" who is "like Melchizedek" has come (7:15). The text says "like" and "order of". It never says Melchizedek was anyone else.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. In Genesis 1 the scene is built and called good before the man stands in it:

What changed is that the scene is now ruled by kings of a burning ground, and the identity has to choose which king's offer to take. In Genesis 1 nothing is offered, because everything is already given. Here two offers stand side by side, and what is taken from the Most High comes with a blessing and a seed, while what is taken from Burning comes with a claim: "I have given wealth to Abram."

What it means for you

  • You are Abram here, "high father", the identity that has left an old state and is building a house. What you have put on (Covering) can be taken when it lives on burnt ground, and it is won back by going to the Judge.
  • Two offers meet you in the same valley. One says "take the goods", and the price is that the goods are credited to Burning. The other brings bread and wine and a blessing. The page shows which is taken: the blessing, and a tenth given back to its source.
  • Melchizedek is the court's priest meeting you, right in his name and complete in his city, with no father line. He does not come out of the old state, so he can bless the identity before the seed arrives.
  • Righteousness is counted after the blessing, not earned first. The next scene in Genesis 15 is seed like the stars.

Remember: two kings meet you in one valley. Burning says "take the goods", and Salem brings bread, wine and a blessing. The identity that takes the blessing, refuses a thread from Burning, and gives a tenth to the first priest has righteousness put to its account in the very next chapter.
