What Do the Greek Words in Revelation 22:12-21 Mean? The Last Ten Verses, Word by Word

The Bible opens with a scene and ends with the same scene back in place. Genesis 1 builds the ground, the trees and the light. Genesis 3 sends the man out and keeps the way to the tree of life. Revelation 22 opens with the river and the tree of life (22:1-2), and the last ten verses, 22:12-21, are the closing words. They are short, and the English hides a lot of them. This page goes through the Greek one word at a time.

The short answer: the ending reads like the close of a court case. Wages are paid back for work (misthos, apodidomi, ergon). The speaker is the first and the last, the archē and the telos, and archē is also the Greek word for a ruler. Robes are washed and the right to the tree is given (exousia, xylon). Outside is whoever loves and makes a lie. The speaker is the root and the genos of David, and genos is the "kind" of "after its kind" (Lesson 7). The Spirit and the bride say "Come" with the same verb as "I come". The water of life is taken freely. Three times the text uses the word for giving evidence in court, and it ends with a warning about adding to the record or taking from it. The seventh day of Genesis 2:1-3 is in the ending too. The word for the work the Judges stop from is the word the wages are paid by, and the verb for "set apart" is in 22:11. The course calls the claim the filing, because the Bible works like a courtroom (Lesson 1).

One note on words: the Greek words and Strong's numbers below are the evidence. The links to Genesis are a reading, and the page says where each one is a word match and where it is only an echo.

The verses, one line each

  • 22:12. "See, I come quickly; and my reward is with me, to give to every man the outcome of his works" (BBE). The verb "come" is in the present tense. Wages are "given back" to each one.
  • 22:13. "I am the First and the Last, the start and the end" (BBE). The BBE leaves out the letters. The Greek has "the Alpha and the Omega" as well.
  • 22:14. "A blessing on those whose robes are washed, so that they may have a right to the tree of life, and may go in by the doors into the town" (BBE).
  • 22:15. "Outside are the dogs, and those who make use of evil powers, those who make themselves unclean, and the takers of life, and those who give worship to images, and everyone whose delight is in what is false" (BBE).
  • 22:16. "I, Jesus, have sent my angel to give witness to you of these things in the churches. I am the root and the offspring of David, the bright and morning star" (BBE).
  • 22:17. "And the Spirit and the bride say, Come. And let him who gives ear, say, Come. And let him who is in need come; and let everyone desiring it take of the water of life freely" (BBE).
  • 22:18-19. A witness is given to everyone who hears the words: if anyone adds to them, the punishments of the book are added to him, and if anyone takes away from them, his part in the tree of life and the holy town is taken away (BBE).
  • 22:20-21. "He who gives witness to these things says, Truly, I come quickly. Even so come, Lord Jesus. The grace of the Lord Jesus be with the saints. So be it" (BBE).

The meanings used

  • I come (erchomai, G2064): present tense in 22:12 and 22:20. The Greek does not say "I will come". In 22:17 and 22:20 "Come" is erchou, the same verb as a command.
  • Quickly (tachy, G5035): soon, without delay.
  • Reward, wages (misthos, G3408): pay for work done. Strong's reads it "pay for service, good or bad".
  • Give back (apodidomi, G591, from apo "back" and didomi "give"): to pay what is owed. Here the form is apodounai.
  • Each (hekastos, G1538) and work (ergon, G2041): each one, and the work of each one.
  • Alpha and Omega (G1 and G5598): the first and last letters.
  • First, last (protos, G4413; eschatos, G2078).
  • Beginning, rule (archē, G746): the beginning, and also rule or office. Luke 12:11 and Titus 3:1 use it for the ruling authorities. End (telos, G5056): the end, the goal, the point where something is complete.
  • Robe (stolē, G4749): a long robe. Wash (plynō, G4150): to wash clothes and other things, not the body.
  • Right, authority (exousia, G1849): the right or authority to do something. Tree (xylon, G3586): the same word as in the rods page, and the Septuagint's word for the tree of life.
  • Dogs (kyōn, G2965); sorcerers (pharmakos, G5333); the sexually immoral (pornos, G4205); murderers (phoneus, G5406); idol-worshippers (eidōlolatrēs, G1496).
  • Love (phileō, G5368), make, do (poieō, G4160) and lie (pseudos, G5579): the three words of 22:15's last phrase.
  • Give witness (martyreō, G3140; symmartyreō, G4828): to testify, as a witness does. Church (ekklēsia, G1577): an assembly.
  • Root (rhiza, G4491) and kind, offspring (genos, G1085): the family, the stock, the kind.
  • Bride (nymphē, G3565); freely (dōrean, G1432): as a gift, for nothing; take (lambanō, G2983).
  • Add (epitithēmi, G2007) and take away (aphaireō, G851); plague, blow (plēgē, G4127).
  • Jesus (Iēsous, G2424) is the Greek form of Yehoshua (H3091), "the existing One saves". David (H1732) is "beloved".
  • Make holy, set apart (hagiazō, G37, from hagios, G40): the Septuagint's verb for the seventh day in Genesis 2:3 (ēgiasen), and the verb in Revelation 22:11 (hagiasthētō). The Hebrew behind Genesis 2:3 is qadash (H6942).
  • Finish, rest, bless (Genesis 2:2-3): kalah (H3615, "finished"), shabath (H7673, "rested"), barak (H1288, "blessed"). The Septuagint has katapauō (G2664) for "rested" and eulogeō (G2127) for "blessed". The work stopped from is melakah (H4399) in the Hebrew and ergon (G2041) in the Septuagint.

How to read it, step by step

  1. Start with the tense. "I come" is present. It is not "I will come" (22:12, 22:20). The coming is stated as now, and the answer is the same verb in the command: "Come" (22:17, 22:20).
  2. Read the pay. Misthos is wages and apodidomi is to give back what is owed. The measure is ergon, the work of each one. This is "after its kind" (Genesis 1:11) in the language of a pay-desk: what comes back matches what was done.
  3. Read the first and last. The speaker is the Alpha and Omega, the first and the last, and the archē and telos. Archē is the first word of the Greek Genesis (en archē, "in the beginning") and the word for rulers in Luke 12:11 and Titus 3:1. The beginning is also the rule. The Judges of Lesson 3 are the ones who begin the scene and rule it.
  4. Read the robes and the right. The robe is the long garment worn. It is washed, and what follows is exousia, the right, "over the tree" (epi to xylon). The same word and the same small word epi are in Revelation 2:26, where the right is "over the nations". So 22:14 can be read as a right over the tree and not only a right to come near it.
  5. Read the outside. The last item in 22:15 is "everyone who loves and makes a lie". The BBE has "whose delight is in what is false" and drops "makes". Poieō, "make", is the verb the Judges use in Genesis 1:26 (Greek poiēsōmen, "let us make"). What is outside is a lie that is loved and made, and it stays outside, as filed.
  6. Read the kind. "The root and the genos of David." Genos is the word in the Greek of Genesis 1:11-12, kata genos, "after its kind". A root and a kind are the two halves of the seed rule: the root is what is planted and the genos is what comes back. With David read as "beloved", the speaker is the root and the kind of the Beloved.
  7. Read the "Come". The Spirit and the bride say erchou. Then the hearer says it, and the thirsty one comes. It is the same verb as "I come" in 22:12. The one who is coming and the ones who say "come" are using one word.
  8. Read the water. Dōrean is "as a gift". Wages in 22:12 are earned, and the water in 22:17 is not. Whoever is willing takes it (lambanō), and nothing is paid for it.
  9. Read the witness. The text uses martyreō in 22:16, 22:18 and 22:20, the word for giving evidence. Then comes the rule about the record: nothing added (epitithēmi) and nothing taken away (aphaireō). A court has the same rule about a record.

The seventh day: the work stops and the record closes

Genesis 1 ends its scene on a seventh day, and Genesis 2:2-3 is where the Judges stop:

And on the seventh day God came to the end of all his work; and on the seventh day he took his rest from all the work which he had done. And God gave his blessing to the seventh day and made it holy: because on that day he took his rest from all the work which he had made and done. — Genesis 2:2-3 (BBE)

Three links to the ending were checked word by word. Two are word matches and one is a reading.

  1. Work (word match, through the Greek Genesis). The Septuagint has the Judges resting "from all his works" with ergon (G2041) in 2:2 and 2:3. Revelation 22:12 pays each one by his ergon, the same word. The day the Judges stop is the work that the ending pays out. The Hebrew of Genesis 2:2-3 uses a different word, melakah (H4399), so this link runs through the Greek only.
  2. Set apart (word match, one verse before the range). The Septuagint says the Judges "set apart" the seventh day with hagiazō (G37). The Septuagint list for G37 opens at Genesis 2:3. Revelation 22:11 has the same verb: "let the holy be holy still" (BBE), hagiasthētō. In Genesis the Judges do the setting apart. In Revelation the verb is passive, so the setting apart is done to the holy one, and the small word eti, "still", says it keeps going. The "holy city" in 22:19 is hagias, from hagios (G40), the word hagiazō is built on. That is the same family and not the same word.
  3. The record closes (a reading, not a word match). Genesis 2:2 says the work came to its end (kalah, H3615), and then nothing more is made. Revelation 22:18-19 says nothing is added (epitithēmi) and nothing is taken away (aphaireō). No word is shared. The link is the order: the work is finished, what is finished is set apart, and a finished record is not reopened.

Where it does not match. The "blessing" of 22:14 is not the blessing of Genesis 2:3. The Septuagint uses eulogeō (G2127) for the seventh day, and 22:14 uses makarios. The BBE says "blessing" for both, so that link is in the English only and the page does not rely on it. The word for rest is not in the ending either. The Septuagint's katapauō (G2664) has four uses in the New Testament, and the lists read for this page name Acts 14:18 and Hebrews 4:4, not Revelation. So the page does not say the ending is the rest. It says the ending has the parts of the seventh day: the work paid, the set-apart state kept, and the record shut.

What this adds to the picture. This part is the reading. In Genesis 1 the Judges speak, name and rule on each thing, and then they stop. In the ending the claim on file is paid by its work (22:12), and the state of the holy one goes on being set apart (22:11). Whoever adds to the record or takes from it is reopening what the seventh day closed. That is why the warning (22:18-19) comes last.

Checking the pattern against the text

The pattern here is the course's own: the Bible keeps one court, and the claim comes back after its kind. Each line below gives the verse and whether the text agreed.

  • 22:12: the pay is matched to the work, each by each. It fits. The Greek is exact: hekastos and ergon, one for one.
  • 22:13: the first and last is also the ruler (archē). It fits, with a limit: archē means both "beginning" and "rule", and the verse alone does not say which one is meant. The other uses (Luke 12:11, Titus 3:1) are what show the second sense is a real one.
  • 22:14: the right over the tree after the robes are washed. It fits the tree rule of the rods page. Washing is the Greek word for clothes, so the robe is what is cleaned and not the person. What the robe stands for is a reading.
  • 22:15: a lie that is loved and made is kept outside. It fits, and it is the word-for-word check the BBE hides.
  • 22:16: root and genos. It fits. This is the clearest word link on the page.
  • 22:17: "come" and "I come" are one verb. It fits. Misthos against dōrean does not look the same at first, and the page does not smooth it over. Both stand in one chapter: what was filed is given back (22:12), and the water of life is not wages (22:17).
  • 22:18-20: three words for witness, then a rule for the record. It fits the court language of Lesson 1. Notice that the BBE has "I say" in 22:18 and "give witness" in 22:16 and 22:20. The Greek has the witness word in all three.

What changed from Genesis 1 to 3

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. In Genesis 3 the hand that reaches toward the tree of life is shut off. In Revelation 22 the same tree is given back. The table lines up the parts.

What changed is that the door is open. In Genesis 3 the way to the tree is kept. In Revelation 22 there is a right over it, and the water is taken freely. The rule did not change: what is planted gives back its kind, and what is made as a lie is kept outside as filed.

Remember: the end of the Bible is the Genesis scene with the way open. The pay is matched to the work, the speaker is the beginning and the rule, the robe is washed and the right is given over the tree, the lie that is loved and made is outside, the root and the genos are one, "I come" and "Come" are one verb, and the record is closed with witness. The seventh day is in it too: the same word for work, the same verb for set apart, and a record that is shut once the work is paid.

The limits of this reading:

  • The text of 22:14. Most Greek texts read "washing their robes" (plynontes tas stolas). The Textus Receptus line, behind the KJV, reads "doing his commandments" (poiountes tas entolas, G1785). The BBE follows the first.
  • The text of 22:12. The Textus Receptus has "as his work shall be" (estai) and modern Greek texts have "is" (estin). The BBE's "outcome" is its own wording.
  • The text of 22:19. The Greek manuscripts have "tree of life" (xylon). The KJV has "book of life", which comes from the Textus Receptus line. The BBE has "tree".
  • The text of 22:18 and 22:21. The witness verb in 22:18 and the wording of the final blessing differ between manuscripts. Check the apparatus for both before building on them.
  • "I am" in 22:13. The Greek of 22:13 has "I the Alpha and the Omega" with no verb written. The English "am" is supplied. 22:16 does have the verb (eimi, "I am the root").
  • The BBE carries part of the argument. "Offspring", "doors", "outcome" and "whose delight is in what is false" are the BBE's words. The Greek behind them is on this page, and the case rests on the Greek.
  • Echoes and not proofs. The epi link to Revelation 2:26, the lambanō link to Genesis 3:22, and archē as "rule" in 22:13 are word matches, but no verse says it is the same scene. They are the reading, offered so you can test them.
  • The seventh-day links. The ergon link runs through the Septuagint, because the Hebrew word is melakah. The Septuagint of Genesis 2:2 also reads "sixth day" where the Hebrew has "seventh", and the ergon and hagiazō links rest on 2:3, which reads the same either way. The "record closes" link has no shared word.
  • Not checked for the seventh day. The Septuagint verb for "finished" in Genesis 2:2 (synetelesen) may share a root with telos in 22:13. Its Strong's entry was not read, so the page does not claim it. The Strong's number for makarios in 22:14 was not pulled either.
  • Still to test: Revelation 22:5 ("no more night") against the seventh day, which has no "evening and morning" in Genesis 2:1-3 (the verses were read, but the formula was not compared across all six days of Genesis 1 in this session); Revelation 21:6 (the same Alpha and Omega, with the water given "freely"), Revelation 2:7 (the tree of life), Revelation 2:28 (the morning star) and Revelation 22:1-11.
