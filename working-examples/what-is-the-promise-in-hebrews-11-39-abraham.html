What Is "the Promise" in Hebrews 11:39? Abraham, Isaac and the Land

Hebrews 11 lists people who acted before anything showed. It ends with a line that surprises many readers:

And not one of these got the good things of the agreement, though they all had a good record through faith — Hebrews 11:39 (BBE)

Yet Hebrews 6:15 says of Abraham, "when he had been waiting calmly for a long time, God's word to him was put into effect" (BBE). So did Abraham get the promise or not? This page follows the promise back to Genesis 12 to 23 to see what it was made of, and what it says to the reader.

The short answer: in this course Abram is you, the reader, taking on a new identity, and the Judges make it real (About this reading). The promise is the court's word that they will. The BBE calls it "the agreement," and Hebrews 6:16 uses court language: "any argument is ended by the decision of the oath." In Genesis the promise comes in three parts, and each part arrives at a different time:

  • The identity shows up. Abraham gets his son Isaac inside his own lifetime (21:2).
  • The ground comes later. The land is given a date, four generations on, and a reason: "the sin of the Amorite is not full" (15:16).
  • In between, he holds the claim. He lives "in tents" (Heb 11:9), and the only ground he owns is a field to bury his wife in (Genesis 23).

Hebrews 6:15 is about the first part. Hebrews 11:39 is about the second. So the verse does not say the claim failed. It says the record was good, the ground was not yet in place, and they could not be "complete without us" (11:40).

What the story tells the reader to expect

  1. Leave the old ground (12:1). "Go out from your country and from your family and from your father's house" (BBE). This is the Genesis 2:24 rule (Lesson 8): you step away from the old state before you can be joined to the new one.
  2. Put the claim on the record before anything shows (15:5-6). The stars cannot be numbered, "even so will your seed be," and "he had faith in the Lord, and it was put to his account as righteousness" (BBE). Nothing has shown yet. The claim comes first. Jesus gives the same order in Matthew 7:7-11, and Mark 11:24 puts it in one verse: "have faith that it has been given to you, and you will have it" (BBE). Romans 4:20 says of this moment, "he did not give up faith in the undertaking of God" (BBE).
  3. The ground has a date, and the Judges set it while you sleep (15:12-18). "A deep sleep came on Abram." In the dark, "a smoking fire and a flaming light" goes "between the parts of the bodies" (BBE). The land is worded as already given: "To your seed have I given this land." The date comes in the same chapter: four hundred years, "I will be the judge of that nation," and "in the fourth generation they will come back here; for at present the sin of the Amorite is not full." Genesis 15:19-21 then names the kinds already living on that ground, and what each -ite name means is on the -ites page. The same word for deep sleep is first used in Genesis 2:21, when the man sleeps and the woman is made and brought to him. In both scenes the Judges do their work while the man is asleep, and he is shown the result afterward.
  4. The identity arrives after its kind (21:1-7). "Sarah became with child, and gave Abraham a son when he was old, at the time named by God" (BBE). The son is named Isaac, "laughter," and Sarah says, "God has given me cause for laughing." A father of a multitude gets a son (Lesson 7). This is the first part of the promise, and it shows in his lifetime.
  5. Hold the claim the same way when the result is put on the altar (22:1-14). Abraham is told to give up Isaac. He answers "Here am I" when he is called, when Isaac speaks and when the voice comes from heaven (22:1, 22:7, 22:11). His answer does not change. Then he sees "a sheep fixed by its horns in the brushwood," and the place is named "In the mountain the Lord is seen" (22:13-14). Hebrews 11:19 says "he did get him back as if from death." The oath follows: "your seed will take the land of those who are against them" (22:17). The full reading of this scene, with the three answers and the place name, is at Lingua Divina: Genesis 22 With Abraham and Isaac.
  6. In between, you hold the claim in tents (Heb 11:9, 10:36). Abraham was "living in tents with Isaac and Jacob, who had a part with him in the same heritage." Hebrews 10:36 says, "you have need of waiting before his word has effect for you." The only ground he owns is a field: "give me some land here as my property, so that I may put my dead to rest" (23:4). Sarah is put to rest there, and "the field and the hollow rock were handed over to Abraham as his property" (23:19-20). The field is named Machpelah, "a fold" or "double," and it fills in pairs: "Abraham and Sarah his wife... Isaac and Rebekah his wife... and there I put Leah to rest" (49:31). It is the leave-and-join rule ending in a shared resting place.
  7. The finished ground comes at the end of the story. Hebrews 11 restates the land as "the strong town, whose builder and maker is God" (11:10) and "a better country," where "he has made ready a town for them" (11:16). It says the group died "not having had the heritage" (11:13). The town comes down in Revelation 21 (How does the Genesis scene finish in Acts and Revelation?). That is the part Hebrews 11:39 calls not yet received.

The names and places, with the meanings put in

A name tells you the state before the story starts, and the story then runs out in line with it (Lesson 9).

IsaacH3327, "laughter (that is, mockery)," from tsachaq (H6711).Abraham laughs (17:17), the name is given (17:19), and Sarah has "cause for laughing" (21:6).

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says Genesis 1 builds the whole scene before the man stands in it: the ground, the seed, the time and the stand. The ground is named Eden, "pleasure" (H5731). The promise stories run the other way. The claim comes first, and the scene follows.

PartGenesis 1 to 3The promise stories
The groundEden, "pleasure," is named before the man stands on it (2:8)Canaan, "humiliated," with a date on it (15:16)
The seedPlants producing seed "of its sort" (1:11-12)Seed like the stars (15:5), and a son named Isaac, "laughter" (21:3)
The timeThe lights of day four mark when something is dueAbram is taken out to number the stars (15:5), and the date follows: four hundred years and the fourth generation (15:13-16)
The standThe identity is given a body and a place to stand"Living in tents" (Heb 11:9), and a burial field as the one place handed over (Gen 23:20)

That is what the promise says to the reader: the identity first, the scene later.

Remember: the promise is the court's word that the identity you claim will be made real. The identity shows up first, in your own lifetime. The ground it stands on is put in place later, on its own date. Until then you hold the claim, and Hebrews 11:39 counts that as a good record, not a failure.

  • Promise (epangelia, G1860): its uses in Hebrews include 6:12, 6:15, 6:17, 10:36, 11:9, 11:13, 11:17, 11:33 and 11:39. The BBE puts "agreement" in 11:9 and 11:39, the same English word it uses for the covenant in Genesis 15:18 and 17:2.
  • Receive: komizo (G2865) is in Hebrews 10:36, 11:19 and 11:39. It is the same verb for Isaac "got back" (11:19) and for the promise "not received" (11:39).
  • Inherit (yarash, H3423): the heir (Genesis 15:3-4) and the land (15:7-8) share this one verb, which is used again in 21:10 and 22:17.
  • Deep sleep (tardemah, H8639): seven uses, the first two Genesis 2:21 and 15:12, then 1 Samuel 26:12, Job 4:13, Job 33:15, Proverbs 19:15 and Isaiah 29:10.
  • Names and places: Abraham (H85), Isaac (H3327), Canaan (H3667), Amorite (H567), Hebron (H2275), Machpelah (H4375, six uses: Genesis 23:9, 23:17, 23:19, 25:9, 49:30 and 50:13, from kaphal, H3717) and Moriah (H4179, two uses: Genesis 22:2 and 2 Chronicles 3:1).
