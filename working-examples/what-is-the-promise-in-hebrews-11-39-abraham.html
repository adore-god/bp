What Is "the Promise" in Hebrews 11:39? Abraham, Isaac and the Land

Hebrews 11 walks through people who acted before anything showed, and it closes with a line that surprises many readers:

And not one of these got the good things of the agreement, though they all had a good record through faith — Hebrews 11:39 (BBE)

Yet Hebrews 6:15 says of Abraham, "when he had been waiting calmly for a long time, God's word to him was put into effect" (BBE). Both lines are in one letter. This page follows the word "promise" back to Genesis 12 to 23 to see what it was made of, and it sets out the checks so you can weigh the readings yourself. As the course reads it, Abram is the identity the reader claims, and the places are the ground that identity stands on (About this reading).

The short answer: in Genesis the promise is made of strands, and the text keeps them apart. The seed arrives inside Abraham's life, in Isaac. The land is given a date four generations on (15:13-16), and the only ground Abraham comes to hold is a burial plot (Genesis 23). Hebrews 11 follows the same split. Sarah gives birth and Isaac is got back "as if from death" (11:19), while the group dies "not having had the heritage" (11:13). Hebrews then restates that heritage as "the strong town" and "a better country" (11:10, 11:16). In the course's reading the heritage is the ground the claim stands on, and the court's timing is part of the filing. The verse does not call the claim a failure. It says the record was good and the group was not yet "complete" (11:40). That is a reading, and the page marks where it goes past the text.

One note on words: the English "promise" is one Greek word, epangelia (G1860), but Hebrews uses three different verbs for receiving it, and the BBE says "agreement" where the Greek says promise. The words section sets these out.

The promise in Genesis, one line each

  • Leave (12:1). "Go out from your country and from your family and from your father's house, into the land to which I will be your guide" (BBE). This is the Genesis 2:24 move, leaving the old ground (Lesson 8). The land is not named yet.
  • Nation and name (12:2). "A great nation, blessing you and making your name great" (BBE).
  • The land goes to the seed (12:7). "I will give all this land to your seed" (BBE). The gift is to the seed, not to Abram.
  • The seed, put to his account (15:5-6). The stars cannot be numbered, "even so will your seed be," and "he had faith in the Lord, and it was put to his account as righteousness" (BBE). In the course's terms this is the filing: a claim entered before anything shows.
  • The land as heritage (15:7-8). "To give you this land for your heritage," and Abram asks, "how may I be certain that it will be mine?" (BBE).
  • The land with a date (15:13-18). The seed will be "living in a land which is not theirs" for four hundred years, Abram will go "to your fathers in peace", and "in the fourth generation they will come back here; for at present the sin of the Amorite is not full" (BBE). Then 15:18 words it in the completed tense: "To your seed have I given this land" (BBE).
  • The names (17:5, 17:8, 17:17-19). Abram becomes Abraham, and the land is "all the land of Canaan for an eternal heritage" (BBE 17:8). Abraham, "laughing, said in his heart, May a man a hundred years old have a child?" and the son is to be named Isaac (BBE).
  • The oath (22:16-17). "I have taken an oath by my name," and "your seed will take the land of those who are against them" (BBE).
  • The ground he holds (23:4, 23:19-20). "I am living among you as one from a strange country: give me some land here as my property, so that I may put my dead to rest" (BBE). Sarah is put to rest in the hollow rock at Machpelah, and "the field and the hollow rock were handed over to Abraham as his property."

What Hebrews says about it

  • 6:12-16. The BBE speaks of "those to whom God has given their heritage, because of their faith and their long waiting" (6:12). The oath quotes the seed promise, "make your numbers very great" (6:14). Verse 15 says the word "was put into effect," and verse 16 says "any argument is ended by the decision of the oath," which is court language.
  • 11:8-10. A place "to be given to him as a heritage," a wanderer "in the land of the agreement, as in a strange land, living in tents," and he "was looking for the strong town, whose builder and maker is God" (BBE).
  • 11:11-12 and 11:17-19. Sarah "had power to give birth, when she was very old," and "from one man, who was near to death, came children in number as the stars" (BBE). Isaac is got back "as if from death" (BBE).
  • 11:13-16. "Not having had the heritage; but having seen it with delight far away," and "their desire is for a better country... he has made ready a town for them" (BBE).
  • 11:39-40. "Not one of these got the good things of the agreement," and it "was not possible for them to become complete without us" (BBE).

Following the words

  • The heir and the land share a verb. In Hebrew the heir in Genesis 15:3-4 and the land in 15:7-8 are one verb, yarash (H3423). It is used again for the heir in 21:10 and for the seed that will "possess the gate" in 22:17. Genesis ties the son and the land together with one word. Hebrews 11:8 uses the Greek kleronomia (G2817), "inheritance."
  • Canaan, "humiliated." Strong's gives H3667 as "humiliated," from kana (H3665), "to be humbled, brought low." Put the meaning in place of the title, as Lesson 9 does, and the land is a ground named for being brought low, where "the Canaanites were still living in the land" (12:6, BBE). The Noah page has the first use of the name, "a servant of servants" (Genesis 9:25, BBE; Noah), and the -ites page reads the name by its story.
  • Isaac, "laughter." Strong's H3327 is "laughter (that is, mockery)," BDB "he laughs," from tsachaq (H6711). Abraham is "laughing" in 17:17, before the name is given in 17:19. The seed that arrives carries a name that fits the story of a child born after the time of life.
  • Three verbs for receiving. Epitygchano is "obtained" in 6:15 and 11:33. Anadechomai is "received" in 11:17. Komizo (G2865) is in 10:36, 11:19 and 11:39. Thayer glosses komizo as "to receive what was previously one's own, to get back," and the same verb is used for Isaac "got back" in 11:19 and for the promise not received in 11:39. Thayer's own entry for G1860 notes that 6:12, 6:15, 6:17 and 11:13, 11:39 "at first sight seem to be in conflict."

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. Genesis 1 builds the whole scene before the man stands in it, and the ground is named Eden, "pleasure" (H5731). The promise stories start from the other end.

What changed is the order. In Genesis 1 the scene is complete before the man appears. In the promise stories the claim is entered first (15:6), the seed comes inside his life, and the ground is named, dated and not yet in his hand. The date is given in the same chapter as the filing (15:6, 15:13-16).

Three ways to read Hebrews 11:39

The text supports more than one reading, and these are not exclusive. The page leaves them side by side.

  • As timing. Genesis 15:13-16 puts the land after Abraham's life and ties it to a date. Hebrews 11:39-40 then reports what Genesis already said. The text does not say why the land is dated, or whether the date belongs to the land or to the one holding the claim.
  • As a change of ground (the course's reading). The heritage is the ground the identity stands on, named "humiliated," and the claim is held "in tents" until it is taken (Heb 11:9). Hebrews itself restates the land as "the strong town" and "a better country" (11:10, 11:16), and calls the group "wanderers and not of the earth" (11:13). The text does not say that the town is an inner state. That step is the course's reading, and the same lines can be read as a place still to come.
  • As holding to the end. Hebrews 3:14 speaks of holding "the beginning of the assurance" to the end, with a word, hypostasis (G5287), that is also the "substance" of 11:1. Hebrews 10:36 says endurance comes before receiving, and Abraham is "waiting calmly for a long time" (6:15). The text does not say how long, and 11:39 says the group held and still did not receive.

Remember: the promise in Genesis is made of strands. The seed arrives in his lifetime, the land is dated beyond it, and the one ground he holds is a grave plot. Hebrews approves the claim, says the heritage was not held, and says the group was not yet complete. Which reading fits is for you to test against the text.

The limits of this reading:

  • Two sets of verses: the BBE of Hebrews 6:12 speaks of heritage as "given," and 11:13 and 11:39 say it was not. Thayer calls them a conflict "at first sight." This page sets them side by side and does not claim to settle it.
  • "The promise" in 11:39: that it is the land strand is an inference from the BBE's "heritage" in 11:8 and 11:13 and the town in 11:10 and 11:16. The verse says "the agreement" and does not list what it covers.
  • Hebrews 11:13: editions differ on the Greek verb for "received" there, so no weight is put on it.
  • The English carries some of the link. The BBE uses "heritage" for the Hebrew in Genesis 15:7 and the Greek in Hebrews 11:8. The Hebrew and Greek words are different, and the shared English is the translator's choice.
  • Counts: two concordances give different totals for yarash. The verses named were checked, and the totals were not reconciled.
  • Canaan: Strong's says "humiliated" and BDB's gloss is "lowland." The page uses Strong's, as the course method does, and "lowland" would also fit a ground brought low.
  • Hebrews 3:14 and 10:36 are read from other translations, because the BBE text was not fetched.
  • Not yet read: Genesis 13 and 21 in the BBE (Isaac's birth is taken from the Strong's list for Isaac), Acts 7:5 (Thayer cites it with kleronomia), the name Sarah, and what Isaac and Jacob hold in the land.
  • Verb numbers: epitygchano and anadechomai are named from the Greek forms in the concordance, and their Strong's numbers were not checked.
