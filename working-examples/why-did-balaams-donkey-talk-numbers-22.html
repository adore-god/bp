Why Did Balaam's Donkey Talk? Numbers 22

People search for Balaam's donkey because the story is so strange. A man hits his donkey three times, the donkey speaks, and an angel with a sword has been standing in the road the whole time. In this course it is not a story about an animal. It is one scene told to one reader, and the donkey is the part of you that carries you along. Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The short answer: the donkey sees the sword first because Balaam is looking at his purpose, not at the road. The old state, Moab (Of His Father), has a king named Waster, and a state that likes to waste things sends for Balaam (Not-of-the-people), a voice from outside the people, to curse a people the Judges have already blessed (Numbers 22:12). The donkey is the patient part of you that has carried you "all your life till this day" (22:30). When she turns aside three times, something is standing in the way. Once his eyes are opened, he can only say what is put in his mouth, and what comes out is blessing.

The meanings used:

  • Balaam (Bil'am, H1109): "not (of the) people, i.e. foreigner". A voice from outside the people.
  • Beor (H1160): "burning", from the root for burning.
  • Pethor (H6604): "soothsayer", the place of the diviners.
  • Balak (H1111): "waster", from a root meaning to lay waste. He is the king of Moab.
  • Moab (H4124): "from (her) father", so Of His Father. The state a person is issued from.
  • Israel (H3478): "God prevails" (Blue Letter Bible), and "God" is the Judges in this course, so Judges-Prevail. Strong's gives "he will rule as God". The people the later verses call "this people".
  • Zippor (H6834): "sparrow", the same word as the bird. Balak is the son of Zippor.
  • Egypt (Mitsrayim, H4714): "double straits", the two-fold form of matsor (H4693), as on the Jude 5 page.
  • Ass (athon, H860): a she-ass, "from her docility" and probably from the same word as "patience" (H386). So the patient one.
  • Angel (malak, H4397): "messenger", one sent as a deputy. It is the same word for the men Balak sends in 22:5.
  • The Lord (YHVH, H3068): the Existing One, you aware right now (Lesson 2). God (Elohim): the Judges (Lesson 3).

How to read it, step by step

  1. A blessing is already on the scene. Balak ("Waster") is king of Moab ("Of His Father"), the old state a person comes out of (Lesson 8). He sees the people, Israel ("Judges prevail"), camped right next to him and he is afraid, so the old state wants the new one wasted. But the Judges have already spoken: "they have my blessing" (22:12). That is the first word of Genesis 1:28, "God gave them his blessing". Balak can hire a curse, but there is nothing for the court to make real. Balaam says so himself later: "How may I put curses on him who is not cursed by God?" (23:8, BBE).
  2. The hired voice. Balaam ("Not-of-the-people") is the voice you hire to speak against the new identity, and he is not of the people. He comes from Pethor ("Soothsayer"), the place where words are used to call things in or out. Balak believes a spoken curse settles the scene: "he on whom you put your curse is cursed" (22:6). Part of that matches the course: a claim is made and the Judges rule to match it (Lesson 1). What Balak leaves out is that the Judges have already ruled on this people.
  3. Asking again. Balaam first says no (22:13). A second, higher-ranking group offers honour, and he says a house full of silver and gold would not move him (22:18). Then he tells them to wait the night "till I have knowledge what more the Lord has to say to me" (22:19, BBE). He is asking again for an answer he has already been given. He is told he may go, but "do only what I say to you" (22:20). He gets up early and goes, and then verse 22 says the Judges were angry that he went. That looks like a clash, and it is the same kind of pair as on Does the Bible Contradict Itself?. The messenger settles it: "your purpose is not pleasing to me" (22:32). The going was allowed. The purpose was the problem.
  4. The donkey sees first. The she-ass ("the patient one") is the part of you that carries you wherever you aim it. Genesis 1:28 puts the man over the creatures, and Balaam does rule her: he strikes her to get her back on his road. The way narrows three times: an open road with a field beside it, a narrow road between two walls, and a narrow place where there is no room to turn right or left (22:23-27). Each time she is the one who sees the sword and each time he hits her. By the third time he is "full of wrath" (22:27, BBE), and Beor means "burning", which is a picture only. When she speaks, he answers: "if only I had a sword in my hand I would put you to death" (22:29, BBE). Then his eyes are opened and the messenger has the sword, and says "I would certainly have put you to death and kept her safe" (22:33, BBE). The patient part of you was the one keeping you alive.
  5. A creature speaks. In Genesis 3:1 a creature speaks, "wiser than any beast of the field" (BBE), and the woman answers it. Here the Existing One gives the she-ass speech (22:28) and the man answers her. The snake's words lead toward the fruit. The donkey's words turn the man around. See The Serpent, Genesis 3.
  6. The eyes open, and the mouth is no longer his. The Existing One opens Balaam's eyes (22:31) and he goes face down. The messenger in the road is the same word, malak, as the men Balak sent in 22:5. One messenger comes from the old state to get a curse. One comes from the Existing One to block it, and the man has to answer one of them. Balaam owns up: "I did not see that you were in the way against me" (22:34, BBE). He is told "say only what I give you to say" (22:35), and by 22:38 he says, "Only what God puts into my mouth may I say" (BBE). In chapters 23 and 24 the hired curse turns into blessing, "a blessing which I have no power to take away" (23:20, BBE). Then he lifts his eyes and "he saw Israel there, with their tents in the order of their tribes" (24:2, BBE). The scene he could not see from the road is now the scene he sees, in order. The name fits: a voice that is not of the people ends up speaking the court's word over the people.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. Here the picture is the blessing, the creature under the man, and a sword set to keep a way:

PartGenesis 1 to 3Numbers 22
The first word"God gave them his blessing" (1:28)"they have my blessing" (22:12)
The creatureThe man is given rule over "every living thing moving on the earth" (1:28)Balaam rides the she-ass "all your life" and strikes her back onto his road (22:30, 23)
A creature speaksThe snake, "wiser than any beast of the field" (3:1)The she-ass is given speech by the Existing One (22:28)
The sword"a flaming sword turning every way to keep the way to the tree of life" (3:24)The messenger "in the road with his sword in his hand" (22:23)
The verdictBlessing first (1:28). The curse lands on the ground (3:17)No curse is made real. The mouth gives blessing (23:20)

What changed is that the man is no longer standing in the scene. He is on a road, riding the patient part of himself toward a curse on what is blessed. The court is the same. It does not make the curse real. It puts a sword in the way, opens his eyes, and gives him a mouth that can only bless.

Remember: Moab (Of His Father) is the old state, and its king, Waster, is the part of it that likes to waste what is new. He sends for Balaam (Not-of-the-people), a voice outside the people, to curse a people the Judges have already blessed, the same first word as Genesis 1:28. The donkey is the patient part of you that goes wherever you aim it, and she turns aside three times because a messenger of the Existing One stands in the road with a sword. When your eyes are open, the only word left in your mouth is the blessing, because the court makes real what it has already blessed.

Words checked

  • Balaam: Strong's says "probably from" two words, so "not of the people" carries the word "probably".
  • Pethor: Strong's says the word is of foreign origin. "Soothsayer" is the Blue Letter Bible gloss, so it is the lexicon's reading and not a certain root.
  • Messenger: in 22:5 the KJV prints "messengers" and the BBE prints "men". The Hebrew word is malak (H4397), the same word as "the angel of the Lord" in 22:22-35, according to the Strong's usage list.
  • Sword: chereb (H2719) is in Genesis 3:24 and in Numbers 22:23, 29 and 31. It is a common word, about 410 uses, so the link rests on the picture of a sword set to keep a way and not on a rare word.
  • The donkey's speech: the BBE and KJV say "ass". The ESV and NIV say "donkey". It is the same she-ass (athon).
  • Israel: the name is built from the Hebrew word for "God" in its short form (El, H410), not the plural Elohim. The page reads it as Judges-Prevail because the course reads "God" as the Judges.
  • Beor and wrath: Beor comes from the root for burning (H1197). I did not match that root to the Hebrew behind "full of wrath" in 22:27, so that link is only a picture.
