What Does "The Blood Is the Life" Mean? Blood and Eating Flesh in the Bible

Every living and moving thing will be food for you; I give them all to you as before I gave you all green things. But flesh with the life-blood in it you may not take for food. And for your blood, which is your life, will I take payment; from every beast I will take it, and from every man will I take payment for the blood of his brother-man. — Genesis 9:3-5, with the meanings put in

Most people read "the blood is the life" as a rule about ritual. The Bible builds it out of ordinary words that were set in Genesis 1 and 2. The word for "life" in this rule, nephesh, is the same word the Bible uses for the creatures of day five and six and for the man himself. The word for "flesh", basar, is the word of "one flesh" in Genesis 2:24. And the word for "blood", dam, is placed by Strong's next to the word for man and the word for ground. This page follows blood and flesh from Genesis 1 to Genesis 9 and then to Leviticus 17 and Deuteronomy 12. It also answers a plain question: if the original scene has no killing, why does the Bible talk about blood and eating flesh at all?

How this page reads the Bible. This course reads every story as having the same three parts: you, the identity you claim, and the judges that make it real, because Genesis 1:26 makes man the one made in the judges' own image (Lesson 6). A name tells you what the state is like before the story starts (Lesson 9). The Existing One (YHVH, "the Lord" in the BBE) is present awareness, and Elohim is the Judges (Lesson 3). Each step below is checked against the verse (BBE), Strong's and the Hebrew word by word. If this is your first page, The Big Idea is the place to start. This page sits beside the Sacrifice page, which follows the killing. This one follows the blood and the flesh.

The short answer: in the original scene nothing with blood is eaten, because the food is plants (Genesis 1:29). The creatures are called nephesh, living beings, and so is the man (1:24, 2:7). Blood is not named until Cain kills Abel and the blood is heard from the ground (4:10). Only after the flood is flesh given as food, and in the same breath the blood is fenced: "flesh with the life-blood in it you may not take for food" (9:4). The Hebrew puts three words side by side there: flesh (basar), its life (nephesh), its blood (dam). Deuteronomy 12:23 says it again: the blood is the life, and you may not eat the life with the flesh. In this reading, flesh is a state you take in until you are one flesh with it (2:24, the same word basar), and the blood is the live part of that state, the feeling and attention in it. You may take a state in. The live part is not yours to feed on, and it is heard.

In six lines:

  1. The original scene has no blood named and no flesh eaten. The food is plants, and the creatures are living beings (nephesh) after their sort (Genesis 1:20-30).
  2. The man is made from the ground and becomes a living being, the same word (2:7). He then names every living being (2:19). The woman is called the man's own bone and flesh, and then the two are "one flesh" (2:23-24).
  3. The first blood named is Abel's, and it is heard from the ground (4:10). Strong's puts blood, man and ground in one family of words (H1818, H120, H127).
  4. After the flood the court gives flesh as food and fences the life-blood. It asks for the blood back from "every beast" and "every man" (9:3-5).
  5. The Law says it again: the blood is the life, it is not eaten, it is drained out (Leviticus 17:11, Deuteronomy 12:23-24).
  6. In the court, the court does not eat flesh or drink blood, and blood on the hands is the charge (Psalm 50:13, Isaiah 1:15).

The course in short

  • The Bible is read as a courtroom inside the reader (Lesson 1). The Judges (Elohim) rule on the identity the reader holds, and the Existing One, the reader's own present awareness, holds it as I AM (Lesson 2).
  • Every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed (Lesson 5).
  • Everything comes back after its kind (Lesson 7): what is filed is what is enforced.
  • Names are identity codes (the names lesson), so every name below is read for its meaning.

The Genesis scene this is built from

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed, and the original scene has four parts: the ground, the seed, the stand and the time. The part of Genesis 1 and 2 that blood and flesh are built from is the living being: the creatures of day five and six, the man made from the ground, and the food that is given.

And the Existing One, the Judges, made man from the dust of the ground, breathing into him the breath of life: and man became a living soul. — Genesis 2:7, with the meanings put in
And the Judges said, See, I have given you every plant producing seed, on the face of all the earth, and every tree which has fruit producing seed: they will be for your food: — Genesis 1:29, with the meanings put in

The ground: the man is made from it (2:7). The seed, the kind: the plants "producing seed" are the food (1:29), and each creature comes after its sort (1:24). The stand: the man is a living being (nephesh), and so are the beasts the man names (2:19). The food: plants, for the man and for the creatures that have the breath of life (1:29-30). The verdict: the Judges look at everything and it is "very good" (1:31).

So in the original scene the man stands on the ground he was made from, among living beings of his own word, the food is plants, no blood is named, and the Judges say it is very good. Everything below is that scene changed.

The meanings used

  • Life, living being (nephesh, H5315): Strong's "properly, a breathing creature, i.e. animal of (abstractly) vitality". About 750 uses, and the count differs a little between lexicons. Its first uses are Genesis 1:20, 1:21, 1:24 and 1:30 (the creatures), then 2:7 (the man) and 2:19 (every living creature the man names), and then 9:4 and 9:5 (the blood). BDB says the phrase "living being" is used of animals in Genesis 1:20, 1:24, 1:30 and of the man in 2:7.
  • Blood (dam, H1818): Strong's "blood (as that which when shed causes death) of man or an animal". 360 uses. Strong's gives it as from H1826 and says "compare" H119. This is a cross-reference, not a proof of where the word comes from.
  • Flesh (basar, H1320): Strong's "flesh (from its freshness); by extension, body, person". 270 uses in 241 verses by the Hebrew text count. BDB lists the flesh of animals, the flesh of men, and "flesh for kindred, blood-relations", with Genesis 2:23-24 and 29:14 as examples of that last sense. The word is in Genesis 2:21, 2:23, 2:24 and 9:4, and in "all flesh" in the flood agreement (9:11-17).
  • Man, ruddy (adam, H120): Strong's "from H119; ruddy, i.e. a human being". Ground (adamah, H127): "from H119; soil (from its general redness)". 225 uses in 211 verses. H119 (adam, the verb): "to show blood (in the face), i.e. flush or turn rosy". It is used only 10 times, for things dyed or made red.
  • Cover (kaphar, H3722): see the Sacrifice page. In Leviticus 17:11 the Hebrew has "to cover over for your lives", with no word for sin.

Step by step, from Genesis 1 to the court

Nothing with blood is eaten in the original scene (Genesis 1:20-30). The living beings of the sea, the air and the land are called nephesh (1:20, 1:21, 1:24). The food for the man and for every creature "wherein there is life" is plants (1:29-30). So the Bible does not start with animals being killed. It starts with living beings of one kind of word, and food that has no blood in it.

The man is made a living being (Genesis 2:7, 2:19). The man is made from the ground and becomes a nephesh, the same word as the creatures. Then he names every living nephesh that is brought to him (2:19). The man and the creatures stand in one word.

Flesh is kin (Genesis 2:23-24). The man calls the woman his own bone and his own flesh, and the man leaves and is joined to her, and they are "one flesh". The word basar here means someone of your own kind, a blood-relation (BDB, with Genesis 29:14 as another example). To become one flesh with something is to take it as your own kind. This is the Genesis 2:24 pattern: leave one state, be joined to another (see the names lesson).

The first blood is a man's, and it is heard (Genesis 4:10). The ground was cursed (3:17), and then Cain puts Abel to death. The voice of Abel's blood is "crying to me from the ground". The blood is named as something that speaks and is heard, and it is named with the ground (adamah). In the Hebrew the word is in the plural, "bloods". This is the first time the Bible names blood, and it is already the life of someone being heard by the court.

Flesh is given, and the blood is fenced (Genesis 9:3-6). After the curse and the flood the court says "Every living and moving thing will be food for you ... as before I gave you all green things" (9:3). "As before" points back to the plants of 1:29. Then, in the next verse, "flesh with the life-blood in it you may not take for food" (9:4). The court asks for the blood back: "from every beast I will take it, and from every man" (9:5). The beasts are asked as well as the man, and 9:6 gives the reason for the man: he was made in the Judges' image (1:26). Both beast and man are nephesh, and both answer for the blood.

The Law says it again (Leviticus 17:11, Deuteronomy 12:23-24). "The life of the flesh is in its blood." In Deuteronomy it is plainer: the blood is the life, and you may not eat the life with the flesh. The blood is not kept in the body or on the plate. It is "drained out ... like water" (12:24). The rule of Genesis 9:4 is the same rule, said to people who now have a door and an altar (see the Sacrifice page).

In the court: it does not eat (Psalm 50:13, Isaiah 1). The court asks whether it takes the flesh of oxen for food or the blood of goats for drink (Psalm 50:13). In the Hebrew both words, basar and dam, are in that verse. And in Isaiah 1:15 the charge is "your hands are full of blood", which is Cain's "blood from your hand" (4:11) again.

Why is anything killed? Why is flesh eaten?

The text gives it in order. Not in the original scene: the food is plants, and no blood is named. Not at the first offerings: the Sacrifice page shows that Abel's and Noah's offerings name no killing. The first blood is a killed man's (4:10). Flesh is given as food only after the curse and the flood (9:3), and the court fences the blood in the same breath (9:4). So in the Bible's own order, eating flesh comes in with the world after the curse, and the rule about blood comes in with it.

What changed

  • The ground. In Genesis 2 the man is made from it. After Genesis 3 it is cursed, and in Genesis 4 it is where the blood is heard from.
  • The food. In Genesis 1 it is plants. After the flood it is flesh, with the blood kept back.
  • The blood. In Genesis 1 and 2 it is not named. It is first named as something that cries out, and then as the life that is not to be eaten.

In this course's reading the man is you, the creatures are the states of a kind that you rule, and flesh is what you take in of them. What moved is the ground. Once the ground is cursed, the man takes states in as food, and the court names the one thing that is not his to take with them.

What the names add

Strong's puts three words in one family. Adam (H120) is "ruddy, i.e. a human being", from H119, "to show blood (in the face)". Adamah (H127) is "soil (from its general redness)", from the same H119. And dam (H1818), blood, is compared with H119 as well. So the man, the ground he is made from, and the blood are all read by their redness. Genesis 4:10 has the blood and the ground in one sentence. The names add the people: Gotten (Cain) takes, Breath (Abel) is gone like breath and his blood is what remains, and Rest (Noah) is the one in whose time the flesh and the blood are first spoken of together. The word nephesh is also "a breathing creature" in Strong's, so the living being and the breath sit close together in the Genesis words. The names say who is standing there, and the story goes the way they say.

What it means for the reader

In this reading, to eat flesh is to take a state of a kind into yourself until you are one flesh with it. That is the same word and the same step as "one flesh" in Genesis 2:24. The blood is the live part of that state: the feeling and attention that is in it. The court's rule is that you may take a state in, but you may not take the live part of it as food. It is to be drained out, and it is heard (Genesis 4:10).

The next time you meet blood or eating flesh in the Bible, ask four things. What is the flesh, the state of a kind? What is its life, the blood in it? Who is taking it in, and into what are they becoming one flesh? And what does the court hear? The court looks at the one who takes in before it looks at what is taken in, as it did with Cain: "If you do well, will you not be lifted up?" (Genesis 4:7).

Remember: in the original scene nothing with blood is eaten, and the creatures and the man carry the one word for life. The first blood is a man's, and it is heard. Flesh is given as food only after the curse and the flood, and the blood is fenced in the same sentence. The blood is the life, and the life is not to be eaten with the flesh.
