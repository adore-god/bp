What Does "As the Mother Is, So Is Her Daughter" Mean? (Ezekiel 16:44-46)

Short answer: it means that whatever state you were born into, you give back, until you stand in something else. "As the mother is, so is her daughter" (Ezekiel 16:44) is the court's rule of after its kind said as a proverb. The mother is the state you came out of, and the daughter is the you that is holding it now. But the same chapter holds a second record, an agreement made with her while she was still lying in her blood (16:8), and that agreement is what gives her the way back (16:60).

People search this as "like mother, like daughter in the Bible", "you are the daughter of your mother" and "Ezekiel 16:45". It sits in the middle of a long chapter, at the verse where the chapter turns from what was done to why it was done. Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

New here? The course in four lines.

  • Every story has three parts: someone present (the Existing One, which is you, aware right now), the identity they claim (I AM), and the judges who make it real (Elohim). Lesson 1 and Lesson 2.
  • Rule One, after its kind: what is planted gives back only what it is. Lesson 7.
  • Rule Two, leave and join: step away from the old state and join the new one (Genesis 2:24). Lesson 8.
  • Names and places are codes: the meaning tells you what the state is like before the story starts. Lesson 9.

The same verses with the meanings put in (in bold; where each meaning comes from is in the words list further down):

And say, This is what the Lord (the Existing One) has said to Founded-peaceful: Your start and your birth was from the land of the Humiliated; a Sayer was your father and your mother was a child of Terror. (16:3) See, in every common saying about you it will be said, As the mother is, so is her daughter. (16:44) You are the daughter of your mother whose soul is turned in disgust from her husband and her children; and you are the sister of your sisters who were turned in disgust from their husbands and their children: your mother was a child of Terror and your father a Sayer. (16:45) Your older (great) sister is Watch-station, living at your left (dark, north) hand, she and her daughters: and your younger (small) sister, living at your right (south) hand, is Burning and her daughters. (16:46)

Where this sits in the story

This is said far from Jerusalem. The prophet is Ezekiel, "God strengthens", and he is among the prisoners by the river Chebar, "far-off", in the land of the Chaldaeans (Ezekiel 1:1-3, BBE). So the people hearing it are already far from the city. The chapter tells the whole story of Jerusalem, "founded-peaceful", in one go: born low (16:3), given an agreement (16:8), turning to other lovers, even to Chaldaea, the far land they are standing in now (16:29), and then the way back (16:55, 16:60).

Who is in the scene

In this course the whole scene is read as taking place inside you, in the court of Lesson 1. The mother, the daughter and the sisters are states, not outside people.

The story, step by step

  1. The saying is the court's rule. "In every common saying about you it will be said, As the mother is, so is her daughter" (16:44, BBE). The word for saying is mashal, which means both "to liken" and "a proverb". In this course the court makes real what is actually held, and each thing gives back its own kind (Lesson 7). Adam "had a son like himself, after his image" (Genesis 5:3, BBE) is the same rule. The saying says it of a family: the daughter gives back the mother. Ezekiel 16:44 does not use the Genesis 1:11 phrase, so the link is the rule, not the word.
  2. Where the line starts (16:3). "Your start and your birth was from the land of the Canaanite; an Amorite was your father and your mother was a Hittite." Names are identity codes (Lesson 9). Canaan is "humiliated", the Amorite is "a sayer", and the Hittite comes from Cheth, "terror". So the state called Founded-peaceful was dug out of low ground, with a prominent saying for a father and terror for a mother. All three are one line: Genesis 10:15-16 puts Canaan's sons Heth and the Amorite together, and Canaan is the one the curse names (9:25). The ending -ite means "belonging to" a name, so the Hittite belongs to Terror and the Amorite to the Sayer (see what Jew, Gentile and the -ites mean).
  3. What the mother did (16:45). She "turned in disgust from her husband and her children". The word is gaal, "to detest, to reject". She turns from the union she was joined to, and from what that union would bring forth. The daughter does the same: "The untrue wife who takes strange lovers in place of her husband!" (16:32). The word is also in Leviticus 26, where the covenant is set out, and there the Existing One says the opposite: "my soul will not be turned away from you in disgust" (26:11), and "I will not let them go, or be turned away from them... my agreement with them will not be broken" (26:44, BBE). So the mother does the one thing the Existing One refuses to do back.
  4. The rule she breaks (Genesis 2:24). "For this cause will a man go away from his father and his mother and be joined to his wife; and they will be one flesh" (Genesis 2:24, BBE). The rule has two halves: leave the father and mother, and be joined (Lesson 8). In Ezekiel 16 both are undone. She is "the daughter of your mother", so she has not left the line. And the agreement of 16:8 ends "you became mine", which is the joined half, so "turned in disgust from her husband" is the joining undone. Daughters are the same rule one step on. Each state has its own: "Samaria and her daughters", "Sodom and her daughters", "you and your daughters" (16:46, 16:55). A daughter is what a state gives rise to, and one that has not left its mother's state becomes that state in turn. Judges 3:5-6 shows the same rule: Prevails lives among these kinds, takes their daughters as wives and serves their Judges (the -ites page). And Lot's daughters carry their father's seed and name the sons for it (Genesis 19:32-38, the Sodom page).
  5. The sisters (16:46). "Your older sister is Samaria, living at your left hand... and your younger sister, living at your right hand, is Sodom." Older is "great", younger is "small", left is "dark, the north" and right is "the south". So a great sister stands on the dark side, a small one on the right, and Jerusalem stands between them. Samaria is "watch-station", from shamar, the word Genesis 2:15 uses of the man's work in the garden. Sodom is "burning". Genesis 13:10 says that ground "was like the garden of the Lord" before it was destroyed, and Ezekiel 16:49 says what stood on it: "pride, a full measure of food, and the comforts of wealth in peace... and she gave no help to the poor or to those in need." They are two more states of the same kind. For what happened in Sodom itself, see what was the sin of Sodom and Gomorrah.
  6. The court's decision (16:47-52). "You have gone deeper in evil than they in all your ways" (16:47). Then the court language: "you have given the decision for your sisters; through your sins, which are more disgusting than theirs, they are more upright than you" (16:52). In Lesson 1 the court makes real what is actually offered. She offered a record worse than her sisters', so the decision comes out in their favour. The saying has not changed. A state that files like its mother gets what its mother gets.
  7. The second record (16:6-8 and 16:53-63). The court has two records on her. The first is the line: Canaan, Hittite, Amorite. The second is the agreement. The Existing One passes by, sees her stretched out in her blood, says "have life" (16:6), and gives "my oath" and "an agreement" (16:8). That is the other way of being made: by the word, not by the line (see the Son of David page and Genesis 5:1-3). Then the chapter turns on one verb, zakar, "to remember". She "had no memory of your early days" (16:22) and "has not kept in mind the days when you were young" (16:43). The Existing One answers: "I will keep in mind the agreement made with you in the days when you were young" (16:60). In Lesson 2, what the Existing One presents is what the Judges must enforce, so the agreement stays on file even while she forgets it. The same verb has the same agreement as its object in Leviticus 26:42 and 26:45. So the saying is what comes back while she holds the line, and the agreement is what is still waiting. The chapter ends with all three going "back to their first condition" (16:55), and with the sisters given to her as daughters "but not by your agreement" (16:61). The Hebrew for first condition is qadmah, "priority in time", a former state. It is never used in Genesis, so the link to the Genesis 1 picture is a matching picture, not a word link.

What this means for you

You are not only the line you came from. The saying is true of the line: the state you were born into gives itself back to you while you keep holding it, and the court does not argue with that.

But the chapter shows a second record. The agreement is made while she is still in her blood, before she has done anything (16:6-8), so it does not depend on what she did afterward. She forgets it, breaks it, and the Existing One still keeps it in mind (16:60). The Existing One is you, aware right now. So the identity said over you at your lowest is still on file, and it is the one the Judges are bound to enforce when you stand in it.

The way back is not to fight the line. It is to stop turning from the union and stand in the agreement. Then what comes back to you follows what you now stand in, not what your mother stood in.

The image, in the same chapter

One word in the middle of the chapter goes straight back to Genesis 1. "And you took the fair jewels, my silver and gold which I had given to you, and made for yourself male images" (16:17, BBE). The word for images is tselem (H6754), the same word as "Let us make man in our image, like us" (Genesis 1:26, BBE). In Genesis the image is given and the son is born in it. Here what was given is turned into images she makes for herself, and she offers them the bread and the children (16:19-20). In the court's terms she files identities of her own, out of what she was given. It is the mother's turn again, from the join to the images.

Three questions for this story

  1. What is the ground, and what does its name mean? The land of the Canaanite, Humiliated, with Jerusalem, Founded-peaceful, founded on it.
  2. Who is standing on it, and what do their names mean? A child of Terror and a Sayer's daughter, with Watch-station and Burning beside her.
  3. What has changed from the Genesis 1 picture? The union is turned from and its fruit is given to images the state made for itself, until the agreement made in her youth is kept in mind.

Remember: the saying is true of the line, and the line is not the only record. The court gives back the state you hold, and the Existing One keeps in mind the agreement made over you. The way back is to stand in the agreement.

Words and sources

  • Jerusalem (H3389): "founded peaceful", with Strong's "probably 'foundation of peace'" (as on Lesson 9).
  • Canaan (H3667): "humiliated", from kana (H3665), in Strong's. Blue Letter Bible's outline gives "lowland". Canaanite is H3669.
  • Amorite (H567): BDB gives "a sayer", and the course reads it as Sayer. In Strong's it is "probably a patronymic from an unused name derived from 559 in the sense of publicity, i.e. prominence; thus, a mountaineer". It is used 87 times, and Ezekiel 16:3 and 16:45 are two of them.
  • Hittite (H2850): "a Chittite, or descendant of Cheth". Heth (H2845): "terror", from chathath, "an aboriginal Canaanite" in Strong's. Heth is used 14 times, and Genesis 10:15 is the first.
  • Ezekiel and Chebar (Ezekiel 1:1-3): Ezekiel is yechezqel (H3168), "God will strengthen". Chebar is kebar (H3529), "length" in Strong's and "far-off" in Blue Letter Bible, a river in the land of the Chaldaeans where the prisoners were.
  • Samaria (H8111): "watch-station", from the active participle of shamar (H8104), in Strong's. "Watch mountain" in Blue Letter Bible. Shamar is used in Genesis 2:15 and Genesis 3:24.
  • Older, younger, left, right (16:46): older is gadol, "great" (H1419); younger is qatan, "small" (H6996); left is semol, "dark (as enveloped), i.e. the north" (H8040); right is yamin, "the right hand or side... the south" (H3225). Gadol and qatan are common words, so no link to Genesis 1:16 (the great and small lights) is claimed.
  • Sodom (H5467): "Burning" in Blue Letter Bible, which is how the course reads it. In Strong's, "burnt (i.e. volcanic or bituminous) district", from a root meaning to scorch. It is used 39 times, including Genesis 13:10 and Ezekiel 16:46, 16:48, 16:49, 16:53, 16:55 and 16:56. The Hebrew behind "in peace" in 16:49 is shalvah (H7962, "ease") and sheqet (H8252, "to repose").
  • Saying, proverb (mashal, H4911): to liken, and to use a proverb or speak a parable. In Ezekiel it is in 12:23, 16:44 (twice), 17:2, 18:2, 18:3, 20:49 and 24:3.
  • Turned in disgust (gaal, H1602): "to detest; by implication, to reject". Ten uses: Leviticus 26:11, 15, 30, 43 and 44, 2 Samuel 1:21, Job 21:10, Jeremiah 14:19 and Ezekiel 16:45 (twice). BDB lists the Lord and his people as the subject in Leviticus 26:11, 30 and 44, and the people as the subject, with the Lord's statutes as the object, in Leviticus 26:15 and 43.
  • Remember (zakar, H2142): "to remember". In Ezekiel 16 it is in 16:22, 16:43, 16:60, 16:61 and 16:63, and BDB also lists the court remembering its covenant in Leviticus 26:42 and 26:45. In 16:60 the Hebrew also has the word for "I" (ani) set in.
  • First condition, former state (qadmah, H6927): "priority (in time)", "former (old) estate" in Strong's. Six uses in four verses: Psalm 129:6, Isaiah 23:7, Ezekiel 16:55 (three times) and Ezekiel 36:11. The word for "go back" in 16:55 is shuv (H7725).
  • Start, origin (mekorah, H4351): "origin (as if a mine)". Three uses: Ezekiel 16:3, 21:30 and 29:14.
  • Images (tselem, H6754): "a phantom, i.e. (figuratively) illusion, resemblance; hence, a representative figure". Seventeen uses, including Genesis 1:26, 1:27, 5:3, 9:6 and Ezekiel 16:17. The BBE has "male images" in 16:17.
  • The Lord in Ezekiel 16:3 is two Hebrew words, adonai (H136) and YHVH written as H3069 (yehovih), which Blue Letter Bible gives as a variation of H3068 used after H136. The BBE gives both as "the Lord". The page reads the YHVH word as the Existing One, as in Lesson 2.
  • Mother (H517) and daughter (H1323) in 16:44.
  • Checked: the BBE of Ezekiel 16 in full, the BBE of Ezekiel 1:1-3 and Genesis 2:24, the BBE of Leviticus 26 in full, and the BBE of Genesis 9, 10 and 13, with the BBE of Genesis 1:11, 1:26, 2:8, 2:24 and 5:3 as already quoted on the course pages.   2. Read Genesis 10:6 and 10:15-16 and see Heth and the Amorite in Canaan's line.
  3. Look up gaal (H1602) and see that two of its ten uses are in 16:45.
  4. Look up tselem (H6754) and see Genesis 1:26 and Ezekiel 16:17 on the same list.
  5. Read 16:8, 16:60 and 16:61 beside 16:44 and see the two records.

Common questions

What does 'As the mother is, so is her daughter' mean in Ezekiel 16:44?

It is a likeness said as a saying (mashal, H4911). Verse 45 gives the likeness: the mother turned in disgust from her husband and her children, and so did the daughter and her sisters. In this course it is the court's rule of after its kind (Genesis 1:11) said as a proverb: the state you hold gives itself back. The same chapter also keeps a second record, the agreement made when she was young (16:8, 16:60).

Who are Jerusalem's mother and father in Ezekiel 16:3 and 16:45?

A Hittite mother and an Amorite father. Genesis 10:15-16 lists Heth and the Amorite among the sons of Canaan. Heth (H2845) means 'terror', and the Amorite (H567) is 'a sayer' in BDB, which is how the course reads it, and 'a mountaineer' in Strong's.

Why does Ezekiel 16 say Jerusalem was born in the land of the Canaanite?

Ezekiel 16:3 gives the start of Jerusalem's line as the land of Canaan, which Strong's reads as 'humiliated' (H3667). The ground the state was founded on was already named before the state was founded on it.

Who are the sisters Samaria and Sodom in Ezekiel 16:46?

Samaria is the older sister at the left hand and Sodom the younger sister at the right hand. Older is gadol, 'great' (H1419), younger is qatan, 'small' (H6996), left is 'dark, the north' (H8040) and right is 'the south' (H3225). Samaria (H8111) is 'watch-station', from shamar (H8104). Sodom (H5467) is 'burning' ('burnt district' in Strong's).

What was the sin of Sodom in Ezekiel 16:49?

In the BBE: pride, a full measure of food, and the comforts of wealth in peace, with no help given to the poor or those in need. The Hebrew behind 'in peace' is shalvah (H7962), 'ease', and sheqet (H8252), 'to repose'. Genesis 13:10 describes the same ground as well watered everywhere, like the garden of the Lord, before it was destroyed. See the Sodom page for Genesis 13, 18 and 19.

What does the Hebrew behind 'turned in disgust' mean?

It is gaal (H1602), 'to detest; by implication, to reject'. It is used ten times in the Bible, and two of them are in Ezekiel 16:45. Five are in Leviticus 26, where the Existing One promises its soul will not be turned away from them (26:11, 26:44).

Is 'like mother, like daughter' the last word in Ezekiel 16?

No. The chapter ends with the agreement made in the days when she was young being kept in mind (16:60), and the sisters being given to her for daughters 'but not by your agreement' (16:61). She has no memory of her youth (16:22, 16:43), and the court remembers it for her, with the same verb, zakar (H2142). The line gives the saying and the agreement gives the end.
