What Does "Sacrifice" Mean? Leviticus 1 and the Offering Brought Near

When anyone of you makes an offering to the Lord [the Existing One], you are to take it from the cattle, from the herd or from the flock. If the offering is a burned offering of the herd, let him give a male without a mark: he is to give it at the door of the Tent of meeting so that he may be pleasing to the Lord [the Existing One]. And he is to put his hand on the head of the burned offering and it will be taken for him, to take away his sin. — Leviticus 1:2-4 (BBE)

Most people picture a sacrifice as giving something up: an animal is killed and a debt is paid. In Leviticus 1 the Hebrew word for the offering is korban, "something brought near", and the verb is qarab, "bring near". The one who brings it is adam, "man". And the last words of 1:4 in the BBE, "to take away his sin", have no word for sin in the Hebrew. Then other places say the Existing One is fed up with the burnt offerings and sacrifices (Isaiah 1, Amos 5, Psalm 50, Hosea 6, Jeremiah 7). This page reads both together, word by word, and asks what is being brought near and who it is brought to.

How this page reads the Bible. This course reads every story as having the same three parts: you, the identity you claim, and the judges that make it real, because Genesis 1:26 makes man the one made in the judges' own image (Lesson 6). A name tells you what the state is like before the story starts (Lesson 9). The Existing One (YHVH, "the Lord" in the BBE) is present awareness, and Elohim is the Judges (Lesson 3). Each step below is checked against the verse (BBE), Strong's and the Hebrew or Greek word by word, and where a link rests only on a picture the page says so. If this is your first page, The Big Idea is the place to start.

The short answer: in Leviticus 1 a man brings something near, from the livestock he is set over in Genesis 1, to the face of the Existing One at the Tent of the Appointed Time. It goes up, it is accepted, and it covers him over. The same verb is used for the sons of Aaron, who bring near fire that was not ordered, and they die. Later, in the court, Isaiah, Amos, Psalm 50 and Hosea say the offerings are refused, and they use the language of Leviticus 1 to say so: coming before the face, and acceptance. What the court asks for in their place is right judgment, mercy, praise and the Judges' knowledge. The course reads the offering as a state of a kind that you hold, brought near to the Judges' face to be accepted. What the text puts at stake is what is brought, and not the animal.

In six lines:

  1. The call comes from the Tent of the Appointed Time, and a man brings near something brought near, from the livestock (Leviticus 1:1-2).
  2. It is brought before the face of the Existing One, the hand is laid on its head, and it is accepted for him, "to cover over him" (1:3-4).
  3. It goes up in the fire, and the priests bring near the blood (1:5-9).
  4. The same verb is used for fire that was not ordered, and the fire from before the Existing One burns the sons of Aaron (10:1-3).
  5. In the court the offerings are refused: "I will not accept" (Amos 5:22), "a weariness to me" (Isaiah 1:11), "I take no pleasure ... let us have an argument together" (Isaiah 1:11, 1:18).
  6. What is asked in their place is right judgment, mercy, praise, the knowledge of the Judges and giving ear to the voice (Isaiah 1:17, Hosea 6:6, Psalm 50:14, Jeremiah 7:23).

The course in short

  • The Bible is read as a courtroom inside the reader (Lesson 1). The Judges (Elohim) rule on the identity the reader holds, and the Existing One, the reader's own present awareness, holds it as I AM (Lesson 2).
  • Every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed (Lesson 5).
  • Everything comes back after its kind (Lesson 7): what is filed is what is enforced.
  • Names are identity codes (the names lesson), so every name below is read for its meaning.

The Genesis scene this is built from

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed, and the original scene has four parts: the ground, the seed, the stand and the time. The part of Genesis 1 that Leviticus 1 is built from is day six, the livestock and the man.

And the Judges said, Let the earth give birth to all sorts of living things, livestock and all things moving on the earth, and beasts of the earth after their sort: and it was so. And the Judges made the beast of the earth after its sort, and the livestock after their sort, and everything moving on the face of the earth after its sort: and the Judges saw that it was good. And the Judges said, Let us make man in our image, like us: and let him have rule over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the air and over the livestock and over all the earth and over every living thing which goes flat on the earth. — Genesis 1:24-26, with the meanings put in
  • The ground: "the earth" brings the living things out (1:24).
  • The seed, the kind: each creature comes "after its sort", and the livestock "after their sort" (1:24-25). The word for livestock is behemah.
  • The stand: the man (adam) is made "like us" and set over the livestock to rule them (1:26).
  • The time: the lights are for "marking the changes of the year", moed, the fixed times (1:14, BBE).
  • The verdict: the Judges see it, and "it was good" (1:25). The verdict comes at once.

So in the original scene the man stands on named ground over the livestock, each kind after its kind, and the Judges say it is good. Leviticus 1 has the same cast, and the next two sections show what is changed.

The meanings used

  • Offering, something brought near (korban, H7133): Strong's "something brought near the altar, i.e. a sacrificial present", from qarab. 82 uses in 78 verses. The KJV renders it "offering" 68 times, "oblation" 12, "offered" once and "sacrifice" once. Its first use is Leviticus 1:2.
  • Bring near (qarab, H7126): "to approach (causatively, bring near) for whatever purpose". 283 uses in 263 verses. In Leviticus 1:2 the form is yaqriv, "he brings near", and in 1:5 vehiqrivu, "and they shall bring near". The KJV renders it "offer" 95 times, "near" 58, "bring" 58, "nigh" 18 and "come" 12.
  • Man (adam, H120): the word of Genesis 1:26. The BBE says "anyone" in Leviticus 1:2.
  • Livestock (behemah, H929): the word of Genesis 1:24 and 1:26, and of "from the livestock" in Leviticus 1:2. The BBE says "cattle" in Genesis 1:24-25.
  • Burnt offering, ascending (olah, H5930): Strong's "a step or stairs, as ascending; usually a holocaust (as going up in smoke)", the feminine participle of alah (H5927). 289 uses.
  • Accept (ratsah, H7521): the verb of venirtsah in Leviticus 1:4 and of lo erTzeh in Amos 5:22.
  • Cover (kaphar, H3722): "to cover (specifically with bitumen); figuratively to expiate or condone, to placate or cancel". 104 uses.
  • Sacrifice, slaughter (zebach, H2077): "slaughter of an animal, i.e. flesh; by implication a sacrifice". 162 uses, first in Genesis 31:54. English "sacrifice" mostly translates this word, and not korban.
  • Before the face of (lifnei, H6440): in Leviticus 1:3 and 1:5, and panai, "my face", in Isaiah 1:12.
  • Bring near, draw near (nagash, H5066): the verb of Amos 5:25. Reason, argue (yakach, H3198): Isaiah 1:18 and Psalm 50:8. Testify against (ud, H5749): Psalm 50:7. Thanksgiving (todah, H8426): Psalm 50:14. Worthlessness (shav, H7723): Isaiah 1:13.
  • The names: Moses (H4872) is "drawn out". Aaron is "Light-bringer", as on the Aaron page, and his sons Nadab is "Generous" and Abihu "He-is-my-father". Israel (H3478) is "God prevails", written here as "He-prevails". The Tent of meeting is ohel moed, the "Tent of the Appointed Time" (moed, H4150, first used in Genesis 1:14).
  • YHVH (H3068): the Existing One, the BBE's "the Lord". Elohim (H430): the Judges. The names of Isaiah, Amos and Hosea were not looked up for this page.

In this course's reading, where the many voices are gathered under one present awareness (see the court language), all of them are in you. The text names a man, a priest, an animal, a tent, a fire and a court. Reading them as states in you is the course's reading, and it is marked as that below.

How to read it, step by step

  1. The call comes from a place named for a time (Leviticus 1:1). The Hebrew is vayyiqra el Moshe vayedabber YHVH eilav me'ohel moed: "and he called to Moses, and the Existing One spoke to him from the Tent of the Appointed Time". Moses is "drawn out", the one the call reaches. Moed is the word of Genesis 1:14, where the lights are set "for signs, and for marking the changes of the year" (BBE), and it is the first thing the chapter gives: a time. In Lesson 5 the original scene has a ground, a seed, a stand and a time, and here the tent that carries the time in its name is where the offering is taught.
  2. Man brings near, and the word is one root three times (1:2). Adam ki yaqriv mikkem korban. The man is adam, the verb is qarab and the thing is korban, from the same root. Nothing in the verse is given up or paid. A man brings something near. That is the meaning of the word, and it is why "sacrifice" is the wrong picture to start from: the English word means a killing, and the word here means a coming near.
  3. From the livestock he is set over (1:2). Min-habbehemah: "from the livestock". The behemah are the land animals of Genesis 1:24, "after its sort", and in 1:26 adam is made to have rule over them. So the man who rules the livestock in Genesis 1 is the man who brings them near in Leviticus 1, and the two chapters use one word. In the course's reading the livestock are the states of a kind that the man holds: each kind gives back what it is (Lesson 7), and what the man brings near is one of those. The text itself says only that the thing is taken from the livestock. The BBE's "male without a mark" was not read in the Hebrew.
  4. Before the face, at the door of the Tent (1:3). Lifnei YHVH is "before the face of the Existing One". In Genesis 1:4 the Judges "saw the light, that it was good", and the first thing the court does with what is put before it is see it. The offering is brought to where it will be seen. The BBE's "so that he may be pleasing to the Lord" renders lirtsono, which Bible Hub glosses "of his own free will", so what is meant by it is an open point (below).
  5. The hand on the head, accepted, covered over (1:4). The Hebrew is vesamak yado al rosh haolah venirtsah lo lekapper alav: "and he shall lay his hand on the head of the ascending offering, and it shall be accepted for him, to cover over him". The man puts his hand on it, which in this reading is the man owning what he brings. Then venirtsah, "it shall be accepted", from ratsah. That is the court's answer, as "good" was in Genesis 1:4: the Judges see and give a verdict. Then lekapper alav, "to cover over him", from kaphar, "to cover". The BBE's "to take away his sin" has no word for sin, and what the Hebrew has is a covering on him.
  6. The man slaughters, and the priests bring near the blood (1:5). Veshachat is active, "and he shall slaughter", and the BBE makes it passive ("is to be put to death"). Then vehiqrivu bnei Aharon hakohanim et-hadam: "and the sons of Aaron the priests shall bring near the blood". The verb qarab is used again, for the blood, and the ones who carry it are the sons of Light-bringer, the identity that does the court's work (the Aaron page).
  7. It goes up (1:9). The offering is an olah, "ascending", from alah, "to go up". The fire is on the altar, and the BBE gives "a sweet smell to the Lord" for reach nichoach, which Bible Hub glosses "an aroma of pleasing". What is brought near goes up to the one before whose face it was brought. The picture is of a thing moving toward the court, and not of a thing destroyed.
  8. The same verb for the wrong fire (10:1-3). Vayyaqrivu lifnei YHVH esh zarah asher lo tsivvah otam: "and they brought near before the face of the Existing One strange fire, which he had not ordered them". The verb is qarab, the verb of Leviticus 1:2 and 1:5. The place is the same, before the face. What differs is one clause: "which he had not ordered them". The text says that is what made it wrong. And the fire "from before the Existing One" burns them, the same court-fire that took the offering. Moses then says "I will be holy in the eyes of all those who come near to me" (10:3, BBE). Whether the Hebrew behind "come near" is the same root was not read. The Aaron page reads this fire as the court accepting what was ordered and burning what was not.
  9. The order to divide comes straight after (10:9-10). The BBE says "make a division between the holy and the common, and between the unclean and the clean". The BBE uses the same phrase, "make a division between", in Genesis 1:4, and the verb is badal (the Aaron page, step on the court's first act). So the order that follows the wrong fire is the court's first act, handed to the priest. What he is to divide is what is holy from what is common, and the text does not say that Nadab and Abihu failed to divide. It says what Aaron is to do afterwards.
  10. In the court: "a weariness to me" (Isaiah 1:11-13). The Hebrew of 1:11 is lamah li rov zivcheikhem: "why to me the many of your slaughters?" and the burnt offerings of rams, of which he says he has had enough. 1:12 is ki tavo'u lera'ot panai: "when you come to appear before my face". That is the coming of Leviticus 1:3, before the face, and the next words are a question: "who sought this from your hand, to trample my courts?" 1:13 gives minchat-shav, "an offering of worthlessness". The BBE has "false offerings". So the same coming is refused, and the thing named is shav, worthlessness. The court does not say the bringing is the wrong. It says what has been brought is worth nothing.
  11. What the hands are full of (Isaiah 1:15-18). "your hands are full of blood" (BBE). The blood on the altar was brought near by the priests (Leviticus 1:5), and here the blood is on the hands of the ones who come. Then the order of what is to be brought: "Take pleasure in well-doing; let your ways be upright, ... give a right decision for the child who has no father, see to the cause of the widow." Then 1:18, venivvakhechah, from yakach, "let us reason together", the word of a hearing. The offerings are refused, and in their place the Existing One offers a case to be argued.
  12. "I will not accept" (Amos 5:22). The Hebrew is olot uminchoteichem lo erTzeh. The verb is ratsah, "accept", the verb of venirtsah in Leviticus 1:4. Leviticus 1:4 says it shall be accepted, and Amos 5:22 says "I will not accept". The same verb, the opposite answer, from the same court. Then 5:24: "let the right go rolling on like waters, and righteousness like an ever-flowing stream". Then 5:25: hazzevachim uminchah higgashtem li bammidbar arba'im shanah, "did you bring near sacrifices and offering to me in the wilderness forty years?" The verb is nagash, another word for bringing near, and the question is asked of He-prevails. The text does not give the answer here.
  13. "The cattle on a thousand hills are mine" (Psalm 50:7-15). The court words are in the Hebrew: ve'a'idah bach (H5749), "I will testify against you", and okhichecha, from yakach, the root of Isaiah 1:18. The speaker is the Judges (Elohim Elohecha), and the first thing said is that the offerings are not the charge (50:8). Then: "every beast of the woodland is mine, and the cattle on a thousand hills" (50:10). The behemah of Genesis 1 and Leviticus 1 are his already. And he does not eat: "Am I to take the flesh of the ox for my food?" (50:13). What he asks for is zevach lElohim todah: "make a sacrifice to the Judges of thanksgiving" (50:14). The word zebach stays, and what is offered becomes thanksgiving.
  14. Mercy and the knowledge of the Judges (Hosea 6:6). Ki chesed chafatsti velo zavach, vedaat Elohim me'olot: "for mercy I delight, and not sacrifice, and the knowledge of Elohim above burnt offerings". The BBE says "offerings" for zavach. The verse sets two things above the sacrifices: mercy, and the knowledge of the Judges. In this course the second is knowing the court that makes a claim real.
  15. "I said nothing about burned offerings" (Jeremiah 7:22-23). The Hebrew of 7:22 is lo dibbarti ... velo tsivvitim ... al divrei olah vazavach: "I did not speak ... nor command them ... concerning the matters of burnt offering and sacrifice". The tension is plain. Leviticus 1:1-2 has the Existing One speaking from the Tent about the offering, and Jeremiah 7:22 has him saying he did not speak of it. This page does not settle it. 7:22 is tied to "the day when I took them out of Egypt", and what 7:23 puts first is "give ear to my voice" (BBE; the Hebrew of 7:23 was not read), with "I will be your God, and you will be my people". It fits Leviticus 10:1: what is brought near has to be what was ordered, and what was ordered first is the voice.

What changed

The cast is the same: the man, the livestock, the ground, a time, and the Judges who give the verdict. Three things change.

  1. The man's work. In Genesis 1 he rules the livestock. In Leviticus 1 he brings one near. The word for bringing near, qarab, is built into the name of the offering, korban.
  2. The verdict. In Genesis 1 the Judges say "good" as soon as they see. In Leviticus 1 the verdict is "accepted" (ratsah), and it is given when the thing is brought before the face. It can be given the other way. The same verb is used in Amos 5:22: "I will not accept".
  3. What the livestock are. In Genesis 1 they are the kind that the man rules. In the court passages they are not what the Judges want: "Am I to take the flesh of the ox for my food?" (Psalm 50:13), and what is asked for in their place is thanksgiving, mercy, right judgment and the Judges' knowledge (Psalm 50:14, Hosea 6:6, Isaiah 1:17).

In this course's reading the man is you, the livestock are the states of a kind that you rule, and in Leviticus you bring one of them to the Judges' face to have the verdict given on it. The text says only that the man brings the livestock near and that it is accepted or refused. The reading of the livestock as states of a kind is the course's.

Which part moved?

In the course's reading what moved is the verdict. In Genesis 1 the verdict follows the making at once. In Leviticus 1 the man brings the thing near and the verdict follows the bringing, and in the court passages the verdict is refused until what is brought is what was ordered. Three questions can be asked of the scene.

  1. What is the ground, and what does its name mean? The Tent of the Appointed Time, a place named for a time.
  2. Who is standing on it, and what do their names mean? Adam, who brings near, and Moses, "drawn out", to whom the call comes. Light-bringer and his sons carry the blood and the fire.
  3. What has changed from the Genesis 1 picture? The man who ruled the livestock brings one near, and the Judges' "good" has become "accepted" or "I will not accept".

What the names add

Moses is "drawn out", the one the call reaches and who passes it on. Light-bringer carries the blood and the fire, and his sons Generous and He-is-my-father bring near fire of their own. Generous is the one who puts in his own offering, and He-is-my-father stays tied to the father, as on the Aaron page. He-prevails is the people to whom the court speaks in Amos and Psalm 50: the one who prevails is asked whether he brought near sacrifices for forty years. The names do not say what the answer is. They say who is standing at the Tent.

What it means for the reader

In this reading to sacrifice is to bring a state of a kind near, to the face of your own present awareness, to be seen by the court and accepted. Nothing is given up. What you hold is brought near, put under the hand, and it goes up and covers you over. The same movement is wrong when the thing brought near is not what was ordered (Leviticus 10:1), when it comes with blood on the hands (Isaiah 1:15), when the court says it will not accept it (Amos 5:22), and when it is meant as food for the Judges (Psalm 50:13).

What the court asks for is the other side. Right judgment for the one with no father and the widow (Isaiah 1:17), the right rolling like waters (Amos 5:24), mercy and the knowledge of the Judges (Hosea 6:6), a sacrifice of thanksgiving (Psalm 50:14), and giving ear to the voice (Jeremiah 7:23). So the question the text asks the reader is not whether to bring something near, since the word itself means bringing near. The question is what is being brought, and whether it is accepted. Read the next time you meet an offering in the Bible as the same question: what is brought near here, who is it brought to, and what does the court say?

Remember: in Leviticus 1 the offering is a korban, something brought near, and the verb is qarab, bring near. The one who brings is adam, the livestock are the livestock of Genesis 1, and what rises is accepted and "covers over" him. The BBE's "to take away his sin" has no sin in the Hebrew. English "sacrifice" mostly translates a different word, zebach, the slaughter. The later passages do not cancel the bringing near. They refuse what was not ordered and say what the court accepts.
