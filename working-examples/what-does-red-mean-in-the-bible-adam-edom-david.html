What Does Red Mean in the Bible? Adam, Esau, Edom and David

And the first came out red from head to foot like a robe of hair, and they gave him the name of Esau. — Genesis 25:25, with the meanings put in

The Hebrew word for man, adam, and the Hebrew word for ground, adamah, come from the same root as the word for "red". Strong's puts them side by side, and the word for blood, dam, is placed next to them with the note "compare". Then the Bible uses the red words for a short list of things: a newborn, a stew, a youth, a beloved, a cow, a covering of skins, a stretch of water. This page follows every one of those uses, checks them against the Hebrew, and shows where red stops being a colour and starts being a name.

How this page reads the Bible. This course reads every story as having the same three parts: you, the identity you claim, and the judges that make it real, because Genesis 1:26 makes man the one made in the judges' own image (Lesson 6). A name tells you what the state is like before the story starts (Lesson 9). The Existing One (YHVH, "the Lord" in the BBE) is present awareness, and Elohim is the Judges (Lesson 3). Each step below is checked against the verse (BBE), Strong's and the Hebrew word by word. If this is your first page, The Big Idea is the place to start. This page follows on from the Blood page, which reads blood and flesh. This one reads the red.

The short answer: man (adam), ground (adamah) and the red words (H119, H122, H132) are one family in Strong's, and blood (dam) is compared with them. The red words are used only a few times, and not only for people or blood: they are also used for wine, skins, horses and a shield. What the text itself shows is Esau. He is born red (Genesis 25:25), asks for the red soup, and is named Edom "for this reason" (25:30). The name follows what he takes in. In this reading, red is the colour of a state that is showing its life, and Esau is the clearest example of a name that records what was taken in.

In six lines:

  1. The man is made from the ground (Genesis 2:7). In Hebrew these are adam and adamah, and Strong's gives both as from the root for red (H119).
  2. The first blood named is Abel's, and it is heard from the ground (4:10). Blood, man and ground are in one family of words.
  3. Esau is born red, eats the red soup, and is named Edom (25:25, 25:30).
  4. David is the only other person called by the same word as Esau, admoni (1 Samuel 16:12, 17:42).
  5. The beloved of the Song is "white and red" (Song 5:10). The tent has a covering of rams' skins dyed red (Exodus 26:14). The water Moab sees is "red as blood" (2 Kings 3:22).
  6. The red words are also used for wine, a shield, horses and the clothing in Isaiah 63:2, so red is not only a word for people.

The course in short

  • The Bible is read as a courtroom inside the reader (Lesson 1). The Judges (Elohim) rule on the identity the reader holds, and the Existing One, the reader's own present awareness, holds it as I AM (Lesson 2).
  • Every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed (Lesson 5).
  • Everything comes back after its kind (Lesson 7): what is filed is what is enforced.
  • Names are identity codes (the names lesson), so every name below is read for its meaning.

The Genesis scene this is built from

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed, and the original scene has four parts: the ground, the seed, the stand and the time. The part that red is built from is the ground and the man made from it.

And the Existing One, the Judges, made man from the dust of the ground, breathing into him the breath of life: and man became a living soul. — Genesis 2:7, with the meanings put in

The ground: adamah, "soil (from its general redness)". The seed: not named here. The stand: adam, "ruddy, i.e. a human being". The time: not in this verse. So the scene has the red ground, and a man who carries its name, standing alive on it. Everything below is that scene changed.

The meanings used

  • Man, ruddy (adam, H120): Strong's "from H119; ruddy, i.e. a human being". Ground (adamah, H127): "from H119; soil (from its general redness)".
  • Be red (adam, H119, the verb): Strong's "to show blood (in the face), i.e. flush or turn rosy". It occurs 10 times in 10 verses: the rams' skins dyed red in the tent covering (Exodus 25:5, 26:14, 35:7, 35:23, 36:19, 39:34), wine (Proverbs 23:31), sins (Isaiah 1:18), the Nazirites (Lamentations 4:7) and a shield (Nahum 2:3).
  • Red (adom, H122, the adjective): Strong's "from H119; rosy". It occurs 9 times in 7 verses: the soup and the name (Genesis 25:30, twice), the cow (Numbers 19:2), the water (2 Kings 3:22), the beloved (Song 5:10), the clothing (Isaiah 63:2) and horses (Zechariah 1:8, 6:2).
  • Ruddy (admoni, H132): Strong's "from H119; reddish (of the hair or the complexion)". It occurs 3 times: Esau (Genesis 25:25), and David (1 Samuel 16:12, 17:42).
  • Blood (dam, H1818): Strong's "from H1826 (compare H119)". The "compare" is a cross-reference, not a proof of where the word comes from. See the Blood page.
  • Edom: the name is given in the text, "for this reason", because of the red soup (Genesis 25:30).

Step by step, from Genesis 2 to the red

The man and the ground share a name (Genesis 2:7). Adam is made from adamah. Strong's gives both as from the root that means to show blood in the face, to flush. So the first man is named for the colour of what he is made from. In this reading it is also the colour of life showing.

The first blood is heard from that ground (Genesis 4:10). Cain puts Abel to death and Abel's blood cries from the ground. Man, ground and blood are in one verse, and the Strong's entries put all three in one family.

Esau is born red (Genesis 25:25). "The first" comes out red from head to foot, and they give him the name Esau. The word is admoni, and it is the first of only three uses.

Esau asks for the red, and is named for it (Genesis 25:30). He asks for "that red soup", and "for this reason" he is named Edom, Red. In the Hebrew the soup is only "the red". So the man who was born red takes the red in, and the text gives him the name. This is the Blood page's step again: what you take in, you become, and the name records it.

David is the second person called by Esau's word (1 Samuel 16:12, 17:42). The same word, admoni. He is sent for, and he is ruddy, with beautiful eyes, and the Existing One says to anoint him (16:12). The Philistine sees the same thing and despises him (17:42). The text gives no reason for the colour, and this page does not give one either.

The beloved is white and red (Song 5:10). The red word (adom) is used of a person only here. The BBE says "white and red", and the Hebrew is tsach ve-adom.

The tent is covered in red skins (Exodus 26:14). The covering of the tent is made of rams' skins dyed red. Six of the ten uses of the red verb are this one covering, repeated as it is made.

Water red as blood (2 Kings 3:22). The Moabites see the water as red as blood. This is the only verse where the red word (adom) and the word for blood (dam) are put side by side.

The red cow, the red garments, the red sins (Numbers 19:2, Isaiah 63:2, Isaiah 1:18). The cow is red and without a mark, and has never been under a yoke. The clothing in Isaiah 63:2 is asked about as red, like that of one who treads grapes. In Isaiah 1:18 the Hebrew has the red verb for sins, and compares them to crimson. The BBE adds "blood" there, and the Hebrew does not.

What changed

  • The ground. It was the red soil the man was made from. It becomes the place the blood is heard from.
  • The man. He was the ruddy one on the ground. He becomes a named line of people: Esau, who is red, and David, who is ruddy.
  • The name. The name adam carries the colour. After Genesis 25 the colour is given as a name, Edom.

What it means for the reader

In this reading, red is the colour of a state that is showing its life. The man is named for it, and the ground he is made from has the same name. Esau shows the whole step in four verses: the state he is (born red), the state he takes in (the red soup), the name that records it (Edom), and the court that enforces it, because the name stays.

The page does not say that every red is blood or that every red means the same thing. Wine, horses and a shield are red too, and the page has not read Zechariah or Nahum. The claim is smaller: the red words come from one root with man and ground, and Esau is the one place where the text itself ties the colour to a name.

The next time you meet red in the Bible, ask three things. What is red, a state that is showing its life? Who is taking it in? And what name does the text give it afterwards?

Remember: man, ground and the red words are one family in Strong's, and blood is compared with them. Only two people are called by the word admoni, Esau and David. Esau is born red, eats the red and is named Red.
