What Do the Rods and Sticks in the Bible Mean? Jacob, Moses, Aaron and Elisha

The Bible opens with a scene: a garden, trees giving after their kind, and a tree of life. In Genesis 3 the way to that tree is shut, and the man is sent out to work cursed ground. The Bible ends with the tree back in place (Revelation 22:2). Story after story builds and breaks that scene, and the stick stories are part of it. Jacob peels young branches and sets them where the flock drinks (Genesis 30). Moses puts a rod down on the earth and it becomes a snake (Exodus 4). Aaron's rod grows buds and fruit overnight (Numbers 17). Elisha throws a stick into the Jordan and an iron blade comes up (2 Kings 6).

The short answer: a stick in the Bible is a piece of that tree. Genesis 1:11 says each tree gives "after its kind" with its seed in itself, so a piece of the tree carries the whole kind (Lesson 7). Out in the cursed ground a piece of tree turns up in a hand. It is set in its place, and the name or the word is spoken, the way the Judges spoke in Genesis 1 (Lesson 3). What comes is what the tree gives after its kind. A stick swung in anger, or used in place of the word, gives nothing. Etz, the Hebrew word for tree, is also the hanging-tree, so the same wood stands on both sides of the curse. The course calls the name or request the filing, because the Bible works like a courtroom (Lesson 1).

One note on words: only Elisha's stick uses the Hebrew word for tree (etz). Jacob's and Moses' rods use other Hebrew words, covered further down. No stick story uses the Hebrew phrase for the tree of life, so that link is a reading, and the page sets out where the evidence for it is firm and where it is an echo.

The stories, one line each

  • Jacob's rods (Genesis 30:37-41). He peels young branches "so that the white wood was seen" and sets them where the flock drinks. The flock conceives "when they saw the sticks" (30:41), and the young come marked. Jacob later says "God has taken away your father's cattle and has given them to me" (31:9). The full page is here.
  • Moses' rod (Exodus 4:2-4, 4:20). "What is that in your hand?" "A rod." Put down on the earth it becomes a snake. Moses puts out his hand, takes it, and it is a rod again. The BBE calls it "the rod of God" (4:20).
  • Aaron before Pharaoh (Exodus 7:12). "For every one of them put down his rod on the earth, and they became snakes: but Aaron's rod made a meal of their rods." Exodus 7-14 is read on the Pharaoh page.
  • Aaron's rod (Numbers 17). Twelve rods, with "every man's name" placed on his rod (17:2), put "before the Lord in the Tent of witness" (17:7). The next day Aaron's rod "had put out buds, and was covered with buds and flowers and fruit" (17:8), and the rest are given back. Aaron's rod is put back "to be kept for a sign" (17:10).
  • Moses at the rock (Numbers 20:8-12). He is told to "give orders to the rock". He "gave the rock two blows with his rod: and water came streaming out" (20:11). Then: "Because you had not enough faith in me to keep my name holy" (20:12).
  • Balaam (Numbers 22:26-31). The ass is stopped in "a narrow place where there was no room for turning" (22:26), and "full of wrath, Balaam gave her hard blows with his stick" (22:27). Then "the Lord made Balaam's eyes open" (22:31). The full page is here.
  • Judah and Tamar (Genesis 38). Judah leaves "Your ring and its cord and the stick in your hand" as a pledge (38:18). Tamar sends them back: "say then, whose are this ring and this cord and this stick?" (38:25). Judah "said openly that they were his" (38:26).
  • David (1 Samuel 17). He "took his stick in his hand" (17:40), and Goliath says "Am I a dog, that you come out to me with sticks?" (17:43). David answers "I come to you in the name of the Lord of armies" (17:45), and "the Lord does not give salvation by sword and spear" (17:47). The stone does the work, and "David had no sword in his hand" (17:50).
  • Elisha's stick (2 Kings 6:5-7). The head of an axe goes into the water, Elisha is shown the place, cuts a stick and puts it into the water, and "the iron came up to the top of the water". The full page is here.
  • Gehazi's stick (2 Kings 4:29-35). Elisha's servant lays the stick on a dead child's face, and there was "no voice, and no one gave attention" (4:31). Elisha has to go in himself.

The meanings used

  • Tree, wood, stick (etz, H6086): the first use is Genesis 1:11, "fruit tree", and Genesis 1:12 has "after its kind" with it. The word also covers a piece of wood, and a hanging-tree or gallows. Elisha's stick (2 Kings 6:6) is this word.
  • Jacob's rods (maqqel, H4731): 18 uses. Strong's reads it "a shoot, i.e. stick (with leaves on, or for walking, striking, guiding, divining)", from a root meaning apparently to germinate. BDB calls the root doubtful.
  • Fresh (lach, H3892): "moist, fresh, of plants". Six uses, including Genesis 30:37.
  • Rod, staff, tribe (matteh, H4294): 251 uses. Strong's reads it "a staff, rod, shaft, branch, a tribe". The word for Aaron's rod and Moses' rod, and also for a tribe (about 183 of the uses, BDB). It is also the word for Judah's stick in Genesis 38:18 and 38:25.
  • Bud, sprout, flourish (parach, H6524): 36 uses, including Numbers 17:5 and 17:8 and Ezekiel 17:24.
  • Snake (nachash, H5175): 31 uses, including the snake in Genesis 3, and the rod that becomes a snake in Exodus 4:3 and 7:15.
  • Tree, club, cross (xylon, G3586): 20 uses. Thayer says the Septuagint uses it for etz. It means tree, club, stocks, a hanging-tree or cross, and the tree of life.
  • Cross (stauros, G4716): 27 uses. "An upright stake, hence a cross."

How to read it, step by step

  1. Start with the tree. In Genesis 1:11 the earth brings forth the tree "in which is their seed, after their sort" (BBE). The seed is inside the tree, so each tree is its own kind. A cutting is a piece of a tree that carries the whole kind.
  2. Look at what was named. Aaron's rod has a name written on it (Numbers 17:2). Judah's stick is known by whose it is (Genesis 38:25). Jacob names his payment before he sets the rods (Genesis 30:32). David comes "in the name of the Lord of armies" (1 Samuel 17:45). Moses' rod is first named by Moses himself: "A rod" (Exodus 4:2). The piece goes with the name.
  3. Find the place. The rods are laid before the Lord (Numbers 17:7). Jacob's rods go in the drinking-places. Moses' rod goes down on the earth (Exodus 4:3). Elisha is shown the place where the iron went in. The stick is set in the place of the trouble, and not carried around.
  4. See what the stick does. It lifts, marks, swallows, buds, or shows whose it is, where the word or the name is already there. In Numbers 20 and 22 the stick is swung and nothing comes of the swinging.
  5. See where it gives nothing. Gehazi's stick in 2 Kings 4, Balaam's blows in Numbers 22 and Moses' blows in Numbers 20 are the cases where the stick is used without the word, in anger, or without the one it was asked for.
  6. Check the tree word. Elisha's stick and the tree of Genesis 1:11 are the same Hebrew word (etz), and the Greek word for tree (xylon) keeps the same range. The other rods use different Hebrew words. The sections below cover this.

Checking the pattern against the other stories

The pattern is first read from one story, the floating axe head, and then checked against the others. Two of them showed the first reading was too narrow, so it was widened.

The first reading comes from the axe head: a piece works when it is set in the place of the trouble by the one whose name it carries. It did not hold on two stories. In Numbers 17 Moses sets the rods, not Aaron, yet Aaron's rod answers to the name on it. And Gehazi's stick was Elisha's own stick, and it still failed.

The wider pattern replaced it:

  1. A piece answers to the filing (the name on it, or what was asked), and not to who carries it.
  2. It works in the place where the court sits or the trouble is, when the filing matches.
  3. A false filing is enforced as filed.
  4. When the piece is tested, it shows what it really is (the magicians' rods in Exodus 7:12 work, then lose).
  5. The piece does not act alone: the word spoken with it is what the Judges answer. (Numbers 20 added this one.)

The results. Each line below gives the passage and whether the text agreed with the rule. Exodus 7, Numbers 16 and Numbers 20 were worked out before the chapter was read. Genesis 38, Numbers 22 and 1 Samuel 17 were read after the pattern was set down.

  • Exodus 7:12: every rod becomes a snake, and Aaron's swallows the others. Worked out in advance, and it fits. The magicians' rods work and then lose, which is a false filing enforced as filed.
  • Numbers 16: Moses sets the test as an outcome: "In the morning the Lord will make clear who are his". Worked out in advance, and it fits.
  • Numbers 17: the claims are the names on the twelve rods, and only one rod answers. The other eleven are given back as they were (17:9). It fits, and it is one of the two stories that widened the first reading.
  • Numbers 20: worked out in advance: water would come and the carrier would be judged, and that is what the text says. The water comes streaming out, and Moses is judged for not keeping the name holy. It fits, and it shows the rod is not acting alone.
  • Genesis 38: Tamar holds Judah's stick, and it answers to Judah's name and not to her, and Judah says openly that they are his. It fits.
  • Numbers 22: three rounds of blows with a stick get nothing. What changes things is that Balaam's eyes are made open, and he says "I did not see that you were in the way against me" (22:34). It fits.
  • 1 Samuel 17: David carries a stick and uses a stone and a name. The stick is a sign, and not the instrument. "The Lord does not give salvation by sword and spear" (17:47). It fits.
  • 2 Kings 4:31: the other story that widened the first reading. The stick was sent on without the man, and nothing comes. The wider pattern explains it: the mother filed for the man, not for the stick ("I will not go back without you", 4:30).

One new thing from Exodus 4. The same piece is a rod in the hand and a snake on the ground. Its state follows where it is. The Hebrew word for snake in 4:3 and 7:15 is nachash, the word of Genesis 3, and Exodus 4 and 7 are the only places a rod becomes a snake. Exodus 7:10-12 may use a different Hebrew word for the snake, so the link to Genesis 3 rests on 4:3 and 7:15.

The tree word: etz, xylon and the cross

Etz is the tree of Genesis 1:11 and the stick of 2 Kings 6:6. It is also used for a hanging-tree: Thayer's cross-references list Genesis 40:19, Deuteronomy 21:23, Joshua 10:26 and Esther 5:14.

The Greek word xylon is the Septuagint's word for etz, and it keeps the same range. It is the tree in Luke 23:31, the clubs of the arrest party (Matthew 26:47, 26:55, Mark 14:43, 14:48, Luke 22:52), the cross as a tree in Acts 5:30, 10:39 and 13:29, Galatians 3:13 and 1 Peter 2:24, and the tree of life in Revelation 2:7 and 22:2, 14 and 19. The cross that is carried is a different word, stauros. Luke has stauros in 23:26 (Simon carries the cross) and xylon in 23:31, five verses later. Galatians has xylon in 3:13 and stauros in 6:14. The walk to Golgotha is read on the cross page.

Green and dry. In Luke 23:31 Jesus says "For if they do these things when the tree is green, what will they do when it is dry?" Ezekiel puts a green tree and a dry tree side by side twice:

  • Ezekiel 17:24 (BBE): "drying up the green tree and making the dry tree full of growth; I the Lord have said it and have done it".
  • Ezekiel 20:47 (21:3 in the Hebrew): a fire is lit "for the destruction of every green tree in you and every dry tree".

BDB marks the green tree there as etz lach and the dry tree as the opposite. Lach is the word for Jacob's fresh rods (Genesis 30:37). The verb for "make flourish" in Ezekiel 17:24 (parach, in its causative form) is the verb for Aaron's rod sprouting in Numbers 17:5 and 17:8. So a dry piece of tree coming into full growth because the word says so is Ezekiel's sentence and Aaron's rod in the one verb. Luke does not quote Ezekiel, so this is an echo and not a proof.

Three Hebrew words, one pattern

The English says "rod" or "stick" every time, but the Hebrew uses three words: Jacob's rods are maqqel, Aaron's and Moses' rods are matteh, and Elisha's stick is etz. No verse links the stories by a shared word. BDB does set matteh and maqqel side by side in Jeremiah 48:17, which treats them as the same sort of thing, a leader's staff. And etz is the tree they are all pieces of. So the link is the tree, the place, and the name, and not a single repeated word.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. In Genesis 1 the earth brings forth the tree after its kind:

And God said, Let grass come up on the earth, and plants producing seed, and fruit-trees giving fruit, in which is their seed, after their sort: and it was so. — Genesis 1:11 (BBE)

What changed is where the power sits. In Genesis 1 the Judges speak and the earth brings forth. In the stick stories a piece of tree is placed and the word is spoken, and what comes is what the place was given.

The ground is the other change. In Genesis 3 the man is sent out of the garden into cursed ground, and the way to the tree of life is kept (3:23-24). The stick stories all happen out there. Nobody stands in the garden, yet a piece of tree is in the hand where the thorns are, and it works only where the name and the word are in place. In this reading the stick is a piece of the tree carried into the cursed ground, and the Genesis 1 rule, after its kind, still runs outside the garden. No stick story uses the Hebrew phrase for the tree of life, so this is a reading and not a word link. The nearest textual support is the Greek word xylon, which names both the tree of life and the cross.

Remember: a stick in the Bible is a piece of a tree, and the tree gives after its kind. Set in the place of the trouble, with what was named or asked, it gives after its kind. Swung in anger, used instead of the word, or sent on without the one it was asked for, it gives nothing. The word spoken is what the Judges answer.

Words and sources

  • Tree (etz, H6086): the first use is Genesis 1:11, and 2 Kings 6:4 and 6:6 are both among its uses.
  • Jacob's rods (maqqel, H4731): 18 uses. Strong's gloss as above, and BDB calls the root doubtful.
  • Fresh (lach, H3892): six uses (Genesis 30:37, Numbers 6:3, Judges 16:7 and 16:8, Ezekiel 17:24 and 21:3).
  • Rod and tribe (matteh, H4294): 251 uses, about 183 of them "tribe" (BDB). Genesis 38:18 and 38:25 are among its uses, and so is Exodus 4:20 ("the rod of God"). BDB sets matteh beside maqqel in Jeremiah 48:17.
  • Bud (parach, H6524): 36 uses. Numbers 17:5 and 17:8 and Ezekiel 17:24 are among its uses, and Ezekiel 17:24 is the causative form.
  • Dry (yabesh, H3002): BDB lists the dry tree in Ezekiel 17:24 and 21:3 as the opposite of etz lach.
  • Snake (nachash, H5175): 31 uses, including Exodus 4:3 and 7:15, and the snake in Genesis 3.
  • Tree and club (xylon, G3586): 20 uses. Thayer says it is the Septuagint's word for etz. Every use named above appears in its occurrence list.
  • Cross (stauros, G4716): 27 uses. Matthew 27:32, Luke 23:26, John 19:17, Galatians 6:14 and others are among its uses.

Drawn from other pages in the course: the details of Genesis 30 are on the Jacob page, the details of 2 Kings 4 to 7 are on the axe head page, and the walk to Golgotha is on the cross page.

The limits of this reading:

  • How far the check goes: six passages were checked, and these are not all the stick stories. The pattern takes in a lot, so a result counts against it only if a true filing gets nothing. None has.
  • Still to test: Exodus 14 and 17 (the rod lifted over the sea and the rock struck at Horeb), Numbers 21:9 (the bronze snake on a pole), Zechariah 11 (the two staffs) and Hosea 4:12.
  • The English carries some of the argument. "Before their eyes" in Numbers 20:8, "narrow" in Numbers 22:26 and "stick" in Numbers 22:27 and 1 Samuel 17:40 and 17:43 are the BBE's words, and the Hebrew behind them is not part of the case. The same goes for the snake of Exodus 7:10-12.
  • Luke 23:31 and Ezekiel: an echo, since Luke does not quote Ezekiel.
  • The stick on its own: the text says the water came out in Numbers 20 and that Moses was judged for not keeping the name holy. That the stick does not act alone is this page's reading.
  • The arrest of Jesus has "swords and clubs" (xylon), iron and wood together. It is a pairing worth noting, and nothing is built on it here.
