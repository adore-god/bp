Aaron and the Priesthood: The Genesis Scene Changed

People ask what Aaron's priesthood is about: the robes, the stones, the oil, the seven days, the two sons burned, the rod that buds. Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. Genesis builds a scene, places the man in it to work and keep it, puts one statute on him (death), clothes him, and sends him out. Aaron's story puts a man back at the edge of that scene, at the door of a tent, with every part changed. This page reads Exodus 28 and 29, Leviticus 8 to 10, and Numbers 3, 8, 16, 17 and 20 (with the calf of Exodus 32 between) against Genesis 1 to 4, and says at each step what the court does (Lesson 1).

The short answer: in this reading the Existing One is the reader's present awareness, and Aaron is the identity it has put on, standing before the court carrying the names it holds. What the court ordered, it accepts. What it did not order, it burns with the same fire. The court's first act in Genesis was to divide, and the priest is the one given that work to keep.

How to read it, step by step

  1. The place is named for a time, and a man sets the lights. The tent is called ohel moed, and moed is the word Genesis 1:14 uses for the "seasons" the lights mark. In Lesson 5 the lights of day four mark when something is due; here the place itself carries that name. In Genesis "God put them in the arch of heaven, to give light on the earth" (1:17, BBE). In the tent "Aaron did so; he put the lights in their places so that they gave light in front of the support" (Numbers 8:3, BBE). What changed is the hand: the court set the lights in Genesis, and a man sets them here. This fits his name, Light-bringer.
  2. The chest holds the names, the lights and the judgment. Over Aaron's heart go the names (Exodus 28:29) and the Urim, which means "lights" (28:30). The BBE says "the power of making decisions"; the Hebrew is mishpat, "judgment", and the KJV reads "Aaron shall bear the judgment of the children of Israel upon his heart". In Genesis 1:4 the court sees the light, calls it good, and divides. The priest wears the lights and the judgment together. In this reading, what is on the chest is what is on file: the names are the identities presented, the lights are what the court has called good, and the judgment is what it rules.
  3. The stones come from the Eden list. The names are cut on two stones worn on the shoulders (Exodus 28:9-12). The Hebrew is shoham, and the BBE says "beryl stones". The same stone is in Genesis 2:12: "the gold of that land is good: there is bdellium and the onyx stone" (BBE), and Ezekiel 28:13 places it in "Eden the garden of God". What changed is that the gold and stone of the river's land are now worn, with the names cut on them.
  4. The priesthood runs after its kind, by seed. Genesis 1:11 has plants "producing seed ... after their sort" (BBE). The Hebrew for seed (zera) is also in "his seed after him" (Exodus 28:43) and in "no man who is not of the seed of Aaron has the right of burning spices before the Lord" (Numbers 16:40, BBE). The robes pass the same way: "Aaron's holy robes will be used by his sons after him" (Exodus 29:29, BBE). Korah is a Levite but not of Aaron's seed, and "fire came out from the Lord, burning up the two hundred and fifty men who were offering the perfume" (16:35, BBE). Nadab and Abihu "had no children" (Numbers 3:4, BBE), so the line goes on through Eleazar ("God is helper") and Ithamar ("Coast-of-palms").
  5. The rod is the same rule in a plant. Twelve rods, each with a name on it, are laid "before the Lord in the Tent of witness" (Numbers 17:7, BBE). "On the day after ... Aaron's rod, the rod of the house of Levi, had put out buds, and was covered with buds and flowers and fruit" (17:8, BBE). The other eleven come back unchanged (17:9). Compare Genesis 1:12: "every tree producing fruit, in which is its seed, of its sort" (BBE). In Genesis the earth gave plants when the court spoke. Here a dry stick with a name on it, left before the court overnight, gives them. The name is the code, and the court's night does the rest. The rod is then kept "for a sign" (17:10). The rods and sticks page reads this rod beside Jacob's, Moses' and Elisha's.
  6. The man's two jobs become the Levites' two jobs. Genesis 2:15 (BBE): "to do work in it and take care of it." The Hebrew is abad and shamar. Numbers 3:7-8 gives the Levites the same two verbs: they keep the tent and its things, and do its service. The BBE reads "the care of the Tent of meeting ... and the work of the House" (3:7). Shamar is also the winged ones' job in Genesis 3:24 ("to keep the way to the tree of life", BBE), Cain's word in "am I my brother's keeper?" (4:9), and the priests' charge at the door ("keep the charge of the Lord", Leviticus 8:35). Levi means "joined": the tribe is "made separate from the children of Israel" (Numbers 8:14, BBE) and put "before Aaron the priest, to be his helpers" (3:6, BBE). That is Lesson 8 in one tribe: leave, then join. In Genesis 3:24 others keep the way at the edge. Here the ones in the garments keep it, at the door.
  7. The court dresses the man; he does not dress himself. "They made themselves coats of leaves stitched together" (Genesis 3:7, BBE). Then "the Lord God made for Adam and for his wife coats of skins for their clothing" (3:21, BBE). The Hebrew for coat in 3:21 (kuttoneth) is the word for the priest's coat in Exodus 28:4, 28:39, 28:40, 29:5, 29:8 and Leviticus 8:7. In Leviticus 8:7 Moses puts it on him. In Genesis 2:25 the man is "without clothing" with "no sense of shame"; in Exodus 28:42-43 the trousers cover the body and the clothes are worn "whenever they go into the Tent of meeting", "so that they may be free from any sin causing death". A coat of leaves is a self-made filing. A coat from the court is a filing the court stands behind. Aaron is to be "clothed with glory and honour" (Exodus 28:2, BBE).
  8. The death statute is still on file, but it now covers the whole approach. Genesis 2:17 (BBE): "on the day when you take of it, death will certainly come to you." In Aaron's story the same word comes back at every edge: the bells keep him "safe from death" (Exodus 28:35), "keep watch ... so that death may not come to you" (Leviticus 8:35), "do not go out from the door ... or death will come to you" (10:7), "any strange person who comes near is to be put to death" (Numbers 3:10). In Genesis the statute sat on one tree. In the tent it sits on the whole approach. The direction is reversed too: in Genesis 3:23-24 the man is sent out, but in Leviticus 8:33-35 and 10:7 the priests are told not to go out from the door, because "the holy oil of the Lord is on you".
  9. The court's first act is now the priest's work. In Genesis the court divides: "God made a division between the light and the dark" (1:4, BBE), the waters from the waters (1:6-7), the day from the night (1:14, 1:18). The Hebrew verb is badal. It is the veil that "divides" the holy place from the most holy (Exodus 26:33), the priest's charge to "make a division between the holy and the common, and between the unclean and the clean" (Leviticus 10:10, BBE), and the Levites being "made separate" (Numbers 8:14; 16:9). The BBE uses the same phrase, "make a division between", in Genesis 1:4 and Leviticus 10:10. When Aaron "took his place between the dead and the living: and the disease was stopped" (Numbers 16:48, BBE), the division is enacted in the camp.
  10. The calf: the identity asked for is a cattle kind. "Come, make us a god to go before us" (Exodus 32:1, BBE). Genesis 1:26 (BBE): "Let us make man in our image." Here the identity made is "the metal image of a young ox" (32:4, BBE), a creature of the kind Genesis 1:24-25 puts under man's rule ("cattle ... after their sort"). Aaron's account is: "they gave it to me, and I put it in the fire, and this image of an ox came out" (32:24, BBE). Compare Genesis 3:12 (BBE): "she gave me the fruit of the tree and I took it". The shape is the same: they gave, I did, it came out. The sons of Levi then come at the call "Whoever is on the Lord's side" (32:26), and Moses says "You have made yourselves priests to the Lord this day" (32:29, BBE). The tribe divided out is the tribe that came when the division was called.
  11. Strange fire: the same fire accepts and burns. At the end of the seven days "fire came out from before the Lord, burning up the offering on the altar and the fat" (Leviticus 9:24, BBE). In the very next chapter "fire came out from before the Lord, burning them up" (10:2, BBE). It is the same fire in the same words. The difference is the line in 10:1: "which he had not given them orders to do". The "he" is the Existing One, the awareness that holds the identity: the sons put in what it had not ordered, and the fire from before it answers by what was ordered. Nadab ("Generous") puts in an offering of his own; Abihu ("He-is-my-father") stays tied to the father. In Genesis 3:24 a flaming sword turns "every way to keep the way to the tree of life". At the tent the court's fire keeps the way by accepting what was ordered and burning what was not. The sons are carried out "in their coats" (10:5, BBE), the same word as the coats of Genesis 3:21, outside the tent-circle, as the man was sent outside. The ordered fire shows the other side: "put in it fire from the altar, and sweet spices" (Numbers 16:46, BBE), and when Aaron does it the plague stops (16:48).
  12. Korah and the earth's mouth: the Genesis 4 pattern, acted out by the names. The chapter opens with a list of names, and each one does what it says. Korah ("Ice") is the son of Izhar ("Shining-oil"), son of Kohath ("Allied"), son of Levi ("Joined") (Numbers 16:1, BBE): his line already carries the oil, the alliance and the joining. Strong's gives Korah as "ice", and ice in this book is water set hard: "water is made hard by his cold" (Psalm 147:17, BBE). The name is a claim that has set hard. He says "all the people are holy, every one of them, and the Lord is among them" and asks "why then have you put yourselves in authority" (16:3, BBE). Moses answers "would you now be priests?" (16:10, BBE). What Korah adds to the oil, the alliance and the joining is the priesthood without the seed of Aaron (16:40). With him are Dathan ("Belonging-to-a-fountain") and Abiram ("Father-of-height"), the sons of Eliab ("God-of-his-father"), and On ("Vigour"), a son of Reuben ("See-a-son") (16:1). Abiram and Eliab both carry the word father, as Abihu does in step 11. The two sons answer Moses with "We will not come up" (16:12, BBE) and call the old place "a land flowing with milk and honey" (16:13, BBE): the state they will not leave. On is named once, in 16:1, and the chapter never names him again. The court answers through the ground: "the earth, opening her mouth, took them in, with their families, and all the men who were joined to Korah" (16:32, BBE). Genesis 4:11 (BBE): "you are cursed from the earth, whose mouth is open to take your brother's blood from your hand." The Hebrew verb for opening the mouth (patsah) is used of the ground in Genesis 4:11 and Numbers 16:30, and of Dathan and Abiram in Deuteronomy 11:6. Cain asks "am I my brother's keeper?", the same keeping word as the Eden job. Korah is a Levite, a keeper by tribe, claiming the standing without the seed, and the ground answers.
  13. The end: the garments come off and pass on. The place is named too. At Mount Hor, "at the edge of the land of Edom" (Numbers 20:23, BBE), "take Aaron's robes off him and put them on Eleazar, his son: and death will come to Aaron there" (20:26, BBE). Hor is "mountain" (H2023) and Edom is "red" (H123), the name Esau is given in Genesis 25:30. So the coat comes off on the Mountain, at the border of Red, and goes on Eleazar ("God is helper"), the one who took the censers out of the fire (16:37-39). The statute is kept by the identity in the coat, and the coat passes down the seed (Exodus 29:29-30).

What changed from Genesis 1 to 4

Aaron's story follows the order of Genesis: scene built and man placed, a wrong filing, the court's fire, the earth's mouth.

The scene is not new, but there is now a man in it who is held to its rules. In Genesis the court meets its own rules by its own acts. In Aaron's story a person in garments meets them, and the court enforces whatever he files.

Remember: Genesis gives the scene and the statute. The priesthood is a man standing in that scene in the court's own coat, with the names and the lights on his chest, keeping the division the court first made, under the same statute of death. What the court ordered it accepts, and what it did not order it burns with the same fire. The kind goes on by seed, and the garment is what passes down.

Meanings used

  • Aaron (H175): Strong's says "of uncertain derivation". The Blue Letter Bible outline glosses it "light bringer", which is why the passages above read "Light-bringer". That gloss is not a Strong's definition. The reading fits what the text shows him doing (Numbers 8:2-3; Exodus 40:25).
  • Levi (H3878): "attached", from lavah (H3867). BDB: "joined to".
  • Nadab (H5070): "liberal" (Strong's), "generous" (BDB). Abihu (H30): "he is (my) father" (BDB). Eleazar (H499): "God (is) helper". El is read as the Judges here (Lesson 3).
  • Korah (H7141): "ice" (Strong's); "bald" in the Blue Letter Bible outline. Strong's explains the noun behind it, qerach (H7140), as "ice (as if bald, i.e. smooth)", so the two glosses share a root.
  • Izhar (H3324): "the same as" H3323, oil "as producing light" (Strong's); "shining oil" in the Blue Letter Bible outline. Kohath (H6955): "allied" (Strong's); "assembly" in the outline.
  • Dathan (H1885): Strong's says "of uncertain derivation". "Belonging to a fountain" is the BDB and Blue Letter Bible outline gloss, not a Strong's definition. Abiram (H48): "father of height (i.e. lofty)" (Strong's); "my father is exalted" in the outline. Eliab (H446): "God of (his) father" (Strong's).
  • On (H203): "vigour" (BDB); "wealth" or "vigour" in the outline. Pallu (H6396): "distinguished" (Strong's). Reuben (H7205): "see ye a son" (Strong's).
  • Hor (H2023): "mountain", another form of har (H2022). Edom (H123): "red" (Strong's), first used in Genesis 25:30. Ithamar (H385): "coast of the palm-tree" (Strong's).
  • Urim (H224): "lights", plural of H217; seven uses. Thummim: Young's Literal gives "the Lights and the Perfections" (Leviticus 8:8). Mishpat: "judgment".
  • Tent of meeting: ohel moed. Moed (H4150) is "an appointment, i.e. a fixed time or season ... by extension, the place of meeting". 223 uses; the first is Genesis 1:14.
  • Coat (kuttoneth, H3801): "a shirt", from a root meaning to cover. 29 uses.
  • Divide, separate (badal, H914): 42 uses.
  • Seed (zera, H2233).
  • Do work in (abad, H5647) and take care of (shamar, H8104).
  • Onyx (shoham, H7718): "a gem, probably the beryl". 11 uses.
  • Open the mouth (patsah, H6475): 15 uses.
  • Eden (H5731): "pleasure" (BDB).
  • Israel: "He shall prevail", as the course key and Lesson 9 give it.
  • Moses: "drawn out", as Lesson 9 gives it.
  • The Lord (YHVH, H3068): the Existing One. Lord God (YHVH Elohim, Genesis 3:21): the Existing One, the Judges.

Open points:

  • Aaron's name: "light bringer" is the Blue Letter Bible outline gloss, not Strong's. The lamp reading rests on Numbers 8:2-3 and Exodus 40:25.
  • Lights in Genesis 1 and Numbers 8: the BBE says "lights" in both, but the Hebrew of Numbers 8:2 and the Hebrew behind Genesis 1:3 were not compared. This link is a matching picture.
  • Thummim and mishpat: "Perfections" is from Young's Literal. The Strong's numbers for Thummim and mishpat were not pulled.
  • Beryl and onyx: the BBE says "onyx" in Genesis 2:12 and "beryl" in Exodus 28:9. Both are shoham on the list. Strong's says "probably the beryl".
  • Numbers 17:8: the BBE says "fruit". The Hebrew was not pulled, so the rod and Genesis 1:12 are linked by shape, not by a word.
  • Leviticus 9:24 and 10:2: the BBE words are the same in both. The Hebrew of the two was not compared.
  • Numbers 16:32: the BBE says "opening her mouth". The verb patsah is confirmed in 16:30, not in 16:32.
  • Exodus 32:1 and 32:4: the BBE says "a god" and the WEB says "gods". The Hebrew was not pulled, so nothing here rests on the number.
  • The blame shape (Exodus 32:24 and Genesis 3:12) and Aaron between the dead and the living (Numbers 16:48) are matching pictures, not word links.
  • The ear, hand and foot: Exodus 29:20 and Leviticus 8:23 put blood on the right ear, thumb and great toe. This page leaves that out because the link to Genesis 3 was not tested.
  • The seven days: the seven days of making priests (Exodus 29:35; Leviticus 8:33) and the seven lights are a count, not a word link to Genesis 1.
  • What was not read: Leviticus 16 and Numbers 18, and the Hebrew of Numbers 20:26-28 in full, were not used.
  • On's father: the BBE reads "Pallu" in Numbers 16:1. The Hebrew reads Peleth (H6431, "swiftness"), and BDB says it should probably be read Pallu. The passage above follows the BBE.
  • Korah's two glosses: "ice" is Strong's and "bald" is the outline. The page uses "ice" because Strong's ties it to water set hard, but the two share a root.
  • Dathan: "belonging to a fountain" is not a Strong's definition. Strong's gives no meaning.
  • Ithamar: BDB marks "land of palms" with a question mark. Nothing in the passages above rests on the palm.
  • Korah as an Edomite name: the name is also on Esau's list (Genesis 36:5, 14, 16, 18). This page does not use that, so it stays a note.
  • Mount Hor: the Hebrew is har ha-Hor, so "Mount Hor" already says mountain twice. The page reads Hor as "Mountain".
  • The Lord: the BBE "the Lord" is read as YHVH (H3068) in the passages above. The Hebrew of each verse was not compared one by one.
