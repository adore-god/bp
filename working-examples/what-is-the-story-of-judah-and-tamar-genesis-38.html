What Is the Story of Judah and Tamar Really About? Genesis 38

The point: Praise goes down from his brothers, takes a wife by sight, and fathers three sons named Watchful, Strong and Request. Watchful and Strong die. The Request is held back because of a fear he keeps in his mind. The woman he named a widow, Palm Tree, then does what he did not do. She covers her face and sits at the opening of Two Eyes, on the road to A Portion Assigned. She takes his seal, his cord and his staff as a pledge, and she conceives. When he sentences her, she sends the pledge out and asks him to recognise it. He does, with the same verb his brothers used on Joseph's coat, and he says "She is more upright than I am". The first use of that word in the Bible is said of her. The twins are named Breach and Rising.

In five lines:

  1. Judah means "praised". He goes down from his brothers (38:1) straight after proposing to sell Joseph (37:26-27), and his sons are named Watchful, Strong and Request (38:3-5).
  2. The text gives Onan's reason: the seed would not be his (38:9). It gives Judah's reason for holding back the Request: a thought in his mind that death might come to him (38:11).
  3. Tamar, "palm tree", is sent back to her father's house (38:11). That is Genesis 2:24 run backwards. She reads the time ("Shelah was now a man", 38:14) and takes the matter in hand.
  4. The pledge is Judah's own: his seal, its cord, and his staff (38:18). Brought out to be burned, she sends it back and asks him to recognise it (38:25).
  5. Judah rules against himself on what is on file: "She is more upright than I am, for I did not give her to Shelah my son" (38:26). Then what comes out of the woman who was brought out is a pair of twins, Breach and Rising.

How to read this story: Every person in it is a state in you, and nothing in it is about bodies. The seed is an identity being planted, as in Genesis 1:11, and the ground is where it lands. The family words are court words. The BBE calls Tamar Judah's "daughter-in-law" and Judah her "father-in-law" (38:11, 38:13), a relation held "in law", under the court's terms. The Hebrew word for her in those verses is kallah, "bride", so she is the identity meant to be joined, held in law by a state that has not joined her. It also comes early in the story, before Joseph rises. This is the first hard try at raising an identity that is different from the one you are standing in. It is planted into ground the old states still hold, the states that cannot carry it fall away, and the one who has to give it ground holds back because of a fear.

Praise goes down, and the first two states do not carry his name

The BBE says Judah "went away from his brothers" (38:1). The KJV and the YLT both read "went down". It comes straight after chapter 37, where Judah says "What profit is there in putting our brother to death and covering up his blood?" and "Let us give him to these Ishmaelites for a price" (37:26-27). The one named "He shall add" is sold, and Praise goes down from the other voices. That is the first step of the pattern on the Joseph page: down.

He sees a woman and takes her (38:2), and three sons follow. Er means "watchful" and the text gives no deed, only "evil in the eyes of the Lord" (38:7). It does not say what the evil was, so this page does not either. Onan means "strong", and here the reason is given. When he is told to "get offspring for your brother" he sees "that the offspring would not be his" and lets the seed "go on to the earth" (38:9). Genesis 1:11 is the rule that a seed gives back after its kind (Lesson 7). Here the seed goes to the earth and nothing is carried from it, for the reason the text gives: "so that he might not get offspring for his brother". Both sons are put to death in the same words (38:7, 38:10).

The Request is held back by what is in his mind

Shelah means "request", and the place he is born is named Chezib, "falsified" (38:5). Judah then says to Tamar "keep yourself as a widow till my son Shelah becomes a man". The next clause gives what he actually holds: "he had in his mind the thought that death might come to him as it had come to his brothers" (38:11). A promise is on the outside and a fear is on the inside. The place name says what the filing is. The court gives after its kind (Rule One), and it works from what is held, so the same verse records the promise and the fear behind it. Job runs the same mechanism: a fear acted out.

The BBE says "go back to your father's house". Genesis 2:24 has the man go away from the father and be joined to the wife. The woman here is sent to the father's house and kept from the joining, which is the rule backwards (Lesson 8).

Palm Tree reads the time and sits at the opening of Two Eyes

Tamar is a palm tree, and Strong's gives the palm as "from an unused root meaning to be erect". Psalm 92:12 is on the list of uses of the palm word (tamar, H8558), where "the righteous" is set beside the palm. The BBE hides the palm there ("The good man will be like a tall tree in his strength"), but the Hebrew list shows the word. So an erect tree is the name of the person who is later called upright.

The text says what she does and why: "she saw that Shelah was now a man, but she had not been made his wife" (38:14). She reads the time. Then she takes off the widow's clothing, covers herself with a veil, and sits "near Enaim on the road to Timnah". The Hebrew reads "in the opening of Enayim" (petach, H6607, "opening, doorway"), as the YLT has it, and the BBE reads "near". Enayim is the dual of ayin, "eye" or "spring", so "two eyes" or "two springs". Timnah is "a portion assigned". She sits at the opening of Two Eyes on the road to what is assigned.

Then the eyes are the question. Judah "took her to be a loose woman of the town, because her face was covered" (38:15), and "he had no idea" who she was (38:16). That is the judging part choosing by what it sees, as on the Nephilim page. The verb for seeing in 38:2, 38:14 and 38:15 is ra'ah (H7200), the word of Genesis 1:4, where the court sees the light and that it is good. Judah sees, and he does not know. The Hebrew behind "had no idea" in 38:16 is yada, "to know" (H3045), the same verb as 38:9 ("Onan knew") and 38:26 ("he knew her again no more"). Seeing comes first in Genesis 1 and the verdict follows. Here the seeing is by the covered face, so the first verdict is wrong, and it is mended only when he is made to recognise.

The pledge is Judah's own

She asks for "a sign" until the young goat is sent (38:17). The Hebrew is arabon (H6162), "a pledge", and the BBE says "sign" each time. She asks for the ring, its cord, and the stick in his hand (38:18). The ring is a seal (chothemeth, H2858, used only here, in 38:25, from chatham, "to seal"). The stick is matteh (H4294), which means "staff", "branch" and "tribe". Its first use in the Bible is Genesis 38:18, and Exodus 31:2 uses the same word in "the tribe of Judah". So what she holds is the mark that makes a thing his, and the one word that means his staff and his tribe.

Judah sends the young goat by Hirah, "to get back the things which he had given as a sign", and the woman "was not there" (38:20). The men of the place say "There was no such woman there" (38:21). The Hebrew word there is not the one in 38:15 and 38:24. It is qedeshah (H6948), five uses in four verses (38:21 twice, 38:22, Deuteronomy 23:17, Hosea 4:14), which Strong's gives as "a female devotee, i.e. prostitute" and the YLT renders "the separated one". The BBE uses "loose woman" for both words. Judah thinks one thing and sends for another, and he cannot find either. His answer is "Let her keep the things, so that we may not be shamed" (38:23). The pledge stays on file with her.

The court rules on what is on file

Three months on, word comes to Judah, and he says "Take her out and let her be burned" (38:24). The BBE says "being taken out" in 38:25 and "came out" in 38:29 and 38:30, and the Hebrew in all three is the same root, yatsa. The woman who is brought out is the one out of whom two come out. (38:24 and 38:28 were not checked in the Hebrew.)

She does not argue. She sends the pledge and says "say then, whose are this ring and this cord and this stick?" (38:25). The Hebrew is hakker-na, "recognise, please" (nakar, H5234). It is the exact words Judah's brothers used on Jacob with Joseph's coat, "is it your son's coat or not?" (37:32), which the BBE gives without the verb. Jacob "saw that it was" (37:33), and the same verb appears again when Joseph recognises his brothers and they do not recognise him (42:8). The question Judah put to his father comes back to him.

Then "Judah said openly that they were his" (38:26). The same verb. He says: "She is more upright than I am, for I did not give her to Shelah my son." The Hebrew verb is tsadaq (H6663), forty-one uses, and this is the first. The second is Genesis 44:16, and it is Judah again. The first two uses of the word in the Bible are both his. In Genesis 4 and Genesis 3 the court asks and the man answers. Here the judge puts his own filing on the table and amends it: not "she is a harlot", but "I did not give her to Shelah". He states what was actually filed.

The verse ends with "he had no more connection with her" (BBE), where the Hebrew is the same yada again (38:26, H3045). The Genesis 4 verses use the same English ("had connection with", 4:1, 4:17, 4:25), but the Hebrew there was not compared.

The breach and the rising

"There were two children in her body" (38:27). One puts out a hand, the woman ties a red thread on it ("scarlet thread" in the KJV) and says "This one came out first". Then he takes his hand back and his brother comes first (38:28-29). The woman says "What an opening you have made for yourself!" and the name is Perez, perets, "breach" (H6557, from H6556, "a break"). The reason for the name is in the same verse, as Babel's is on the front page. The one with the thread is Zerah, which the Blue Letter Bible glosses "rising" (H2226). The thread is what marks him, and the one marked first comes out second.

This is the third step of the pattern. Down (Judah), held (the Request, the widow at her father's house), brought out. And what comes out after the amendment is two, one of them a breach.

What Judah does next

The pledge word has a source. Arabon (H6162) is "from arab (H6148, in the sense of exchange)", and its only uses are 38:17, 38:18 and 38:20. The verb arab has 23 uses, and the first two are Genesis 43:9 and 44:32, both Judah: "I myself will be surety for him" (43:9). In 44:32 the BBE says "I made myself responsible for the boy to my father". The man who left a pledge in Tamar's hand becomes the pledge for Benjamin. In 44:33 he says "let me be my lord's servant here in place of the boy" (the BBE says "in place of" in 4:25 too), and the opposite answer to Cain's "am I my brother's keeper?" (4:9). And in 44:16 the same tsadaq comes back in his own mouth: "how may we put ourselves right in his eyes?" This page does not say that 38:26 caused this. It says the text puts these words in this order, on one man, and that his name means praise, "To you, Judah, will your brothers give praise" (49:8).

The names and places, with the meanings put in

The method on Lesson 9 is to find the name in Strong's, put the meaning in place of the title, and watch the story run in line with it. The text spells out two of the reasons itself. Judah: "This time I will give praise to the Lord: so he was named Judah" (29:35). Perez: "What an opening you have made for yourself! So he was named Perez" (38:29).

Where this fits in the course

Every story is the Genesis 1 scene, changed (Lesson 5). Here the ground names the state before anyone stands on it: A Portion Assigned, Two Eyes, Falsified. Rule One says what is planted gives back its own kind (Lesson 7), and the chapter turns on the Genesis 1 word zera. Rule Two says to leave the old state and join the new identity (Lesson 8), and the woman here is sent back to the father's house, which is why the whole chapter is about a joining that is held off. Lesson 9 says the name shows the state (Lesson 9), and the names here run in order: Praise, Watchful, Strong, Request, Palm Tree, Breach, Rising. The rods page tests its rule against Genesis 38 as well. The Emmaus page has the same turn: the one beside you is not known, then the knowing lands on the identity. There the eyes open at the bread, and here Judah is made to recognise the pledge.

A question to ask of any story: whose seal, cord and staff are on file, and what does the one holding the file say when the question is put back to the judge?

Remember: Praise goes down, plants a promise with a fear behind it, and holds the Request back. Palm Tree, kept at the father's house, reads the time, sits at the opening of Two Eyes, and holds his own seal, cord and staff. Brought out to be burned, she asks him to recognise them. He uses the verb he used on Joseph's coat, and says "She is more upright than I am". What comes out of the one who was brought out is Breach and Rising.

Where the link rests on English only: the goat. 38:17, 38:20 and 38:23 use "young goat", and 37:31 has "a young goat" too, but the Hebrew in 37:31 was not compared. The same is true of "no idea" in 4:9 (the Hebrew in 38:16 is yada) and "connection" (38:26 and 4:1, 4:17, 4:25). The Hebrew in Genesis 3:7 for the eyes opening was not compared with petach. Genesis 3:15 and 38:27 are an echo, not a word match. The "in place of" in 44:33 and 4:25 is BBE English only.
