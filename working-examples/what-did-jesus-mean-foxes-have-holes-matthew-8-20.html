What Did Jesus Mean by 'Foxes Have Holes'? Matthew 8:18-22

"The foxes have holes, and the birds of heaven have a resting-place; but the Son of man has nowhere to put his head" is Matthew 8:20, and the same saying is in Luke 9:58. People ask whether Jesus is saying he was homeless, or that following him means being poor. In this course the scene is read as taking place inside the reader. Existing-One-is-salvation is you aware right now, the voices that step forward are your own, and the saying is the answer to a promise one of them makes. The saying then compares the creatures, which each have a place in the scene, with the human being, who has none yet. Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The passage (BBE, Matthew 8:18-22):

Now when Jesus saw a great mass of people about him, he gave an order to go to the other side. (8:18) And there came a scribe and said to him, Master, I will come after you wherever you go. (8:19) And Jesus said to him, The foxes have holes, and the birds of heaven have a resting-place; but the Son of man has nowhere to put his head. (8:20) And another of the disciples said to him, Lord, let me first go and give the last honours to my father. (8:21) But Jesus said to him, Come after me; and let the dead take care of their dead. (8:22) — Matthew 8:18-22 (BBE)

The same verses with the meanings put in:

Now when Existing-One-is-salvation saw a great mass of people about him, he gave an order to go to the other side. (8:18) And there came a writer and said to him, Master, I will follow you wherever you go. (8:19) And Existing-One-is-salvation said to him, The foxes have burrows, and the birds of heaven have an encamping-place; but the Son of the human being has nowhere to put his head. (8:20) And another of the learners said to him, Lord, let me first go and give the last honours to my father. (8:21) But Existing-One-is-salvation said to him, Follow me; and let the dead take care of their dead. (8:22)

The meanings used:

  • Jesus (Iesous, G2424): from the Hebrew Yehoshua, "Jehovah is salvation". Jehovah is YHVH, the Existing One (Lesson 2), so the name reads Existing-One-is-salvation (see Lesson 19).
  • Scribe (grammateus, G1122): "a writer, i.e. (professionally) scribe or secretary".
  • Follow (akoloutheo, G190): "to follow one who precedes, join him as his attendant". The BBE gives it as "come after".
  • Hole (pholeos, G5454): "a burrow or lurking-place". Resting-place (kataskenosis, G2682): "an encamping, i.e. (figuratively) a perch".
  • Man (anthropos, G444): "man-faced, i.e. a human being".
  • Disciple (mathetes, G3101): "a learner, i.e. pupil".

Who is in the scene

The text names a great mass of people, a writer, another learner and the one they speak to. In this course's reading, where the many voices are gathered under one present awareness (see the court language), all of them are in you.

Existing-One-is-salvation (8:18)You aware right now (Lesson 2)

A writer (8:19)The voice that promises in words
Another of the learners (8:21)The voice that puts the old state first
The Son of man (8:20)The one who claims (Lesson 6)

The text says only "a great mass of people". Reading them as voices in you is this course's reading, not something the verse states.

How to read it, step by step:

  1. The order. A great mass of people is around Existing-One-is-salvation, and he gives an order to go to the other side (8:18). The order is to cross. The text then says "his disciples went after him" (8:23, BBE) and does not say the crowd did. The writer is not named again after 8:20, and the text never says whether he went. So the many voices gather round, and only some of them go.
  2. The writer's promise. The word for scribe means "a writer", a man of words. He says: "I will come after you wherever you go" (8:19, BBE). His promise has no condition in it, only "wherever". It is the voice in you that promises in words. The verb "follow" is the same at 8:19, 8:22 and 8:23: he promises it, he is told it, and the learners do it.
  3. He is answered with a list of places. Existing-One-is-salvation does not say yes or no, as in Matthew 22, where the question is also turned into a different one. He names who has what: "The foxes have holes, and the birds of heaven have a resting-place" (8:20, BBE). Both creatures come from the Genesis 1 list. The fox is a land creature, "the beast of the earth after its sort" (Genesis 1:25, BBE), and its place is a burrow. The bird is one of the "winged bird" made "after its sort" (Genesis 1:21, BBE), flying "under the arch of heaven" (1:20, BBE), and its place is an encamping-place, a perch pitched for a while. So each has a place that suits its kind. This is Rule One at work: each thing gives back what it is, and a state comes with its identity.
  4. The human being has no kind-place. In Genesis 1 the creatures are made first, each after its sort, and then comes "let him have rule over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the air and over the cattle" (Genesis 1:26, BBE). The man is made last and set over them, not placed among them as one more kind. A kind has a den because a kind is fixed. The one who claims an identity has no den of his own to go back to, because his place is the identity he is holding. So "nowhere to put his head" means there is no old state to retreat to.
  5. The second voice puts the old state first. "Lord, let me first go and give the last honours to my father" (8:21, BBE). The father is the thing named in Genesis 2:24 (BBE): "For this cause will a man go away from his father and his mother and be joined to his wife; and they will be one flesh". The rule there is leave, then join. The answer is "Come after me; and let the dead take care of their dead" (8:22, BBE). The old state looks after itself and does not need you in it. Leave first, then follow.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. That picture is a scene of pleasure. The ground has a name before anyone stands on it (Eden, "pleasure"), the creatures fill it each after their kind, and the man is put on the ground and given rule over them. This saying is that scene with one part missing.

PartGenesis 1-2This scene
The groundNamed before anyone stands on it: Eden, "pleasure" (Genesis 2:8, Lesson 5)No ground is named in the passage, only "the other side" (8:18)
The creatures after their sortLand creatures "after their sort" (1:24-25), and "every sort of winged bird" (1:21)The foxes have burrows and the birds have an encamping-place. Each keeps the place that fits its kind (8:20).
The man"there he put the man whom he had made" (2:8), with rule over "the fish of the sea and over the birds of the air and over the cattle" (1:26)"nowhere to put his head" (8:20). He has no stand.

In Genesis the creatures are settled in their places before the man arrives, and then he is put on the ground and set over them. In 8:20 the creatures' half of the picture is already in order. The man's half is not. The writer's "wherever" and the second voice's "first" are two attempts to find him a stand, and Existing-One-is-salvation answers neither with a place. He gives a list of the creatures' places, and tells both voices to follow.

The same chapter goes straight into a storm on the sea (8:24). The learners say "destruction is near" (8:25, BBE), and he says "Why are you full of fear" (8:26, BBE). Then he speaks, there is a great calm (8:26), and the men ask "What sort of man is this, that even the winds and the sea do his orders?" (8:27, BBE). In this course's reading, the man with no stand still has the rule Genesis 1:26 gives him over the sea. What he lacks is the ground.

Where this sits in the storyline

This is the middle of the story, not the end. In this course's reading, every later story is the scene of pleasure changed, and the reader is working back toward it. Matthew 8:20 shows how far along that work is. The creatures are in place, the man is not yet standing on anything, and the identity that is the way out, Existing-One-is-salvation, has not been given its ground.

The scene is finished near the end of the Bible. "And I saw the holy town, new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, like a bride made beautiful for her husband" (Revelation 21:2, BBE). The sea is gone: "there was no more sea" (21:1, BBE). The place is made with men: "the Tent of God is with men, and he will make his living-place with them" (21:3, BBE). And the word is "See, I make all things new" (21:5, BBE). So 8:20 is the reader on the way, still short of that place, and Revelation is where the ground arrives.

Remember: the creatures in the saying each have a place that suits their kind, taken from the Genesis 1 list. The man is the missing part of the picture: he is made last and set over them, yet he has nowhere to put his head. The voice that says "wherever" and the voice that says "first" are both told to leave and follow. This is the middle of the story, and the scene only comes down in Revelation.
