What Does "Set Me as a Seal on Your Heart" Mean? Song of Solomon 8:6-7, Word by Word

In Song of Solomon 8:6 the speaker asks to be set as a seal on a heart and on an arm. The BBE says "sign" where most Bibles say "seal". The Hebrew word is chotham, a stamp ring that carries a name. The verse then gives its reasons: love is as strong as death, its claim is as hard as the underworld, water cannot put it out, and no amount of wealth can buy it. This page goes through those words one at a time, then through the two vineyards in 8:11-12 that answer the verse. It also reads the verse where it sits, in the last chapter of the Song, which looks back to the vine-garden she did not keep in 1:6 and on to the voice that is asked for in 8:13.

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. The course model is in Lesson 1: the Existing One (YHVH, "the Lord" in the BBE) offers an identity, I AM is that identity, and the Judges (Elohim) make it real. Lesson 8 gives the rule for taking on an identity: leave the old state and be joined to the new one. Lesson 9 reads names and places by their meaning. In this reading the people in the Song are the reader's own inner parts. The one addressed is the reader whatever your gender, and the woman who asks to be set as a seal is the identity being taken on (see the woman as the assumed identity).

The short answer: to be set as a seal is to be the name stamped on the heart. A seal (chotham) is the thing that carries a name and makes a paper, a stone or a person belong to the name on it. In Exodus 28 the names of the twelve tribes are cut "as on a stamp" and carried over Aaron's heart, where the Judges' decisions are carried too (28:29-30). So the request is to be the identity the heart carries. The reasons that follow say the claim cannot be undone: love is as strong as death (8:6), water cannot put it out and wealth cannot buy it (8:7). Verses 11-12 show the other side. Solomon's vineyard is let out to keepers for silver, and hers is her own and not for hire. Read inside the whole Song, the verse is the turn between two lines about one vineyard: "my vine-garden I have not kept" (1:6) and "My vine-garden, which is mine, is before me" (8:12).

The verses, one line each

  • 7:10. "I am for my loved one, and his desire is for me" (BBE). The verse before the chapter opens, and the last verse of chapter 7.
  • 8:1-2. "Oh that you were my brother, who took milk from my mother's breasts!" and "I would take you by the hand into my mother's house" (BBE). The chapter opens on family words: brother, mother, mother's house.
  • 8:4. "I say to you, O daughters of Jerusalem, do not let love be moved till it is ready" (BBE).
  • 8:5. "Who is this, who comes up from the waste places, resting on her loved one? It was I who made you awake under the apple-tree, where your mother gave you birth" (BBE).
  • 8:8-9. "We have a young sister, and she has no breasts; what are we to do for our sister in the day when she is given to a man?" and "if she is a wall, we will make on her a strong base of silver; and if she is a door, we will let her be shut up with cedar-wood" (BBE).
  • 8:10. "I am a wall, and my breasts are like towers; then was I in his eyes as one to whom good chance had come" (BBE).
  • 8:13. "You who have your resting-place in the gardens, the friends give ear to your voice; make me give ear to it" (BBE).
  • 8:14. "Come quickly, my loved one, and be like a roe on the mountains of spice" (BBE).

The three verses this page rests on:

The meanings used

  • Put, set (suwm, H7760): to put, place, set. The command is masculine singular, so it is spoken to one man.
  • Seal (chotham, H2368): "a signature-ring", from chatham (H2856), "to seal". Fourteen uses in thirteen verses (listed below). BDB: seal or signet-ring.
  • Heart (leb, H3820) and arm (zeroa, H2220): the two places the seal is set.
  • Love (ahabah, H160), strong (az, H5794), death (maveth, H4194): "love is strong as death".
  • Jealousy, zeal (qinah, H7068): "jealousy or envy", from qana (H7065). Blue Letter Bible's outline lists ardour, zeal and jealousy, including a husband's jealous disposition. The KJV renders it jealousy (25 times), zeal (9) and envy (8). The BBE says "wrath" here.
  • Hard, cruel (qasheh, H7186) and Sheol (sheol, H7585): the BBE says "bitter as the underworld".
  • Coals, flames (resheph, H7565, twice; esh, H784, "fire"; shalhebeth, H7957, "a flare of fire"): the burning words of 8:6.
  • Solomon (Shelomoh, H8010): "peaceful", from shalom (H7965). 293 uses.
  • Jerusalem (Yerushalaim, H3389): Strong's "founded peaceful", from yarah (H3384) and shalam (H7999). Blue Letter Bible's outline gives "teaching of peace". In 8:4 it is the home of the daughters who are told not to stir up love.
  • Baal-hamon (Baal Hamon, H1174): "possessor of a multitude", from baal (H1167) and hamon (H1995). One use only, Song of Solomon 8:11, as the place of Solomon's vineyard.
  • Desire (teshuqah, H8669): a stretching out after, a longing. Three uses in the whole Bible: Genesis 3:16, Genesis 4:7 and Song of Solomon 7:10.
  • Moved, awake (uwr, H5782): to rouse, to wake. It is the verb behind "moved" in 8:4 and "awake" in 8:5.
  • Despise (buz, H936): to hold in contempt. It is in 8:1 ("looked down on") and again in 8:7, where the BBE says the price would be "judged ... not great enough".
  • Waters, rivers (mayim, H4325; nahar, H5104): the Hebrew of 8:7 is "many waters" and "rivers". The BBE's "deep waters" is the word for rivers. The deep of Genesis 1:2 is a different word (tehom, H8415), and it is not in 8:7. Put out is kabah (H3518), to quench.
  • House (bayit, H1004) and substance (hon, H1952, wealth): the "mother's house" of 8:2 and the "substance of his house" of 8:7.
  • Shut up (tsur, H6696): to confine, to enclose (8:9).
  • Peace (shalom, H7965): the Hebrew behind "good chance" in 8:10. Shulammite (H7759, in 6:13) comes from shalam (H7999), the second half of Jerusalem's name.
  • Keeper (natar, H5201): to guard. Nine uses in the Bible, four of them in this Song: 1:6 (twice), 8:11 and 8:12.
  • Garden (gan, H1588), voice (qol, H6963), hear (shama, H8085) and friends (chaverim, H2270, companions): the words of 8:13. Come quickly is berach (H1272) and spice is besamim (H1314) in 8:14.
  • YHVH (H3068): the existing One. Elohim (H430): the Judges, as in the other worked examples (Why is "God" plural in Genesis 1?).

How to read it, step by step

  1. Start with the ground. The chapter names two places and a king. Jerusalem is "founded peaceful" (8:4). Solomon is "peaceful" (8:11). Baal-hamon, where his vine-garden stands, is "possessor of a multitude" (8:11). With the meanings put in, 8:4 and 8:11 read: do not let love be moved, in the Founded-Peaceful; Peaceful had a vine-garden in Possessor-of-a-multitude. These are the questions of Lesson 5: what the ground is called, who stands on it, and what has changed.
  2. Read the verse before the chapter (7:10). "I am for my loved one, and his desire is for me" (BBE). "Desire" is teshuqah, used three times in the Bible. In Genesis 3:16 (BBE) it is "still your desire will be for your husband, but he will be your master". In Genesis 4:7 it is the desire at the door that Cain is told to rule. Song of Solomon 7:10 is the third use, and the only one with no ruling in the verse: the belonging goes both ways in one line. The request of 8:6 comes straight after it.
  3. Read the chapter's opening (8:1-2). The first words are a wish that he were her brother, who took milk from her mother's breasts, so she could take him into her mother's house. The words are brother, mother and mother's house. That is the family state, the one Lesson 8 says is left. The Song set it up in 1:6: "my mother's children were angry with me; they made me the keeper of the vine-gardens" (BBE). So 8:1-2 is a wish to be taken into the house that put her to work in vines that were not hers. The word "looked down on" is buz, and it comes back in 8:7.
  4. Read the verse before (8:5). A woman comes up from "the waste places" resting on her loved one, and the BBE then puts a tree, an awakening and a birth in one verse: "It was I who made you awake under the apple-tree, where your mother gave you birth." The ground of 8:6 is a place of waking and birth. Two more links are in that verse. The verb for "awake" is uwr, the same verb as "moved" in the refrain of 8:4. That refrain began in 2:7 ("by the roes of the field, do not let love be moved till it is ready") and came again in 3:5 ("let not love be moved till it is ready"), and in 8:4 the roes are gone. And the apple-tree is the one he was named for in 2:3: "As the apple-tree among the trees of the wood, so is my loved one among the sons." The same Hebrew question, "Who is this?", is asked at 3:6, 6:10 and 8:5. In 3:6-7 the one who comes up from the waste places has "pillars of smoke" and "sixty men of war" about the bed of Solomon. In 8:5 there is only her loved one to rest on.
  5. Read the request (8:6). The verb is suwm, "put, set", the same family of word as a thing placed on a spot. The thing set is chotham, a signature-ring. The BBE says "sign" and Exodus 28 in the BBE says "stamp", but the Hebrew is one word. A seal carries a name and makes what bears it belong to that name. The verb suwm is the verb of Genesis 2:8 (BBE: "there he put the man whom he had made") and of Genesis 4:15 (BBE: "the Lord put a mark on Cain so that no one might put him to death"). The one who asks to be set in 8:6 asks for the same placing.
  6. Read the two places. The seal goes "on your heart" and "on your arm". In Exodus 28:29-30 (BBE) Aaron has the names "on the priest's bag over his heart", and the Urim and Thummim go "on Aaron's heart" so that he has "the power of making decisions for the children of Israel before the Lord at all times". So the heart is where the Judges' decisions are carried. The arm is the place of doing. To be set on both is to be the identity held and the identity acted out.
  7. Read the reasons (8:6). The verse goes on "for" love is strong as death. The claim named here is qinah, the same word the course meets for a husband's claim and for the zeal of the Existing One: "The zeal of the Lord of hosts will do this" (Isaiah 9:7 and 2 Kings 19:31 in the word list). The comparison is Sheol, the underworld, the end that is not argued with. Coals and flames of fire close the verse. A claim like that is not weakened by asking. Death and fire are both Genesis words. The one penalty Genesis states is "death will come to you" (3:3, BBE), and the way to the tree of life is kept by "a flaming sword turning every way" (3:24, BBE). In 8:6 the fire keeps what was set.
  8. Read the water and the price (8:7). Much water cannot put it out and the deep waters cannot overcome it. If a man gave all the substance of his house for it, the price would still be judged "not great enough". Water is what puts a fire out, and wealth is what buys things. The verse says neither works on this. The Hebrew behind "much water" is mayim, the waters of Genesis 1:2, and "deep waters" is neharot, rivers, as in the river of Genesis 2:10 that goes out of Eden to water the garden. So the waters of Genesis 1 and the river of Genesis 2 are both named, and neither puts it out. The Song had already called her "a spring of living waters" (4:15, BBE). The price is the house again. "House" is the same word as "my mother's house" in 8:2, and "not great enough" is buz, to despise. In 8:1-2 the house was where she would not be looked down on. In 8:7 it is the house's wealth that is despised.
  9. Read the wall and the door (8:8-10). The family speaks: "We have a young sister, and she has no breasts; what are we to do for our sister in the day when she is given to a man?" (8:8, BBE). Their plan is built from outside: a strong base of silver if she is a wall, cedar-wood and "shut up" if she is a door (8:9). "Shut up" is tsur, to confine. In 4:9 and 4:12 "sister" stood beside "bride": "A garden walled-in is my sister, my bride" (4:12, BBE). In 8:8 it is the family's word for her. She answers in her own voice: "I am a wall, and my breasts are like towers; then was I in his eyes as one to whom good chance had come" (8:10). The Hebrew behind "good chance" is shalom, peace, the root of Solomon (H8010) and of Shulammite (H7759). So the family puts a silver base on her from outside, and she says she is the wall herself and has found peace in his eyes. The first boundary in Genesis is "a solid arch stretching over the waters, parting the waters from the waters" (1:6, BBE), a boundary that is set. It is a different Hebrew word from the wall here, so this page rests on the picture only. The sisters, the silver and the cedar boards are taken word by word in the next section.
  10. Read the vineyards (8:11-12). Peaceful lets out his vine-garden to keepers, and each must give a thousand bits of silver for its fruit. She says "My vine-garden, which is mine, is before me". He keeps his thousand and the keepers their two hundred. His is a vineyard worked for silver. Hers is not let out. This is the answer to 8:7: what could not be bought with a house is not rented for silver either. The word for "keepers" is natar, four uses in this Song and all of them about vineyards: 1:6 twice ("they made me the keeper of the vine-gardens; but my vine-garden I have not kept"), 8:11 and 8:12. So 8:12 answers 1:6. In 1:6 she keeps other people's vines and has not kept her own. In 8:12 her own is "mine" and "before me". Both lines carry the Hebrew word for "my own" (sheli). This is day three of Genesis 1, fruit after its sort (1:11).
  11. Read the voice and the call (8:13-14). "You who have your resting-place in the gardens, the friends give ear to your voice; make me give ear to it" (8:13, BBE). The Hebrew is feminine throughout, so the voice asked for is the identity's. The three words, garden, voice and hear, are the three of Genesis 3:8-10, where the man hides among the trees and says "Hearing your voice in the garden, I was full of fear" (3:10, BBE). Here nothing hides. The one who rests in the gardens has a voice, the friends give ear to it, and the asking is to hear it. Then the last verse: "Come quickly, my loved one, and be like a roe on the mountains of spice" (8:14, BBE). In 2:17 the same figure stood on "the mountains of Bether", the word for pieces (see the Song 1 to 2 page). In 8:14 the mountains are "spice", and in 4:6 they were "the mountain of myrrh". The roe is a land creature of day six (Genesis 1:24-25), the creature called as witness in 2:7 and 3:5 and left out of 8:4. The Song does not end on the seal being asked for. It ends with the vineyard hers and a call to come.

The sisters, the wall and the cedar boards (8:8-10)

This is the part of the chapter that puzzles most readers: who is speaking, why the young sister has no breasts, and why she is to be shut in with cedar. The text names the speakers only as "we", and the one they speak of is one "young sister". Here are the words.

  • "We have a young sister" (8:8). Sister is achoth (H269) and young is qetannah (H6996), little or small. The family voice does not name itself. It is the same family voice as "my mother's children" in 1:6, who set her to keep vines that were not hers. That link is this course's reading, since the text does not say who "we" are.
  • "She has no breasts" (8:8, shadayim, H7699). They name her by what she lacks. In 8:10 she answers with the opposite: "my breasts are like towers" (migdal, H4026, tower).
  • "In the day when she is given to a man" (8:8). The BBE says "given". The Hebrew verb is dabar (H1696), to speak, so the line is the day she is spoken for. It is the family who speak about her, and the verse goes straight on to say what they will build.
  • The wall and the door (8:9). Wall is chomah (H2346) and door is delet (H1817). Both are lines in the family's plan: one answer if she is a wall, another if she is a door.
  • The silver base (8:9). The Hebrew says "we will build upon her" (banah, H1129) a tirah (H2918) of silver. The BBE says "base". The interlinear glosses tirah as a tower or palace, and the KJV reads "palace". It is something built on top of the wall, and its material is silver, the old house's money in this chapter.
  • The cedar boards (8:9). "Shut up" is tsur (H6696), to enclose. What she is enclosed with is luach (H3871) of erez (H730). The BBE says "cedar-wood". Luach is "a tablet (as polished), of stone, wood or metal" in Strong's. It is used 43 times in 33 verses, and this is how it is used elsewhere: the tablets of stone in Exodus (24:12, 31:18, 32:15-16, 34:1, 34:4, 34:28-29), in Deuteronomy 4:13, 5:22 and 9 to 10, in 1 Kings 8:9 and in 2 Chronicles 5:10; the boards of the altar (Exodus 27:8, 38:7); the plates of 1 Kings 7:36; the ship boards of Ezekiel 27:5; something written on a tablet (Isaiah 30:8, Habakkuk 2:2); the tablet of the heart (Proverbs 3:3, 7:3, Jeremiah 17:1); and Song of Solomon 8:9.

The word the BBE turns into "cedar-wood" is a tablet. This is what a tablet is in the rest of the Bible: the thing a name or a law is written on. The family plan is to enclose her with tablets and a built tower of silver. This course reads that as a writing put on from outside by the old house, and it sets it against 8:6, where the seal is set by the identity on the heart. The same Hebrew word, luach, is used for the tablet of the heart (Proverbs 3:3, 7:3, Jeremiah 17:1). So the chapter has two writings: the old house's tablet around her, and the name set on the heart. The text does not say this in so many words. It puts the two next to each other, and the BBE hides the tablet.

The cedar has already appeared in the Song. Erez is the word in 1:17: "Cedar-trees are the pillars of our house" (BBE). There it is the pillars of the house the pair share. In 8:9 it is what the family would use to shut her in. It is the same wood in two houses.

Her answer is the wall. "I am a wall, and my breasts are like towers" (8:10). The wall is chomah, the same Hebrew word the family used in 8:9, and she speaks it of herself. The family would build a tower of silver on a wall that is theirs to plan. She says she is the wall, and has towers of her own. Then: "in his eyes as one to whom good chance had come" (8:10). The Hebrew is shalom, peace: she is as one who finds peace. The verb in that line is hayah (H1961, "I have become"), the root of the I AM verb in Exodus 3:14. The BBE's "was I" is the verb of becoming.

The wall belongs to the garden picture too. Strong's gives gan (H1588), garden, as "a garden (as fenced)". In 4:12 he said of her "A garden walled-in is my sister, my bride" (BBE), with sister and bride together. In 8:8 the family uses "sister" without "bride". In 8:10 she speaks as the wall herself.

The family's "we" and the building words (Genesis 2:24, 2:22, 4:17 and 11:4)

The "we" and Genesis 2:24. Genesis 2:24 (BBE) says "a man will go away from his father and his mother". The people he leaves are the house he came out of. Chapter 8 is full of that house: "my mother's house" (8:2, bet immi, H1004 and H517), the "substance of his house" (8:7), and a "we" who speak about "our sister" (8:8). The text does not say who "we" are. This course reads them as the voice of the house that is left, because the Song has already named that house and its children: "my mother's children" in 1:6, who set her to work in vines that were not hers. In 2:24 the leaving comes before the joining. In the chapter the seal is asked for first (8:6), and the family speaks after it (8:8-9), still planning for her as if she were theirs to place.

The building verb. The family's plan is a building plan. The Hebrew behind "we will make on her" is nivneh alehah, "we will build upon her". The verb is banah (H1129), to build, and the BBE turns it into "make". It is used 377 times in 345 verses. In Genesis it is used in 16 verses: 2:22, 4:17, 8:20, 10:11, 11:4, 11:5, 11:8, 12:7, 12:8, 13:18, 16:2, 22:9, 26:25, 30:3, 33:17 and 35:7. Three of those are the ones that matter here.

  • Genesis 2:22. The Hebrew is vayiven, "and built". The Existing One, with the Judges acting as one, built the rib taken from the man into a woman (the BBE says "the Lord God" and "made into a woman"). This is the only building in Genesis where the builder is the Existing One with the Judges. Every other use in the list is people building.
  • Genesis 4:17. A town is built.
  • Genesis 11:4. The plural voice builds: nivneh lanu, "let us build for us" a city and a tower (migdal, H4026). The BBE: "let us make a town, and a tower whose top will go up as high as heaven".

So 8:9 and 11:4 are the same Hebrew verb in the same form, "we will build" and "let us build", both said by a plural voice. The woman of Genesis 2:22 was built once, by the Existing One with the Judges. In 8:9 a "we" proposes to build on one who is already built.

The name. In Genesis 11:4 the builders want a name: the Hebrew is shem (H8034), name. In 8:6 the request is to be set as a seal, and a seal is a name set on the heart. This course reads the two as opposite ways to have a name: a name made by the builders for themselves, and a name set on the heart. The text puts a name and a building in both places. It does not say they are opposites.

The house. Strong's says bayit, house (H1004), is "probably from" banah, to build. The BDB calls that derivation dubious, so the page does not depend on it. If it holds, house and build are one family of words. Either way the Song's two buildings are in view: the pair's house, with "Cedar-trees ... the pillars of our house" (1:17), and the family's building on her (8:9). The wall or the door, the cedar and the silver, are the materials of a house.

Which Genesis scene is it? There are two candidates, and the Hebrew supports the second more than the first. The first is the garden shut after the man is sent out: the way to the tree of life is kept (3:24). The family's door "shut up" with boards (8:9) matches that as a picture. The second is the building scene of Genesis 11. That one matches in the Hebrew: the same verb, the same plural form, a tower, and a name. This course reads the family's plan as the Genesis scene run again: a plural voice builds, and a claim is set against it. In Genesis 11 the plan is broken when the Lord comes down (11:5, 11:8). In the Song no judgment is stated against the family. The plan is answered by her own voice (8:10), and by the claim of 8:6-7, which water cannot put out and the substance of a house cannot buy. The page does not say more than the text does.

The tower in the Gospels: Mary Magdalene (John 20)

The tower has a second place in the Bible, and it is a name. The Mary Magdalene page reads the names this way: Mary is Miriam (H4813), "rebellion", and Magdalene (G3094) is "inhabitant of Magdala". Magdala (G3093) is, in Strong's, "of Chaldee origin (compare H4026); the tower". So the name carries the tower, something built up. The Hebrew word for tower, migdal (H4026), is the tower of Genesis 11:4, built by the plural "we", and the towers of 8:10, which she says are her own. So the tower now stands in three places: the one the "we" build (Genesis 11:4), the ones she has (8:10), and the name of a woman in a garden (John 20:1).

Genesis 2:22 says the woman was built. The name Magdalene says built up. The page does not claim more than that the two words sit together. What it can compare is the scene. John 20 is the garden scene, and it has the same parts as the end of Song 8.

The Mary page reads John 20 as the Eve scene played backwards. Set beside Song 8 it adds one thing. In both the woman is in the garden, the voice is heard and nobody hides. In the Song the voice is asked for, and "the friends give ear". In John 20 it is spoken as her name, and she is sent to carry a word to "my brothers". The word for "brother" is English only here: the Song's ach and the Gospel's Greek are not claimed as one word.

This course reads the tower as a built-up state. At Babel it is built by a plural voice to make a name. In the Song it is the one who has been built, in her own voice. In John 20 it is the name of a woman who stays in the garden when the others go back to their houses. The text does not say that these three are one tower. It puts the same word, or a name glossed as that word, in each place.

The garden of Genesis against this chapter

The Song is full of garden words, and Genesis 2 to 4 is where the garden is first set out. Two Hebrew words are in play, and the BBE hides the difference. The vine-garden of 1:6 and 8:11-12 is kerem (H3754), a vineyard. The garden is gan (H1588), "a garden (as fenced)". Gan is used in 13 verses of Genesis (2:8, 2:9, 2:10, 2:15, 2:16, 3:1, 3:2, 3:3, 3:8, 3:10, 3:23, 3:24 and 13:10) and in six verses of the Song: 4:12, 4:15, 4:16, 5:1, 6:2 and 8:13. In the whole Bible it is 42 uses in 37 verses.

Genesis 2 and 3 run: put, work, name, leave, take, hide, voice, desire, sent out, the way kept. The Song comes back to these same things, and its words are not new. What is different is where it ends. In Genesis the voice is heard in the garden and the man hides, and the way back is kept by fire. In the Song the voice is asked for in the gardens (8:13) and the last words are a call to come (8:14). In 8:6 the fire belongs to the seal's claim and is not a guard at a gate.

This is the garden of Genesis rebuilt as a person. In 4:12 she is the garden. In 8:10 she is the wall of it, in 8:12 she has the vineyard, and in 8:13 the one who rests in the gardens has a voice. The keeping, the fence, the water, the fruit and the voice are all in one place, which is what the course reads as an identity that has been taken on and is kept by the court.

Where else the Bible uses these words

The word chotham (H2368) occurs 14 times in 13 verses. All of them were read from the occurrence list.

  • Judah's pledge (Genesis 38:18). Judah gives Tamar his seal, his cord and his staff. These are the signs of who he is, left with the other person.
  • The names cut as on a stamp. Exodus 28:11 and 28:21 (the twelve names on the stones), 39:6 and 39:14 (the stones cut "like a seal" with the names), and the gold plate of 28:36 and 39:30 cut with "HOLY TO THE LORD", that is, holy to the Existing One (BBE, 28:36).
  • Ahab's seal (1 Kings 21:8). Letters written in Ahab's name are sealed with his seal and sent, and the elders act on them.
  • Clay under the seal (Job 38:14). The earth is changed "as clay under the seal". The seal gives the clay its shape. In Job 41:15 the scales are "shut up together with a close seal".
  • The signet that can be pulled off (Jeremiah 22:24). Even if Coniah were the signet on his right hand, he would be pulled off. A seal can be removed. The comparisons of Song 8:6 are the ones that cannot.
  • The chosen one made a ring (Haggai 2:23). "I will take you, O Zerubbabel, my servant... and will make you as a jewelled ring: for I have taken you to be mine" (BBE). The BBE says "jewelled ring" where the Hebrew word is again chotham.
  • The BBE hides the match. For this one Hebrew word it says "sign" (Song 8:6), "stamp" (Exodus 28), "jewelled ring" (Haggai 2:23) and "close seal" (Job 41:15 in the KJV). Read in the Hebrew, the same word runs from Judah's pledge to the names on Aaron's heart to Zerubbabel taken to be mine to the Song.

Qinah (H7068) comes up in 44 verses by the Hebrew word list. Its uses include a husband's jealous claim (Numbers 5:14), the zeal of the Existing One (2 Kings 19:31; Isaiah 9:7, 37:32, 42:13, 59:17), "zeal for your house" (Psalm 69:9) and the Song's only use at 8:6.

What this shows: a seal is a name pressed on a thing so that it belongs to the name. It is put on documents, stones, clay and a chosen servant, and in Exodus 28 it is carried over the heart where decisions are carried. 8:6 asks for that place.

What this adds to the picture

In the course's model the man claims an identity (Lesson 6) and the Judges make it real (Lesson 1). Read that way, 8:6 is the claim itself. The identity asks to be the name the heart carries, and 8:6-7 say what such a claim is like once it is carried: as strong as death, not put out by water, not bought by wealth. The seal gives the shape (Job 38:14) and the Judges keep it, which is Lesson 7: what is stamped comes back as its own kind. The chapter then moves from family words (brother, mother's house) to a seal, which is the leave-and-join rule of Lesson 8 told as a request. The names fit it: Peaceful lets his ground out for silver at Possessor-of-a-multitude, and the one who asks to be sealed keeps her own vineyard.

Read through the whole chapter, the verse is the middle of one movement. It starts with the longing that runs both ways (7:10), goes back through the family house (8:1-2), and arrives at the seal (8:6). After the seal come the two prices, water and wealth (8:7), then the family's wall of silver (8:9) against her own wall (8:10), the let-out vineyard against the vineyard that is hers (8:11-12), and the voice and the call (8:13-14). Each stage uses a word that Genesis used first.

Checking the pattern against the text

The pattern here is the course's own: the claim is carried by the court and comes back after its kind. Each line gives the verse and whether the text agreed.

  • 8:6: the seal is a name that makes a thing belong. It fits. Genesis 38:18, 1 Kings 21:8, Exodus 28 and Haggai 2:23 all use chotham for something that carries a name and a claim.
  • 8:6: the heart is where decisions are carried. It fits Exodus 28:29-30 (BBE): names "over his heart" and "the power of making decisions".
  • 8:6: the claim is enforced. It fits. Qinah is the word used for the zeal of the Existing One that "will do this" (2 Kings 19:31; Isaiah 9:7).
  • 8:7: neither water nor wealth works on it. It fits the verse as written: "Much water may not put out love" and the price is "not great enough".
  • 8:11-12: the two vineyards. They fit. One is let out for silver, and the other is "mine" and "before me".
  • 7:10: the longing is a Genesis word. It fits. Teshuqah is used three times in the Bible: Genesis 3:16, Genesis 4:7 and Song of Solomon 7:10.
  • 8:4-5: the refrain's "moved" and the "awake" of the apple-tree are one verb. It fits. Both are uwr.
  • 8:7: the waters and the river are the Genesis words. It fits for mayim and nahar. It does not fit for "deep": the BBE's "deep waters" is the word for rivers, not tehom of Genesis 1:2, so the page does not lean on that English word.
  • 8:8-10: a boundary built from outside, and a boundary that is her own. This rests on the picture. The wall and the Genesis arch are different Hebrew words.
  • 1:6 and 8:12: the keeper and the vineyard. It fits. Natar is used four times in the Song, all of them of vineyards.
  • 8:13: garden, voice and hear. It fits Genesis 3:8-10, where the same three words are used.
  • 8:9: the cedar boards are tablets. It fits the Hebrew. Luach is "a tablet" in Strong's and is the word for the tablets of stone, and for the tablet of the heart in Proverbs 3:3, 7:3 and Jeremiah 17:1. The BBE's "cedar-wood" does not show this.
  • 8:9 and Genesis 11:4: the family's "we will build" and Babel's "let us build". It fits the Hebrew: nivneh in both, from banah (H1129). The BBE says "make" in both, so the match is hidden in English. The tower word of 11:4 (migdal) is the one in 8:10, but the family's own word in 8:9 is tirah.
  • Genesis 2:22: the woman is "built" by the Existing One with the Judges. It fits (vayiven). This is the only building in Genesis with the Existing One and the Judges as the builder.
  • 8:9-10: wall against wall. It fits. The family's "wall" (8:9) and her "wall" (8:10) are the same Hebrew word, chomah.
  • John 20: Mary Magdalene stays in the garden when the others go to their houses. It fits the BBE: "So then the disciples went away again to their houses" (20:10), and "taking him for the gardener" (20:15). The tower match is in Strong's: G3094 is "feminine of a derivative of G3093", and G3093 is "of Chaldee origin (compare H4026); the tower".
  • Garden words: the garden of Genesis and the garden of the Song are one Hebrew word, gan, 13 verses in Genesis and six in the Song. The vine-garden of 8:11-12 is a different word, kerem, so it is a vineyard and not the garden of Genesis 2. The page does not claim the two are one.
  • Names as codes (Lesson 9): Solomon "peaceful", Jerusalem "founded peaceful", Baal-hamon "possessor of a multitude". The first two fit a chapter that starts with "do not let love be moved till it is ready" (8:4) and a king who lets his ground out.

Remember: to be set as a seal on the heart is to be the name the heart carries. The Hebrew is one word, chotham, from Judah's pledge to the names on Aaron's heart to the Song. Love is then as strong as death, water cannot put it out, and silver cannot buy it or rent it.
