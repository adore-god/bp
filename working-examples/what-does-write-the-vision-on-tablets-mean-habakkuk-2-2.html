What Does "Write the Vision on Tablets" Mean? Habakkuk 2:2 and Isaiah 30:8

And the Lord [the Existing One] gave me an answer, and said, Put the vision in writing and make it clear on stones, so that the reader may go quickly. — Habakkuk 2:2 (BBE)
Now go, put it in writing before them on a board, and make a record of it in a book, so that it may be for the future, a witness for all time to come. — Isaiah 30:8 (BBE)

Two prophets are told to write something down. In the BBE one writes on "stones" and the other on "a board". The Hebrew word is the same in both, luach, the tablet word of the Ten Commandments and of the tablet of the heart (the tablets of stone page). This page goes through both verses word by word, then reads them beside Genesis 1 and the pages you have already seen on the seal, the names and the tablet.

How this page reads the Bible. This course reads every story as having the same three parts: you, the identity you claim, and the judges that make it real, because Genesis 1:26 makes man the one made in the judges' own image (Lesson 6). A name tells you what the state is like before the story starts (Lesson 9). The Existing One (YHVH, "the Lord" in the BBE) is present awareness, and Elohim is the Judges (Lesson 3). Each step below is checked against the verse (BBE) and Strong's, and where a link rests only on a picture the page says so. If this is your first page, The Big Idea is the place to start.

The short answer: in both verses the writing is put on a tablet (luach), and in Habakkuk the verb for "make it clear" is baar, which in Strong's means to dig and, by analogy, to engrave. So the instruction is closer to "engrave the vision on the tablets" than to "make it easy to read", although Strong's also gives "explain" as the last sense. The reason given is that the one who reads it may run with it. The vision then waits for its fixed time (moed) and "will not be false". Isaiah 30:8 gives the same tablet a second purpose: it is to be "a witness for all time to come". In this reading the tablet is where a claim is put on record, and the record stands as a witness until its time arrives.

The verses, one line each

  • Habakkuk 2:1. "I will take my position and be on watch, placing myself on my tower, looking out to see what he will say to me, and what answer he will give to my protest" (BBE).
  • Habakkuk 2:2. "Put the vision in writing and make it clear on stones, so that the reader may go quickly" (BBE).
  • Habakkuk 2:3. "For the vision is still for the fixed time, and it is moving quickly to the end, and it will not be false: even if it is slow in coming, go on waiting for it; because it will certainly come, it will not be kept back" (BBE).
  • Isaiah 30:8. "Now go, put it in writing before them on a board, and make a record of it in a book, so that it may be for the future, a witness for all time to come" (BBE).
  • Isaiah 30:9. "For they are an uncontrolled people, false-hearted, who will not give ear to the teaching of the Lord [the Existing One]" (BBE).
  • Isaiah 49:16. "See, your name is marked on my hands; your walls are ever before me" (BBE).
  • Job 19:23-24. "If only my words might be recorded! if they might be put in writing in a book! And with an iron pen and lead be cut into the rock for ever!" (BBE).

The meanings used

  • Tablet (luach, H3871): "a tablet (as polished), of stone, wood or metal". Habakkuk 2:2 has haluchot, "the tablets", plural. Isaiah 30:8 has luach, singular.
  • Write (kathab, H3789): kethov in Habakkuk 2:2 and kothvah, "write it", in Isaiah 30:8.
  • Vision (chazon, H2377).
  • Make plain (baar, H874): Strong's "to dig; by analogy, to engrave; figuratively, to explain". Three uses in the Bible: Deuteronomy 1:5, Deuteronomy 27:8 and Habakkuk 2:2. The NASB renders it "inscribe" once.
  • Engrave, make a record (chaqaq, H2710): Strong's "to hack, i.e. engrave; by implication, to enact (laws being cut in stone or metal tablets in primitive times)". Nineteen uses in nineteen verses, including Isaiah 30:8, Isaiah 49:16 and Job 19:23.
  • Book, scroll (sefer, H5612).
  • Run (ruts, H7323) and reader, the one calling (qore, H7121): the Hebrew of "so that the reader may go quickly" is lema'an yarutz qore vo, "that he may run who reads it".
  • Fixed time (moed, H4150): "an appointment, i.e. a fixed time or season", and by extension the place of meeting. 223 uses, and the first is Genesis 1:14, as on the Aaron page. The tent of meeting is ohel moed.
  • Will not be false (lo yekhazzev, from kazab, H3576, to lie). The same root is behind Chezib, "falsified" (H3580), the place where Shelah is born on the Judah and Tamar page.
  • Witness (ed, H5707) and testimony (edut, H5715): as on the tablets page. See the open point below on Isaiah 30:8.
  • Rebellion (meri, H4805): the word in Isaiah 30:9 behind "uncontrolled". Strong's gives Miriam (H4813), "rebelliously", as coming from this word, as on the Mary Magdalene page.
  • The names: Habakkuk (H2265) is "embrace" in Strong's, from chabaq (H2263), to embrace, with the letters doubled.
  • YHVH (H3068): the Existing One, the BBE's "the Lord".

How to read it, step by step

  1. The watcher takes his stand (Habakkuk 2:1). The Hebrew gives a guard-post (mishmeret, H4931) and a rampart (matsor, H4692). He watches "to see what he will say to me". The BBE's "my tower" is the rampart word, and this page does not take it for the tower of the Song or of Genesis 11. What it does take is the order: first the watching, then the answer.
  2. The answer is a command to write (2:2). "And the Lord gave me an answer" is vayya'aneni YHVH, and the first word of the answer is kethov, "write". The Existing One does not say how it will come about. It says to put the vision on record. In Genesis 1 the first thing said is "Let there be light" and the next thing is the seeing and the naming. Here the thing seen (chazon) is to be written.
  3. "Make it plain" is the engraving word (2:2). Baar has three uses in the Bible. Deuteronomy 1:5 and 27:8 are the other two (neither was read for this page). Strong's puts "dig" and "engrave" first and "explain" last, and the NASB renders it "inscribe" once. So "write the vision, and engrave it on the tablets" is a fair reading of the Hebrew. In the BBE "make it clear" can be read as being easy to read, and the Hebrew sense is cutting.
  4. The tablets are plural and the same word (2:2). Haluchot is the plural of luach with the article, "the tablets". It is the plural of the same word as luchot in Exodus 31:18, the tablets of the testimony. The tablets page shows that the first tablets are "the writing of Elohim" (Exodus 32:16) and that the record is broken and written again with the same words. Habakkuk is told to do the same kind of thing with a vision: put it on record, and cut it.
  5. The reader runs (2:2). Lema'an yarutz qore vo: "that the one who calls (reads) with it may run". The BBE says "go quickly". The record is not for display. It is for the one who takes it and goes. That is the purpose clause of the verse: the writing is there so that the reader moves.
  6. The vision waits for its moed (2:3). Lamoed is "for the fixed time". Moed is the word of Genesis 1:14 and of ohel moed, the tent that carries a time in its name (the Aaron page, step 1). The lights of day four mark when something is due (Lesson 5), and the vision is held for that kind of time. Then "it breathes toward the end", it "will not lie", "wait for it", "it comes, it will not delay".
  7. It will not lie, and Chezib is a lie (2:3). Lo yekhazzev is from kazab (H3576). The Judah and Tamar page reads Shelah, "request", born at Chezib, "falsified", from the same root: the promise is on the outside, and the fear is in the mind (Genesis 38:5, 38:11). Habakkuk 2:3 is the other side of that picture, a vision that is held to a time and does not lie. The text puts the same root in both places. That the two pictures are meant to answer each other is the course's reading, not the verse's.
  8. Isaiah 30:8 puts it on a tablet "with them". Kothvah al luach ittam: "write it on a tablet with them". Ittam is "with them", so the record is kept in their presence. Then chukkah, "engrave it", ve'al sefer, "and on a book". Two surfaces are named: the tablet and the book. The BBE says "board" and "make a record".
  9. The purpose: a witness (30:8). The record is to be "for the day to come, a witness for all time". The BBE says "witness". The Hebrew word is la'ad (open point below). If it is read as ed, witness, this is the word behind edut, the testimony, the name of the tablets in Exodus 31:18. On the tablets page the tablets are "the tablets of the testimony", a court record. Isaiah 30:8 says that of a tablet and a book that a prophet is told to write: they are to stand as a witness.
  10. Who it is for (30:9). "For" is the opening word of 30:9: the record is written because "they" are am meri, a people of rebellion, banim kechashim, false sons, who "would not hear the teaching (torah) of the Existing One". The Mary Magdalene page gives Miriam, "rebelliously", as coming from meri (H4805). That is the same word, and nothing more is claimed here about it. The Hebrew for what they will not hear is torat YHVH. The BBE says "teaching" for torah here.
  11. The name on the hands (Isaiah 49:16). The BBE says "your name is marked on my hands; your walls are ever before me". The verb is chaqaq, the same engraving word as Isaiah 30:8. The page did not read who is speaking in 49:15-16. What is cut is on the hands, with the walls before the speaker. The seal page has a seal on the heart and on the arm (Song 8:6), and walls in 8:9-10. These are pictures that sit near each other. The Hebrew of 49:16 was not compared, so the link rests on the BBE and the engraving word only.
  12. Job asks for the same (Job 19:23-24). "If only my words might be recorded!... put in writing in a book!... with an iron pen and lead be cut into the rock for ever!" The pen of iron is the tool of Jeremiah 17:1 on the tablets page, and "in a book" is Isaiah 30:8's second surface. The BBE gives the wish. The Hebrew of 19:23-24 was not compared word by word, but chaqaq is on Job 19:23 in the occurrence list.

What changed is who is told to write and what the writing is for. In Exodus the Judges write the testimony. Here a prophet is told to write a vision and put it where it will stand until its time. The tablet is the same word, the engraving is the same kind of work, and the purpose is a record that stands as a witness.

Which part moved?

In the course's reading this is again the record: what the court has on file. Here the record is made by the one who sees. The vision is seen (Habakkuk 2:1), written and engraved (2:2), held for its time (2:3) and kept as a witness (Isaiah 30:8). Three questions can be asked of the scene.

  1. What is the ground, and what does its name mean? A watch-post and a rampart (Habakkuk 2:1). The names of the places in Isaiah 30:6-7 were not looked up on this page.
  2. Who is standing on it, and what does the name mean? Habakkuk, "embrace" (H2265), is the one who watches and is told to write.
  3. What has changed from the Genesis 1 picture? The word that was said is now a vision to be written, put on a tablet, and kept until its fixed time.

Remember: the tablets of the Ten Commandments, the tablet of the heart, the cedar boards of the Song and the tablets of Habakkuk are one word, luach. In Habakkuk the vision is written and engraved on the tablets so the reader can run, and it waits for its fixed time and does not lie. In Isaiah the tablet and the book are to stand as a witness. The BBE hides the word under "stones", "board" and "cedar-wood".
