How Does the Genesis Scene Finish in Acts and Revelation?

The point: Luke and John put back the man: the eyes opened, the breath given. That leaves four things Genesis lost and the Gospels do not finish. The nations were mixed and sent away at Babel. The image was dust. The serpent's seed was at war with the woman's. And the place was cursed, with the way to the tree of life kept shut. Acts answers the first, the letters the second, Revelation 12 the third, and Revelation 20 to 22 the court and the place. The names show it before the story does: Babel means "confusion by mixing" and the language is mixed, Pentecost means "fiftieth" and the harvest comes in, Jerusalem means "founded peaceful" and the people are gathered and the town is built on its bases, and Satan means "opponent" and the opponent is forced down and chained.

In five lines:

  1. Genesis 11 mixes the language and sends the people over all the earth. Acts 2 gathers people from every nation, and each hears in the language he was born to.
  2. The letters name the change: the first man from the earth, the last Adam a life-giving spirit, and a new man made new in knowledge after the image of his maker.
  3. Revelation 12 is the Genesis 3:15 scene again: the old snake, the woman, and "her seed", in the same Greek words the Septuagint uses.
  4. Revelation 20 is the court in session: high seats, the right of judging given, books open, and a book of life.
  5. Revelation 21 and 22 put the place back: no more sea, no more curse, the light before the lights, the river and the tree, and the face seen instead of hidden from.

What is still lost after the Gospels

Genesis does not stop at the man's eyes and breath. In Genesis 3 the Judges name the snake's seed and the woman's seed, and set them against each other (3:15). The ground is cursed on the man's account and thorns come up (3:17-18). The man and his wife hide "away from the eyes of the Lord God" (3:8). The man is sent out, and winged ones and a flaming sword are put "to keep the way to the tree of life" (3:24). Genesis 4 follows with Cain on that cursed ground (Lesson 17). Genesis 11 ends the first run of the story with one language, one great name made by the builders, and a town named Babel, where "the Lord took away the sense of all languages" and sent them away "over all the face of the earth" (11:9). Everything after the Gospels is the scene being put back from there.

The names and places, with the meanings put in

A name in the Bible tells you the state before the story starts, and the story then runs out in line with it (Lesson 9). Genesis 11:9 says so outright: the town is named Babel "because" of what happened there. These are the names and places in Acts and Revelation that carry the plot.

The titles, in plain language

Some words in the comparisons below are titles, not names. A title says what job or position someone holds, and the story then runs out in line with it, the same as with a name. Here they are in plain words.

Oil was poured on the head of a priest (Exodus 29:7) or a king (1 Samuel 10:1, 16:13).A title, not a surname. It means "the one who has been set in the office". In the plot, it is the identity that has been put in place over the scene. Luke 4:18 has it as an act: "he has anointed me".
Jesus"The existing One saves" (see the Revelation 22:12-21 page).A name that states a state: the existing One, you aware right now, is the one who saves. "Jesus Christ" reads as "the existing One saves, the anointed".

In Genesis the BBE's "the Lord" stands for YHVH, "the existing One", you aware right now (Lesson 2). In Acts 2:36 "Lord and Christ" says two things: the one who has the say, and the one who has been set in place.
GodHebrew Elohim, plural. Greek theos (G2316), which Strong's also gives as "figuratively, a magistrate".The Judges and rulers, acting as one (Lesson 3). "The Lord God" in Genesis 3:8 is the Existing One of the Judges: awareness, with the Judges' rule behind it.

Thayer lists "breath of the nostrils or mouth", "the breath of life" (Revelation 11:11, with Genesis 2:7) and "a life-giving spirit" (1 Corinthians 15:45). Holy means set apart (the holy page).Breath. It is the thing that made the man a living being in Genesis 2:7. "Holy Spirit" is breath that has been set apart. In Acts 2 a gathered people receive it with a sound like a wind, and in John 20:22 it is breathed on them.

The old snake, the dragon, the Evil One, SatanSatan is H7854 and G4567, "an opponent", "the accuser". Revelation 20:2 puts the four names side by side as one figure.The opposing voice of Genesis 3, named four ways. "Old" points back to the first snake (the serpent page).

Read Genesis 11 and Acts 2 twice. First as written: one language, a plain, a great name, a town named Babel, and a people sent away; then a day of Pentecost, a gathered people, every nation, and each one hearing his own language. Then with the meanings put in: the whole earth has one language, and settles on a plain in the country of two rivers. The builders say "let us make a great name for ourselves" in a town whose name means confusion by mixing, and the language is mixed and they are sent away (Genesis 11:1-9). In Acts the day is the fiftieth, when the harvest is complete. The gathered people include those from between the rivers, and they hear in the language of their birth. About three thousand are joined to them, in a place whose name means founded peaceful, and they are united together in the breaking of bread (Acts 2:1-5, 2:41-42). The state named at Babel is mixing and the story is mixing. The state named at Pentecost is a harvest in, and the story is a harvest joined.

Acts and the letters, piece by piece

Acts and the lettersThe Genesis scene being put back
Acts 2:1-4: on the day of Pentecost, "the fiftieth", "they were all together in one place", a sound "like the rushing of a violent wind", and they are "talking in different languages, as the Spirit (the breath) gave them power".Genesis 11:1-9, played the other way. At Babel, "confusion by mixing", the people are one and the language is taken from them. Here a gathered people speak, and the languages are many.
Acts 2:5-11: Jews "from every nation under heaven", including "those living in Mesopotamia", and "every one of us is hearing their words in the language which was ours from our birth" (2:8). (Jew is built from Judah, "praise", and "nation" is a massed people: see the Jew, Gentile and -ites page.)Genesis 11:8-9: the people were sent away into "every part of the earth". Now they are gathered out of every part, from the land between the rivers too, and the sense of the language is given back to each one.
Zechariah 8:23: "ten men from all the languages of the nations will put out their hands and take a grip of the skirt of him who is a Jew, saying, We will go with you, for it has come to our ears that God is with you."Acts 2 has the same three things in one place: a Jew, the languages, and the nations. In Genesis 11 the languages divide the people. Here the languages come to one person, and the saying is "the Judges are with you". The Hebrew word for nation in Zechariah is a massed people, as on the Jew and Gentile page.
Acts 2:36-41: "God has made Lord and Christ" (the one who has the say, and the one set in place) the one they put to death, "baptism in the name of Jesus Christ", and about three thousand "were joined to them that day".Genesis 11:4: "let us make a great name for ourselves, so that we may not be wanderers". At Babel the builders make the name. In Acts the name is "Christ", the anointed, the one set in the office, and the people are baptised into it, and they are joined, not scattered.
Acts 2:42: they "were united together in the taking of broken bread and in prayer".The table of Luke 24:30-31, where the eyes were opened over broken bread. Here it is held by a gathered people, not one pair of walkers.
1 Corinthians 15:22: "as in Adam death comes to all, so in Christ will all come back to life".Genesis 3:19 and the dust: Adam, the ruddy man, is of the soil, and the line ends in the ground. Christ, the anointed, answers it.
1 Corinthians 15:45-49: "The first man Adam was a living soul. The last Adam is a life-giving spirit." (The first man is the man of the soil, and the last Adam is the man in whom the breath gives life.) "The first man is from the earth, and of the earth: the second man is from heaven." "We have taken on us the image of the man from the earth, so we will take on us the image of the one from heaven."Genesis 2:7 and 1:26. The man formed from the ground and given breath is "the first man", and the image of Genesis 1:26 is carried on in the second. See the Son of God and Son of man page.
Colossians 3:9-10: "you have put away the old man with all his doings, and have put on the new man, which has become new in knowledge after the image of his maker".Genesis 1:26-27 (the image) and Genesis 3:5-7 (the knowledge). The knowledge that opened the eyes to lack is made new, and it is made "after the image". The court asked where that knowledge came from in Genesis 3:11 (the naked page). "Put on" is also a clothing word, and Genesis 3 has two coverings: the leaves the two sew (3:7) and the skins the Judges make (3:21) (the fig leaves and skins page).

Acts does not make the nations speak one language again. It gives each one the sense of his own. The mixing of Babel is answered by understanding inside the mixture, and the people are gathered by that, in the one place. The letters then say what the gathered people are: a new man after the image, where the first man, the ruddy one, was of the earth.

12:1: "a woman clothed with the sun, and with the moon under her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars".Genesis 1:14-19: the sun, the moon and the stars on the fourth day, now set on a woman. Genesis 3:20: the wife is named Eve, "the mother of all who have life".
12:9 and 12:14-17: "the old snake", named Satan, the opponent, "forced down"; the woman in "the waste land", a river sent out of the snake's mouth, and the dragon going "to make war on the rest of her seed" (12:17).Genesis 3:1-15, the voice and the war between "your seed and her seed" (3:15). Revelation 12:17 has the Greek phrase tou spermatos autēs, "of her seed", and Genesis 3:15 in the Septuagint has the same phrase. The waste land is where Luke began (the temptation page). The snake itself is on the serpent page.
20:1-2: an angel with "the key of the great deep", and the dragon, "the old snake, which is the Evil One and Satan", chained.Genesis 1:2: the deep, the first condition. The deep is held and keyed, as in the Jonah article, where it comes before everything is delivered. The opponent is chained, so the name stops.
20:4: "high seats, and they were seated on them, and the right of judging was given to them". 20:6: they will be "ruling with him".Genesis 1:26-28: the Judges and the rule given to the image. The right of judging is given again, and the rule with it. See Lesson 3.
20:11-15: "a great white seat", the earth and heaven in flight, "no place for them", the dead before the high seat, "the books were open, and another book... the book of life", the sea giving up its dead, and "if anyone's name was not in the book of life" (20:15).The court of the whole Bible, shown at its close: a seat, a record, and a ruling by what is on file (Lesson 1). The first earth and heaven go, so the scene can be built again.
21:1: "a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were gone; and there was no more sea."Genesis 1:1 and 1:9-10: the heaven, the earth, and the gathered waters named Seas, the scene as first built. Here the first is gone and the sea is not there.
21:2-3 and 21:14: the holy town, new Jerusalem, "like a bride made beautiful for her husband", with "twelve bases", and "the Tent of God is with men".Jerusalem, "founded peaceful", is founded: it has bases. The bride is Genesis 2:22-24 (the woman, and leave and join: Lesson 8). Genesis 3:8 is undone: the sound of the Lord God (the Existing One of the Judges) walking in the garden sent the man and his wife into hiding, and here the dwelling is with the people.
21:4: "no more death, or sorrow, or crying, or pain; for the first things have come to an end".Genesis 3:16-19: "Great will be your pain in childbirth... in sorrow will your children come to birth", and the return to dust.
21:23-25: "no need of the sun, or of the moon", for "the glory of God did make it light"; "the nations will go in its light", and "there is no night there".Genesis 1:3: light on the first day, before the sun, moon and stars of the fourth. The nations of Genesis 11 and Acts 2 are now inside the light.
22:1-2: "a river of water of life", and "the tree of life... giving its fruit every month; and the leaves of the tree give life to the nations".Genesis 2:9-10 and 3:22-24: the tree of life, and the way to it kept by winged ones and a flaming sword (the same winged ones that are on the veil, on the temple curtain page). Here the tree is in the middle of the street, with a river each side. Eve, the life-giver, has her name's word in the tree.
22:3: "there will be no more curse".Genesis 3:17: "the earth is cursed on your account". The first ruling on the ground is lifted.
22:4: "they will see his face; and his name will be on their brows".Genesis 3:8-10: the man and his wife go "away from the eyes of the Lord God", and the man says, "I kept myself from your eyes". The hiding is undone, and the name of Acts 2:38 is worn. The hiding and the covering that came before it are on the naked page.
22:5: "no more night", and "they will be ruling for ever and ever".Genesis 1:5 (night named with the day) and Genesis 1:26-28 (rule given to the image). Both stand, with the night gone.
22:12-21: the pay for each one's work, the tree's right, the root and kind, "Come", and the record that is closed.Covered word by word on the Revelation 22:12-21 page.

Revelation does not start with the place. It starts with the war on the woman's seed (12), then sits the court (20), and only after the court does the new heaven and earth come (21:1). The place is built last, once the opponent is chained, the ruling is done and the first things are gone.

What the pieces show together

The Gospels put back the man. Acts and the letters put back the people: gathered out of the scattering, each with the sense of his own language, joined in one name, and made new in knowledge after the image. Revelation puts back what was left: the war on the woman's seed, the court that rules, and the place with its curse lifted, its sea gone, its light kept, its tree open and its face seen. It is the Genesis scene finished in the order it was lost: the man (the Gospels), the nations (Genesis 11, Acts 2), the snake's war (Genesis 3:15, Revelation 12), the ruling (Genesis 1:26, Revelation 20) and the ground (Genesis 3:17, Revelation 22:3).

The names carry it from one end to the other. Babel is mixing, and the story is mixing. Pentecost is the fiftieth day of the harvest, and the story is a harvest joined. Jerusalem is founded peaceful, and the town ends up on its bases. Satan is the opponent, and the opponent is forced down and chained. Adam is the ruddy man of the soil, and the first man is "of the earth". Each piece still comes by the word and not by the hands, as it did in the waste land, and each one grows after its kind from the one before: the man first, the people next, the place last. The end of the Bible is not a new story. It is the first scene with every Genesis 3 word answered.

The words checked, in one place

  • Babel (H894): "confusion (by mixing)", from balal (H1101), "to mix". The word is used 262 times in the Hebrew, and in Genesis only at 10:10 and 11:9. Genesis 11:9 gives the reason in the same verse.
  • Shinar (H8152): used 8 times (Genesis 10:10, 11:2, 14:1, 14:9, Joshua 7:21, Isaiah 11:11, Daniel 1:2, Zechariah 5:11). Strong's says "a plain in Babylonia" and "of foreign derivation", so it gives no Hebrew meaning. The BBE's "stretch of flat country" in Genesis 11:2 is a different word, biqah (H1237, "a plain, a valley", 20 uses), so the plain is not the meaning of Shinar. The Blue Letter Bible outline glosses it "country of two rivers". The Mesopotamia link rests on that gloss and on the common sense of both names, not on one shared word.
  • Mesopotamia (Mesopotamia, G3318): "between the two rivers". Used twice in the New Testament, Acts 2:9 and Acts 7:2.
  • Pentecost (pentēkostē, G4005): "fiftieth", the day counted from Passover. Used three times: Acts 2:1, Acts 20:16 and 1 Corinthians 16:8.
  • Jerusalem (H3389): "founded peaceful" in Strong's, from yarah (H3384) and shalam (H7999). The Blue Letter Bible outline reads "teaching of peace", and Brown-Driver-Briggs marks the root as uncertain. All three have peace in them.
  • Christ (Christos, G5547): "anointed", from chriō (G5548), "to smear or rub with oil, that is, to consecrate to an office". Chriō is used five times in the Textus Receptus (Luke 4:18, Acts 4:27, Acts 10:38, 2 Corinthians 1:21, Hebrews 1:9), and in the Septuagint for the oil on Aaron, Saul and David (Exodus 29:7, 1 Samuel 10:1, 1 Samuel 16:13). It is used in Revelation at 1:1, 1:2, 1:5, 11:15, 12:10, 20:4, 20:6 and 22:21.
  • Lord (kyrios, G2962): "supreme in authority, that is, a controller", from kuros, "supremacy". Used 748 times in the KJV count, in Acts 2:36 and in Revelation 19:1 and 19:6 among them.
  • Spirit (pneuma, G4151): "a current of air, that is, breath (blast) or a breeze", and "by analogy or figuratively, a spirit". Thayer lists "breath of the nostrils or mouth", "the breath of life" (Revelation 11:11, with Genesis 2:7) and "a life-giving spirit" (1 Corinthians 15:45).
  • Adam (H120): "ruddy, that is, a human being", from adam (H119), "to show blood in the face, to flush". Adamah (H127) is "soil (from its general redness)".
  • Satan (H7854, G4567): "an opponent", "the accuser". The Greek is in Revelation at 2:9, 2:13, 2:24, 3:9, 12:9, 20:2, 20:7 and 20:10 and not after that.
  • Sea (thalassa, G2281): Revelation 21:1 has hē thalassa ouk estin eti, "the sea is no more", and the same word is used in 20:13, where the sea gives up its dead. The BBE also says "sea" in "sea of fire" (20:14, 20:15) and "sea of ever-burning fire" (21:8), but there the Greek is a different word, limnē (G3041), "a lake, a pond". It is the word for the lake of Gennesaret in Luke 5:1-2 and 8:22-23, and for the lake the pigs ran into in Luke 8:33, where the Legion went (Luke 8 in the Luke and John page). In the Textus Receptus it is in ten verses: Luke 5:1, 5:2, 8:22, 8:23, 8:33 and Revelation 19:20, 20:10, 20:14, 20:15, 21:8.
  • New (kainos, G2537): Revelation 21:1 has "a heaven new and an earth new" and says the first heaven and the first earth "went away".
  • Her seed (sperma, G4690): Revelation 12:17 is the only use of this word in Revelation, and the Greek there is tou spermatos autēs. The Septuagint of Genesis 3:15 has tou spermatos autēs in the same case, with tou spermatos sou, "your seed", before it.

Where this fits in the course

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed (Lesson 5). This page runs it to the end: after the Gospels the picture is changed three more times, to the nations, to the image, and to the place. Rule One says what is planted gives back only its own kind (Lesson 7), and the ending is the proof: the seed of Genesis 3:15 comes back as "her seed" in Revelation 12:17, and the planting of Luke and John comes back at Pentecost as a harvest. Lesson 9 says the name shows the state (Lesson 9). Babel, "mixing", is the state of Genesis 11, and Acts 2 is that state answered. The Jeremiah 29 page shows Babel's state returning as Babylon (Jeremiah 29).

A question to ask of any story: which part of the Genesis scene is being put back at this moment, the man, the people or the place? And what do the names say before the story starts?

Remember: the Gospels put the man back, Acts and the letters put the people back, and Revelation puts the place back. Each ending answers a Genesis word: the mixing of Genesis 11, the image of Genesis 1:26, the seed of Genesis 3:15, the curse of Genesis 3:17 and the kept way of Genesis 3:24. And the story turns out as the name says: mixing, harvest, founded, opponent, soil.
