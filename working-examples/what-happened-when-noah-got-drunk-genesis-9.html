What Happened When Noah Got Drunk? Genesis 9:18-27

The point: Noah means "rest". Just before this page the ground is let off its curse and Noah is blessed with the words of Genesis 1. Then he becomes a man of the ground and plants a vine-garden, with the same verb as the garden of Genesis 2:8. He drinks the wine and is uncovered in his tent. Ham, "hot", sees it and tells his two brothers outside. Shem, "name", and Japheth, "expansion", take one robe and go in backward with their faces turned away, and cover him without looking. Noah wakes and knows. He says "Cursed be Canaan" ("humiliated"), praise to the Existing One, the Judges of Name, and may the Judges make Japheth great, living in the tents of Name. It is the Genesis 3 scene again: fruit, nakedness, a telling, a covering and a curse. "Told" is the verb of Genesis 3:11. What changes is who covers, and where the curse lands.

In five lines:

  1. Noah's name was given for rest from the hard work of the hands, "because of the earth which was cursed" (5:29). The Existing One then says he will not again put a curse on the earth (8:21), and the Judges give the Genesis 1 blessing again (9:1).
  2. Noah becomes a man of the ground and plants a vine-garden (9:20). The verb is the one of Genesis 2:8, and these are its first two uses. He drinks the wine, is overcome, and is uncovered in his tent (9:21).
  3. Ham sees his father's nakedness and tells his two brothers outside (9:22). "Told" is the verb of "Who told you that you were naked?" (3:11), and these are its first two uses.
  4. Shem and Japheth take the robe, go in backward and cover him. Their faces are turned away and they do not see (9:23).
  5. Noah wakes and knows what was done (9:24). He curses Canaan, blesses the Existing One, the Judges of Name, and Japheth is made great and lives in the tents of Name (9:25-27).

Rest is let off the curse, then plants

Noah's name is "rest". Genesis 5:29 gives the reason: "he will give us rest from our trouble and the hard work of our hands, because of the earth which was cursed by God." After the flood Noah builds an altar, and the Existing One says in his heart "I will not again put a curse on the earth because of man" (8:21). The "sweet smell" that comes up just before it is the Hebrew nichoach, which comes from the same root as Noah's name, "to rest". The name and the smell are one word family. Seed time and harvest are set going again (8:22), and the Judges give the Genesis 1 blessing in the same words: "Be fertile, and have increase, and make the earth full" (9:1), with "God made man in his image" (9:6) and an agreement "with you and with your seed after you" (9:9). So the scene Noah steps into is the Genesis 1 picture put back: blessing, seed, image, and ground that is no longer cursed.

Then Noah becomes "a man of the ground" (ish ha-adamah) and plants. The BBE says "In those days Noah became a farmer, and he made a vine-garden" (9:20). The Hebrew verb is nata, "to plant". It is the verb of Genesis 2:8, which the BBE also gives as "made a garden". Genesis 2:8 is its first use and Genesis 9:20 is its second. In Genesis 2 the Existing One, the Judges, plant the garden and put the man in it. In Genesis 9 the man plants his own. The identity is standing on the ground with the seed in its own hands (Lesson 5).

The fruit is taken and the man is uncovered

The vine gives its fruit and Noah drinks it: "he took of the wine of it and was overcome by drink; and he was uncovered in his tent" (9:21). The Hebrew has three verbs. He drank (shatah). He became drunk (shakar, "to become tipsy", and Strong's adds "to satiate with a stimulating drink"). And he uncovered himself (galah, in a reflexive form). The plant words always carry an identity claim (Lesson 7). The vine is what was planted, and the wine is the vine's own fruit, so what Noah takes in is what he planted, after its kind.

Notice what the text does not say. It gives no verdict on the drinking. Genesis 2:25 has the man and his wife "without clothing", with "no sense of shame", so being uncovered is not the problem in this story by itself. The text turns at once to what each son does with the sight.

Hot sees it and tells it outside

Ham means "hot" (Strong's H2526). The sons are listed as Shem, Ham and Japheth, and the same verse adds "Ham is the father of Canaan" (9:18). It is said again at the moment of the sight: "Ham, the father of Canaan, saw his father unclothed, and gave news of it to his two brothers outside" (9:22). A father-title says what a name is issued from. Canaan, "humiliated", is on file as what the Hot state fathers before anything has happened (Lesson 9).

Two things happen in 9:22. Ham sees (ra'ah, the verb of Genesis 1:4 and of Genesis 3:6), and Ham tells (nagad). Nagad is the verb of Genesis 3:11, the question put to the man, "Who gave you the knowledge that you were without clothing?" Genesis 3:11 is its first use in the Bible and Genesis 9:22 is its second. So the question of Genesis 3 has an answer here. It is the hot part that looks at the exposure and tells it outside, to the two who are "outside" the tent (chuts, "outside"). The word for the nakedness (ervah) is a new word too. Its first two uses are 9:22 and 9:23.

Name and Expansion cover him and do not look

"And Shem and Japheth took a robe, and putting it on their backs went in with their faces turned away, and put it over their father so that they might not see him unclothed" (9:23). The Hebrew gives the order. They took (laqach) the garment (simlah), laid it on the shoulder of both, went in backward, covered the nakedness (kasah), and "their faces were backward, and they did not see their father's nakedness". "Took" is the verb of Genesis 3:6, "she took of its fruit". The garment word, simlah, is used for the first time here.

So there are two ways of meeting the same sight. In Genesis 3 the eyes open first, they see the lack, and then the coverings are made (3:7). In Genesis 9 the covering goes on with the eyes turned away and the lack is never looked at. The telling goes out, and the covering goes in. It is also one garment on both their shoulders. Name and Expansion carry the cover together.

Rest wakes and knows

"And, awaking from his wine, Noah saw what his youngest son had done to him" (9:24). The Hebrew word for "saw" is "knew" (yada), and the doer is called "his small son" (haqqatan, H6996, "small", in age or importance). What Noah knows on waking is what was done to him: the seeing and the telling of verse 22. The identity does not stay in the tent. It comes back to itself and speaks.

The curse lands on Humiliated, not on Hot

"Cursed be Canaan; let him be a servant of servants to his brothers" (9:25). The word is arar. It is used 63 times, and its first five uses are the snake (3:14), the ground (3:17), Cain (4:11), the ground again (5:29) and Canaan (9:25). The ground was let off its curse at 8:21, so this curse does not go to the ground. It goes to what the telling fathered. Ham is not named in it.

Rule One says what is planted gives back its own kind (Lesson 7). Seeing the exposure and telling it outside, from a hot state, gives back Humiliated. Humiliated ends as "a servant of servants", the lowest place, serving "his brothers", who are Name and Expansion. The name runs out its meaning, as the method on Lesson 9 says it will.

Praise to the Existing One, the Judges of Name

Then comes the blessing, and it is the framework's own pattern in one line: "Praise to the Lord, the God of Shem" (9:26). The Hebrew is barukh YHVH Elohei Shem, "Blessed the Existing One, the Judges of Name". The Judges are named by the identity they enforce, and the identity is Name (Lesson 2).

"May God make Japheth great, and let his living-place be in the tents of Shem" (9:27). The verb for "make great" (yapht, H6601, "to make spacious") comes from the same root as Japheth's name, so the Judges say his own name over him: Expansion is made expansive. And it lives in the tents of Name. "Tent" (ohel) is the same word as the tent where Rest lay uncovered (9:21). Expansion lives inside Name, and Humiliated is its servant. Verses 26 and 27 both end the same way: "let Canaan be his servant".

What comes next in the chapter

"And Noah went on living three hundred and fifty years after the great flow of waters; all the years of his life were nine hundred and fifty: and he came to his end" (9:28-29). The verdict was spoken, and Noah lives on. His years are counted to the end.

Where this fits in the course

A man claims an identity and the court makes it real (Lesson 6). Here the identity is Rest, standing on ground that has just been let off its curse, with the garden in its own hands. Rule One says what is planted gives back only its own kind (Lesson 7). Rest plants a vine and takes the fruit, and the sight of the exposure is met in two ways. The hot part tells it outside and fathers Humiliated. Name and Expansion cover it with the eyes turned away and end up blessed and enlarged. The names are the code (Lesson 9), and the story is the court making each one real.

The fig leaves page has the first coverings (Genesis 3:7 and 3:21), and the "Who told you that you were naked?" page has the first telling. The Emmaus page has the eyes opening on the lack and then on the identity, and the Judah and Tamar page has the seeing that does not know, and the verdict that is only right once he recognises what was filed.

A question to ask of any story: when the exposed part is seen, who tells it outside, and who covers it with their eyes turned away?

Remember: Rest is let off the curse, plants a garden with the verb of Genesis 2:8, takes the fruit and is uncovered. Hot sees it and tells it outside, with the verb of Genesis 3:11, and fathers Humiliated. Name and Expansion take one robe, go in backward, and cover him without looking. Rest wakes, knows, and curses Humiliated. The Judges of Name are praised, and Expansion is made great in the tents of Name.
