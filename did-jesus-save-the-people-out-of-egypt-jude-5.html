Did Jesus Save the People Out of Egypt? Jude 5

People ask why the New Testament says Jesus saved a people out of Egypt, when the Exodus is told around Moses. The verse is Jude 5. The BBE and KJV print "the Lord". The ESV and the Greek print "Jesus".

The short answer: there is no clash. In this course it is one reader taking up different identities, the way you argue with yourself in your own head. Lord, Jesus, Moses and Jude are all names for who you are being at that moment.

The passage (BBE, Jude 1:4-5):

For certain men have come among you secretly, marked out before in the holy Writings for this evil fate, men without the fear of God, turning his grace into an unclean thing, and false to our only Master and Lord, Jesus Christ. Now it is my purpose to put you in mind, though you once had knowledge of all these things, of how the Lord, having taken a people safely out of Egypt, later sent destruction on those who had no faith; — Jude 1:4-5 (BBE)

The identities in the scene

Each name says which identity is being lived in (Lesson 9, names carry YHVH).

The story

  1. Jude opens as an identity: I AM praised. That is where the reading starts.
  2. One of you is called Master and Lord, Jesus Christ. Jude 1:4 puts Lord and Jesus on one person before verse 5 begins.
  3. That one takes the people out of Egypt. Whether verse 5 says "the Lord" or "Jesus", it is the same one: the existing One, who saves. "Jesus" is the Greek form of Joshua, "the existing One saves". Moses is "drawn out", and Numbers 13:16 shows a name gaining the existing One: Hoshea becomes Joshua.
  4. The people are the voices. Egypt is the double straits. The many voices are taken out of the old state together.
  5. The voice that gave no credit is put away. Genesis 2:24: leave, then join. What is not credited does not come along (after its kind).
  6. The Master and the way of Cain. Jude 1:4 is about being false to "our only Master". Jude 1:11 says "They have gone in the way of Cain" (BBE). In Genesis 4:7 the warning is "do not let it be your master" (BBE). The question is the same in both: what rules the scene? Read Lesson 17 for that story.

Remember: the Lord, Jesus, Moses and Jude are one reader in different identities. The reader takes the people out of the old state and lets go of the voice that gives no credit.

Words checked

  • Jude: Ioudas (G2455), from Yehudah (H3063), "probably 'praised'".
  • Lord: kurios (G2962), "he to whom a person or thing belongs". The Greek Old Testament uses it for YHVH (H3068). That is the link this course uses. The verse itself does not carry the Hebrew name.
  • Jesus: Iesous (G2424), from Yehoshua (H3091), "Jehovah-saved". Saved: sozo (G4982). People: laos (G2992). Matthew 1:21 uses the same two Greek words.
  • Egypt: Mitsrayim (H4714), the dual of matsor (H4693), "hemming in". Moses: Mosheh (H4872), "drawing out".
  • Master: despotes (G1203), "absolute ruler". The Greek and the Hebrew in Genesis 4:7 differ, so the link to Cain rests on the picture and not a shared word.
  • Translations: in verse 5 the BBE and KJV print "the Lord" and the SBLGNT and ESV print "Jesus". Verse 4 in the KJV reads "the only Lord God, and our Lord Jesus Christ", so Step 2 follows the BBE.
