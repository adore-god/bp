Why Did God Ask Abraham to Sacrifice Isaac? Genesis 22

In Genesis 22 God asks Abraham to offer his son Isaac as a test, but the text never says what is being tested. In this course the answer is short: it checks whether you hold the identity or only the proof of it. Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The passage (BBE, selected verses):

Now after these things, God put Abraham to the test, and said to him, Abraham; and he said, Here am I. And he said to him, Take your son, your dearly loved only son Isaac, and go to the land of Moriah and give him as a burned offering on one of the mountains of which I will give you knowledge. Then Isaac said to Abraham, My father; and he said, Here am I, my son. And he said, We have wood and fire here, but where is the lamb for the burned offering? And Abraham said, God himself will give the lamb for the burned offering: so they went on together. And stretching out his hand, Abraham took the knife to put his son to death. But the voice of the angel of the Lord came from heaven, saying, Abraham, Abraham: and he said, Here am I. And he said, Let not your hand be stretched out against the boy to do anything to him; for now I am certain that the fear of God is in your heart, because you have not kept back your son, your only son, from me. And lifting up his eyes, Abraham saw a sheep fixed by its horns in the brushwood: and Abraham took the sheep and made a burned offering of it in place of his son. And Abraham gave that place the name Yahweh-yireh: as it is said to this day, In the mountain the Lord is seen. — Genesis 22:1-2, 7-8, 10-14 (BBE)

The same verses with the meanings put in:

Now after these things, the Judges and Rulers put Father of a Multitude to the test, and said to him, Father of a Multitude; and he said, Here am I. And he said to him, Take your son, your dearly loved only son Laughter, and go to the land of Seen of Yah and give him as a burned offering on one of the mountains of which I will give you knowledge. Then Laughter said to Father of a Multitude, My father; and he said, Here am I, my son. And he said, We have wood and fire here, but where is the lamb for the burned offering? And Father of a Multitude said, the Judges and Rulers themselves will give the lamb for the burned offering: so they went on together. And stretching out his hand, Father of a Multitude took the knife to put his son to death. But the voice of the angel of the Existing One came from heaven, saying, Father of a Multitude, Father of a Multitude: and he said, Here am I. And he said, Let not your hand be stretched out against the boy to do anything to him; for now I am certain that the fear of the Judges and Rulers is in your heart, because you have not kept back your son, your only son, from me. And lifting up his eyes, Father of a Multitude saw a sheep fixed by its horns in the brushwood: and Father of a Multitude took the sheep and made a burned offering of it in place of his son. And Father of a Multitude gave that place the name the Existing One Sees: as it is said to this day, In the mountain the Existing One is seen.

The meanings used: Abraham is "father of a multitude" (H85). Isaac is "laughter" (H3327). Moriah is "seen of Jah", from the verb to see (H4179, H7200). Elohim, the word behind "God", is "judges" and "rulers" (H430). The Lord (YHVH) is "the existing One" (H3068). Yireh, in Yahweh-yireh, is built on the same verb, to see.

Three notes on the meanings. First, the BBE says "sheep" and "brushwood" in verse 13. The KJV says "ram" and "thicket", and the picture is the same: something held fast by its horns. Second, verse 1 says "the test" but gives no reason. The reason only appears in verse 12, after the test is over. Third, the common reading is a test of obedience. Hebrews 11:18-19 gives the logic behind it: it had been said "From Isaac will your seed take their name", so Abraham was "judging that God was able to give life even to the dead" (BBE). That is holding the promise when the proof is gone.

How to read it, step by step:

  1. The back story: two laughs. Isaac's name was set by laughter before he was born. Abraham "went down on his face, and laughing, said in his heart, May a man a hundred years old have a child?" (Genesis 17:17, BBE). Sarah, "laughing to herself, said, Now that I am used up am I still to have pleasure" (Genesis 18:12, BBE). When the child came, Sarah said, "God has given me cause for laughing" (Genesis 21:6, BBE), and "Abraham gave to his son, to whom Sarah had given birth, the name Isaac" (Genesis 21:3, BBE). So the story acts out the name: the promise arrives as laughter. Abraham is the identity, "father of a multitude", and Isaac is the proof of it.
  2. The test. The Judges and Rulers are the judging part of you (Lesson 1). They ask for "your dearly loved only son" (Genesis 22:2, BBE). The question is not "do you love me more than him?" It is: do you hold the identity, or the proof of it? When the proof is the only thing you can see, it is easy to let go of the identity and cling to the proof.
  3. The mountain and the answer. Moriah means "seen of Jah". Abraham "saw the place a long way off" on "the third day" (Genesis 22:4, BBE), and day three in Genesis 1:9-10 is where the dry land, the ground, appears. Then he says "Here am I" three times: at the start (22:1), on the way up with the proof walking beside him, "Here am I, my son" (22:7), and with the knife in his hand (22:11). The words never change. The identity is the same at every point.
  4. The altar and the stop. Abraham "put him on the wood on the altar" (Genesis 22:9, BBE). Putting the proof on the altar means being willing to hold the identity with nothing to show for it. Then the voice stops him: "Let not your hand be stretched out against the boy" (22:12). The Judges were never after the proof. They were checking that the identity was still held and that nothing was "kept back".
  5. The ram and the name. "Lifting up his eyes, Abraham saw a sheep fixed by its horns in the brushwood" (Genesis 22:13, BBE). It was already there. He only had to lift his eyes. He offers it "in place of his son", and names the place Yahweh-yireh: "In the mountain the Lord is seen" (22:14). The Existing One is you, aware right now. What you need was in place before you needed it, and you see it when you stop gripping the proof.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. In Genesis 1 the judges speak, the ground appears and the seed brings forth after its kind. Genesis 22 uses the same parts.

PartGenesis 1This scene
GroundThe dry land appears on the third day (Genesis 1:9-10)The place is seen on the third day (Genesis 22:4)
SeedSeed brings forth after its kind (Genesis 1:11)Laughter, the seed, is the one placed on the altar
The judgesSpeak, and what they speak standsSpeak twice: the test (22:1) and the stop (22:12)

The identity was never in danger. Only the proof was. In Genesis 1 the judges make real the identity that is spoken. At Moriah they check that it is still the one being spoken when the proof is out of sight. It was, so the proof was spared.

Remember: Abraham means father of a multitude, and Isaac means laughter. The story is named after the two laughs that came before it. The test is whether you hold the identity when the proof is on the altar. The identity held, the proof was spared, and what was needed was already in place.
