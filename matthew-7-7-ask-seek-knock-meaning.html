What Does "Ask and It Will Be Given" Mean? Matthew 7:7-11

The short answer: the one asking is meant to rule the scene, not be ruled by it. In this course, asking is filing a claim about who you are, and the court rules to match what was filed. Matthew 7:9-10 tests it with two swaps, bread for a stone and a fish for a snake, and says neither happens. What you ask for is already under you, so it does not come back as something that takes charge of you.

The passage (BBE):

Make a request, and it will be answered; what you are searching for you will get; give the sign, and the door will be open to you: Because to everyone who makes a request, it will be given; and he who is searching will get his desire, and to him who gives the sign, the door will be open. Or which of you, if his son makes a request for bread, will give him a stone? Or if he makes a request for a fish, will give him a snake? If you, then, being evil, are able to give good things to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give good things to those who make requests to him? — Matthew 7:7-11 (BBE)

Verse 2 gives the rule the whole chapter runs on: "with your measure will it be measured to you" (Matthew 7:2, BBE). What you hand out is what comes back.

How to read it, step by step:

  1. Asking is filing a claim. This is the courtroom from Lesson 1. YHVH, your present awareness, puts forward an identity, the I AM, and Elohim, the judges, make it real (Lesson 2).
  2. Ask, search, knock. Three moves, each with its result: ask and it is answered, search and you get, knock and the door opens. A door is the way from one scene into the next, so the third move is the one that changes the scene. Verse 8 says "everyone", so the result depends on what is filed, not on who is asking.
  3. Bread or stone. Bread is made from flour, and flour comes from the seed-bearing plants of Genesis 1:11. A stone is bare dry land (Genesis 1:9-10) with nothing planted in it. The seed gives after its kind (Lesson 7), so bare ground is not handed back for the seed's result.
  4. Fish or snake. A fish is a water creature of day five (Genesis 1:20-21). A snake is a beast of the field (Genesis 3:1), a land creature of day six. They are different kinds, so one is not handed back for the other. The snake is also the creature that speaks to the woman and is followed (Genesis 3:1-6), so a snake in place of a fish would put a speaking voice in charge of the one who asked.
  5. The one asking rules the scene. Genesis 1:26 puts "the fish of the sea" first in the list of what the man rules, and the land creatures are on the same list (Lesson 6). So both are under the man. Asking from that position is asking for something already under you, and verses 9-10 say it does not come back as something that rules you.
  6. The measure comes back. Verse 11 ends on "good things", and in Genesis 1 the court's verdict is "good". Verse 12 closes the loop: what you would have done to you is what you do. So ask in the kind you want, because the kind you hand out is the kind you get back.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. Genesis 1 puts man over the creatures:

And God said, Let us make man in our image, like us: and let him have rule over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the air and over the cattle and over all the earth and over every living thing which goes flat on the earth. — Genesis 1:26 (BBE)

The scene keeps all its parts: ground, seed, fish, snake. What matters is who is in charge. In Genesis 1 the man rules the creatures. In Matthew 7 the one asking stays over them.

Around it. The same two questions run through the chapter: which kind is this, and what is it measured by. Verses 16-20 say it in plain words, a good tree gives good fruit, which is after its kind again. Verses 24-27 give two houses in the same storm. Lesson 25 follows that ending. For the opposite picture, the man ruled by a creature, see Lesson 23 (the serpent) and Lesson 12 (Job).

Remember: the one asking is made to rule the scene, not to be ruled by it. A fish is not swapped for a snake, so what comes back does not take charge of the asker. What you hand out is the measure that comes back.

Words checked

  • Ask: aiteo (G154). Search: zeteo (G2212), "to seek in order to find" (Thayer's).
  • Knock: the BBE says "give the sign" for krouo (G2925), which Strong's gives as "to rap, knock". Door: thura (G2374), "a portal or entrance".
  • Bread: artos (G740). Stone: lithos (G3037). Fish: ichthys (G2486). Snake: ophis (G3789), and Thayer says the Septuagint mostly uses it for nachash (H5175), the snake of Genesis 3:1.
