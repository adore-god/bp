What Does "Ask and It Will Be Given" Mean? Matthew 7:7-11 With the Meanings Inserted

Short answer: in this course, asking is filing a claim about who you are, and the court rules to match what was filed. Matthew 7:9-10 tests that with two swaps, bread for a stone and a fish for a snake, and treats both as swaps that do not happen. A fish belongs to the water creatures of Genesis day five. A snake belongs to the land beasts of Genesis 3:1. The court gives after its kind, so a request made in one kind never comes back as another. The one asking is the identity Genesis 1:26-28 places over the scene, so what is asked for is already under its rule. The scene can change, as it does in Jonah. The kind does not.

Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in. The rest of this page shows how to get there, one piece at a time.

The passage

Make a request, and it will be answered; what you are searching for you will get; give the sign, and the door will be open to you: Because to everyone who makes a request, it will be given; and he who is searching will get his desire, and to him who gives the sign, the door will be open. Or which of you, if his son makes a request for bread, will give him a stone? Or if he makes a request for a fish, will give him a snake? If you, then, being evil, are able to give good things to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give good things to those who make requests to him? — Matthew 7:7-11 (BBE)

The same passage with the word meanings put in:

Ask, and it will be answered; what you are seeking in order to find you will get; rap, and the entrance will be open to you: Because to everyone who asks, it will be given; and he who is seeking will get his desire, and to him who raps, the entrance will be open. Or which of you, if his son asks for bread, will give him a stone? Or if he asks for a fish, will give him a serpent? If you, then, being evil, are able to give good things to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give good things to those who ask him?

What changed, and why:

  • "Make a request" is aiteo (Strong's G154), to ask.
  • "Searching" is zeteo (G2212), to seek. Thayer's lexicon opens its entry with "to seek in order to find".
  • "Give the sign" is krouo (G2925). Strong's gives "to rap", meaning to knock. It occurs nine times in the New Testament: Matthew 7:7-8, Luke 11:9-10, Luke 12:36, Luke 13:25, Acts 12:13, Acts 12:16 and Revelation 3:20.
  • "Door" is thura (G2374), "a portal or entrance (the opening or the closure)".
  • "Bread" is artos (G740), "bread (as raised) or a loaf". Thayer describes it as food of flour mixed with water and baked.
  • "Stone" is lithos (G3037). "Fish" is ichthys (G2486).
  • "Snake" is ophis (G3789). Strong's gives "serpent" and adds that it is used figuratively "as a type of sly cunning". Thayer says the Septuagint mostly uses it for the Hebrew nachash (H5175, "a snake, from its hiss"), the word of Genesis 3:1, and he cites Genesis 3:1 under it.

There are no personal or place names in these verses, so the method from Lesson 9 has no names to work on here. The meanings come from the verbs and nouns instead.

Five ideas you need first

1. Asking is filing a claim, and the court rules to match

This is the setting from Lesson 1. You make a claim about who you are, and the judges rule to match it. In the three-part model from Lesson 2, YHVH, your present awareness, puts forward an identity, the I AM, and Elohim, the court, make it real. The same chapter states the rule in two other places:

For as you have been judging, so you will be judged, and with your measure will it be measured to you. — Matthew 7:2 (BBE)
All those things, then, which you would have men do to you, even so do you to them: because this is the law and the prophets. — Matthew 7:12 (BBE)

Verse 12 ends on court words, law and prophets, the same kind of words described in the Bible's court language. What you hand out is what is measured back to you.

2. The court gives after its kind

This is the seed rule from Lesson 7. It is stated in Genesis 1 for plants and for land creatures:

And God said, Let grass come up on the earth, and plants producing seed, and fruit-trees giving fruit, in which is their seed, after their sort: and it was so. — Genesis 1:11 (BBE)
And God made the beast of the earth after its sort, and the cattle after their sort, and everything moving on the face of the earth after its sort: and God saw that it was good. — Genesis 1:25 (BBE)

Note that the BBE words the water creatures differently, "every sort of living and moving thing" (Genesis 1:21), where it words the plants and land creatures "after their sort". The idea is the same in both: each kind is its own kind.

3. A fish and a snake sit in different parts of the Genesis 1 scene

And God said, Let the waters be full of living things, and let birds be in flight over the earth under the arch of heaven. And God made great sea-beasts, and every sort of living and moving thing with which the waters were full, and every sort of winged bird: and God saw that it was good. — Genesis 1:20-21 (BBE)

The water creatures belong to the fifth day (Genesis 1:23). In the seven groups from Lesson 4, day five is what holds you and carries you through, and day six is the land creatures with man and woman. The snake is named in Genesis 3:1 as one of the beasts of the field, a land creature, and the land beasts are made on the sixth day, the same day as the man (Genesis 1:24-31). Day six is the identity itself.

Now the snake was wiser than any beast of the field which the Lord God had made. And he said to the woman, Has God truly said that you may not take of the fruit of any tree in the garden? ... And the snake said, Death will not certainly come to you: For God sees that on the day when you take of its fruit, your eyes will be open, and you will be as gods, having knowledge of good and evil. — Genesis 3:1, 3:4-5 (BBE)

Look at what the snake does with words. It questions what was said (3:1), contradicts it (3:4), and offers a different identity, "as gods" (3:5). In the course that is a false filing, a claim that contradicts the identity it says it holds. This is the pattern behind missing the mark.

4. The identity is placed over the scene

And God said, Let us make man in our image, like us: and let him have rule over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the air and over the cattle and over all the earth and over every living thing which goes flat on the earth. — Genesis 1:26 (BBE)
And God gave them his blessing and said to them, Be fertile and have increase, and make the earth full and be masters of it; be rulers over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the air and over every living thing moving on the earth. — Genesis 1:28 (BBE)

The scene is built first, and then the identity is placed over it with rule. Look at the list. The fish of the sea is the first thing named. A snake is a land creature (Genesis 3:1), so it falls under the land creatures at the end of the list. Both a fish and a snake are things the man rules. In Genesis 3 that order is turned round: the snake speaks, and the woman acts on what it says (3:1-6). In this reading, that is the identity going by the scene's voice instead of ruling the scene. This is Lesson 6: man is the one who claims, and once an I AM is standing it rules the creature groups of days five and six. They do not rule it. Lesson 23 reads the snake the same way, as a made creature the man was meant to rule, and points to the man naming the beasts of the field (Genesis 2:19-20) as how a ruler handles them.

5. Jonah changes the scene without changing the kind

Jonah is thrown into the sea. A great fish swallows him (Jonah 1:17). Inside it he prays. The fish puts him out on dry land (Jonah 2:10). The Lingua Divina Jonah article sets this out in full and reads the prayer in Jonah 2:9 as the I AM taken up inside the fish before the delivery. For this page, the point is the order of the scenes: the deep (Genesis 1:2, day one), a sea creature (day five), dry land (Genesis 1:9-10, day three). Each one is a Genesis category, and each follows from the one before. The fish is the enclosure from the cheat sheet, what holds you before you are set free. Lesson 23 reads Jonah as a man under a creature, not over it.

Now read the scene

  1. Verse 6 comes first.
    Do not give that which is holy to the dogs, or put your jewels before pigs, for fear that they will be crushed under foot by the pigs whose attack will then be made against you. — Matthew 7:6 (BBE)
    A valuable thing is put before the wrong kind, and what comes back is an attack on the giver. Verses 9-10 then run a test the other way round: a request is made, and the question is whether the wrong kind comes back. That is a reading of how the verses sit together, since the text does not link them in words.
  2. Verse 7 gives three moves, each with its result. Ask, and it is answered. Search, and you get. Rap, and the door opens. A door is an entrance (G2374), the way from one scene into the next, so in this reading the third move is the one about changing scene.
  3. Verse 8 says "everyone". The result is not tied to who is asking. That fits a court that rules on what was filed, not on the one who filed it.
  4. Verse 9: bread or stone. Both belong to day three. Bread is food of flour mixed with water and baked (Thayer), and flour is made from the seed-bearing plants of Genesis 1:11. A stone is ground with no seed in it, the dry land of Genesis 1:9-10 without anything planted. The first swap stays inside day three and puts bare ground where the seed's result was asked for.
  5. Verse 10: fish or snake. The second swap crosses from day five to day six. A fish is the part of the scene that holds and carries you through. It is also the first creature named under the man's rule in Genesis 1:26, so asking for one is asking for something already placed under the asker. A snake is the one creature in Genesis that speaks to the woman, and what it says swaps one claim for another. The swap would take the carrying category and put in its place the voice that contradicts the claim.
  6. Verse 11 uses the court's verdict word. The giving is "good things". In Genesis 1 the court's verdict on what it made is "good" (Genesis 1:4, 1:10, 1:12, 1:21, 1:25). Verse 11 also sets "evil" beside "good", the pair the snake names in Genesis 3:5. The argument is "how much more": if even the ones the verse calls evil give good things when asked, what comes from the far end of that argument is good things too.
  7. Verse 12 closes the loop. What you would have done to you is what you do. That is verse 2's measure, stated again, and it ends on "the law and the prophets".

That is the difference. In Jonah the scene changes three times, and every scene is a Genesis category in its own place. The fish holds him and then puts him on dry land. At no point is the fish replaced by a snake. In Matthew 7 the rule behind that is stated: ask in a kind, and the kind is what returns. A scene can change, from deep to fish to dry land. A kind cannot be swapped for another kind, from fish to snake.

How the scene changes the Genesis 1 picture

Now the three questions from Lesson 5:

  1. What is the ground, and what does its name mean? These verses name no place, so there is no name to read. The ground is the unnamed scene the son stands in, with two kinds of ground in view: bread and stone from day three, and a door to the next scene.
  2. Who is standing on it, and what do their names mean? No names here either, only roles. The son asks. The giver is "your Father". "You, being evil" are the ones addressed. The son is the seed, as on the Moses page.
  3. What has changed from Genesis 1? In Genesis 1 the scene is built and called good, and then the identity is placed over the fish, the birds and the land creatures (1:26-28). Here the identity is asking out of that scene, from days three and five, and the swap on offer would put a day six voice, the snake, in place of what the asker rules. The verses treat that swap as one the giver will not make. So the answer matches the order Genesis 1 sets: the identity over the scene, not the scene over the identity.

So what does "ask and it will be given" mean?

In this course, asking is not begging an outside giver. It is filing a claim about who you are, and the court, Elohim, rules to match what was filed. Verse 8 says it is the same for everyone. Verses 9-10 say what the court does not do: it does not hand back a different kind from the one filed.

The same rule cuts the other way, and verse 2 says so in so many words: with your measure it will be measured to you. So the two ideas belong together. Ask in the kind you want, because that kind is what comes back. And the kind you hand out is the kind you get back. That is why verse 12 ends on law, the court's own word.

And the one asking is the identity Genesis 1:26-28 places over the scene. The fish and the land creatures are on the list of what it rules, so asking from that position is asking for something already under it. The snake is the one creature that speaks, and when its claim replaces the one filed, the order of Genesis 1 is turned round. Lesson 25 follows this same chapter to its end, where the house on the rock stands and the house on the sand falls (Matthew 7:24-27).

Notes on the wording

  • The BBE renders krouo as "give the sign" in verses 7 and 8. Strong's gives "to rap, knock". This page uses the Strong's meaning for the word meanings and the BBE for the quotes.
  • The BBE says "snake" in verse 10. Strong's G3789 says "serpent". Both translate the one Greek word, ophis.
  • Matthew 7:10 uses ichthys (G2486). The fish in Matthew 12:40 is a different Greek word, ketos (G2785), which occurs once in the New Testament. In Hebrew, Jonah 1:17 and 2:10 use dag (H1709). So the link between Matthew 7:10 and Jonah is by creature category, water creatures of day five, and not by a shared word.
  • Verse 11's "evil" is poneros (G4190). The link to "good and evil" in Genesis 3:5 rests on the English words in the BBE and on the verdict they state. I have not matched the Greek of verse 11 to the Hebrew of Genesis.
  • Genesis 1:26 says "rule" and Genesis 1:28 says "masters" and "rulers" in the BBE. I have not pulled the Hebrew occurrence lists for these words, so this page reads the rule from the BBE English and from the order of the list.
  • "Heaven" in verse 11 is placed with day two because the BBE names the arch Heaven in Genesis 1:8 and Lesson 4 lists it in the day two group. That link is by the English word. I have not checked the Greek of verse 11 against the Hebrew of Genesis 1:8.
  • The day 4 label "set times" is the Lesson 4 label, and verses 7-8 give no date, so that row only notes that every result is stated in the future.
  • The verse names the giver as "your Father", not Elohim. This page keeps to the court reading from Lesson 1 and does not decode "Father" further, because these verses do not.

Remember: asking is filing a claim, the identity is placed over the scene, the court gives after its kind, a scene can change but a kind is not swapped for another kind, and what you hand out is the measure that comes back.
