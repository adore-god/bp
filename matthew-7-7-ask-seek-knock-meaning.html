What Does "Ask, Seek, Knock" Mean? Matthew 7:7-11 and Believing Before You See It

The short answer: the one asking is meant to rule the scene, not be ruled by it. In this course, asking is filing a claim about who you are, and the court rules to match what was filed. Matthew 7:7 says the claim is answered. Other passages say when to hold it: before anything shows. Matthew 21:22, Mark 11:24 and Romans 4 all put the believing first and the seeing second. Matthew 7:9-10 then tests it with two swaps, bread for a stone and a fish for a snake, and says neither happens. What you ask for is already under you, so it does not come back as something that takes charge of you.

The passage (BBE):

Make a request, and it will be answered; what you are searching for you will get; give the sign, and the door will be open to you: Because to everyone who makes a request, it will be given; and he who is searching will get his desire, and to him who gives the sign, the door will be open. Or which of you, if his son makes a request for bread, will give him a stone? Or if he makes a request for a fish, will give him a snake? If you, then, being evil, are able to give good things to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give good things to those who make requests to him? — Matthew 7:7-11 (BBE)

Verse 2 gives the rule the whole chapter runs on: "with your measure will it be measured to you" (Matthew 7:2, BBE). What you hand out is what comes back.

How to read it, step by step:

  1. Asking is filing a claim. This is the courtroom from Lesson 1. YHVH, your present awareness, puts forward an identity, the I AM, and Elohim, the judges, make it real (Lesson 2).
  2. Ask, search, knock. Three moves, each with its result: ask and it is answered, search and you get, knock and the door opens. A door is the way from one scene into the next, so the third move is the one that changes the scene. Verse 8 says "everyone", so the result depends on what is filed, not on who is asking.
  3. Bread or stone. Bread is made from flour, and flour comes from the seed-bearing plants of Genesis 1:11. A stone is bare dry land (Genesis 1:9-10) with nothing planted in it. The seed gives after its kind (Lesson 7), so bare ground is not handed back for the seed's result.
  4. Fish or snake. A fish is a water creature of day five (Genesis 1:20-21). A snake is a beast of the field (Genesis 3:1), a land creature of day six. They are different kinds, so one is not handed back for the other. The snake is also the creature that speaks to the woman and is followed (Genesis 3:1-6), so a snake in place of a fish would put a speaking voice in charge of the one who asked.
  5. The one asking rules the scene. Genesis 1:26 puts "the fish of the sea" first in the list of what the man rules, and the land creatures are on the same list (Lesson 6). So both are under the man. Asking from that position is asking for something already under you, and verses 9-10 say it does not come back as something that rules you.
  6. The measure comes back. Verse 11 ends on "good things", and in Genesis 1 the court's verdict is "good". Verse 12 closes the loop: what you would have done to you is what you do. So ask in the kind you want, because the kind you hand out is the kind you get back.

Believe it before you see it: the same order in the rest of the Bible

Matthew 7:7 says the claim is answered. It does not say when the answer shows. Other passages do, and every one of them puts the holding first and the sight second. In this course that holding is you standing in the claim while the Judges do their work (Lesson 2).

  1. Matthew 6:8, the chapter before.
    So be not like them; because your Father has knowledge of your needs even before you make your requests to him. — Matthew 6:8 (BBE)
    The need is known before the request is made. So asking is not how the court finds out something new. It is how you take your place in the scene.
  2. Matthew 21:21-22, later in the same gospel.
    Truly I say to you, If you have faith, without doubting, not only may you do what has been done to the fig-tree, but even if you say to this mountain, Be taken up and put into the sea, it will be done. And all things, whatever you make request for in prayer, having faith, you will get. — Matthew 21:21-22 (BBE)
    The saying comes first and "it will be done" comes after. The fig-tree went first: Jesus speaks to it, and the disciples then see it "dry and dead" (21:19-20). "Without doubting" is the verb diakrino (G1252), "to separate thoroughly" and so "to hesitate" in Strong's. A claim that is filed and then argued against by the one who filed it is two claims at once, and the Judges have nothing clear to rule on.
  3. Mark 11:22-24, the same teaching in Mark.
    Whatever you make a request for in prayer, have faith that it has been given to you, and you will have it. — Mark 11:24 (BBE)
    The order is inside the verse: "has been given" first, "you will have it" second. The fig-tree in Mark runs the same way. Jesus speaks to it on one day (11:14), and the next morning they see it "dead from the roots" (11:20). Verse 23 has "no doubt in his heart", which is diakrino again.
  4. Romans 4:17-22, Abraham.
    (As it is said in the holy Writings, I have made you a father of a number of nations) before him in whom he had faith, that is, God, who gives life to the dead, and to whom the things which are not are as if they were. Who without reason for hope, in faith went on hoping, so that he became the father of a number of nations, as it had been said, So will your seed be... Still, he did not give up faith in the undertaking of God, but was made strong by faith, giving glory to God, And being certain that God was able to keep his word. For which reason it was put to his account as righteousness. — Romans 4:17-22 (BBE, 4:19 left out)
    Here is the whole order in one man. The name Abraham, "father of a multitude" (H85), is given before there is a child (Genesis 17:5). His own body "seemed to him little better than dead" (4:19), so the sight says no. He holds the claim anyway, and verse 22 says it "was put to his account", which is the filing language of the court. "Did not give up faith" is diakrino again at 4:20, the same verb as "without doubting" in Matthew 21:21. The Greek for "God" here is theos (G2316), which Strong's also gives as "figuratively, a magistrate". So "God, who gives life to the dead, and to whom the things which are not are as if they were" reads as the Judges, who speak and the thing stands, as in Genesis 1. The son arrives in his lifetime and the ground comes later: What Is "the Promise" in Hebrews 11:39?
  5. Hebrews 11:1-3 and 11:13.
    Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, and the sign that the things not seen are true... By faith it is clear to us that the order of events was fixed by the word of God, so that what is seen has not been made from things which only seem to be. — Hebrews 11:1, 3 (BBE)
    The seen is made from what was said, the same order as Genesis 1, where each thing is spoken and then stands. Verse 13 says the people in the chapter "came to their end in faith, not having had the heritage", and 11:39 says the same of the agreement. That holding-until-the-ground-arrives is the promise page above.

What this adds to Matthew 7:7. The order is the same in all five: the claim goes on the record first, the result shows second, and in between you hold it. The swaps in Matthew 7:9-10 say what turns up at the end. It is the same kind that was asked for, a fish and not a snake, because the seed gives after its kind (Lesson 7). So believing before you see is not guesswork. It is standing in the kind you asked for until that kind arrives.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. Genesis 1 puts man over the creatures:

And God said, Let us make man in our image, like us: and let him have rule over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the air and over the cattle and over all the earth and over every living thing which goes flat on the earth. — Genesis 1:26 (BBE)
PartGenesis 1Matthew 7:7-11
Seed and groundSeed-bearing plants after their kind (1:11) and dry land (1:9-10)Bread and stone (7:9)
Water creaturesEvery living thing in the waters (1:20-21)The fish (7:10)
Land creaturesThe beast of the earth after its sort (1:25)The snake, a beast of the field (Genesis 3:1)
RuleMan to "have rule over" the creatures (1:26)The son asks from the ruling position (7:9-10)
VerdictThe court saw that it was good (1:25)The giving is "good things" (7:11)

The scene keeps all its parts: ground, seed, fish, snake. What matters is who is in charge. In Genesis 1 the man rules the creatures. In Matthew 7 the one asking stays over them.

Around it. The same two questions run through the chapter: which kind is this, and what is it measured by. Verses 16-20 say it in plain words, a good tree gives good fruit, which is after its kind again. Verses 24-27 give two houses in the same storm. Lesson 25 follows that ending. For the opposite picture, the man ruled by a creature, see Lesson 23 (the serpent) and Lesson 12 (Job).

Remember: the one asking is made to rule the scene, not to be ruled by it. The claim goes on the record before anything shows, and you hold it without doubting until it does. A fish is not swapped for a snake, so what comes back does not take charge of the asker. What you hand out is the measure that comes back.

Words checked

  • Ask: aiteo (G154). Search: zeteo (G2212), "to seek in order to find" (Thayer's).
  • Knock: the BBE says "give the sign" for krouo (G2925), which Strong's gives as "to rap, knock". Door: thura (G2374), "a portal or entrance".
  • Doubt, waver: diakrino (G1252), 19 uses, including Matthew 21:21, Mark 11:23, Romans 4:20 and James 1:6. The BBE has "without doubting" (Matthew 21:21), "no doubt in his heart" (Mark 11:23) and "did not give up faith" (Romans 4:20). God: theos (G2316), Strong's "figuratively, a magistrate". Abraham: H85, "father of a multitude".
  • Verse text (BBE): Matthew 6:8, 7:1-14 and 21:18-22, Mark 11:12-24, Romans 4:1-25, Hebrews 11:1-3, 11:8-19 and 11:39.
  • Bread: artos (G740). Stone: lithos (G3037). Fish: ichthys (G2486). Snake: ophis (G3789), and Thayer says the Septuagint mostly uses it for nachash (H5175), the snake of Genesis 3:1.
