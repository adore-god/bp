What Does Jeremiah 29:11 Really Mean? Read It Inside Its Letter

Jeremiah 29:11 is one line in a letter, and the letter is addressed to people in Babylon. The line reads differently once you see where it was sent and what stands on either side of it. Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The passage (BBE):

Now these are the words of the letter which Jeremiah the prophet sent from Jerusalem to ... Babylon; ... This is what the Lord of armies, the God of Israel, has said to all those whom I have taken away prisoners from Jerusalem to Babylon: Go on building houses and living in them, and planting gardens and using the fruit of them; Take wives and have sons and daughters, ... and be increased in number there and do not become less. And be working for the peace of the land to which I have had you taken away prisoners, and make prayer to the Lord for it: for in its peace you will have peace. ... For this is what the Lord has said: When seventy years are ended for Babylon, I will have pity on you and give effect to my good purpose for you, causing you to come back to this place. For I am conscious of my thoughts about you, says the Lord, thoughts of peace and not of evil, to give you hope at the end. And you will go on crying to me and making prayer to me, and I will give ear to you. And you will be searching for me and I will be there, when you have gone after me with all your heart. — Jeremiah 29:1, 4-7, 10-13 (BBE)

The same passage with the meanings put in:

Now these are the words of the letter which Yah Will Rise the prophet sent from Founded Peaceful to ... Confusion; ... This is what the Existing One of armies, the Judges of He Shall Prevail, has said to all those whom I have taken away prisoners from Founded Peaceful to Confusion: Go on building houses and living in them, and planting gardens and using the fruit of them; Take wives and have sons and daughters, ... and be increased in number there and do not become less. And be working for the peace of the land to which I have had you taken away prisoners, and make prayer to the Existing One for it: for in its peace you will have peace. ... For this is what the Existing One has said: When seventy years are ended for Confusion, I will have pity on you and give effect to my good purpose for you, causing you to come back to this place. For I am conscious of my thoughts about you, says the Existing One, thoughts of peace and not of evil, to give you hope at the end. And you will go on crying to me and making prayer to me, and I will give ear to you. And you will be searching for me and I will be there, when you have gone after me with all your heart.

The meanings used: Jeremiah is "Yah will rise" (Strong's H3414, built from H7311 and H3050). Yah is the short form of YHVH (H3050, a contraction of H3068). Babylon is "confusion" (H894, from H1101, to mix). Jerusalem is "founded peaceful" (H3389). The Lord (YHVH) is "the existing One" (H3068). God (Elohim) is "the judges", as in Lesson 3. See the Bible's court language. The marked words in the last lines are the five Hebrew words of verse 11: thoughts (H4284), peace (H7965), evil (H7451), end (H319) and hope (H8615). Israel is "he shall prevail" (H3478, from H8280 and H410), the name given to Jacob in Genesis 32:28. Armies and the other personal names are left as written, because this page only follows the ground, the one verse and the name the letter comes under.

Five notes on the meanings. First, Jeremiah: Strong's gives "Jah will rise". The Blue Letter Bible adds the gloss "whom Jehovah has appointed", and one lexicon's short definition reads "Yah loosens", with BDB marking its root as uncertain. This course uses Strong's. Second, Jerusalem: Strong's says "probably" from two roots and gives "founded peaceful". BDB's own entry gives "teaching of peace" and calls the root and meaning dubious. Every reading keeps the word peace, and peace is what this page needs. Third, the word the BBE and KJV give as "thoughts" (H4284) covers thought, plan, purpose and device. The NAS renders it "thoughts" 19 times and "plans" 14 times out of 56, so "plans" in newer Bibles is within its range. Fourth, in verse 7 the BBE says "land", but the Hebrew word in the interlinear is "city" (H5892). The letter tells them to seek the peace of the city named Confusion. Fifth, Israel: Strong's gives "he will rule as God" for H3478, and BDB gives "God prevails". The text itself gives the reason when the name is first given: "Your name will no longer be Jacob, but Israel: for in your fight with God and with men you have overcome" (Genesis 32:28, BBE). This course reads the name as "he shall prevail", and the words of that verse support it.

How to read it, step by step:

  1. The ground. The letter goes from a place named Founded Peaceful to a place named Confusion (Jeremiah 29:1, 29:4). Babylon is the same Hebrew word as Babel in Genesis 10:10 and 11:9 (H894). The people did not choose the move: they were "taken away prisoners". Ask the Lesson 8 question anyway: which state is left, and which is joined? The letter's first job is to say what to do on the ground they were placed on. It also comes under a name. The sender is "the Judges of He Shall Prevail" (Jeremiah 29:4), and Israel is the name Jacob was given in Genesis 32:28 because he overcame. The letter does not drop that name when it reaches Confusion. People placed on ground named Confusion are still addressed under the name that means prevail.
  2. The first order is to build. "Go on building houses and living in them, and planting gardens and using the fruit of them" (Jeremiah 29:5, BBE). Then: take wives, have sons and daughters, be increased in number there (Jeremiah 29:6, BBE). Nothing here says to wait. This is the Genesis picture rebuilt on the ground named Confusion: plants and fruit, increase, and the wife, who in Lesson 6 is the identity you take on. What is planted still gives back its kind (Lesson 7).
  3. The peace is asked for before it is promised. Verse 7 uses the Hebrew word for peace (H7965) three times: seek the peace of the city, and in its peace you will have peace. Verse 11 says "thoughts of peace", and the word is the same, H7965. So the peace in verse 11 is the peace verse 7 tells them to hold while they are still in Confusion.
  4. The count comes before the thoughts. Verse 11 begins with "For", and verse 10 has just set the length: "When seventy years are ended for Babylon". The line is attached to a counted period, not to a feeling. Voices that say otherwise are named (Jeremiah 29:8-9, BBE: "what is false in my name"), and a rival letter quotes the message back: "The time will be long" (Jeremiah 29:28, BBE). See Numbers and Time.
  5. The thoughts. The word "thoughts" (H4284) is used twice in verse 11. In this course the Existing One is you, aware right now, so "I am conscious of my thoughts" is that awareness noticing which thoughts it is holding. The verse names them: peace, "not of evil". The same two Hebrew words, thoughts (H4284) and evil (H7451), sit together in Genesis 6:5. See the comparison below.
  6. The end. The Hebrew has two nouns where the BBE has one phrase. The first is "end" (H319): Strong's gives "the last or end, hence the future; also posterity". BDB sets it against the word for "beginning" (H7225), which is the word that opens Genesis 1:1, and lists Jeremiah 29:11 under a happy close. The second is "hope" (H8615): Strong's says "literally, a cord (as an attachment); figuratively, expectancy". Read together, it is an end with a cord tied to it. The KJV joins the two into "an expected end".
  7. The terms that follow. The verses after it do not drop the condition: crying and prayer (Jeremiah 29:12), then "searching for me and I will be there, when you have gone after me with all your heart" (Jeremiah 29:13, BBE). In this course the heart is the mind (see Lesson 18). The whole mind goes after it, not part of it.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. Genesis 1 gives the increase and the food:

And God gave them his blessing and said to them, Be fertile and have increase, and make the earth full and be masters of it — Genesis 1:28 (BBE)
PartGenesis 1Jeremiah 29
GroundThe dry land and the plants (day 3)A city named Confusion (Jeremiah 29:1, 29:7)
FoodSeed-bearing plants "for your food" (Genesis 1:29)Gardens and "the fruit of them" (Jeremiah 29:5)
Increase"Be fertile and have increase" (Genesis 1:28)"be increased in number there" (Jeremiah 29:6)

The scene keeps all its parts: plants, fruit, wives, increase. What changed is the name of the ground. The instruction is to run the Genesis 1 life inside it, and the thoughts held there are named in verse 11.

Compare Genesis 6:5. There the same two words stand together, with the opposite result:

And the Lord saw that the sin of man was great on the earth, and that all the thoughts of his heart were evil. — Genesis 6:5 (BBE)

The BBE says "sin" for the first word and "evil" for the second, but the Hebrew word in both places is H7451. The "thoughts" are H4284. In Genesis 6:5 the thoughts of the heart come out as evil. In Jeremiah 29:11 the thoughts come out as peace, "not of evil". Same two words, opposite verdict, and in both places what is held decides what the ground gives back.

Is "plans to prosper you" wrong? No. The Hebrew word for peace (H7965) also covers welfare and prosperity, and Strong's lists both. What the verse does not give is a promise cut loose from its letter. It is sent to people in Confusion, it sits after a count of seventy years, and it is followed by terms.

See names as identity codes.

Remember: a verse sits inside its letter. Peace is held first in the ground named Confusion, the years are counted, and the end comes after its kind.
