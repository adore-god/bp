Why Did God Harden Pharaoh's Heart? Exodus 7-14

The text says both that the Lord made Pharaoh's heart hard and that Pharaoh made it hard himself, and people ask how God can do the first and then punish the second. In this course it is one event seen from two sides: the heart is the mind, and a hard heart is a mind that has not changed. Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The passage (BBE, selected verses):

And I will make Pharaoh's heart hard, and my signs and wonders will be increased in the land of Egypt. (7:3) But Pharaoh's heart was made hard, and he did not give ear to them, as the Lord had said. (7:13) And the Lord said to Moses and Aaron, Pharaoh's heart is unchanged; he will not let the people go. (7:14) But when Pharaoh saw that there was peace for a time, he made his heart hard and did not give ear to them, as the Lord had said. (8:15) And the Lord made Pharaoh's heart hard, and he would not give ear to them, as the Lord had said. (9:12) And the Lord said to Moses, Go in to Pharaoh: for I have made his heart and the hearts of his servants hard, so that I may let my signs be seen among them. (10:1) And I will make Pharaoh's heart hard, and he will come after them and I will be honoured over Pharaoh and all his army, so that the Egyptians may see that I am the Lord. And word came to Pharaoh of the flight of the people: and the feeling of Pharaoh and of his servants about the people was changed, and they said, Why have we let Israel go, so that they will do no more work for us? (14:4-5) — Exodus 7:3, 13-14; 8:15; 9:12; 10:1; 14:4-5 (BBE)

The same verses with the meanings put in:

And I will make Great House's mind hard, and my signs and wonders will be increased in the land of Egypt. (7:3) But Great House's mind was made hard, and he did not give ear to them, as the Existing One had said. (7:13) And the Existing One said to Moses and Aaron, Great House's mind is unchanged; he will not let the people go. (7:14) But when Great House saw that there was peace for a time, he made his mind hard and did not give ear to them, as the Existing One had said. (8:15) And the Existing One made Great House's mind hard, and he would not give ear to them, as the Existing One had said. (9:12) And the Existing One said to Moses, Go in to Great House: for I have made his mind and the minds of his servants hard, so that I may let my signs be seen among them. (10:1) And I will make Great House's mind hard, and he will come after them and I will be honoured over Great House and all his army, so that the Egyptians may see that I am the Existing One. And word came to Great House of the flight of the people: and the mind of Great House and of his servants about the people was changed, and they said, Why have we let Israel go, so that they will do no more work for us? (14:4-5)

The meanings used: Heart is leb (H3820), which Strong's says is used for "the feelings, the will and even the intellect", so here it is the mind. The Lord (YHVH) is "the existing One" (H3068). Pharaoh is parʿô (H6547), of Egyptian origin, "great house", the common title of the king of Egypt, so it is a title and not a personal name. The BBE says "hard" each time, but the Hebrew has three words: chazaq, "strengthen" (H2388), kabed, "be heavy" (H3513), and qashah, "be hard" (H7185).

Three notes on the meanings. First, the pattern in the three words is general, with exceptions: when the Lord does it the word is mostly chazaq (9:12, 10:20, 14:4), but 7:3 uses qashah and 10:1 uses kabed. Second, the BBE says "unchanged" in 7:14 and "feeling" in 14:5, where the KJV says "hardened" and "heart". Third, the common readings say God overrides Pharaoh's will, or only foresaw what Pharaoh would do. The text says the Lord announced it in advance (Exodus 4:21; 7:3), but the first time it says he did it is 9:12, the sixth plague, after Pharaoh's heart is already hard again and again (7:13-14; 8:15; 8:32).

How to read it, step by step:

  1. The heart is the mind. The Hebrew for heart is leb (H3820), and Strong's says it stands for "the feelings, the will and even the intellect". So a hard heart is a hard mind. The BBE gives the meaning away: "Pharaoh's heart is unchanged; he will not let the people go" (Exodus 7:14, BBE). A hard heart is an unchanged one.
  2. The great house. The one whose mind is unchanged is named by a title, "great house" (H6547), not by a personal name. The house follows the head: the Lord says he has made hard "the hearts of his servants" as well (Exodus 10:1, BBE), and at the sea the change is in "Pharaoh and of his servants" together (14:5, BBE). So the state held in the great house is held by the whole house, not by one mind alone.
  3. Two sides of one event. The Lord says "I will make Pharaoh's heart hard" (Exodus 7:3, BBE), and Pharaoh "made his heart hard" himself (8:15, BBE). The Existing One is you, aware right now, so these are one event seen from two sides: you as you are now, and the mind that holds the state. The Hebrew shows the two sides. When Pharaoh does it, the word is mostly kabed, "heavy" (8:15, 8:32): a mind weighed down. When the Lord does it, the word is mostly chazaq, "strengthen" (9:12, 10:20, 14:4): the held state made firm.
  4. Pressure, promise, relief, back again. The signs press on Pharaoh and he gives way. Then "when Pharaoh saw that there was peace for a time, he made his heart hard" (Exodus 8:15, BBE), and "again Pharaoh made his heart hard and did not let the people go" (8:32, BBE). The mind goes back to the old state as soon as the pressure lifts. That is why it never softens: it only moves while the pressure is on.
  5. Why the signs keep coming. The Lord says "my signs and wonders will be increased" (Exodus 7:3, BBE), and "so that I may let my signs be seen among them" (10:1, BBE). In this reading the signs are the held state shown back to the mind. They increase while the mind stays the same, because an unchanged mind keeps getting the same answer.
  6. The mind can change, both ways. At the sea "the feeling of Pharaoh and of his servants about the people was changed, and they said, Why have we let Israel go" (Exodus 14:5, BBE). The Hebrew word is still heart. The mind that was unchanged for chapters changes, and it changes back to the old state. Then "the Lord made the heart of Pharaoh hard, and he went after the children of Israel" (14:8, BBE). So the heart is not fixed by God against Pharaoh. It follows the state held, and it softens only when the mind changes and stays changed.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. The same speaking and the same standing are here, but they work on a different thing.

PartGenesis 1This scene
The wordThe Judges speak, and "it was so" (Genesis 1:9)The Existing One speaks, and "as the Lord had said" it stands (Exodus 7:13; 8:15; 9:12)
What standsThe identity spoken stands as declaredThe state held stands: "unchanged" (Exodus 7:14) until the mind changes (14:5)

What changed is what the word is applied to. In Genesis 1 what is spoken is a new identity, and it stands. Here the state is the old one, and it stands just as firmly. The mind did not need to be forced. It only needed to stay where it was.

See the Moses lesson, which comes just after the Lord first says he will harden Pharaoh's heart (Exodus 4:21).

Remember: the heart is the mind, and a hard heart is an unchanged one. The Lord making it hard and Pharaoh making it hard are one event: you as you are now, and the mind that holds the state. The signs keep coming while the mind stays the same, and the heart softens only when the mind changes and stays changed.
