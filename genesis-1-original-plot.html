The Original Plot of Genesis 1

Genesis 1 is the master picture. The whole setting is built before the man appears: the deep and the light, the dry land, the plants with their seed, the lights for the seasons and the creatures. Only then is the identity placed in it.

The light, the dry land and the plants are each called good before the man is there. For example:

And grass came up on the earth, and every plant producing seed of its sort, and every tree producing fruit, in which is its seed, of its sort: and God saw that it was good. — Genesis 1:12 (BBE)

The framework reads Genesis 1 as the whole way it works, written out. It has four parts:

  1. The ground. It has a name before anyone stands on it, and the name shows what the state is like. In the original picture the ground is named Eden, "pleasure" (Strong's H5731).
  2. The seed. The claim planted in that ground, which gives back after its kind. The plants come up "producing seed" and every tree has "its seed" in it (Genesis 1:11-12).
  3. The stand. The I AM is given a body and a place to stand on that ground. This is the image of Genesis 1:26, looked at in Lesson 6.
  4. The time. The lights of day four mark when something is due.

The name of the ground is given in the next chapter, where the man is placed on it:

And the Lord God made a garden in the east, in Eden; and there he put the man whom he had made. — Genesis 2:8 (BBE)

The ground is Eden, "pleasure", before the man stands on it. So the original picture is a state of pleasure, and every later place name is a change from it.

The time is written into the picture in the same way. The lights are made before the man, and they have a job:

And God said, Let there be lights in the arch of heaven, for a division between the day and the night, and let them be for signs, and for marking the changes of the year, and for days and for years. — Genesis 1:14 (BBE)

The creatures fill this same scene. They are made after their kind, and the identity is then given rule over them (Genesis 1:26, in Lesson 6).

Every later story is this picture, changed. A new place is new ground. A new character is a new identity standing on it. The creatures, lights and plants that appear are the same groups as before. The court is the same, and it makes real the picture that is actually offered.

The picture at work in six stories

Each story below changes a different part of the picture. Read each one with the four parts in mind, and ask which part has moved. Every story has its own full page.

Ruth 1: the ground changes

Example, Ruth 1. The ground changes from House of Bread, Praise to Of His Father. The identities standing on it are named Pleasant, Sick and Pining. The original picture has been changed into a different scene, and the court makes that scene real. Lesson 11 walks through it.

The temple curtain: the ground is named, then the light fails

Luke 23 keeps the order of Genesis 1. A garden is promised first (23:43), and the Greek word for it is the one the Greek Old Testament uses for Eden in Genesis 2 and 3. So the ground is named before anything moves. Then the land goes dark from the sixth hour to the ninth, and the light of the sun fails (23:44-45). Those are the lights Genesis 1:17 says were put in the arch of heaven "to give light on the earth". Only after that does the curtain part. The picture is being taken apart in the order it was built. See why the temple curtain tears.

Rods and sticks: the seed

Genesis 1:11 is the first place the Hebrew word for tree and wood (etz, H6086) is used, and it comes with "after its sort" and a seed in it. The stick stories read as pieces of that tree turning up in a hand. Aaron's rod has his name placed on it, and overnight it puts out buds, flowers and fruit (Numbers 17). A stick swung in anger, like Balaam's, or used in place of the word, gives nothing. The seed rule is the one to watch: what is set in its place with the right claim gives back after its kind. See what the rods and sticks mean.

The fig leaves and the skins: the stand

The man and the woman stand on the ground named Eden, without clothing (Genesis 2:25). In Genesis 3:7 they make a covering from leaves. Genesis 1:11-12 names the seed, the fruit and the tree, and never names leaves, so they are building from the one part of the plant the picture does not name. In 3:21 a different maker gives a covering of skin. The page follows who makes each covering, what it is made of, and what the man still says afterwards (3:10). The body that the identity stands in is the part that is changing. See the fig leaves and the skins.

Render unto Caesar: the stand again

Example, render unto Caesar. The image of Genesis 1:26 is now stamped on a coin, and the question is whose image it is. The image is the same, but it sits on a different thing. See what Jesus meant by "render unto Caesar".

Daniel 8: the time and the beasts

Genesis counts time as "evening and morning", for example "there was evening and there was morning, the fourth day" (1:19). Daniel 8 ends on a count in those same words: two thousand three hundred evenings and mornings (8:14). The beasts are in the scene too. In Genesis 1 the judges make the "great" lights and the "great" sea-beasts (1:16, 1:21). In Daniel the sheep, the he-goat and the horn each make themselves great (8:4, 8:8, 8:10), and the chapter never has the judges give it. See the ram and the goat in Daniel 8.

The whole picture at once: Luke and John

Luke starts where Genesis 3 ends, in the waste land with the line of Adam, and John starts at "In the beginning". Through both books the scene is put back piece by piece in the order Genesis built it: the word, the light, the sea and its fish, rule, food and the garden. Luke ends on the bread broken and the eyes opened, using the verb of Genesis 3:7. See how the Genesis scene changes through Luke and John, and the Emmaus story on its own at the road to Emmaus.

Which part moved?

  • Ruth 1: the ground, from House of Bread and Praise to Of His Father.
  • The temple curtain: the ground named first, then the lights failing.
  • Rods and sticks: the seed, a piece of the tree set in a hand.
  • Fig leaves and skins: the stand, the covering on the body.
  • Caesar's coin: the stand, the image on a different thing.
  • Daniel 8: the time and the beasts, counted in evenings and mornings.
  • Luke and John: all four, put back in order.

The same four parts turn up every time. Only the names and the arrangement change.

Three questions for any story:

  1. What is the ground, and what does its name mean?
  2. Who is standing on it, and what does their name mean?
  3. What has changed from the original picture in Genesis 1?

Remember: every story is the Genesis 1 scene, changed. New ground, new identity, same court.
