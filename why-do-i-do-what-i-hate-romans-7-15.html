Why Do I Do What I Hate? Romans 7:15 Worked Example With the Meanings Inserted

Short answer: the wish and the doing come from two different claims. The "I" in this passage takes pleasure in the Judges' rule and wishes for the good. But the claim being worked out is "missing the mark", and the Judges rule on the claim, not the wish. That is why the doing brings misery instead of pleasure. Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The passage (BBE):

And I have no clear knowledge of what I am doing, for that which I have a mind to do, I do not, but what I have hate for, that I do. ... So it is no longer I who do it, but the sin living in me. ... In my heart I take pleasure in the law of God, But I see another law in my body, working against the law of my mind, and making me the servant of the law of sin which is in my flesh. How unhappy am I! who will make me free from the body of this death? I give praise to God through Jesus Christ our Lord. So with my mind I am a servant to the law of God, but with my flesh to the law of sin. — Romans 7:15, 17, 22-25 (BBE)

The same passage with the meanings put in:

And I have no clear knowledge of what I am working out, for that which I choose to do, I do not, but what I detest, that I do. ... So it is no longer I who work it out, but the missing of the mark living in me. ... In my heart I take pleasure in the assigned rule of the Judges, But I see another assigned rule in my body, working against the assigned rule of my mind, and making me the servant of the assigned rule of missing the mark which is in my flesh. How miserable am I! who will make me free from the body of this death? I give praise to the Judges through the Existing One is salvation, the Anointed, our Existing One. So with my mind I am a servant to the assigned rule of the Judges, but with my flesh to the assigned rule of missing the mark.

The meanings used: Law is nomos (G3551), "that which is assigned". Sin is hamartia (G266), "missing the mark". The "doing" is katergazomai (G2716), "to work fully, accomplish". "Have a mind to do" is thelo (G2309), "to choose, prefer, wish". "Have hate for" is miseo (G3404), "to detest". "Take pleasure" is synedomai (G4913), "to rejoice in with oneself", built on the same root as hedone, the Greek word for pleasure. "Unhappy" is talaiporos (G5005), "enduring trial, miserable". Christ is "anointed" (G5547). Jesus (G2424) comes from the Hebrew Yehoshua, "Jehovah is salvation", read here as "the Existing One is salvation". Lord is kyrios (G2962), "supreme in authority", read here as the Existing One (YHVH, H3068), you aware right now. God is the Judges, as in Lesson 3.

Two notes on the meanings. First, Romans was written in Greek, so "the Judges" for God and "the Existing One" for Lord are this course's readings from Lessons 1 and 3, not words the Greek spells out. Second, the speaker's name does not appear in the passage, so nothing here is built on it.

How to read it, step by step:

  1. The frame (7:1-6). The chapter opens with a marriage. When the husband is dead the wife is free and may be "joined to another" (verses 2-4). That is the Lesson 8 rule: a ruling lasts while the state it rules is alive.
  2. The wish and the doing (7:15). The wish is a choice. The doing is a different word: "work fully, accomplish". Verse 8 uses the same word for sin "working in me every form of desire". So what is being worked out is not the wish. The Judges rule on the claim, not the wish (Lesson 1).
  3. "No longer I" (7:17). Sin is "missing the mark". It is first set out in Lesson 17, "waiting at the door", with the man told to rule it (Genesis 4:7). Here it "lives in me" and he is "the servant" of it. The Judges are not the fault: the law is "holy" (verse 12) and "good" (verse 16).
  4. Pleasure and misery (7:22-25). Pleasure is where the identity first stood: Eden means "pleasure" (Lesson 17). In verse 22 he takes pleasure in the Judges' rule. Then the other claim does the ruling, and verse 24 is "How unhappy am I!" Verse 25 ends with both still named. The way out has the shape of verse 4: the old state dies and another is joined (Lesson 8).

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. In Genesis 1 the Judges look at what they have made and call it good, the identity stands in pleasure, and man rules the creatures:

And God said, Let us make man in our image, like us: and let him have rule over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the air and over the cattle and over all the earth — Genesis 1:26 (BBE)
PartGenesis 1Romans 7
Good"God saw everything which he had made and it was very good" (Genesis 1:31)"in me, that is, in my flesh, there is nothing good" (Romans 7:18)
PleasureThe man is put in Eden, "pleasure" (Genesis 2:8)"In my heart I take pleasure in the law of God" (7:22), then "How unhappy am I!" (7:24)
What is spoken"Let there be light: and there was light" (Genesis 1:3)"the good which I have a mind to do, I do not" (7:19)
RuleMan to "have rule over" the creatures (Genesis 1:26)"making me the servant of the law of sin" (7:23)

The Judges, their rule and the pleasure are all still there. What changed is the claim being worked out. In Genesis 1 what the Judges speak is done, and it is good. In Romans 7 the wish is spoken and not done, because another claim is being worked out, and that claim brings no pleasure.

Compare the serpent. Verses 7-11 retell Genesis 3. The commandment about desire uses the same Hebrew word as "to be desired" in Genesis 3:6, and "tricked" in verse 11 is the word 2 Corinthians 11:3 uses of Eve. Lesson 23 shows the man answering a creature he was made to rule. Romans 7 shows the same reversal from the inside.

Remember: the wish is a choice. The doing is the claim being worked out. The Judges rule on the claim, and pleasure follows the claim that matches Genesis 1.
