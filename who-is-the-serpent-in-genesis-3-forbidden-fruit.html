Who Is the Serpent in Genesis 3? Worked Example With the Meanings Inserted

Short answer: the serpent is a made creature that man was meant to rule, and the fruit is judging by how things look. In this scene the man listens to the creature instead of ruling it. Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The passage (BBE):

And the Lord God made a garden in the east, in Eden; and there he put the man whom he had made. ... Now the snake was wiser than any beast of the field which the Lord God had made. And he said to the woman, Has God truly said that you may not take of the fruit of any tree in the garden? ... For God sees that on the day when you take of its fruit, your eyes will be open, and you will be as gods, having knowledge of good and evil. And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and a delight to the eyes, and to be desired to make one wise, she took of its fruit, and gave it to her husband. — Genesis 2:8, 3:1, 3:5-6 (BBE)

The same passage with the meanings put in:

And the Existing One, the Judges made a garden in the east, in Pleasure; and there he put the man whom he had made. ... Now the hisser was more crafty than any beast of the field which the Existing One, the Judges had made. And he said to the woman, Have the Judges truly said that you may not take of the fruit of any tree in the garden? ... For the Judges see that on the day when you take of its fruit, your eyes will be open, and you will be as the Judges, having knowledge of good and evil. And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and a delight to the eyes, and to be desired to make one wise, she took of its fruit, and gave it to her husband.

The meanings used: Eden is "pleasure" (H5731). The snake, nachash, is "a snake, from its hiss" (H5175). Wiser is arum, "crafty" or "cunning" (H6175). The Lord (YHVH) is "the existing One" (H3068). God (Elohim) is the plural "judges", as in Lesson 3.

Two notes on the meanings. First, arum is used 11 times. Genesis 3:1 is the only time it describes a creature. The other uses are people, mostly "prudent" in Proverbs, so the word is not always bad. Second, arum sounds almost the same as the word for "naked" in Genesis 2:25, arummim (H6174). The lexicons disagree about whether the two share a root, so the course treats it only as a sound link and builds nothing on it.

How to read it, step by step:

  1. The creature. Genesis 3:1 says the Existing One and the Judges made it, and Genesis 2:19-20 shows the man naming the beasts of the field, which is how a ruler handles them. It was made in the scene Genesis 1 called good. What changes is the order: the creature speaks and the man answers. Dominion is not taken from him. He hands it over by listening.
  2. The question. It does not give an order. It asks, and it changes the ruling. Genesis 2:16-17 allowed every tree but one, yet the snake says "any tree". The woman answers with "put your hands on it" (3:3), which is not in 2:17. The claim has already drifted.
  3. The fruit. The woman judges the tree by its looks. But every tree in Eden already looked good: "delighting the eye and good for food" (Genesis 2:9, BBE). The tree gives after its kind (Lesson 7), so its fruit is the habit of calling things good or evil by how they look. Genesis 3:22 confirms they took the Judges' seat: "Now the man has become like one of us, having knowledge of good and evil."
  4. The ruling. The man hides, then blames the woman, and she blames the snake. The Existing One asks "Where are you?" (3:9). The ground is cursed (3:17), and the man is sent out of Pleasure (3:23). The Judges make real what was claimed. The creature is then put back under: it is cursed and told "you will go flat on the earth" (3:14), and the war that follows ends with its head crushed by the woman's seed (3:15).

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. Genesis 1 puts man over the creatures:

And God said, Let us make man in our image, like us: and let him have rule over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the air and over the cattle and over all the earth — Genesis 1:26 (BBE)
PartGenesis 1Genesis 3
Creatures"God saw that it was good" (Genesis 1:25)A beast of the field speaks first (Genesis 3:1)
RuleMan to "have rule over" the creatures (Genesis 1:26)The man and woman take the creature's word (Genesis 3:5-6)
Seeing"God saw everything which he had made and it was very good" (Genesis 1:31)The woman sees the tree is "good for food, and a delight to the eyes" (Genesis 3:6)
FoodSeed-bearing trees "for your food" (Genesis 1:29)One tree's fruit taken against the ruling (Genesis 2:17, 3:6)
GroundThe dry land named Earth, and "good" (Genesis 1:10)"the earth is cursed on your account" (Genesis 3:17)

The scene keeps all its parts: creatures, trees, fruit, ground. What changed is who is in charge. In Genesis 1 man rules the creatures. In Genesis 3 a creature gets him to follow its question.

Compare Job and Jonah. The same reversal runs there. In Job the sea creatures run wild instead of being ruled, and in Jonah a great fish has the man. In each story the man is under a creature, not over it.

Remember: man is made to rule the creatures. In Genesis 3 he listens to one instead, and the ruling follows what he submitted to. The fruit is judging by looks.
