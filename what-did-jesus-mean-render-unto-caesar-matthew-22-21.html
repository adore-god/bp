What Did Jesus Mean by 'Render Unto Caesar'? Matthew 22:15-22

Short answer: Jesus does not say yes or no to the tax. He asks whose image is on the coin. They say Caesar's, so the coin goes back to Caesar. The same test then applies to the other half: what carries the image of the Judges (Genesis 1:26-27) is the Judges'.

"Render unto Caesar the things that are Caesar's" is Matthew 22:21, and the same scene is in Mark 12:13-17 and Luke 20:20-26. People ask whether Jesus is telling them to pay tax, or to split life between two masters. In this course the answer is in the question he asks instead of the one he is given: whose image is on it? Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The meanings used

  • Pharisees (pharisaios, G5330): "a separatist, i.e. exclusively religious", from the Hebrew word for "to separate".
  • Herodians (hērōdianoi, G2265): "partisans of Herod".
  • Jesus (Iesous, G2424): from the Hebrew Yehoshua, "Jehovah is salvation". Jehovah is YHVH, "the existing One" (Lesson 2), so the name reads Existing-One-is-salvation.
  • Caesar (kaisar, G2541): "a title of the Roman emperor" in Strong's. Blue Letter Bible's entry gives the name as "severed", and that is the meaning used here.
  • False ones (hypokrites, G5273): "an actor under an assumed character".
  • God (theos, G2316): "a deity", and also "figuratively, a magistrate". In this course it is read as the Judges (Lesson 3).
  • Image (eikon, G1504): "a likeness, i.e. (literally) statue, profile, or (figuratively) representation, resemblance".
  • Inscription (epigraphe, G1923): "an inscription, title". The BBE renders it "name" in 22:20.
  • Give back (apodidomi, G591): "to give away, i.e. up, over, back". The question uses a different word for give, covered below.

How to read it, step by step

  1. The names set the scene. The Pharisees are "separatists", they bring Herod's partisans, and the question is about tax to Caesar, "Severed". Everything in it is cut in two: yes or no, Caesar or God. "Is it right to give tax to Caesar, or not?" (22:17, BBE). It is the next attempt by the same people who, in the chapter before, "had a desire to take him" (21:46, BBE).
  2. Actors in an assumed character: why it was a trap. They flatter him first: "we see that you are true" and you "have no fear of anyone" (22:16, BBE). Their meeting was "to take him" (22:15, BBE), so either answer would give them something to use. Jesus "saw their trick" and calls them "false ones" (22:18, BBE). The Greek word means "an actor under an assumed character". They come in the character of admiring disciples and hold a different aim. So the scene begins with a mind showing one identity while holding another.
  3. He changes the question. He does not say yes or no. "Let me see the tax money. And they gave him a penny" (22:19, BBE). He does not produce the coin. They do. Then he asks a different kind of question: "Whose is this image and name on it?" (22:20, BBE). They asked whether to give, which is a question about an act. He asks whose, which is a question about what is stamped on a thing.
  4. The image decides. "They say to him, Caesar's" (22:21, BBE). They answer it themselves, and the text does not say which Caesar. The coin carries Caesar's image, so it is Caesar's, and the answer is "give back", not "give". Giving back what carries the owner's image is not losing anything. Then the same test on the other half: "and to God the things which are God's" (22:21, BBE). The text does not list them, but it has just given the test: what carries whose image? The first image in the Bible is in Genesis 1: "Let us make man in our image" (Genesis 1:26, BBE). So what carries the image of the Judges is the Judges'. In this course that is you, the one made in the image, and the Judges are the standing rulers of your own mind (the Bible as a courtroom). See also the Bible's court language.
  5. Why they went away. "And hearing it, they were full of wonder, and went away from him" (22:22, BBE). They came "to take him" (22:15, BBE) and they "went away from him" (22:22, BBE). His answer is behind neither door of their question, because it moves the question from the tax to the image. That is the name acted out: Existing-One-is-salvation, you aware right now, is the way out of a cut with only two doors.

What the usual reading misses

  1. The Greek has two words for give. The KJV says "render", the NIV says "give back" and the BBE says "give", but the Greek has two verbs. The question uses the plain word for give (dounai, G1325) in Matthew 22:17, Mark 12:14 and Luke 20:22. The answer uses apodidomi (G591) in Matthew 22:21, Mark 12:17 and Luke 20:25. That is the word used for paying back what is owed (Matthew 18:30; 21:41; Luke 12:59). So the question is about giving, and the answer is about giving back.
  2. The image is the Genesis 1:26 vocabulary. The Greek eikon (G1504) is the word on the coin in Matthew 22:20, Mark 12:16 and Luke 20:24. Thayer's lexicon notes that the Greek Old Testament mostly uses it for the Hebrew tselem (H6754), the word for "image" in Genesis 1:26-27. So "whose image?" is asked in the vocabulary of the creation of man.
  3. The coin carries a name as well as an image. The BBE says "image and name" (22:20). The Greek word, epigraphe, is used only five times in the New Testament: three times of this coin (Matthew 22:20; Mark 12:16; Luke 20:24) and twice of the writing put over Jesus on the cross (Mark 15:26; Luke 23:38). In this course a name tells the state a thing belongs to (Lesson 9).

Matthew, Mark and Luke

Mark adds a second question after the first: "Are we to give or not to give?" (Mark 12:15, BBE). All three accounts use the same three Greek words, set out below. The BBE words Luke 20:22 as "make payment of taxes", but the Greek verb there is the same plain word for give (dounai).

Check this yourself

  1. Read Matthew 22:15-22 in the BBE, with the chapter around it.
  2. Look up G1325 (dounai) and G591 (apodidomi) in Strong's. Note which word is in the question and which is in the answer.
  3. Look up G1504 (eikon) and G1923 (epigraphe), and see where each is used.
  4. Ask whether the reading fits the words on the page. If it does not, say so.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. The same image is here, but it sits on a different thing. In the Genesis 1 word list, man and the image belong to day six, the identity itself.

In Genesis 1 the image is on the one made, and rule comes with it. Here the image is on a coin, a thing that can be handed over, and the test is the same: whatever carries an image is marked as belonging to the one whose image it is.

Remember: the question was whether to give, and the answer is whose image. What carries Caesar's image goes back to Caesar. What carries the image of the Judges, the one made in Genesis 1:26-27, is the Judges'.

Common questions

What did Jesus mean by 'render unto Caesar'?

Jesus does not answer yes or no to the tax question. He asks whose image is on the coin. They say Caesar's, so it goes back to Caesar. In this course, what carries the image of the Judges (Genesis 1:26-27) is the Judges'.

Is 'render unto Caesar' about paying taxes?

The question put to Jesus was about tax, but his answer does not say 'pay' or 'do not pay'. He changes the question to whose image is on the coin. Some readers take the verse as an instruction to pay tax and others say it is not about tax at all.

Is 'render unto Caesar' about church and state?

The phrase is widely quoted that way, but the passage itself names no church and no state. It names a coin, an image, Caesar, and the Judges (theos, 'God'), and it applies one test: whose image is it?

What is the difference between 'give' and 'render' in Matthew 22?

The KJV says 'render', the NIV says 'give back' and the BBE says 'give', but the Greek has two words. The question in 22:17 uses the plain word for give (dounai, G1325). The answer in 22:21 uses apodidomi (G591), 'to give away, i.e. up, over, back'. The question is about giving, and the answer is about giving back.

What are 'the things that are God's' in Matthew 22:21?

The passage does not list them. It gives the test: whose image is it? The first image in the Bible is in Genesis 1:26-27, so in this course what carries the image of the Judges is the Judges'.

Whose image and inscription was on the coin in Matthew 22:20?

Caesar's (Matthew 22:21). The BBE words the question as 'image and name'. The Greek word for the second part is epigraphe, 'inscription' (G1923).
