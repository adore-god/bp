What Did Jesus Mean by 'Render Unto Caesar'? Matthew 22:15-22

"Render unto Caesar the things that are Caesar's" is Matthew 22:21, and the same scene is in Mark 12:13-17 and Luke 20:20-26. People ask whether Jesus is telling them to pay tax, or to split life between two masters. In this course the answer is in the question he asks instead of the one he is given: whose image is on it? Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The passage (BBE, Matthew 22:15-22):

Then the Pharisees went and had a meeting to see how they might make use of his words to take him. (22:15) And they sent to him their disciples, with the Herodians, saying, Master, we see that you are true, and that you are teaching the true way of God, and have no fear of anyone, because you have no respect for a man's position. (22:16) Give us, then, your opinion of this: Is it right to give tax to Caesar, or not? (22:17) But Jesus saw their trick and said, Oh false ones, why are you attempting to put me in the wrong? (22:18) Let me see the tax money. And they gave him a penny. (22:19) And he said to them, Whose is this image and name on it? (22:20) They say to him, Caesar's. Then he said to them, Give to Caesar the things which are Caesar's, and to God the things which are God's. (22:21) And hearing it, they were full of wonder, and went away from him. (22:22) — Matthew 22:15-22 (BBE)

The same verses with the meanings put in:

Then the separatists went and had a meeting to see how they might make use of his words to take him. (22:15) And they sent to him their disciples, with Herod's partisans, saying, Master, we see that you are true, and that you are teaching the true way of the Judges, and have no fear of anyone, because you have no respect for a man's position. (22:16) Give us, then, your opinion of this: Is it right to give tax to Severed, or not? (22:17) But Existing-One-is-salvation saw their trick and said, Oh actors in an assumed character, why are you attempting to put me in the wrong? (22:18) Let me see the tax money. And they gave him a denarius. (22:19) And he said to them, Whose is this image and inscription on it? (22:20) They say to him, Severed's. Then he said to them, Give back to Severed the things which are Severed's, and to the Judges the things which are the Judges'. (22:21) And hearing it, they were full of wonder, and went away from him. (22:22)

The meanings used: Pharisee is pharisaios (G5330), "a separatist, i.e. exclusively religious", from the Hebrew root for "to separate" (H6567). The Herodians are hērōdianoi (G2265), "partisans of Herod". Jesus is Iesous (G2424), from the Hebrew Yehoshua (H3091), and the Blue Letter Bible gives it as "Jehovah is salvation". Jehovah is YHVH, "the existing One" (Lesson 2), so the name reads Existing-One-is-salvation. Caesar is kaisar (G2541), of Latin origin, "a title of the Roman emperor", so like Pharaoh it is a title and not a personal name, and the Blue Letter Bible name entry glosses it "severed". Image is eikon (G1504), "a likeness, i.e. (literally) statue, profile, or (figuratively) representation, resemblance". Inscription is epigraphe (G1923). The tax is kensos (G2778), "properly, an enrollment ("census"), i.e. (by implication) a tax", and the penny is denarion (G1220), a denarius, also of Latin origin. The false ones are hypokrites (G5273), "an actor under an assumed character". God is theos (G2316), which Strong's gives as "a deity" and also "figuratively, a magistrate", and it is the word the Greek Genesis uses for the Judges in Genesis 1:26 (Lesson 3).

Three notes on the meanings. First, "render" is the KJV's word. The BBE says "give" both in the question (22:17) and in the answer (22:21), but the Greek has two words. The question uses the plain word for give (dounai, from didomi, G1325). The answer uses apodidomi (G591), "to give away, i.e. up, over, back", the word used for paying back what is owed (Matthew 18:30; Luke 12:59). Second, the BBE says "name" in 22:20, where the Greek says epigraphe, "an inscription", which Thayer's lexicon says can be the inscription on a coin. The word is used five times (Matthew 22:20; Mark 12:16; 15:26; Luke 20:24; 23:38). So the coin carries two marks, an image and an inscription. Third, the common readings say either that Jesus is telling people to pay tax, or that he is dividing life into two realms. The text never answers the yes or no he was asked, and it never lists what the things of God are. It gives a test, whose image, and the word image sends the reader back to Genesis 1:26-27.

How to read it, step by step:

  1. The names set the scene. The Pharisees are "separatists" (G5330), from the Hebrew word for "to separate". They bring Herod's partisans, and the question is about tax to Caesar, "Severed" (G2541). Everything in the question is cut in two: yes or no, Caesar or God. The one they ask is Jesus (G2424), "Jehovah is salvation", and Jehovah is YHVH, the Existing One, you aware right now. So the scene acts out his name: the Existing One is salvation, the way out of a cut that has only two doors.
  2. The question has two doors. "Then the Pharisees went and had a meeting to see how they might make use of his words to take him" (Matthew 22:15, BBE). The question can only be answered yes or no: "Is it right to give tax to Caesar, or not?" (22:17, BBE). The chapter before shows the same aim. In the temple the chief priests and those in authority asked "By what authority do you do these things?" (21:23, BBE), and after his stories the chief priests and the Pharisees "had a desire to take him" (21:46, BBE). So this is the next attempt, by the same people.
  3. Actors in an assumed character. They flatter him first: "we see that you are true" and you "have no fear of anyone" (22:16, BBE). Jesus "saw their trick" and calls them "false ones" (22:18, BBE). The Greek word is hypokrites (G5273), "an actor under an assumed character". They come in the character of admiring disciples and hold a different aim. So the scene begins with a mind showing one identity while holding another.
  4. He changes the question. He does not say yes or no. "Let me see the tax money. And they gave him a penny" (22:19, BBE). He does not produce the coin. They do. Then he asks a different kind of question: "Whose is this image and name on it?" (22:20, BBE). They asked whether to give, which is a question about an act. He asks whose, which is a question about what is stamped on a thing.
  5. The image decides. "They say to him, Caesar's" (22:21, BBE). They answer it themselves. The coin carries Caesar's image and inscription, so it is Caesar's. Then the verb changes: the answer is "give back" (apodidomi, G591), not "give". Giving back what carries the owner's image is not losing anything.
  6. The same test on the other half. "and to God the things which are God's" (22:21, BBE). The text does not list them, but it has just given the test: what carries whose image? The first image in the Bible is in Genesis 1: "Let us make man in our image" (Genesis 1:26, BBE) and "in the image of God he made him" (1:27, BBE). The old Greek translation of Genesis uses the same word, eikon, in both verses. So by the same test, what carries the image of the Judges is the Judges'. In this course that is you, the one made in the image, and the Judges are the standing rulers of your own mind. See the Bible's court language.
  7. Why they went away. "And hearing it, they were full of wonder, and went away from him" (22:22, BBE). They came "to take him" (22:15, BBE) and they "went away from him" (22:22, BBE). They came to make use of his words and left with none to use. His answer is behind neither door of their question, because it moves the question from the tax to the image. That is the name acted out: the Existing One is salvation, and the cut with two doors does not take him. The test was run on a coin, but it is the same test for the one asking.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. The same image is here, but it sits on a different thing.

PartGenesis 1This scene
The imageThe Judges make man "in our image" (Genesis 1:26)An image and an inscription stamped on a coin (Matthew 22:20)
What it marksThe one made carries the Judges' image, and rule goes with it: "let him have rule" (Genesis 1:26)The coin carries Caesar's image, so it is Caesar's and goes back to him (Matthew 22:21)

What changed is what carries the image. In Genesis 1 the image is on the one made, and rule comes with it. Here the image is on a coin, a thing that can be handed over, and the test is the same: whatever carries an image is marked as belonging to the one whose image it is. Caesar's image is on the coin. The image of the Judges is on you.

Remember: the question was whether to give, and the answer is whose image. What carries Caesar's image goes back to Caesar, and what carries the image of the Judges, the one made in Genesis 1:26-27, is the Judges'. The scene acts out the name Jesus, "Jehovah is salvation": the way out of a cut with two doors is the image. The coin was the test, and it is the same test for the one asking.
