Who Are the Nephilim? Genesis 6:1-4

The verse never says who they were. In this course the Nephilim are an identity, not a species: the bully that forms when your judging side starts choosing by looks. Read the passage first, then read it again with the meanings put in.

The passage (BBE):

And after a time, when men were increasing on the earth, and had daughters, The sons of God saw that the daughters of men were fair; and they took wives for themselves from those who were pleasing to them. And the Lord said, My spirit will not be in man for ever, for he is only flesh; so the days of his life will be a hundred and twenty years. There were men of great strength and size on the earth in those days; and after that, when the sons of God had connection with the daughters of men, they gave birth to children: these were the great men of old days, the men of great name. — Genesis 6:1-4 (BBE)

The same passage with the meanings put in:

And after a time, when the claims you make about yourself were increasing, and had daughters, the sons of the Judges and Rulers saw that the daughters of claims were fair; and they took wives for themselves from those who were pleasing to them. And the Existing One said, My spirit will not be in claims for ever, for he is only flesh; so the days of his life will be a hundred and twenty years. There were the Fellers on the earth in those days; and after that, when the sons of the Judges and Rulers had connection with the daughters of claims, they gave birth to children: these were the mighty men, the men of the name.

The meanings used: Elohim, the word behind "God", is "judges" and "rulers" (H430). The Lord (YHVH) is "the existing One" (H3068). Man, in this course, is the one who makes claims (Lesson 6). Nephilim is "a feller, i.e. a bully or tyrant" (H5303), from the root "to fall" (H5307). Mighty men is "powerful, warrior, tyrant" (H1368). Noah is "rest" (H5146).

Three notes on the meanings. First, the BBE leaves the word Nephilim out of verse 4 and says "men of great strength and size". The Hebrew word is there, and the same BBE keeps it in Numbers 13:33. Second, the lexicons say the exact sense of Nephilim is uncertain. This course uses "fellers", which is Strong's own gloss. Third, the common reading is angels and giants (the KJV says "giants"). The gap: Genesis 6 itself never says angels.

How to read it, step by step:

  1. The judge and the claims. Start with seed. In Genesis 1:11 seed brings forth after its kind, so a son or a daughter is what grows from an identity: something of the same kind as what spoke it. The sons of God are what the judging part of you (Lesson 1) brings forth. Psalm 82:6 shows it is spoken: "I said, You are gods; all of you are the sons of the Most High" (BBE). The daughters of men work the same way one step down: they are what your claims about yourself bring forth, and they are temporary: "he is only flesh" (Genesis 6:3). So these are not outside visitors. They are offspring, and offspring match their seed.
  2. The look. The sons of God "saw that the daughters of men were fair" and took wives "from those who were pleasing to them" (Genesis 6:2, BBE). They chose by how it looked. Psalm 82:2 names the fault: "having respect for the persons of evil-doers" (BBE). A judge who judges by the face is no longer judging.
  3. The fall. Nephilim is a plural word like Elohim, but built on fall instead of judge. So the court has a mirror: many judges, and many fellers. Psalm 82:7 says "falling like one of the rulers of the earth" (BBE), and the Hebrew for fall there is naphal (H5307), the same root. Where the judges rule by the word, the Nephilim rule by force.
  4. The name. The union produced "men of great name" (Genesis 6:4, BBE). A name is a reputation, not a mark of agreement (compare Genesis 17:11). The Nephilim also come back after the flood, in the spies' report: "we seemed to ourselves no more than insects, and so we seemed to them" (Numbers 13:33, BBE). The size comes from your own claim about yourself. A bloodline wiped out by water does not return. A state does.
  5. The way out. The passage ends with the court answering: a set time, "a hundred and twenty years" (Genesis 6:3), then the water (Genesis 6:17). The set time is a full measure: in this reading twelve is the whole bench of judges, and ten is a complete code. Then an agreement goes to Noah, rest: "But with you I will make an agreement" (Genesis 6:18, BBE). The way out is not a fight. It is an agreement and rest.

What changed from Genesis 1

Lesson 5 says every story is the Genesis 1 picture, changed. In Genesis 1 the judging side speaks an identity and the identity rules. Genesis 6 keeps the same parts, but they are changed.

PartGenesis 1This scene
GroundThe dry land and the seed after its kind (Genesis 1:11, day 3)The earth, "full of violent ways" (Genesis 6:11), with wives taken by sight (Genesis 6:2)
The judgesSpeak, and what they speak rulesLook at the face, and fall (Psalm 82:7)
What holds youThe creatures, under the man's rule (Genesis 1:26)The Nephilim hold you down: "no more than insects" (Numbers 13:33)
RestThe identity held as already done (day 7)Noah, "rest", who "had grace in the eyes of God" (Genesis 6:8)

The judging side was made to rule by the word. What changed is that it chose by looks, and what it bred was a name, not a mark. The court makes real what was offered, and the bully is only as big as the claim behind it.
